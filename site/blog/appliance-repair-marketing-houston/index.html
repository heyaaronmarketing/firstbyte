Saturday, 2 p.m., the second week of August. A family in Kingwood opens the fridge and it’s 52 degrees inside. Someone searches “refrigerator repair near me” and calls the first listing with decent stars. It says “Kingwood Appliance Repair.” It’s a call center two states away that sells the job to whichever tech it can reach, and the family pays a $129 “service fee” before anyone looks at the compressor.

You were 11 minutes away with an open slot. You never got the call.

Appliance repair marketing in Houston comes down to being the real, visible, answerable company in a category crowded with fakes and lead brokers. That means a Google Business Profile that proves you exist, Local Services Ads where you can get them, and search ads built on brand plus problem keywords that stay inside Google’s trademark rules. Clear diagnostic fees and honest parts timelines do the rest, along with a book of landlords and property managers who call you before anyone else.

Below is how we’d set it up for an independent or brand-authorized shop anywhere from Sugar Land to Conroe.

Brand-authorized or independent: pick the story you tell

The first marketing decision is what you are. Both models work in Houston, and they sell differently.

Brand-authorized shops get warranty dispatches and a credibility line they can use (“factory-authorized for [brand]”), but warranty work tends to pay less per job and comes with paperwork and parts rules set by the manufacturer. Independents can service everything, set their own prices and market harder on speed, but they have to earn trust without a logo.

Whatever you are, say it precisely. If you’re authorized for two brands, name those two and nothing more. “Factory trained” and “authorized” mean specific things to customers, and stretching them invites complaints, chargebacks and, if a manufacturer notices, a letter from their lawyers. Independents do better with “we repair all major brands, including [list],” which is true and descriptive.

A lot of Houston shops do both: warranty work for one or two brands to keep techs busy midweek, and direct work at full price for everything else. That’s fine. Just track the two separately so you know which one actually pays.

Warranty and home-warranty work: volume, not a marketing plan

Manufacturer warranty jobs and home-warranty company dispatches can fill a schedule. In the accounts we’ve seen across home services, they rarely build a business by themselves, because the customer belongs to the warranty company and the rate is set for you.

Use that volume deliberately:

  • Every warranty customer is a real person in a real Houston neighborhood who can leave a real review. Ask them the same way you ask direct customers.
  • Leave a magnet or a sticker with your direct number on every appliance you touch. The next repair, out of warranty, is often a direct call.
  • Use warranty jobs to fill gaps in routes near your direct customers, not to send a tech from Pearland to Tomball for a flat fee.

If more than half your revenue comes through warranty companies, the most valuable marketing you can do is anything that shifts that mix toward direct calls. That’s the rest of this post.

The fake-listing problem, and how real shops fight it

Appliance repair sits in the same corner of Google Maps as locksmiths and garage doors: urgent jobs, unfamiliar companies, lots of money in the call. That attracts fakes.

A a CBC Marketplace investigation found that in some areas up to 50% of appliance repair companies returned in a Google Maps search appeared to be fake, with local-sounding names pinned to coffee shops, banks and strip malls, and calls routed to a single call center. In March 2025, Google sued a network behind more than 10,000 illegitimate listings and said scams cluster in “duress verticals,” services people need urgently, according to CBS News. Unlike locksmith and garage door companies, appliance repair isn’t on Google’s restricted local services page that requires advanced verification to run search ads in the U.S., so lead brokers can still buy their way in.

You can’t out-fake the fakes, and you shouldn’t try. You win by being obviously real:

  • Follow Google’s Business Profile guidelines to the letter. Service-area businesses should hide their address, virtual offices aren’t eligible unless staffed during business hours, and the business name must match the real-world name without extra keywords. “Bayou City Appliance Repair Same Day Fridge Washer Dryer” is a suspension waiting to happen.
  • Upload photos a fake can’t produce: your techs by name, your trucks with your logo, your parts shelves, real jobs in real Houston kitchens.
  • Put your owner’s name, your license and certification details, and a physical photo of the team on your website’s About page.
  • Report fake listings near you with “Suggest an edit” in Google Maps. It’s slow, but it works over time.

Same-day service, diagnostic fees and parts delays

Callers ask three questions, in this order. Can you come today? What’s it going to cost to look at it? How long will the part take?

Answer all three on your website and on the phone before a competitor does.

Same-day. If you can do it, say so with conditions (“same-day when booked by noon, most of Houston and Spring”). If you can’t, say “next-day” and mean it. Customers forgive a later slot if it’s a real one.

Diagnostic fee. Publish it. State if the fee is credited toward the repair if they approve the work, and say it in one line on every landing page. The fake listings hide their fees until the tech arrives. Your transparency is the contrast.

Parts. This is where reviews go wrong. A control board on back-order for three weeks turns a five-star tech visit into a one-star review if nobody calls the customer. Text a parts update at a set rhythm, even when there’s no news. “Still waiting on the board from the distributor, next update Thursday” prevents more bad reviews than any reputation software.

Train whoever answers the phone to give the fee, the soonest real slot and a plain expectation on parts. Then book the visit before hanging up. Our speed-to-lead guide explains why the first company to answer usually gets the job.

Google Local Services Ads for appliance repair

Google’s Local Services Ads category list includes “Appliance repair services,” so Houston shops can run Local Services Ads in their own category. LSAs sit above regular search ads on many queries, you pay per lead (a call or message) instead of per click, and you can dispute leads that weren’t real customers.

The screening that comes with LSA is the point. Background checks plus license and insurance checks (which vary by category and state) all work against the fake-listing problem, and customers see the result in the ad. Ranking leans heavily on reviews and how fast you answer, so a shop with 200 reviews that picks up in three rings beats a bigger competitor with 40.

Set your LSA service area tighter than you think. A lead in Katy is no good to a shop in Humble on a Saturday. Choose the appliance job types you actually want (refrigerators and washers, maybe not built-in wine coolers), and set hours to match when someone answers. Google Ads: brand plus problem keywords, and the trademark rules

Appliance searches are unusually specific. People type the brand, the appliance and the symptom, sometimes the error code off the display. That’s a gift: every one of those searches tells you what the job is.

Now the trademark question. Under Google’s trademark policy, using brand names as keywords isn’t restricted. Ad text is different: if a trademark owner files a complaint, ads using that term in the headline or description can be restricted. The policy carves out resellers and informational sites, and the reseller exception explicitly covers “components, replacement parts, or compatible products or services corresponding to the trademark,” with the landing page making the relationship clear. The policy text doesn’t mention repair services by name, which is why repair ads with brand names sometimes get limited.

What we do in practice: brand names in keywords freely, brand names in ad text only when it’s descriptive and accurate (“We Repair Samsung Refrigerators”), never “Samsung Service” or “Official” anything, and a landing page that says plainly you’re independent (or exactly which brands you’re authorized for). If an ad gets limited, try the generic version of the headline and keep the brand in the keyword and on the page.

Error code pages are excellent SEO for appliance shops, by the way. A page that explains what “OE on an LG washer” means, what the owner can safely check, and when to call you ranks well and brings in exactly the right customer. Houston heat turns a dead fridge into an emergency

Appliance repair has a weather pattern here like everything else. Summer 2023 was the warmest summer on record for Houston, with the city hitting 109°F twice in August and triple digits on all but three days that month, per ABC13’s 2023 weather recap. Refrigerators and freezers in hot garages work hardest in exactly those weeks, and a failed compressor costs a family a week of groceries.

Power events add to it. After Beryl in July 2024, CenterPoint was working through 2.26 million outages, according to the Texas Tribune. In our experience across home services, the days after power returns bring a wave of calls about fridges, ice makers and electronics that don’t come back right.

So shift budget with the season. Raise refrigerator and ice maker bids from May through September and after any big outage. Push oven and range ads in the two weeks before Thanksgiving and before Christmas. Run dryer and washer campaigns steadily, since those don’t care about weather. And keep a short “after the power comes back” page ready for hurricane season.

Certifications and licenses: say what you hold

Two credentials come up most in appliance marketing.

For sealed-system refrigerator and freezer work, the EPA requires technicians who could release refrigerant to be certified, and Type I certification covers servicing small appliances, per EPA Section 608. “EPA 608 certified technicians” is a credibility line worth adding to refrigerator pages, as long as it’s true for the techs doing that work.

On the electrical side, Texas licenses residential appliance installers and installation contractors through TDLR, and TDLR’s electrician licensing exemptions notes that residential appliance dealers, manufacturers or their authorized designees performing electrical work while repairing or servicing an existing appliance are exempt from electrician licensing. Where your shop falls depends on how you’re set up, so check with TDLR before you put “licensed” in an ad. Precise beats vague here too.

Landlord and property manager accounts

Houston has a lot of rental homes and apartments, and every one has a fridge, a washer and a dishwasher that will fail at some point. Property managers don’t search Google each time. They call the vendor who made the last job easy.

What property managers in Houston, Katy and The Woodlands want from an appliance vendor is boring and specific: a not-to-exceed amount they can approve without a call, photos before and after, an invoice that names the unit and the tenant issue, and a tech who contacts the tenant directly to schedule. Bonus points if you’ll quote “repair vs. replace” with a real number, because many of them would rather replace a 14-year-old washer than pay to fix it twice.

Start with a vendor packet: W-9, certificate of insurance, service area, rates and diagnostic fee, response times, and an onboarding contact. Then email it to the 30 property management companies nearest your shop and follow up by phone. Reviews that sell the next call

Reviews are the clearest signal that you’re real, and the fakes either have none or a suspicious wall of five-star reviews posted the same week.

Ask every customer, by text, shortly after the repair is done, with the same link for everyone. Google’s policy bans incentives and review gating, and in April 2026 Google added language against telling staff to collect a set number of reviews or reviews with specific content, per PPC Land. So don’t script “please mention the Samsung fridge and Sugar Land.” Customers usually mention the appliance and the speed on their own. Our post on the FTC fake reviews rule covers what else is off limits.

Reply to every review. On a bad one about parts delays, explain what happened in a sentence and offer to call. The next customer reads that reply and decides if you’re the kind of company that communicates, which is the whole worry with appliance repair.

What to track, and a first 90 days

Track booked jobs by source and by appliance, completed versus canceled, average ticket, and the share of revenue from direct customers versus warranty dispatch. Record calls so you can hear which ones you lost and why. Our call tracking guide covers setup.

  • Days 1 to 30. Clean up the Business Profile (real name, hidden address if you’re a service-area business, real photos). Publish your diagnostic fee and same-day terms. Apply for Local Services Ads.
  • Days 31 to 60. Launch Google Ads with appliance and symptom ad groups and a full negative list. Start the review request flow. Write the first five error code or symptom pages for the brands you see most.
  • Days 61 to 90. Build and send the property manager packet. Set seasonal budget rules for summer and the holidays. Review lost calls and fix the phone script.

Frequently asked questions

How do appliance repair companies get more customers in Houston?

Look obviously real on Google. Keep a Business Profile with your real name, real photos and steady reviews, apply for Local Services Ads in the appliance repair category, and run search ads on appliance and symptom keywords with brand names where they're accurate. Publish your diagnostic fee and same-day terms, and build accounts with property managers who send repeat work.

Can I use brand names like Samsung or Whirlpool in my Google Ads?

Using brand names as keywords isn't restricted under Google's trademark policy. Ad text is different: if a trademark owner has filed a complaint, ads using the term can be limited. The reseller exception covers replacement parts and compatible products or services, so keep usage descriptive, such as We Repair Samsung Refrigerators. Never say official or authorized unless the manufacturer has actually authorized you.

Is there a Local Services Ads category for appliance repair?

Yes. Google's Local Services Ads list includes Appliance repair services. You pay per lead rather than per click, and businesses go through background, license and insurance screening, which helps separate real Houston shops from lead brokers. Ranking depends heavily on reviews and responsiveness, so answer every lead quickly and keep your service area tight enough to reach customers the same day.

Why are there so many fake appliance repair listings on Google Maps?

Appliance repair is an urgent, high-value job, so lead brokers create local-sounding listings and route calls to call centers. A CBC investigation found up to half of appliance repair listings in some areas appeared fake, and Google sued a network behind more than 10,000 fake listings in 2025. Report suspect listings with Suggest an edit and make your own profile unmistakably real.

Should an appliance repair company publish its diagnostic fee?

Yes. Callers want to know what it costs to have someone look at the appliance, and many fake listings hide their fees until the tech arrives. Publish the fee, say whether it is credited toward the repair if the customer approves the work, and have whoever answers the phone say the same thing. Clear fees reduce no-shows and bad reviews in Houston.
