Picture Monday morning at a two-bay detail shop off FM 2978. The owner scrolls through the weekend’s messages: eleven people asking “how much for a wash,” two asking if he does headlights, one asking for a quote on a full front PPF for a new Tahoe. The Tahoe guy never hears back until Tuesday. By then he’s booked somewhere in Spring.

Cheap work finds a detail shop on its own. The $1,800 ceramic coating and the $2,500 front-end PPF don’t, and most of auto detailing marketing in The Woodlands and Houston is about closing that gap. Those buyers search with different words and want a reply the same evening. They also want proof you’ve done this exact job on this exact kind of car. We’ve built ad accounts for service businesses all over north Houston, and the detailing ones fail in a pretty predictable way. They spend like a car wash and price like a body shop.

Below is how we’d set it up if you handed us the keys, for a fixed shop or a mobile rig.

Why the phone rings for washes and not for coatings

Wash and interior customers are easy to find and hard to keep. They shop on price, they book whoever answers, and a lot of them would rather drive through a $25 tunnel next month. Coating and PPF buyers can spend two or three weeks researching, watching install videos and asking a Tesla or Bronco owners’ Facebook group who in Houston does good work. Usually there’s one specific car involved, and they just spent a lot of money on it.

So you’re running two businesses with one logo. Maintenance keeps the bays full and the techs paid, while the margin sits almost entirely on the protection side. We’d market the cheap services as the way in.

A rough map of the tiers follows. The price ranges are ballpark figures to frame the conversation; check what shops near you actually publish before you set yours.

How to get more ceramic coating customers from Google Ads

Search is still the best place to catch a coating buyer, because they type what they want. “Ceramic coating near me,” “ceramic coating the woodlands,” “XPEL installer houston,” “PPF Model Y cost.” Those are hand-raisers. “Car wash near me” is a different person with a different budget.

Split the account so washes can’t eat the coating money. We’d run a PPF campaign with keywords around PPF, clear bra, the film brands you’re authorized for and model-specific searches (“PPF for Tesla,” “Bronco PPF”). Coating and paint correction gets its own campaign, built on the coating brands you install plus “ceramic coating cost” and “paint correction near me.” Regular detailing goes in a third, with a lower budget cap and tighter hours. If you only do interiors on Saturdays, the detailing campaign shouldn’t be bidding on Wednesday night.

Use phrase and exact match to start. Broad match can work once you have a steady flow of tracked conversions for it to learn from (we like to see 30 or more a month); most single-location shops never get there on coatings alone, and we’d rather you didn’t pay for that education.

The negatives that save the most money

Coating searches are full of do-it-yourselfers. Someone searching “best ceramic coating spray” wants a $25 bottle from AutoZone, not you. Add these as negatives on day one, then read your search terms report every week for a month:

One detail that catches people: Google’s own help page on negative keywords says negatives don’t match close variants. If you add “kit,” you also need “kits.” If you add “diy,” add “do it yourself.” Write out the plurals.

Under Locations, use “Presence” so you’re not paying for someone in Dallas reading about Houston shops. Mobile detailers should draw the radius by real drive time; 25 minutes from Shenandoah gets you into Spring and Conroe on a weekday morning, but the same radius on a Friday at 5 p.m. is fiction. And put a bid adjustment or schedule on the coating campaign around the hours someone can pick up the phone.

A coating lead that goes to voicemail at 7 p.m. is often gone by morning.

What about Local Services Ads? Google keeps reshuffling the automotive categories (Search Engine Land’s rundown of the 2026 category changes covers the August 2026 shake-up, which split old “auto repair” into narrower types). Check whether a detailing category is live for your ZIP when you read this, but we wouldn’t build the plan around it.

Budget-wise, a single-location shop around The Woodlands can learn a lot from $1,500 to $3,000 a month on the PPF and coating campaigns. That’s an example range, not a promise; your cost per click depends on how many installers are bidding in your ZIPs that month. Our post on Houston cost-per-click benchmarks has more on that.

Google Business Profile photos and video that sell paint work

After the ad, a coating buyer goes to your Business Profile and looks at the photos before the prices. A grid of blurry phone shots of dirty floor mats says “wash guy.”

Pick “Car detailing service” as the primary category if that’s your main business, and add secondary categories only for work you really do (window tint, for example). Then load the gallery with protection work. Coated hoods under a swirl-finder light. A PPF edge wrapped cleanly around a headlight. A clean bay, if yours stays clean, which is rarer than you’d think.

Most shops ignore video on the profile. Per Google’s Business Profile photo and video rules, profile videos can run up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher. Thirty seconds is plenty for a water-beading shot on a fresh coating or a slow walk around a satin PPF wrap. The same page says photos should “represent reality,” so skip heavy filters. A buyer will notice when their car doesn’t look like your Instagram.

Mobile detailers: if customers don’t come to you, set up as a service-area business and hide the home address. Listing your apartment as a storefront is the fastest way we know to get a profile suspended (we wrote about getting reinstated if that already happened).

Add your coating and PPF packages as Products or Services with a “starting at” price, because the people comparing three shops at 10 p.m. want a number. Posting a fresh job every week or two also helps, if only because a profile whose newest photo is from 2023 looks closed.

Before-and-after content on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube

Detailing is one of the few local trades where the content basically makes itself. A 50/50 tape line on a swirled black hood will stop a thumb faster than anything a copywriter can come up with.

Most of it gets shot badly, though. The before is from the left fender under fluorescent light, the after is from the hood outside at noon, and one of them is vertical. Viewers can’t tell what changed, or they assume you edited it.

Instagram Reels and TikTok are for reach and for the “oh, that’s satisfying” reaction. Don’t get excited by 80,000 views if 79,000 of them came from Ohio; for a local shop, what matters is the handful of comments from Spring and Kingwood. YouTube is where serious PPF buyers go to research. A seven-minute “full front PPF on a 2026 Silverado, start to finish” video will keep getting found by people typing nearly that exact phrase for years.

If you’re paying to boost anything on Meta, boost your best before-and-after to a radius around the shop and send it to a quote form, not your profile grid. Our guide to paid social covers the setup, and our platform comparison helps if you only have time for one.

A small admission: we can’t tell you which hook will work on your audience. Nobody can. Shoot four versions of the same job, run them for a week each at a small budget, and keep the one that gets quote requests.

Lovebug season, sun and dust: a Houston detailing calendar

Houston hands detailers a better seasonal story than most cities, and it’s easy to oversell.

Lovebugs are the obvious one. The Texas A&M Forest Service says Texas gets two generations a year, with adults usually emerging in May and September, and that the “slightly acidic body fluids of smashed lovebugs will damage paint on vehicles if not removed within a few days.” AgriLife Today gives the same May and September timing. That is about as clean a reason to sell a coating or front-end PPF as you’ll find, and it comes twice a year.

We’d launch lovebug ads and posts two to three weeks before each flight, so mid-April and mid-August, showing a bug-splattered bumper and then the same bumper wiping clean after a coating. Budget goes up in those windows and comes back down in the slow stretch after the holidays.

Summer gets its own angle. The National Weather Service’s 1991 to 2020 normal high at Bush Intercontinental is 94.5°F in July and 94.9°F in August (National Weather Service normals for Bush Intercontinental). A car parked outside a Hughes Landing office all day takes that sun, and owners know it. Talk about protecting the finish and making washing easier, and stop short of promising a coating prevents all fading, because you’d have to stand behind that.

And then there’s what every north Houston driver sees: spring pollen coating everything under the oaks, and construction dust from the endless widening along the Grand Parkway and I-45. You don’t need a statistic to use those. A photo of a yellow-green windshield in March says it for you.

Reaching new-car buyers before the dealership’s coating package does

The best coating customer is someone who took delivery in the last 30 days. The paint is still clean, they haven’t built a washing habit yet, and they care a lot. The problem is that the dealership’s finance office already tried to sell them a “paint protection package.” Some bought it. Many said no and are now wondering if they should have.

New owners search with the model name, so build model-specific landing pages: “Model Y PPF in The Woodlands,” “F-150 Lightning ceramic coating.” Beyond that:

  • Local Facebook groups and forums for specific models. Be useful there first; nobody wants a coupon dropped in a thread.
  • Salespeople. Some will pass along a card if you take care of their customers. Ask what the dealership allows before offering anyone a referral fee.
  • New-home neighborhoods. Master-planned communities on the west and north sides keep adding families with new garages and often new cars. A geo-fenced campaign around a busy dealership row is another option, though we’d test it small.

We’d skip trying to target “recently bought a car” on Meta. Purchase-based targeting has been thin there for years, and what’s left is too vague to bet a budget on. Model-specific creative pointed at a local radius does the filtering better.

Certified installer status and warranties in your ads

A coating buyer pays for your hands and for the manufacturer’s promise behind them. Put both in the ad.

Manufacturer programs exist for exactly this reason. XPEL’s warranty page lists 10 years of coverage on Ultimate Plus and Stealth film, starting at installation, and the coverage applies to film that’s professionally installed, with claims handled through the authorized installer who did the work. Ceramic Pro’s Elite Dealer announcement described dealers who pass factory training and carry Ceramic Pro and KAVACA products exclusively. Whatever brands you carry, put the certification in your headline if you have it. “Authorized XPEL installer in The Woodlands” beats “Best PPF in town” every time, because one is checkable.

Before you bid on the manufacturer’s name in Google Ads, reread your brand agreement. Some brands have rules about how dealers use their trademarks, and finding out from a cease-and-desist is no fun.

Warranty language also has federal guidance behind it. The FTC’s guides on advertising warranties say an ad mentioning a warranty should let buyers see the written terms before the sale, and that “lifetime” claims should disclose whose life or what life they mean. “Lifetime coating” on its own invites complaints. Say “lifetime of your ownership, registered with the manufacturer” if that’s what the paperwork says.

Link the actual warranty PDF from your coating page. It’s a small thing that tells a careful buyer you’re not hiding anything.

Reviews that mention the car, the product and the town

A five-star review that says “great job!!” helps a little. One that says “Full front XPEL on my 4Runner, edges wrapped perfectly, they’re 10 minutes from my house in Spring” reads as real to the next buyer and puts the words people search for right on your profile.

You can’t write reviews for customers, and you shouldn’t offer discounts for good ones (the FTC rule on that is covered in our post on fake review rules). Timing is the part you control. We’d ask at delivery, after the walkaround, when the customer is standing next to a car that looks better than the day they bought it. Text the review link right then, with one line suggesting they mention the car and what you did.

Reply to every review, including the 3-star from the wash customer who wanted it done in 40 minutes. Future coating buyers read the replies to see how you handle problems. Our full guide to Google reviews goes deeper.

Where to start this week

Open Google Ads, go to the search terms report, set the date range to the last 90 days and sort by cost. Read the top 50 terms out loud. If you hear “spray,” “kit,” “how to” or “jobs,” you’ve found money to move into your PPF campaign before lovebug season.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more ceramic coating customers?

Separate coating marketing from wash marketing. Run a Google Ads campaign just for coating and PPF keywords with DIY negatives, fill your Business Profile with protection work instead of floor mats, post consistent before-and-after video, and name your manufacturer certification and warranty in ads. Then answer coating leads fast, because those buyers usually get three quotes in the same evening.

Are Google Ads worth it for an auto detailing business?

For high-ticket work like PPF, paint correction and ceramic coating, usually yes, because one booked job can pay for weeks of clicks. For basic washes, usually not. Searchers there shop on price and many will book a tunnel wash instead. Keep the two in separate campaigns with separate budgets so cheap searches don't drain the money meant for coating leads.

What negative keywords should a detailing business use?

Start with diy, do it yourself, spray, kit, kits, how to, amazon, walmart, autozone, best product, review, jobs, hiring, salary, course, training and free. Add plurals yourself, because Google's negative keywords don't match close variants. Then read your search terms report weekly for the first month and add anything that clearly isn't a paying customer.

Should a mobile detailer put an address on Google Business Profile?

Not if customers don't come to that location. Set the profile up as a service-area business, list the cities or ZIP codes you actually drive to, and hide the street address. Showing a home or apartment as a storefront breaks Google's guidelines and is a common reason detailing profiles get suspended.

When is the best time to advertise ceramic coating in Houston?

Plan around the two lovebug flights. Texas A&M Forest Service says adults usually emerge in May and September, and their acidic remains can damage paint within a few days. Start coating and front-end PPF ads two to three weeks earlier. Summer heat and spring pollen give you secondary angles, and new-car season around year-end helps too.
