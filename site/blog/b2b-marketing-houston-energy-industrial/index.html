B2B Marketing in Houston for Energy, Industrial and Oilfield Services

The sales VP at a 60-person valve and actuator distributor off the Sam Houston Tollway (a hypothetical, but you’ve met him) forwards the monthly marketing report. 212 leads. He replies with one line: “Which of these turned into a PO?”

Nobody can answer.

We see some version of that at most Houston energy, industrial and oilfield services companies. The marketing work is real enough, but the report counts the wrong thing, and the sales cycle is so long that by the time a deal closes nobody remembers which campaign or trade show started it.

Industrial B2B marketing in Houston works when you aim at the whole buying committee and plan for a long cycle. In practice that usually means LinkedIn for named accounts and job titles, Google Ads for the handful of searches that signal a live project, and technical content that engineers will actually read. Trade shows like OTC produce meetings. A CRM has to track pipeline, because form fills tell you very little.

Below: how we’d set up each piece, current costs, and where firms throw money away.

Long sales cycles and buying committees: who you’re really selling to

A pump, a coating contract or a turnaround crew is rarely bought by one person. At an operator, a midstream company or a Ship Channel plant, the decision usually touches engineering, operations, procurement and safety, and someone above them signs. Add vendor prequalification (many operators require contractors to be approved through platforms like ISNetworld or Avetta before a PO can be cut) and an MSA that legal has to bless, and six months is fast.

Each of them reads your marketing looking for something different.

RoleWhat they care aboutContent that answers it
Engineer / technical leadSpecs, tolerances, standards, compatibilitySpec sheets, drawings, data sheets, test reports
Operations or maintenance managerDowntime, response time, crew qualityCase studies with timelines, service area map, response guarantees
Procurement / supply chainPrice, lead times, terms, approved vendor statusCapability statement, line card, certifications, insurance limits
HSESafety record and programsEMR, TRIR, safety program summary, prequal status
Executive sponsorRisk, cost over the life of the assetShort ROI case study, references, company stability

Most industrial websites talk to exactly one of these people, usually the engineer, usually through a PDF from 2017. Firms that land the longer contracts tend to give every role something they can forward down the hall.

Buyers are less local than you’d think, too. Houston is where many of them work, from the Energy Corridor along I-10 to Greenspoint and ExxonMobil’s campus in Spring, which Community Impact reported would become its corporate headquarters. But an oilfield services firm based in Conroe may also sell into Midland, Lafayette and Oklahoma City. Your targeting should follow the buyers. Where your yard happens to be is mostly irrelevant.

LinkedIn Ads for B2B: targeting, formats and cost

LinkedIn is the only major ad platform where you can say “show this to maintenance managers and reliability engineers at these 150 companies” and have it mostly work. You pay for that privilege.

Targeting that matters for energy and industrial firms

Campaign Manager lets you target by company name, industry and company size, then layer on job title, function, seniority or skills. Start with titles and functions. Skills targeting gets broad fast.

The more useful tool for most industrial firms is a Matched Audience built from a company list. Per LinkedIn’s company list documentation, a CSV can hold up to 300,000 records, audiences take 24 to 48 hours to build, and a campaign can serve once the list matches at least 300 members. LinkedIn suggests 1,000+ companies for best results. Most Houston service firms don’t have 1,000 realistic targets, and a list of 150 named operators still works; expect to pay more for it. You can also upload CRM contacts and retarget people who looked at your spec sheet pages.

Before launch, uncheck Audience Expansion and look hard at the LinkedIn Audience Network setting. Both push delivery past the people you chose, which is the opposite of what a tight industrial audience needs.

Formats worth testing

Document Ads let people read a document in the feed, and you can gate it behind a lead form or share it openly. A six-page technical brief on, say, corrosion under insulation inspection intervals is a good fit. Lead Gen Forms pre-fill from the member’s profile, so a field engineer on a phone doesn’t have to thumb-type into your website form. LinkedIn says 90% of its pilot customers beat their cost-per-lead goals with them (a vendor stat, so weigh it accordingly).

How much do LinkedIn ads cost for B2B?

Per LinkedIn’s own budget guide, the minimum daily budget is $10 for any format, and a new campaign needs at least a $100 lifetime budget. Those minimums are not a realistic budget. Metadata.io’s 2025 B2B benchmarks, drawn from 138 advertisers on LinkedIn in 2025, show what real spend buys:

At around $200 a lead, a $5,000 month buys roughly 25 leads, and a few of those will be students, competitors or somebody trying to sell you software. Fine if your average contract is $250,000. Painful if you sell $3,000 jobs. We’d point LinkedIn money at a defined account list and let Google pick up the broader demand. Google Ads for high-intent industrial searches

Industrial search volume is tiny, and we’ve stopped apologizing for it. One search for “hydrostatic testing services houston” or “api 6a gate valve supplier” from the right person beats a thousand LinkedIn impressions.

We build these accounts on exact and phrase match, pairing services with locations and products with spec terms: part numbers, standards (API, ASME, NACE), material grades, “rental,” “repair,” “supplier,” “near me,” plus city names like Pasadena, Baytown, La Porte and Channelview.

The negative keyword list goes in on day one, and it’s long. Jobs, careers, salary, training, certification, course, OSHA 10, PDF, free, used, Wikipedia, definition. Nothing attracts job seekers and students quite like industrial search terms.

  • Set location to “Presence.” Google’s location options page shows the default is “Presence or Interest,” which counts people merely interested in your area. For a field service company that’s wasted spend.
  • Leave Performance Max off until you have real conversion data. On a handful of conversions a month it has nothing to learn from and drifts toward cheap, irrelevant traffic.

And send product searches to product pages, never the homepage or the capability PDF.

Microsoft Advertising deserves a test here. Its LinkedIn profile targeting lets you adjust search bids by company, industry and job function, and plenty of corporate desktops in Houston still default to Bing.

A word on the Metadata.io’s 2025 B2B benchmarks: their Google Ads cost per lead was $524, well above LinkedIn’s $202. We wouldn’t conclude much from that. Search leads often show up with a project already in hand, so we’d compare channels on opportunities and revenue and treat cost per form as a footnote. Technical content engineers actually use: spec sheets, case studies, capability statements

An engineer wants to know whether your product will work in their application, and would rather find out without a sales call. Brochures don’t help much with that.

Start with spec sheets. One buried in a PDF is invisible to search and miserable on a phone, so put the specs on an HTML page and offer the PDF as a download. Don’t gate it.

Case studies need real numbers: the problem, the constraints, what you did, how long it took. “Completed a 21-day turnaround scope in 17 days” will get forwarded; “delivered exceptional value” won’t. Get the client’s written approval, or anonymize it if they won’t be named.

  • A capability statement, one or two pages, covering what you do and where, NAICS codes, certifications (ISO 9001, API Q1 or Q2, ASME stamps, whatever applies), insurance limits, EMR and TRIR, prequal platforms and a contact. Procurement will ask for it regardless, so put it on the site.
  • A line card if you distribute: brands, product lines, stocking locations.
  • Application notes on narrow problems like sour service, high-temperature sealing or H2S monitoring. Unglamorous, and they’re the pages that rank for long-tail technical searches.
  • Service area and response-time details for field crews, ideally on a map.

Save the gate for the deep material, like a detailed technical guide, a calculator or a webinar recording. Gate a spec sheet and the engineer just goes to a competitor who didn’t.

For energy tech and SaaS firms selling into operators, the same structure applies, with product demos in place of spec sheets; our technology industry page covers that side.

Account-based marketing for a target list of operators

ABM sounds like enterprise software. For a 40-person industrial firm it’s mostly a spreadsheet and some discipline.

The list matters more than anything. Fifty accounts that actually buy what you sell, in places you can serve, beat 2,000 pulled from a database. Pick them with your sales team, not for them.

We’d also push hard for sales and marketing to sit down with the same account list every two weeks. LinkedIn’s reporting shows which companies are seeing and clicking your ads, so when five people at one target account are suddenly reading your document ad, your salesperson has a reason to call and something specific to say.

ABM ads with no sales follow-up, filed under “brand awareness,” are a slow way to spend a year’s budget. We’ve watched it happen at more than one industrial company.

Trade shows in Houston: OTC and the rest of the calendar

The Offshore Technology Conference is the big one. OTC’s 2027 site and AIME’s event listing list OTC 2027 for May 3 to 5, 2027, in Houston at NRG Park (the 2027 toolkit still uses the venue’s old name, Reliant Park). Houston also hosts CERAWeek every March and the Turbomachinery and Pump Symposium, among many smaller regional shows. Check each show’s site for the current year’s dates before you book anything.

Shows are expensive, and in our experience most of the waste happens in the weeks after the booth comes down. A timeline we’d use:

  1. Four weeks out: start a LinkedIn campaign to your account list and to relevant titles in Greater Houston, with your booth number and a link to book a meeting. Pre-booked meetings are the whole game.
  2. Two weeks out: email customers and open opportunities with something specific to see at the booth, like a live demo or a technical session.
  3. Show days: scan badges into a form that tags each contact with the show name and a note on what they asked about. A location-based campaign around NRG Park can add reach, but keep expectations modest; our geofencing guide explains why.
  4. Within five business days: follow up with every scan, personally for the good ones. Load them into the CRM tagged “OTC 2027” so you can see what the show produced 12 months later.

Can’t justify a booth? Walk the floor with a full meeting schedule. A surprising number of good industrial relationships started in a hallway near the coffee line.

Getting leads into the CRM and out to sales

Plenty of B2B campaigns die right here, quietly. The lead exists. It’s sitting in a LinkedIn CSV download or a shared inbox nobody opens.

Wire every lead source straight into the CRM. LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms sync with HubSpot, Salesforce and others, and website forms should write a CRM record rather than just firing off an email. Add hidden fields for UTM parameters and the Google click ID (gclid) so each record carries the campaign, keyword or ad that produced it.

  • Write down what counts as a qualified lead, who calls it and how fast.
  • Route by territory or product line. A Permian inquiry shouldn’t sit in the Gulf Coast rep’s queue for a week.
  • Close the loop. When an opportunity is created or won, send it back to Google Ads (offline conversion import) and LinkedIn (Conversions API or offline uploads) so the platforms learn which clicks turned into business.
  • Recheck the form fields quarterly. Someone always adds a mandatory “budget” dropdown.

Honestly, we’d rather see a simple HubSpot setup that sales actually uses than an elaborate Salesforce instance nobody updates. Our landing page guide covers form design, which affects lead quality more than most people expect.

Measuring pipeline, not leads

Lead counts are easy to inflate. Pipeline is hard to fake.

The numbers we’d put on a monthly B2B report:

  • Target accounts engaged (anyone from the account clicked, downloaded or met with you)
  • Meetings held with qualified contacts
  • Opportunities created, with dollar value
  • Pipeline by source (LinkedIn, search, trade show, referral, outbound)
  • Win rate and average sales cycle length
  • Cost per opportunity, alongside cost per lead

Expect a lag. A campaign that launches in January may not show closed revenue until the following fall, which is normal in industrial sales and makes opportunity creation the best early signal you’ll get. Our posts on marketing metrics and cost per lead make the same case for local businesses. In B2B the gap between cheap leads and good ones is just wider.

When oil prices move, marketing budgets move

Anyone who has worked in Houston energy knows the pattern. Prices drop, capex gets cut, and marketing is the first line item to go.

Per FRED, the annual average WTI price fell from $93.17 a barrel in 2014 to $43.29 in 2016, hit $39.16 in 2020, rebounded to $94.90 in 2022, and averaged $65.39 in 2025. Service companies feel those swings on a delay, as operators adjust drilling and maintenance budgets.

Nobody knows where the price goes next, whatever your brother-in-law at the operator says. You can still plan for it.

Keep a floor. Cut ad spend if you must, but keep the website, GBP, case studies and LinkedIn company page alive, because rebuilding visibility from zero costs more than holding it. Downturns also tend to thin out the LinkedIn and search auctions as competitors pull back, so the dollars you do keep often go further.

  • Lean into efficiency in your messaging. When prices drop, operators care about cost per barrel, downtime and headcount on site, and your case studies should speak to those.
  • Widen the target list. LNG, petrochemicals, power, water and industrial manufacturing buy many of the same services. A firm that sells only to upstream operators lives and dies by the rig count.

Frequently asked questions

How much do LinkedIn ads cost for B2B companies?

LinkedIn’s minimum is $10 a day and $100 lifetime for a new campaign, but real B2B budgets are much higher. Metadata.io’s 2025 benchmarks across 138 LinkedIn advertisers show an average cost per click of $9.39, a CPM of $63.19 and a cost per lead around $202. Most Houston industrial firms we would advise start somewhere between $2,500 and $10,000 a month in spend.

Is LinkedIn or Google Ads better for oil and gas companies?

They do different jobs. LinkedIn lets you reach specific companies and job titles, so it suits account-based campaigns and long sales cycles. Google Ads catches people already searching for a product, part or service, often with a project in hand. Most energy and industrial firms benefit from both, judged by opportunities and revenue rather than cost per lead.

What should an oil and gas service company put on its website?

Give each member of the buying committee something useful: spec sheets as web pages with PDF downloads, case studies with real timelines and results, a capability statement with certifications, safety statistics and insurance limits, a service area map, and clear contact options. Avoid gating spec sheets. Engineers want answers without a sales call, and procurement wants your prequalification details in one place.

How long is the sales cycle for industrial B2B sales?

It varies by deal size and customer, but six to eighteen months is common for larger industrial and energy contracts, especially when vendor prequalification and a master service agreement are involved. Smaller repair or rental jobs can close in days. Plan marketing around opportunity creation as the early signal, because closed revenue may lag a campaign by a year or more.

Is the Offshore Technology Conference worth it for small companies?

It can be, if you treat it as a place to hold meetings. OTC 2027 is scheduled for May 3 to 5 at NRG Park in Houston. Book meetings with target accounts weeks ahead, tag every scanned contact, and follow up within five business days. If a booth is out of budget, attending with a full meeting schedule often pays back better.
