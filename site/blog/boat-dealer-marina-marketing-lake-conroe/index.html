The first warm Saturday in March at a marina on Lake Conroe. The fuel dock line is six boats deep. A family from Spring is at the sales desk asking about the pontoon they saw on Facebook, which sold two weeks ago. The service department has 40 boats that haven’t been run since October and every owner wants theirs by Memorial Day. And somebody’s on the phone asking if you have a covered slip.

That one Saturday holds the whole business: inventory, financing, service, slips, and a season that arrives all at once.

Boat dealer and marina marketing on Lake Conroe works when you build demand in January and February, around the Houston boat show, and keep inventory listings accurate on the channels that accept boats. Then the service department brings owners back every year. You also need to know the rules: Google won’t run boats in vehicle ads, Meta treats financing as a special category, and TPWD and Texas law limit how far a dealer can advertise a brand’s prices.

Lake Conroe’s market: who’s buying and when

Lake Conroe covers about 19,640 acres at its conservation pool elevation of 201 feet, across Montgomery and Walker counties, and was completed in January 1973, per the Texas Water Development Board. the San Jacinto River Authority, which operates the dam, puts the shoreline at about 150 miles. That’s a lot of lake homes, and a short drive from The Woodlands, Conroe, Montgomery and Willis.

Your buyers fall into a few groups. Lakefront owners in communities like Walden, April Sound and Bentwater. Weekenders driving up I-45 from Spring, Cypress and north Houston. Fishing buyers who also run the Gulf from Galveston. And first-time buyers who just moved to a Montgomery County master-planned community and noticed everyone has a boat.

Nationally, pontoons are the biggest segment. NMMA’s 2025 powerboat sales report counted 156,754 new powerboats sold in 2025, with pontoons making up 33.6% of units. On a lake full of families and lake-house owners, that tracks with what you’d see from the 105 bridge on a summer Sunday.

Seasonality is extreme. Inquiries climb from January (show season) through May, peak in spring, hold through summer, then drop hard after Labor Day. Service runs on its own curve: spring prep, summer breakdowns, fall storage. Your budget should follow both curves rather than sitting flat at a monthly retainer.

Boat show season: plan January through March backward

The Houston boat show is the start of the selling year. For 2027 it’s combined with the Houston Auto Show as the AutoBoative show, January 27 to 31 at NRG Park, with one ticket for both, per Houston Boat Shows and the Houston Auto Show.

Whether you exhibit or not, the show moves buyers. People walk the floor, look at 30 boats, and go home to search the brands they liked. If you’re the local dealer for those brands, January searches are yours to lose.

What we’d do:

  • December: build a show page with the models you’ll have, show-only offers with a real end date, and a form to book a time at the booth or a lake demo after.
  • Two weeks out: email and text past customers and leads with the dates and what you’re bringing. Run Meta video of the show boats to people within driving distance of NRG Park and the lake.
  • Show week: capture every booth conversation in the CRM the same day, with model interest and timeline. Paper forms in a box get entered in March, which is too late.
  • The two weeks after: call every booth lead, book lake demos for the first nice weekend, and retarget show-page visitors.

Inventory ads: what Google and Meta will and won’t take

Car dealers have it easy here. Boat dealers have to know which channels accept boat inventory.

Google vehicle ads don’t accept boats. Google’s vehicle ads policies allow passenger and non-commercial vehicles, including RVs, ATVs, UTVs, campers and non-motorized trailers, and list boats explicitly among the excluded types, with motorcycles and planes. a May 2026 expansion added ATVs, UTVs and travel trailers in the U.S. Boats stayed out. If you also sell ATVs or UTVs, those can now go in a vehicle feed; boats go into Search campaigns instead.

Meta accepts boats in vehicle catalogs. Meta’s vehicle catalog reference lists BOAT as a valid vehicle type. That means a catalog of your inventory can drive dynamic ads that show the exact boat a person looked at on your site. It’s the closest thing to a car dealer’s inventory ads, and it’s underused by boat dealers.

Facebook Marketplace changed. In January 2023, Meta removed vehicle listings posted from business Pages on Marketplace, per CBT News. The coverage talked about vehicles and didn’t spell out boats, so test what your Page can post, and read Meta’s commerce policies before you ask salespeople to list dealer inventory on personal profiles.

The boat portals. Boats Group owns Boat Trader, boats.com and YachtWorld. For used inventory especially, that’s where shoppers compare. Judge them on cost per sold boat, not leads.

ChannelTakes boat inventory?Best use
Google vehicle adsNo (boats excluded)ATVs, UTVs, trailers if you sell them

Whatever the channel, the website is the source of truth. Every boat gets its own page with price (or “call for price” if your agreement requires it), photos, engine hours for used units, and a “schedule a lake demo” button. Mark sold boats sold the same day. Nothing burns a buyer faster than driving up from Katy for a boat that’s gone.

Google Search for boat dealers around Lake Conroe

Search is the closer. Buyers search brands and models after the show, after a test ride with a friend, or after a Saturday staring at other people’s boats.

Campaigns we’d build:

  • Brand and model: each brand you carry plus “for sale,” “dealer,” “price,” and the town names (Conroe, Montgomery, The Woodlands, Houston).
  • Category: “pontoon boats for sale,” “wake boats Houston,” “bay boats for sale near me,” “used boats Conroe.”
  • Service: “boat repair near me,” “outboard service Lake Conroe,” “boat mechanic Montgomery TX.” Service search is steady and cheap compared with sales.
  • Marina: “boat slip rental Lake Conroe,” “dry stack storage Lake Conroe,” “boat storage Conroe TX.”

Now the rule many dealers miss. Texas Occupations Code section 2352.0522, summarized in TPWD’s dealer license packet, says a dealer may not advertise or promote a manufacturer’s boats outside its assigned territory, online included, unless the ad shows no price or shows only the manufacturer’s suggested retail price. A dealer can still sell to an out-of-territory buyer who comes in on their own. In practice, match the location targeting of any priced ad to your dealer agreement’s territory, and keep priced ads off broad national targeting. Ask your manufacturer rep what your territory actually is; we often find the dealer isn’t sure.

Financing ads under Meta’s Financial Products and Services category

Most boats are financed, so monthly payments sell. They also bring rules.

On Meta, ads promoting credit offers such as loans and long-term financing run under the Financial Products and Services special ad category. Age and gender targeting are locked, the location must include at least a 15-mile radius, ZIP targeting and lookalike audiences go away, and instant forms can’t ask for things like age or location, per Jon Loomer’s guide to Meta’s special ad categories. If the ad is about a payment, choose the category. Try to work around it and you risk rejected ads or an account restriction.

The copy has rules too. Under Regulation Z, section 1026.24, stating a payment amount, a number of payments or term, a down payment or a finance charge means you must also state the down payment, the repayment terms and the APR. “$389 a month” alone doesn’t pass. Your lender or manufacturer finance program usually supplies compliant language.

The radius rule matters less on Lake Conroe than you’d think, since a 15-mile circle around the lake already covers The Woodlands, Conroe, Montgomery and Willis. The bigger loss is detailed targeting. So the creative has to find the buyer: a video of a specific tritoon pulling a tube past the 105 bridge, the payment with full disclosure on the landing page, and a “book a lake demo” button.

Slip rentals and dry stack: marketing a Lake Conroe marina

Marinas sell space, and space runs out. The marketing job changes with occupancy.

When slips are full, run a waitlist page with slip sizes, covered or uncovered, power, and an honest estimate of the wait. When you have openings, publish them with “from” monthly rates, photos from the water and the parking lot, and what’s included (fuel dock, pump-out, ship store, security). People comparing marinas want the rate, the walk from parking to the slip, and how the place looks at 6 p.m. on a Saturday.

Your Google Business Profile matters a lot here. Pick the marina category as primary, add boat storage and boat repair if you do them, keep hours accurate by season, and post photos often. Many people search “marina near me” while they’re on the water.

Lake level is a content topic your tenants care about. SJRA’s flood mitigation FAQ describes the seasonal lowering program that ran through at least 2023, with spring releases to 200.5 feet and fall releases down to 200.0 feet, a foot below normal pool, at the City of Houston’s request. Whatever the current plan, a short page or post explaining lake level and what it means for your docks answers a question your office gets every year.

And plan for storms. Hurricane season runs June through November. Have a storm-prep page and a text template ready for tenants before the first named storm.

The service department is the retention engine

A dealer might sell a customer one boat every five or six years. The service department can see them twice a year. That’s where loyalty, trade-ins and referrals come from.

  • Spring prep reminders in January and February, before the rush. Book appointments early with a schedule you can keep.
  • Mid-summer check-ins for impellers, batteries and anything the Houston heat is cooking.
  • Fall storage and service offers in September.
  • Registration renewal reminders. TPWD’s registration and titling FAQ says owners can renew up to 90 days before expiration. A reminder from you is good service and a reason to book a visit.
  • New-owner orientation. Texas requires boater education for operators born on or after September 1, 1993, running a vessel with a motor over 15 horsepower, per TPWD’s boater education rules. Add a line to every delivery checklist and a page about it on your site. Parents buying a boat for teenagers search for this.

Use text for service updates and email for marketing, with separate consent for marketing texts. Our SMS marketing post covers the Texas rules.

TPWD dealer rules that touch your marketing

A few Texas rules are worth knowing because they show up in ads and on websites.

Per TPWD’s dealer license packet, a dealer is anyone who buys, sells, consigns, displays for sale or exchanges at least five vessels, motorboats or outboard motors in a calendar year. The license costs $500 for two years, each place of business needs its own license, and the license has to be displayed at that location. If you’re running ads for a second location or a seasonal lot, check that it’s licensed first.

Sales tax is part of the buyer’s real cost. TPWD collects 6.25% tax on boats and outboard motors, due within 45 working days of the sale, per TPWD’s registration and titling FAQ. Out-the-door pricing that explains tax, title and registration on the website saves an awkward conversation at the desk, and buyers from out of state appreciate it.

None of this is legal advice. Your manufacturer agreements and your attorney get the last word, especially on territory.

Reviews from buyers, slip tenants and service customers

Three kinds of customers, three moments to ask. Buyers the week after delivery, once they’ve had the boat on the water. Service customers the day after pickup. Slip tenants once a year, and after you fix something they asked about.

The rules, from Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps: no incentives, no asking only happy customers, no discouraging negatives. Same link to everyone.

Service reviews will be your most numerous and your most critical. Reply to every one with specifics and a name to call. Buyers read service reviews before they buy, because they know they’ll be back. Our complete guide to Google reviews has request templates.

Frequently asked questions

How do boat dealers on Lake Conroe get more buyers?

Start before the season. Build a show page and contact list in December, follow up every Houston boat show lead within two weeks, and book lake demos for the first warm weekends. Keep every boat on its own website page with accurate pricing, run Google Search on your brands and models, use Meta catalog ads to retarget boats people viewed, and keep service customers coming back.

Can boat dealers use Google vehicle ads?

No. Google’s vehicle ads policies allow passenger and non-commercial vehicles, including RVs, campers, ATVs, UTVs and non-motorized trailers, but list boats among the excluded vehicle types. Boat dealers should use Google Search campaigns that send model and brand searches to individual inventory pages. If you also sell ATVs, UTVs or trailers, those can go in a vehicle ads feed in the U.S.

Do boat financing ads on Facebook need a special ad category?

Generally yes. Meta’s Financial Products and Services category covers ads promoting credit offers such as loans and long-term financing. It locks age and gender targeting, requires at least a 15-mile radius, removes ZIP code and lookalike targeting, and limits instant form questions. The copy also has to follow Regulation Z: a stated monthly payment requires the down payment, repayment terms and APR.

Do you need a license to sell boats in Texas?

Yes, in most cases. Texas Parks and Wildlife requires a dealer license for anyone who buys, sells, consigns, displays for sale or exchanges at least five vessels, motorboats or outboard motors in a calendar year. The license costs $500 for two years, each place of business needs its own, and it must be displayed at that location. Some very small boats and motors are exempt.

When is the 2027 Houston Boat Show?

The 2027 Houston boat show runs January 27 to 31, 2027, at NRG Park, combined with the Houston Auto Show as the AutoBoative show with one ticket for both, according to the show organizers. Lake Conroe dealers should have show pages, offers and booth lead capture ready in December, then follow up with lake demos in February and March.
