Thursday, trivia night, a taproom off the Grand Parkway. Thirty-eight people, which is fine, but last spring it was 70. The Meta ad for the new hazy IPA got rejected because the audience was set to 18 and up. The Untappd menu still lists the Oktoberfest that kicked two weeks ago. It isn’t, at least not on-premise.

Brewery, winery and taproom marketing in Houston and The Woodlands works when you give people a reason to show up on a specific night, keep your menus and hours accurate everywhere they look, and stay inside TABC’s advertising rules and the ad platforms’ alcohol policies. Beer to go and wine shipping turn one visit into a second sale. The rest of this post is the detail: what’s allowed, what to post, and how to tell which nights are earning their keep.

The Houston craft beer market you’re selling into

The boom years are over. Texas had 440 breweries in 2024 and 420 in 2025, per Brewers Association figures reported by Axios. Openings fell below 25 in each of those years while closures stayed at 25 or more. Production dipped 8.7% in 2024, then recovered to about 1.42 million barrels in 2025. The same story notes that Texas heat and long summers are hard on taprooms with patios that aren’t climate controlled, and that attractions like a soccer field or an arcade have become “table stakes.”

Houston felt it. Axios Houston listed Buffalo Bayou Brewing, Ingenious Brewing and Twisted Acre Brewery among the area’s recent closures or mergers, while Saint Arnold, the city’s oldest craft brewer, grew its barrels 5.2% in 2024.

What that tells us: the taprooms that keep winning give people a plan for the evening. Trivia, run clubs, food trucks, a release party, a patio with shade and fans, space for kids and dogs. Beer quality is the entry ticket, and most Houston drinkers already have three good breweries within 20 minutes.

TABC advertising rules: what a taproom or tasting room can and can’t say

This is the part most brewery and winery marketing guides skip, and it’s where Texas is stricter than people expect. We’re marketers, not lawyers, and your permit type matters, so run anything borderline past TABC or your attorney. Here’s what the commission’s own materials say.

What an ad can’t contain. Under 16 TAC §45.122 in TABC’s administrative rules, alcohol advertising can’t include false or misleading statements, statements disparaging a competitor’s product, anything obscene or indecent, or statements about intoxicating qualities. Curative or therapeutic claims are also out. So “the strongest beer in Montgomery County” and “our red wine is good for your heart” are both problems.

Online counts. The same rules apply to advertising on the internet, and §45.105(d) says retailer internet advertising has to follow the on-premise promotion, coupon and sweepstakes rules. Your Instagram post is an ad as far as TABC is concerned.

Promotions. TABC’s marketing practices FAQ says coupons for alcohol purchases aren’t allowed and mailing a drink coupon is prohibited. A retailer can give one complimentary drink per legal-age customer per business day, but not through a ticket or coupon. On-premise two-for-one sales are illegal, and reduced drink prices have to end by 11 p.m. A meal package with a discounted or free drink is fine if no coupon is required to get it, and you can advertise that package on social media.

The tier rules, if you also distribute. If your beer or wine is in bars, restaurants or H-E-B, the FAQ is clear that you can’t pay a retailer for featuring your brand, and you can’t give retailers branded apparel or glassware for free (promotional items have to be sold). Entertaining a retailer is capped at an “occasion” worth no more than $500 per person, and you have to be there. Listing the bars that carry you on your website is fine, as long as nobody pays for the listing.

Meta and Google alcohol ad policies: 21+ and nothing that looks like excess

The ad platforms add their own layer. According to Sprout Social’s rundown of platform alcohol policies (August 2026), Meta allows alcohol ads that follow local laws and use age and country targeting, but Meta’s default minimum age is 18, so U.S. advertisers set 21+ themselves. Google allows alcohol ads but bars targeting minors and promoting excessive or underage drinking. TikTok has allowed alcohol ads since August 2024 and bans offering alcohol as a prize.

The setup we’d use on Meta:

Creative matters as much as targeting. Show people of obvious legal age, keep drinking in a social setting, and leave out anything about getting drunk, chugging or driving. Food, music, trivia teams and dogs on the patio all perform well for taprooms in our experience, and they’re easy to approve.

On Google, the most useful campaigns for a taproom or winery are small search campaigns on terms like “brewery near me,” “breweries in The Woodlands,” “winery near Magnolia TX,” “trivia night Spring TX” and “wine tasting near Houston,” sent to a landing page that shows tonight’s events and hours. Our search marketing team sets these up with the right exclusions, and our paid social team handles the Meta side.

Beer to go, wine shipping and direct sales in Texas

The to-go fridge is a marketing channel, and Texas law shapes how big it can be.

Texas breweries have been allowed to sell beer to go starting September 1, 2019. Houstonia reported the cap at the time as 288 ounces per person per day, which is 24 cans, a case, or nine 32-ounce crowlers. That limit is why release-day posts should say “limit one case per person” in plain words, so nobody drives in from Katy expecting more.

Shipping beer directly to consumers still isn’t legal in Texas. the Brewers Association reported on May 30, 2025 that HB 2441, the direct-to-consumer shipping bill nicknamed “Beers To You,” failed in the 2025 session, along with HB 4773, which would have let breweries move beer between their own locations.

Wineries have more room. Per TABC’s wine shipping page, a Texas winery with a Winery Permit (G) can ship to consumers anywhere in the state, up to 9 gallons per consumer per month and 36 gallons in any 12-month period. A carrier with a Carrier’s Permit has to deliver, and an adult of at least 21 has to sign with valid ID.

For a winery, that makes a wine club with shipping the most valuable thing your marketing can build. Every Houston day-tripper who joins is a customer who doesn’t have to drive back to Plantersville to buy again. For a brewery, the equivalent is a release list (email and text) that turns new cans into a reason to come in this weekend.

Events, trivia nights and a calendar people can plan around

Recurring events are the backbone of taproom marketing. A weekly trivia night, a Tuesday run club and a monthly makers’ market give regulars a habit and give you something new to post every week.

A few things we’ve seen work in north Houston:

  • Keep the weekly lineup stable for at least three months before you judge it. Trivia nights take a while to build teams.
  • Put every event on a single events page on your website, then copy it to Facebook events, Google Business Profile posts and Untappd. One source, many places.
  • For ticketed events (release parties, wine dinners, beer and food pairings), sell through a ticketing link and track sales back to the ad. Our guide to selling event tickets in Houston covers the setup.
  • Plan around the weather. Patio events do well in March through May and October through November; summer events belong indoors or after sunset.
  • Family-friendly hours with a playground or yard games bring in parents from Creekside Park, Harmony and Woodforest on weekend afternoons, and those visits often turn into regulars.

Food truck partnerships

If you don’t have a kitchen, a reliable food truck rotation keeps people from leaving after one pint. The best setups we see have a fixed truck on fixed nights and a shared posting plan: a collab post on Instagram, both calendars updated, and the truck tagged in every story.

Keep the money simple. In the setups we’ve seen, many taprooms don’t charge the truck at all; others take a small flat fee or ask for a minimum number of nights. Whatever you agree, write it down, and keep the beer-price messaging on your side of the partnership since TABC rules apply to you, not the truck. We wrote a whole guide on food truck marketing in Houston that covers the truck’s side, and it’s worth sending to the trucks you work with.

Untappd, Google Business Profile and the menu problem

Beer drinkers check three places before they come in: your Instagram, Google Maps and Untappd. If those three say different things, you look closed.

Untappd. Per Untappd for Business’s pricing page, the Essentials plan includes verified venue status, digital, print and QR menus, one-click menu publishing to your website and the app, POS integrations (Toast, Square, GoTab and others), push alerts to Untappd followers when your menu changes, and an events manager. Premium adds wine, spirits and ready-to-drink support, unlimited menu items and API access. Pricing is by quote and varies by market. For a brewery with a rotating tap list, the menu sync alone saves an hour a week and stops the “is the Oktoberfest still on?” question.

Google Business Profile. Pick the primary category that matches what you are: Brewery, Brewpub, Winery or Wine bar. Add secondary categories only for things you really do, like Beer garden or Event venue. Keep hours exact, including holiday hours. Upload photos of the taproom, the patio, the kids’ area if you have one and the to-go fridge. Post every event. Answer the common questions (dogs, kids, outside food, parking) in your profile description and on your website.

Our map pack guide covers the ranking side, and our reviews guide covers getting more of them without breaking Google’s rules. A taproom with 900 reviews at 4.7 stars tends to outrank one with 140 at 4.9 for “brewery near me,” in our experience.

Wine trails, day-trippers and tourism north of Houston

Wineries and some breweries north and west of The Woodlands sell to visitors as much as locals. Visit Houston’s wine trail itinerary describes the Texas Bluebonnet Wine Trail as running between I-45 and U.S. 290, with wineries in towns like Plantersville, Navasota, Chappell Hill, New Waverly and Huntsville, and the closest group about an hour and a half from Houston. Islands counted more than 10 member wineries in July 2025.

Visitors plan these trips on their phones the week before, with searches like “wineries near The Woodlands,” “winery near Magnolia TX,” “day trip from Houston wine” and “wine tasting near Montgomery TX.” So:

  • Build a “Plan your visit” page with hours, tasting prices, reservations, group policy, the drive time from The Woodlands and Houston, and nearby stops (other wineries, a lunch spot, Lake Conroe or downtown Montgomery).
  • Run search ads on day-trip terms on Thursdays and Fridays, when people plan the weekend.
  • Use Meta video of the vineyard, the porch and the tasting room, targeted 21+ to Houston, The Woodlands, Katy and Cypress.
  • Partner with neighboring wineries and B&Bs on a shared itinerary page. Visitors want a whole day, and you get links from it.

Email, texts and the mug club

Your best customers already gave you their email when they joined the mug club or the wine club. Most taprooms send them one newsletter a quarter. Send a short one every week instead: what’s on tap, what’s new, this week’s events.

Texts work for release days and sellouts. Get explicit opt-in, keep marketing texts separate from order texts, and remember TABC: no drink coupons in the text. Our SMS rules for Texas businesses covers the consent side.

What to track, and a first-month plan

Track four things weekly: door count or transactions by night, to-go sales, event attendance by event, and new email or text sign-ups. Tie ads to those, not to likes.

A first month we’d run for a taproom in The Woodlands, Spring, Conroe or Tomball:

  1. Week one: fix Google Business Profile, Untappd and website hours so they match. Set Meta ad accounts to 21+.
  2. Week two: audit every promotion on your social accounts against the TABC FAQ and take down anything that reads like a coupon or a two-for-one.
  3. Week three: lock a weekly event lineup and a food truck rotation for the next 12 weeks, on one events page.
  4. Week four: start a small search campaign for brewery-near-me and event terms, and a Meta campaign for your biggest weekly event.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Texas brewery or bar post drink specials on social media?

Yes, with limits. TABC treats online posts as advertising, so the same promotion rules apply. You can post prices and specials, but not alcohol coupons, on-premise two-for-one offers, or reduced drink prices after 11 p.m. A meal package with a discounted drink is allowed if no coupon is needed. Ads also can’t make claims about intoxicating effects or health benefits. Check borderline promotions with TABC.

How do I target alcohol ads to 21+ on Facebook and Instagram?

Meta’s default minimum age is 18, so U.S. breweries, wineries and bars should set the age range to 21 and older in every ad set and use the age restriction setting on the Facebook Page and Instagram account. Keep creative focused on people of clear legal age in social settings, and avoid anything that suggests excessive drinking or driving, which both Meta and Google prohibit.

How much beer can a Texas brewery sell to go?

Texas breweries have been allowed to sell beer to go since September 1, 2019, with a limit reported at the time of 288 ounces per person per day, which is 24 cans or one case. Direct shipping of beer to consumers is still not allowed; a 2025 bill to permit it failed. Texas wineries with a G permit can ship wine to consumers anywhere in Texas.

What are the best marketing ideas for a taproom in The Woodlands or Houston?

Run a stable weekly lineup like trivia, run clubs and food trucks for at least three months, keep Google, Untappd and your website menu and hours identical, and post every event in all three places. Add family and dog-friendly hours if your space allows. Build an email and text list for releases, and run small search ads on brewery-near-me and event terms.

Is Untappd for Business worth it for a small brewery?

For most breweries with a rotating tap list, yes. The Essentials plan includes verified venue status, menus that publish to your website and the Untappd app in one click, POS integrations, push alerts to followers when your menu changes and an events manager. Pricing is quote-based and varies by market, so judge it by the staff time it saves and whether menus stay accurate.
