Try this before reading further. Open your call log for last week, pick out the calls from new customers, and write next to each one where that person found you. Google Ads, your Google Business Profile, the truck wrap, the mailer, a neighbor on Nextdoor.

Most owners we sit down with in Houston and The Woodlands get through three or four before they start guessing. The phone is their best lead source and the one they know least about.

The short answer: call tracking gives each marketing source its own phone number (or swaps the number on your website for each visitor), forwards every call to your real line, and records which source produced it. A small business setup typically runs $50 to $250 a month with a provider like CallRail, and Google’s forwarding numbers for ad calls cost nothing extra.

That’s the whole idea. The rest of this guide is the setup we’d actually run for a service company, clinic or shop in Greater Houston: what it costs, which numbers to buy, how to keep it from hurting your map rankings, how to feed calls back into Google Ads, and what Texas law says about recording.

How much does call tracking cost for a small business?

Less than one bad lead, usually. Per CallRail’s pricing page as of November 2026, CallRail sells four plans at $50, $95, $150 and $195 a month. Each includes five local numbers and 250 minutes. Extra local numbers are $3 each, and extra minutes run 5 to 6 cents (the page lists both rates in different spots, so confirm on your quote). Competitors like CallTrackingMetrics and WhatConverts price in the same neighborhood.

Minutes are where the bill grows. A plumbing company answering 600 calls a month at four minutes each burns 2,400 minutes, which is about 2,150 over the included amount.

Two pieces cost nothing beyond your ad spend. Google Ads gives you Google forwarding numbers for calls from ads and for calls to your website number after an ad click. And Local Services Ads already assign a tracked number to every lead. You still want a call tracking provider for everything Google doesn’t see: organic visitors, your Business Profile, Facebook, Nextdoor, the billboard on I-45 and the yard signs in Spring.

Is it worth $100 to $200 a month? If you spend more than about $1,500 a month on marketing, yes, without much debate. Below that, start with one static number on your Business Profile and Google’s free ad tracking, then add the rest when spend grows.

How does call tracking work?

There are two kinds of tracking numbers, and most businesses need both.

Static numbers are one number per source that never changes. One on the truck wrap, one on the EDDM mailer, one on the Business Profile. Anyone who dials it came from that source (or saved it from that source, which is close enough).

Dynamic number insertion (DNI) handles your website. A small script reads how each visitor arrived, using the referrer, UTM tags or Google’s click ID, and swaps the phone number on the page for one from a pool assigned to that source. When the visitor calls, the provider knows the source, the landing page and often the keyword.

Pool size matters. If two visitors share one number at the same time, the provider can’t tell them apart. Providers size the pool from your traffic, and for most local service sites that means somewhere between four and ten numbers. Set it too small and you’ll see a pile of calls labeled “unknown.”

The caller never notices any of this. The call rings through to your normal line (you can add a short whisper message only your staff hear, like “Google Ads call”), and it lands in your call log with the source attached.

Which tracking numbers does a Houston business actually need?

Buy numbers that look local. Houston’s overlay now has five area codes, 713, 281, 832, 346 and, since January 23, 2025, 621, per ABC13. Conroe, Willis and Montgomery are mostly 936 territory. A 936 number on a Conroe roofer’s mailer reads as a neighbor. A toll-free number reads as a call center in another state, and in our experience answer rates on outbound callbacks drop with it too.

Will call tracking hurt my Google Business Profile ranking?

Not if you set it up the way local SEO people have for years. Google lets you list a primary number and up to two more, per Google’s Business Profile contact info page. Put the tracking number in the primary field and your real main number in the first additional slot. Darren Shaw at Whitespark has recommended that setup since 2018 and reports no ranking harm in the accounts he’s watched. That’s anecdotal, but it matches what we see.

The additional slot is what keeps your name, address and phone consistent with your citations on Yelp, the BBB, the Greater Conroe or Woodlands chamber directory and the rest. Google can still connect those listings to your profile. Don’t spray a different tracking number across every free directory. That’s how citation messes start.

Two more Business Profile details. Add UTM tags to your profile’s website link (something like utm_source=google, utm_medium=organic, utm_campaign=gbp) so profile visitors who call from your site get credited to the profile, not lumped into “organic.” And don’t judge the profile by its own “Calls” number in the Performance report. That counts taps on the call button. The tracking number counts calls that connected, how long they lasted, and what was said.

How do I track phone calls from Google Ads?

Google offers four kinds of call conversions, per Google’s phone call conversion tracking page:

  • Calls from ads. Calls made directly from a call asset on your ad. Needs call reporting turned on.
  • Calls to a phone number on your website. Google swaps in a forwarding number after an ad click, much like DNI.
  • Clicks on a mobile website number. Only the tap, not the call. Useful as a backup signal, weak as a primary goal.
  • Imported calls. Calls logged in another system (your call tracking provider or CRM) sent back to Google Ads, ideally only the ones that became booked jobs or sales, with values.

Two of these matter more than the others. Calls from ads and website calls give Google fast feedback. Imported calls tell it which callers actually paid you. If Smart Bidding only sees “a call happened,” it will happily find you more 45-second calls from people asking if you sell parts.

Set the minimum call length to fit your business. We usually start at 60 to 90 seconds for home services and adjust after listening to a week of recordings. A dental office scheduling a new patient might need two minutes; a locksmith’s real calls can be short.

One change to plan for: call-only ads are going away. Google removed the option to create new call ads in February 2026, and existing ones stop serving in February 2027, per Google’s notice on retiring call ads. If you still run them, rebuild them now as responsive search ads with call assets, before February forces it.

What counts as a qualified call?

Call length alone is a blunt filter. A 3-minute call can be a vendor pitching SEO. A 50-second call can be a homeowner in Tomball who just wants you there Thursday.

A better definition combines a few signals. First-time caller (not an existing customer calling about an invoice). Long enough to be a real conversation. And either a tag from staff or an automated score that says it was about a service you sell. Most providers now offer AI call summaries and keyword spotting, so “asked about AC replacement” or “booked appointment” can be tagged without anyone listening to every call.

The junk is predictable, and it’s worth knowing your mix. Vendors and robocalls. Existing customers. Wrong numbers. Job seekers, especially the week after you post on Indeed. Callers from outside your service area, a common one for Houston companies whose ads drift into Beaumont or Bryan. If a source sends mostly junk, that’s a finding, and often a cheaper one to fix than you’d think.

Is it legal to record customer calls in Texas?

Texas is a one-party consent state. Per the Texas State Law Library, a recording generally needs the consent of just one participant, and the statute is Section 16.02 of the Texas Penal Code. Your business is a party to the call, so recording your own customer calls is generally allowed here.

The catch is the caller. Some states require every party to consent, and people from California, Illinois or Florida call Houston businesses all the time. the Reporters Committee’s Texas recording guide is a good place to read how those rules can collide. The simple, cheap fix is a short greeting before the call connects: “This call may be recorded for quality.” Every major provider supports it.

A few practical rules we follow. Limit who can play recordings. Set retention to what you need (90 days to a year covers most coaching and dispute needs). Medical, dental and therapy offices should treat recordings as patient information and loop in whoever handles compliance before turning recording on. This is general information, not legal advice.

What call reports show that nothing else will

The first month of call data usually changes one decision right away, and it’s rarely the one the owner expected.

Often it’s missed calls. A tracking report sorted by hour shows exactly when the phone rings and nobody picks up: lunch, the 4:30 to 6 p.m. stretch when techs are calling in, and Saturdays.

Missed calls now cost money directly on Local Services Ads. Since October 1, 2026, a missed call during your business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds can be charged as a valid lead, per PPC Land. Paying for leads you didn’t answer is a bad trade. Our speed-to-lead guide covers overflow answering, missed-call text-back and callback rules.

The other reports worth a monthly look: first-time callers by source (the real “leads by channel” chart), keywords and landing pages that produce calls versus forms, and average call length by source. A billboard that produces 30 calls averaging 40 seconds is telling you something different from a Business Profile producing 30 calls averaging four minutes.

Call tracking mistakes we see in Houston accounts

  • Counting every call as a lead. Existing customers and vendors inflate results and teach Google the wrong lesson.
  • Tracking numbers on free citations. A different number on every directory breaks your name-address-phone consistency.
  • A DNI pool that’s too small. Lots of “unknown source” calls usually means this.
  • Forgetting offline media. The mailer to Sugar Land and the radio flight share one number, so neither can be judged.
  • Letting numbers lapse. Old wraps, signs and magazine ads keep ringing for years.
  • Never listening. Fifteen minutes of recordings a week is the cheapest sales training you’ll find.
  • Two systems, two answers. Google Ads, the provider and the CRM all count calls differently. Pick one as the source of truth for booked jobs and import from it.

A 30-day call tracking setup plan

This is roughly how we roll it out for a new client. Nothing here needs a developer if your site runs on WordPress, Webflow or Squarespace.

Week 1: pick a provider, buy local numbers (DNI pool plus one per offline source), and set forwarding and the recording greeting.

Week 2: install the DNI script, update the Business Profile (tracking primary, main number additional, UTM on the website link), and swap numbers on wraps, signs and print going forward.

Week 3: connect the provider to Google Ads and GA4, set call conversion actions and minimum lengths, and make one of them primary.

Week 4: define a qualified call, set up tags or scoring, and start importing booked jobs back into Google Ads.

After that it’s a 30-minute monthly review alongside your form leads. If you’d like a senior strategist to build the tracking and the reporting around your goals, get in touch.

Frequently asked questions

Does call tracking work with Google Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads assign their own tracked number to every lead, so you don’t need a provider number inside LSA. Turn on call recording in your LSA or Google Ads settings and review leads there. Your provider still tracks everything else, and you can forward LSA calls through the same line so your staff see one call log. Just don’t replace Google’s number, or LSA can’t count or charge leads correctly.

Can I use call tracking numbers on Yelp and other directories in Houston?

Use tracking numbers on paid listings, like Yelp ads or a paid Angi profile, where you need to judge the spend. Keep your main business number on free citations so your name, address and phone stay consistent across the web. If a directory lets you add a second number, you can add the tracking number there, but the main number should be the one that matches your Google Business Profile’s additional number.

How many tracking numbers do I need for dynamic number insertion?

Enough that two visitors from the same source rarely see the same number at the same time. For most local service websites in Houston and The Woodlands, that’s four to ten numbers in the pool, depending on traffic. Your provider will suggest a size. If your reports show many calls with an unknown source, the pool is probably too small. Add a few numbers and check again after two weeks.

Will customers notice I’m using a call tracking number?

No. A local tracking number looks like any other local number, and the call rings straight through to your line. Buying numbers in the area codes your customers use, such as 281, 832 or 713 in Houston and 936 around Conroe, keeps it that way. The only thing callers might hear is a short recording disclosure, which most people expect from a business anyway.

Is CallRail worth it for a small business in The Woodlands?

For a business spending more than about $1,500 a month on marketing, almost always. Its plans start at $50 a month with five local numbers and 250 minutes, which covers a small clinic or a service company getting started. The value is knowing which ads produce booked jobs, so you can cut the ones that don’t. Competitors such as CallTrackingMetrics and WhatConverts do the same job at similar prices.
