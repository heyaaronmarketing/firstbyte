Used Car Dealer Marketing in Houston: Ads, Listings and the Rules

Saturday, 11 a.m., a lot on the North Freeway feeder. Sixty-two cars, two salespeople, and a phone buzzing with Messenger notifications. Forty-one people have asked “is this still available?” about the same 2016 F-150 since Thursday. Three of them will actually show up. One has a $1,500 down payment.

That’s the independent dealer’s marketing problem in a single morning, whether the lot is off the North Freeway, out on FM 1960 or up I-45 toward Conroe and The Woodlands: plenty of attention, very little of it ready to buy.

Used car dealer marketing in Houston works when your inventory shows up where buyers search (Google vehicle ads, listing sites, Meta inventory ads), every ad follows the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles advertising rules, financing messages stay inside federal and Meta limits, and someone follows up with every lead in minutes, in English or Spanish. Then you judge it all by cost per car sold. Cost per lead doesn’t pay the floor plan.

This guide is for independent and buy-here-pay-here dealers from Pasadena to Conroe. Franchise stores have co-op rules and OEM programs that are a different post.

Who buys from Houston’s independent lots

Start with the buyer, because most dealer ads are written for somebody else. Cox Automotive research released in June 2026 on independent-dealer buyers found that 79% said they needed a vehicle rather than simply wanting one, and 77% of buyers of 11- to 20-year-old vehicles had household incomes under $75,000. They averaged 16.5 hours researching online, 77% used third-party listing sites, and 83% started without knowing which vehicle they wanted. Only 21% said they’d return to the same dealer next time.

Read those together. Your buyer is often in a hurry because the old car died, flexible on make and model, budget-driven, and comparing you to five other lots on their phone. They want to know three things fast: is it here, what will it cost me per month and up front, and will you approve me.

Houston adds two more. Harris County is 46.1% Hispanic or Latino, and 45.0% of residents age 5 and up speak a language other than English at home, per Census QuickFacts. And a lot of buyers commute long distances on I-45, US-59 and the Beltway, which is why high-mileage trucks and reliable sedans move and two-seat convertibles sit.

Google vehicle ads: put the inventory in the search results

Google’s vehicle ads show a car’s photo, price, mileage and your dealership name right in Google results, and a click goes to that car’s page on your site. Per Google’s vehicle ads overview, they’re fully released in the U.S., support new and used vehicles from dealers (brokers and private sellers aren’t allowed), and run through Performance Max or Standard Shopping campaigns. You link Google Ads to Merchant Center and to your Google Business Profile.

The fine print from Google’s vehicle ads policies trips up independents more than franchise stores:

  • The price in your feed has to exactly match the price on the vehicle’s landing page.
  • Vehicles with title defects, such as salvage titles, aren’t eligible.
  • You need a valid dealer license where required and a brick-and-mortar location in the state where you list cars.
  • Each Merchant Center account can list vehicles in only one state.
  • Mileage must be accurate in both the feed and on the page.

Most independents get the feed from their website or inventory management provider. Ask yours whether they’re set up for Google vehicle ads, and check that the price your website shows is the same number the feed sends. Mismatches are the most common reason we see cars get disapproved.

Run regular search campaigns next to it for the searches that aren’t about a specific car: “buy here pay here Houston,” “used trucks Pasadena TX,” “car lots that finance near me,” “carros usados en Houston,” “dealer sin crédito Houston” (only if your financing honestly fits that search). Negatives to load on day one: parts, junkyard, salvage, auction, rental, “for rent,” jobs, salesman salary, Carfax free, KBB, “sell my car” (unless you buy cars), and the names of national online retailers. Facebook Marketplace and Meta inventory ads

Marketplace is where a lot of Houston car shopping happens, and the rules for dealers changed in early 2023. Per Motor1, dealers can no longer publish vehicle listings through a Facebook business page; Meta moved dealers toward paid advertising for used inventory. Individuals selling their own cars weren’t affected.

Plenty of lots still have salespeople post cars from personal profiles. We wouldn’t build a business on that. It can run against Meta’s commerce rules, the listings aren’t tracked, and when that salesperson leaves, their Marketplace audience leaves with them.

The paid route is automotive inventory ads. You upload a vehicle catalog, and Meta shows relevant cars from your inventory to people whose activity suggests interest, per Meta’s automotive ads documentation. Meta recommends optimizing toward vehicle detail page views and measured actions through the Meta Pixel. In practice:

  • Keep the catalog synced daily so sold cars drop out. Nothing burns a lead faster than “sorry, that one just sold” on a car you’re still paying to advertise.
  • Use real lot photos, front three-quarter first. Twenty photos per car beats six.
  • Put the price, mileage and a short honest description in the catalog fields.
  • Retarget people who viewed a car on your site with that car, plus similar ones in their price range.

Messenger leads are high volume and low intent. Set up an instant reply that asks for a phone number and offers a test drive time, and route them into the same CRM as everything else. Our post on fixing bad Facebook leads covers form questions that cut the noise. If you’d rather hand off Meta, that’s our paid social team.

Financing ads on Meta and Google: the special category rules

Once an ad is about credit (“we finance everyone,” “bad credit OK,” “$500 down drives today”), the platforms treat it differently from an inventory ad.

On Meta, the old Credit special ad category was replaced by Financial Products and Services on January 14, 2025, per Meta’s developer changelog. Ads promoting credit opportunities, including auto loans, fall under it. Age is locked to 18 to 65+, gender to all, location targeting must include a 15-mile radius around any point, ZIP code targeting is gone, many detailed targeting options disappear, and lookalike and saved audiences aren’t available, per Jon Loomer’s guide to Meta’s special ad categories. For a dealer in Humble, a 15-mile minimum radius reaches a long way into Houston.

Google applies similar restrictions to credit ads in the U.S.: no targeting or excluding by age, gender, parental status or marital status, and no ZIP code targeting, per Google’s housing, employment and credit policy FAQ.

So let the creative do the targeting. A video of the actual lot with a salesperson saying, in Spanish and English, “first job, no credit history, come talk to us” finds the right people without a demographic filter. Keep the inventory ads in their own campaigns with normal targeting, and keep financing ads in the special category. Mixing them is how ad accounts get restricted.

TxDMV advertising rules every Texas dealer ad has to follow

Texas has detailed dealer advertising rules in 43 Texas Administrative Code Chapter 215, Subchapter F, and they apply to internet and social ads, not just newspaper ads. TxDMV’s dealer advertising guide summarizes them. The ones independents break most:

RuleWhat it means for your ads
§215.250, featured priceThe advertised price must be available to any retail buyer and include dealer prep and markup. Only taxes, title, registration and license fees, and other fees expressly allowed by law can be excluded. No “with trade” or “with dealer-arranged financing” qualifiers.
§215.250, “internet price”Terms like “internet price” or “e-price” that imply a different price online are prohibited.
§215.250, savings claimsSavings claims are allowed only on new vehicles. No “Was $18,995, now $15,995” or book-value comparisons on used cars.
§215.245, availabilityPrice ads for a specific vehicle need the stock number or VIN, and a used car can only be advertised once you have it and the title is assigned to you.
§215.251 and §215.254Disclose year, make, model and say “used” or “pre-owned.” “Program car” alone doesn’t count.
§215.247“Everybody financed” and “guaranteed approval” are prohibited statements.
§215.263, financing termsMentioning a down payment, payment amount, number of payments, term or finance charge requires the down payment, repayment terms and APR.
§215.266, superlatives“Lowest price in Houston” and “best deal” are out. “Meet or beat” offers must disclose the conditions.

Per the same guide, an online disclosure can work on hover or one click away if the ad clearly says where to click and the disclosure sits close to the claim. The Board can impose civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, with each day counting as a separate violation, though a first violation generally gets a cure letter. The full text of 43 TAC §215.250 is worth printing for whoever writes your ads.

Doc fees come up constantly. Under Texas Finance Code §348.006, you must charge the doc fee to cash and credit buyers alike, the buyer’s order and contract must carry the required notice (“A documentary fee is not an official fee...”), and the notice must be posted where sales are finalized. A Texas fee of $225 or less needs no cost filing with the Office of Consumer Credit Commissioner, per the OCCC’s 2024 rule adoption. How the doc fee interacts with your advertised price under §215.250 is a question to put to your dealer association or attorney, and the answer should go in writing to whoever builds your ads.

The FTC CARS Rule is gone. Deceptive-ad enforcement isn’t.

The FTC’s Combating Auto Retail Scams (CARS) Rule would have required offering price disclosures and banned certain add-on practices. On January 27, 2025, the Fifth Circuit vacated it 2-1 before it took effect, finding the FTC skipped a required advance notice of proposed rulemaking, per Holland & Knight’s summary. The court didn’t rule on the substance.

That doesn’t make hidden fees and bait pricing legal. The FTC has continued to go after dealers over misleading financing terms, add-on charges and bait-and-switch ads, and state regulators work alongside it, per the same summary. In Texas, TxDMV’s own rules already ban most of what the CARS Rule targeted in ads. Write your ads as if a buyer will screenshot them and bring them to the desk, because they will.

Credit applications: FCRA, Reg B and the Safeguards Rule

A lot of independent dealer websites have a “get pre-approved” form that collects a Social Security number, date of birth and income, then emails it to a Gmail inbox. That form is a compliance problem dressed up as a lead magnet.

Three things to know, none of them legal advice:

  • Adverse action notices. Most dealers count as creditors under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act because they participate in credit decisions. Notices are required when credit is denied, not offered on the terms requested, or when an application isn’t submitted for approval at all, and courts have said a lender’s notice doesn’t cover the dealer’s obligation, per Auto Dealer Today. If your website collects an application and nobody acts on it, that can be an adverse action.
  • “Pre-approved” language. Don’t promise approval in ads or on forms. Texas bans “guaranteed approval,” and federal credit-reporting rules govern prescreened offers. Let your compliance vendor write those words.
  • The FTC Safeguards Rule. It covers car dealers who finance or lease, requires encryption of customer information at rest and in transit, and since May 13, 2024 requires notifying the FTC within 30 days of a breach involving 500 or more customers’ unencrypted information, per WardsAuto. In June 2025 the FTC reminded dealers that they must also oversee their service providers, per Cybersecurity Dive. Your website host, form plugin, CRM and marketing agency are service providers.

The fix is usually simple: send credit applications through your F&I or credit-app provider’s secure form, keep your marketing forms to name, phone, email and the car they’re interested in, and make sure nobody forwards applications by email.

Buy-here-pay-here and Spanish-language buyers

If you hold the paper, you’re a lender as well as a retailer. Texas requires a Motor Vehicle Sales Finance license from the OCCC for businesses that hold retail installment contracts, as in a buy-here-pay-here model, even when no finance charge is charged, per the OCCC’s licensing page. Your marketing should match that seriousness.

BHPH ads that work in Houston tend to be specific about the process and quiet about promises: what documents to bring (pay stubs, proof of residence, references), how long approval takes, how payments work (weekly or biweekly, in person or by app), and what the cars are. Skip “no credit check” unless it’s literally true and your attorney is fine with it. And remember that a down payment amount is a triggering term, so “$500 down” in an ad needs the full financing disclosures.

For Spanish-speaking buyers, a translated homepage isn’t enough. What we’d set up:

  • Spanish search campaigns with Spanish ad copy and a Spanish landing page, not Google’s auto-translate.
  • A bilingual person answering the phone and Messenger on Saturdays, the busiest day for most lots.
  • Spanish versions of every financing disclosure in a Spanish ad. That’s our view of the safer course.
  • Paperwork that matches the conversation. Texas law already requires that if the sales presentation is in a different language than the contract, the buyer gets a written copy of the doc fee notice in the language used, per Texas Finance Code §348.006.

Reviews and CRM follow-up: where most car sales are won or lost

Buyers shopping five lots read reviews on all five. Ask every buyer on delivery day, by text, while they’re still in the car: “Thanks for buying from us today. If we did right by you, a Google review helps a lot: [link].” Ask service and BHPH payment customers too, since they visit more often. Stay inside Google’s prohibited content policy: no gas cards for reviews, and no sending the link only to happy buyers. Reply to every negative review with specifics and a name to call. Our Google reviews guide has templates, and the FTC fake reviews rule explains what not to do.

Then follow-up. A lead from a Google vehicle ad at 9 p.m. Thursday needs a reply that night, by text if they asked for text, with the car’s photos and a test drive time. Every lead goes into one CRM tagged with source and stock number, so you can tell which channel sold which car. Set tasks for day 1, 3, 7 and 14, then move cold leads into a monthly “new arrivals in your price range” email. See our speed-to-lead guide for scripts.

Texting is regulated. Texas brought marketing texts under its telephone solicitation law through SB 140, effective September 1, 2025, with registration requirements and a private right of action, though some exemptions apply, per Morgan Lewis’s summary of SB 140. Get consent for marketing texts separate from transaction updates, and our Texas SMS rules post covers the rest.

Frequently asked questions

How do used car dealers in Houston get more customers?

List every car where Houston buyers search: Google vehicle ads, major listing sites and Meta automotive inventory ads, with prices that match your website exactly. Run search campaigns for financing and Spanish-language searches, keep every ad inside TxDMV advertising rules, answer leads within minutes in the buyer's language, ask every buyer for a Google review on delivery day, and measure cost per car sold.

Can independent dealers use Google vehicle ads in Texas?

Yes. Google vehicle ads are fully released in the U.S. for new and used vehicles from dealers. You need Merchant Center linked to Google Ads and your Business Profile, a valid dealer license, a physical location in the state, prices that match the landing page exactly, and no salvage or other title-defect vehicles. Each Merchant Center account can list cars in only one state.

Is the FTC CARS Rule in effect for car dealers?

No. The Fifth Circuit vacated the FTC's CARS Rule on January 27, 2025, before it took effect, because the FTC skipped a required advance notice of proposed rulemaking. The court did not rule on the substance. The FTC and state regulators still pursue deceptive pricing and financing ads, and Texas dealers must follow TxDMV advertising rules in 43 TAC Chapter 215.

Can a Texas dealer advertise guaranteed approval or a Was/Now price on a used car?

No. TxDMV's advertising rules prohibit statements like guaranteed approval and everybody financed, and savings claims such as Was and Now prices are allowed only on new vehicles. Advertised prices must be available to every retail buyer, can't require a trade or dealer financing, and can exclude only taxes, title, registration and license fees, and other fees expressly allowed by law.

Do car financing ads on Facebook have targeting limits in Houston?

Yes. Ads promoting auto loans or other credit run under Meta's Financial Products and Services special ad category, which replaced the Credit category in January 2025. Age is locked to 18 to 65+, all genders are included, ZIP targeting is removed, location targeting needs at least a 15-mile radius, and lookalike audiences aren't available. Inventory ads without credit offers can run separately.
