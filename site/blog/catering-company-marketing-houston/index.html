Tuesday, 7:12 a.m. An executive assistant at a research office in the Texas Medical Center emails your general inbox: lunch for 40 on Thursday, six vegetarian, two gluten-free, individually labeled, invoice to a PO number. You see it at 11:30, after the morning deliveries. You reply with a menu PDF at noon.

She booked someone on ezCater at 8:05.

That gap, about four hours, is where most Houston caterers lose corporate business. The food is good. The answer just came too late, and nothing on the website could have answered it sooner.

Catering company marketing in Houston works when you make it easy for the people who order (office managers, executive assistants, event planners, couples) to see prices, check dates and book fast. Then you earn the repeat order. This post covers corporate catering in the Energy Corridor and the Medical Center, weddings, marketplaces like ezCater, Google Ads and LinkedIn, pricing pages, tastings, venue preferred-vendor lists and the Houston catering calendar.

Who actually buys catering in Houston

Houston has a lot of big workplaces close together, which is good news for caterers. Texas Medical Center describes itself as the largest medical complex in the world, with more than 120,000 employees and about 10 million patient encounters a year. the Energy Corridor District says its employers, including bp, Shell, ConocoPhillips, CITGO, Baker Hughes and MD Anderson West Houston, account for more than 56,000 local jobs. Add the office towers along Research Forest, Lake Robbins and Hughes Landing in The Woodlands, the Westchase and Greenway Plaza offices, and the plants and engineering firms on the east side, and you have a steady weekday market.

Corporate catering: what admins and office managers need from you

The person ordering corporate lunch isn’t a foodie. They’re someone with a dozen other jobs who will be blamed if lunch is late or the vegan option is missing. Your marketing should promise them a quiet Thursday.

Two things set caterers apart in this market.

First, speed. If someone emails at 7 a.m., an auto-reply with your online ordering link and a phone number that a human answers will save half those orders. Better still, put online ordering for drop-off catering on your own site, with a cutoff time and per-person pricing, so the admin can finish the order without you. Our speed-to-lead guide covers the response side.

Second, paperwork. Hospitals, universities and big energy companies often need vendor setup before they can pay you: a W-9, a certificate of insurance, sometimes a vendor portal registration. Put a “Corporate accounts” page on your site that lists what you can provide and how billing works. Admins forward that page to their accounts payable team, and you look like a vendor who’s done this before.

Then keep them. A standing Thursday lunch for a 30-person team is worth more than any one-off wedding over a year. After the third order, offer a standing order or a monthly menu rotation and a dedicated contact.

ezCater and the marketplaces: what they cost and how to use them

ezCater is where many corporate orders start. Admins like it because it handles billing, receipts and reviews in one place, and some companies require it. You can’t ignore it, and you shouldn’t build your whole business on it either.

ezCater doesn’t publish a single commission rate for every caterer, and its fees depend on the services you use. One third-party estimate, from Owner.com’s 2026 comparison of catering apps (a company that sells a competing ordering product), puts ezCater commissions at roughly 15% to 25% per order, higher with marketing or delivery support. The same piece lists Grubhub catering marketing tiers at 5%, 15% and 20%. Ask for your current rate in writing before you sign, and run your own numbers.

How we’d use marketplaces:

  • Treat them as a discovery channel for new corporate clients, and price your marketplace menu so the commission doesn’t erase your margin.
  • Build ratings there. Admins sort by rating and on-time delivery, so those reviews are worth earning.
  • Make your own channel better for repeat orders: faster ordering, saved preferences, net-30 billing for approved accounts. Read your marketplace agreement on what you can and can’t say to customers you met there, then compete on service.
  • Track which clients came from where. A client who finds you on ezCater and then books a 300-person holiday party directly is a good outcome.

LinkedIn for corporate catering: small budget, specific people

LinkedIn isn’t for weddings. It’s good for one thing caterers need: reaching the specific people who order lunch for Houston offices before they need lunch.

Per LinkedIn’s budget guidance, the minimum daily budget is $10 for any ad format. Small budgets can work if the audience is tight. Target job titles like office manager, executive assistant, administrative assistant, HR coordinator, workplace experience manager and events coordinator, in the Houston metro, at companies with 50 or more employees. Narrow further by industry (oil and energy, hospitals and health care, engineering) when you have menus and references that fit.

What to say:

  • A short video of a boxed lunch delivery being set up in a conference room.
  • A downloadable corporate menu with per-person pricing.
  • A first-order offer for new corporate accounts (a free dessert tray or delivery fee waived).

Organic LinkedIn helps too. The owner posting photos of a Research Forest office breakfast setup, with the client’s permission, reaches the people who order for the office down the hall. Our B2B marketing guide for Houston energy and industrial firms explains how those buyers think.

Menus and pricing pages that book events

The single biggest fix on most caterer websites is pricing. Couples and admins compare three caterers in one evening, and the one with no prices gets skipped.

What a good pricing page shows:

  • Per-person “starting at” prices for each package, with what’s included.
  • Order minimums, delivery fees by area (inside the Loop, The Woodlands, Katy, Sugar Land), and the cutoff for orders.
  • Service charge vs. gratuity, explained in a sentence. Clients get annoyed when they think they’re tipping twice.
  • Staffing, rentals and bar service as add-ons, with ranges.
  • A downloadable PDF, plus the same menu as text on the page so Google can read it.

Use “[your price]” in drafts if you’re building this page from scratch; never leave a placeholder live. A range is better than “call for pricing.”

Tasting appointments for weddings and big events

For weddings and large corporate events, the tasting is the sale. Marketing’s job is to get qualified people into one.

Put a tasting booking link on every wedding and event page, with available dates. Some caterers charge a tasting fee and credit it to the booking, which filters out people who aren’t serious. Ask three questions before you confirm: date, guest count and venue. Then follow up within a day of the tasting with a written proposal, and again a week later.

Getting on venue preferred-vendor lists

Many Houston and Montgomery County venues give couples a short list of approved caterers, and some don’t allow anyone else. Getting on those lists can bring a steady stream of weddings and corporate events without ad spend.

Venue coordinators recommend caterers who make their day easier. Arrive on time, follow load-in rules, clean up completely, and send the coordinator good photos from the event within a week (they need content too). Ask after your second or third event at a venue whether they keep a preferred list and what it takes to get on it.

Then build a page on your site for each venue you work often: “Catering at [venue name],” with photos from events there, a sample menu that fits their kitchen setup, and notes about the space. Couples search that exact phrase, and coordinators like having a link to send.

The Houston catering calendar

Catering demand in Houston has a rhythm, and your marketing should move ahead of it.

  • November and December. Holiday parties. Corporate planners book in September and October, so start holiday party ads and LinkedIn in early September.
  • Rodeo season. RodeoHouston runs March 2 through 21, 2027. Offices host Go Texan days and team lunches around it, and barbecue and Tex-Mex caterers can promote rodeo-themed packages in February.
  • Spring. Weddings, galas and graduation parties. May is busy with graduations across Cy-Fair, Katy, Conroe and The Woodlands school districts.
  • Summer. Slower for weddings in the heat, but corporate training and summer intern events continue. Indoor venues and drop-off menus do better.
  • Fall. The second wedding peak, plus corporate kickoffs and conferences.
  • Hurricane season. June through November. Put a weather and rescheduling policy in your contracts and on your site.

Reviews and permits: the trust details

Reviews do more for caterers than for most restaurants, because a corporate buyer is risking their reputation on you. Ask every client for a Google review after the event, with the same link and no incentive, per Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps. Ask happy corporate clients for a short written testimonial with their title and company (with permission) for your corporate page. Our Google reviews guide has templates.

Permits are a trust signal too. In the City of Houston, the Houston Health Department’s food permit FAQ says anyone who sells or serves food needs a food dealer’s permit, and food prepared in an unlicensed private home is prohibited. Fees through the Houston Permitting Center range from $224.44 to $739.44 by risk level, plus inspection fees. Outside the city, Harris County, Fort Bend County and Montgomery County and the cities within them have their own processes. Put “Licensed and inspected commercial kitchen” and your insurance coverage on your corporate page. Office managers at the Medical Center and the Energy Corridor will ask, and it’s better if they don’t have to.

What to track, and where to start

Track these monthly: quote requests by source, booked events by source, average order value, repeat corporate clients and marketplace share of revenue. If marketplace share keeps climbing while direct orders stay flat, your own site needs work.

Where we’d start this month:

  1. Publish pricing and a corporate accounts page.
  2. Set up an auto-reply and online ordering for drop-off catering.
  3. Launch a small Google Ads search campaign for corporate catering in your delivery area.
  4. Email five venue coordinators you’ve worked with about their preferred lists.
  5. Plan your holiday party campaign for early September.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get corporate catering clients in Houston?

Make ordering easy for office managers and executive assistants: per-person prices online, online ordering for drop-off catering, labeled meals, invoice and PO billing, and a corporate accounts page with insurance and vendor paperwork. Then reach them through Google search ads, LinkedIn ads targeted by job title, and marketplaces like ezCater. Offer standing orders after the third order, since repeat corporate lunches are where caterers make steady money.

How much does ezCater charge caterers?

ezCater doesn’t publish one commission rate for every caterer; fees depend on the services you use. A 2026 third-party estimate from Owner.com, which sells a competing product, put ezCater commissions at roughly 15% to 25% per order. Ask ezCater for your current rate in writing, price your marketplace menu so the commission doesn’t erase your margin, and track which clients come from the marketplace.

Should a catering company put prices on its website?

Yes. Buyers compare several caterers at once and skip the ones that say call for pricing. Show starting-at per-person prices for each package, order minimums, delivery fees by area, and how service charges and gratuity work. Add staffing, rentals and bar service as add-ons with ranges. Put the menu as text on the page, not only in a PDF, so Google can read it.

Do I need a permit to run a catering business in Houston?

Yes. The Houston Health Department says anyone who sells or serves food in the city needs a food dealer’s permit, and food prepared in an unlicensed private home is prohibited. Permit fees range from about $224 to $739 depending on risk level, plus inspection fees. Outside city limits, check with Harris, Fort Bend or Montgomery County and your city.

When is catering season in Houston?

Holiday parties in November and December are the biggest corporate peak, and planners book in September and October. Rodeo season in late February and March brings team lunches and Go Texan events. Spring and fall are wedding peaks, and May is busy with graduation parties. Summer is slower for weddings but corporate drop-off continues. Plan campaigns six to eight weeks ahead of each peak.
