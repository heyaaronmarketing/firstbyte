$250. That’s the cheapest published chamber membership we found in Greater Houston, the Bronze level at the Asian Chamber of Commerce. The most expensive is $120,000 a year for an Executive Partner seat at the Greater Houston Partnership.

Somewhere between those numbers is a membership that will bring your business real customers. There are also plenty that will bring you a plaque, a directory listing nobody reads and a lanyard collection.

Below we put the published dues side by side for The Woodlands, Conroe, Tomball, northwest Houston and the citywide chambers, then cover BNI, industry associations, how to judge the return before you join and how to work a membership so the return actually shows up. Where a group doesn’t publish prices, we say so instead of guessing. Prices change every year, so confirm on the organization’s site before you budget.

The Woodlands Area Chamber: what each level buys

The Woodlands Area Chamber publishes more detail than most, which makes it easier to price. From The Woodlands Area Chamber’s join page:

  • Small Business, $500. Meant for micro businesses of ten or fewer employees, nonprofits and individuals. One location and category, one representative, a ribbon cutting at the Chamber office, a $100 advertising credit, one Networking Breakfast credit and a half-day of meeting room time each month.
  • Community, $1,000. Up to two category listings and three representatives, a ribbon cutting within 12 miles, a $350 advertising credit and one membership mailing list.
  • Professional, $1,750. Up to three listings and four representatives, a $550 advertising credit and two mailing lists.
  • Premier, $3,000. Up to five listings and nine representatives, a $1,000 advertising credit, a $400 event sponsorship credit and verbal recognition at Business Connection Events.
  • Executive ($6,000), Signature ($9,000) and Corporate Partners ($15,000). Larger credits, Executive Leadership Council access and, at Signature, a podcast interview.

Two things we’d weigh. The number of representatives matters more than it looks, because a membership only produces business when people show up, and a sales team of four can cover four times the events. And the advertising credits are real money only if you would have bought chamber advertising anyway. If you wouldn’t, value them at zero when you do the math.

The ribbon cutting is underrated. It gives you photos, a reason to invite customers and neighbors, and a ready-made hook for a pitch to local media. Post the photos to your Google Business Profile the same day.

Greater Houston Partnership: when $3,000 makes sense

The Partnership is a different animal. It acts as the region’s chamber and economic development group, and its 2026 guide prices membership at $3,000 (Limited Partner, one committee), $5,000, $10,000, $30,000, $60,000 and $120,000, per the Partnership’s 2026 membership guide. Higher levels add committee access, briefings, board eligibility and executive programming.

Its small business page describes the Houston Business Exchange (HBX), a member program for small and mid-sized companies that want to build relationships with corporate and institutional decision-makers, plus Export Houston for businesses looking at international markets, per the Partnership’s small business page.

Our view: it’s worth a hard look if you sell to big organizations, such as an engineering services firm, a commercial cleaning company or an industrial supplier chasing energy-sector accounts. Our B2B marketing guide for Houston energy and industrial companies covers the rest of that sales cycle. It’s a poor fit for a nail salon in Magnolia. Consumer businesses get more from a local chamber where the members are also their customers and neighbors.

Identity chambers: Hispanic, Asian, Black and LGBT business groups

Houston has strong chambers built around communities, and for the right business they beat a geographic chamber easily.

the Houston Hispanic Chamber describes itself as “the leading regional advocate for the economic and civic interests of the Hispanic business community,” with seminars, networking, business panels, mentoring and an Emerging Leaders Institute. It doesn’t publish dues on its About page, so ask.

The Asian Chamber of Commerce does publish them: Bronze $250, Silver $500, Gold $1,000, Diamond $1,500, Platinum $3,000 and Titanium $5,000, per the Asian Chamber’s 2026 membership levels. The useful detail is in the benefits grid. Every tier gets seats in the Supplier Development Academy, Gold and above get email marketing to the chamber’s network, and Diamond and above get exhibitor tables at selected events and at the Business Expo. If supplier diversity programs matter to your sales, those lines are worth more than the luncheon tickets.

The City of Houston also lists the Greater Houston Black Chamber, the Greater Houston LGBT Chamber and the Houston West Chamber among the region’s chambers, per the City of Houston’s chamber list. Pricing wasn’t on the pages we could check, so call.

If your customers are mostly Spanish-speaking, membership pairs well with the work in our Hispanic marketing guide.

North and west: Conroe, Montgomery, Tomball, northwest Houston and Katy

The Conroe/Lake Conroe Chamber lists Business ($500), Gold ($1,000), Ruby ($1,225), Sapphire ($1,825), Emerald ($3,000) and Platinum ($5,000), with 20% off any level for nonprofits, per the Conroe/Lake Conroe Chamber’s member benefits sheet. In March 2024 the Montgomery Area Chamber, with about 240 members (roughly 30% of whom already belonged to both), merged into it, as Community Impact reported the merger. For a business in Montgomery, Willis or around the lake, that consolidation means one room to work instead of two.

The Greater Tomball Area Chamber’s 2025 guide runs from Primary Investor ($350) through Principal ($650), Classic ($1,000), President’s Club ($2,500), Chairman’s Circle ($5,000) and Elite ($10,000), per the Greater Tomball Area Chamber’s 2025 investment guide.

The Houston Northwest Chamber’s May 2024 packet offered monthly billing: Engage at $40 a month or $400 a year, Business Builder at $800, Business Developer at $1,200, then investor levels at $3,200, $6,000 and $10,000, with discounted two-year rates for new members at the lower tiers, per the Houston Northwest Chamber’s May 2024 packet. Check for a newer packet before you join.

Katy and Fulshear businesses should look at the Katy Area Chamber of Commerce. We couldn’t verify its current price list, so we left it blank above. Our notes on marketing in Katy and Cypress and reaching customers in Conroe and Montgomery cover the rest of the local picture.

BNI and referral groups

A chamber is a big room with loose ties. A referral group is a small room with an obligation. BNI is the largest of them: founded by Ivan Misner in 1985, it reports 355,000 members in 11,800 chapters, 17.8 million referrals and $26.9 billion in member-generated business, per BNI’s About page. Each chapter allows one professional per specialty, so the electrician in your chapter is the only electrician there.

Chapters keep opening around here. Community Impact reported in June 2026 on a new chapter for New Caney and Porter in east Montgomery County. Chapters meet across the Houston area, and most meet weekly, so visit the one closest to your customers before you commit to a year of mornings.

BNI doesn’t publish one national price. Fees are set at the chapter and region level, so ask the chapter’s director for three numbers before you visit twice: the annual membership fee, any one-time application fee, and the weekly meeting or meal cost. Multiply that last one by about 50 weeks. It often matters more than the membership fee.

Referral groups work best for trades and professional services that refer each other naturally: a realtor, a lender, a title company, an inspector, a roofer, an insurance agent. They work poorly for businesses that need volume from strangers, like a restaurant or a retail shop.

Industry associations often beat chambers

If your customers are other businesses in one industry, go where that industry meets. The Greater Houston Builders Association describes itself as the place “where Houston’s residential construction industry comes together.” For a cabinet maker, a pool builder, a title company or a marketing firm selling to builders, a GHBA luncheon is a room full of buyers. A general chamber mixer is a room full of other sellers. GHBA doesn’t publish dues on its membership page, though it offers guests a first luncheon or education course.

The same logic applies to dental and medical societies for anyone who sells to practices, property management associations for contractors, and HR or finance groups for B2B service firms. Look for the Houston chapter of the national association your customers belong to, then go as a guest before you join.

How to evaluate chamber ROI before you write the check

Start with total cost, not dues. Then work out how many customers you’d need to break even, and ask whether that’s believable.

Here’s a worked example. The numbers are illustrative, not a benchmark.

Dues$1,000A mid-level local chamber membership
Events and lunches$600Two paid events a month at about $25
Owner time$3,6004 hours a month, 12 months, valued at $75 an hour

Four clients a year from a room you visit twice a month is realistic for a lot of service businesses. Fourteen wouldn’t be. If the math needs more clients than you can picture, pick a cheaper level, a different group, or skip it.

Then track it. Add “Chamber” and “BNI” as lead sources in your CRM, ask every new client how they found you, and review it at renewal. Our guide to measuring marketing success covers how to set up that tracking.

How to work a membership so it pays

The members who say their chamber is worthless usually attended three mixers and waited. The ones who get business treat it like a part-time job with a short list of habits.

A few specifics. Volunteer for the ambassador team or one committee, because the people who run events meet everyone. Bring a referral before you ask for one. Follow up within two days with something useful, not a brochure. Our speed-to-lead guide applies to handshakes as much as web forms. And fill out your directory listing completely, with your website, hours and categories. A chamber directory listing is a legitimate local citation that helps Google confirm your business details, which supports your map pack rankings. It won’t rank you by itself.

A quick tax note on dues

Dues to a business league such as a chamber are generally deductible as a business expense, except the share the organization tells you goes to lobbying or political activity. Under sections 162(e) and 6033(e), chambers that lobby must notify members of that nondeductible share when dues are paid, or pay a proxy tax instead, per IRS technical guidance on sections 162(e) and 6033(e). Look for that percentage on your invoice and hand it to your CPA. This is general information, not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to join The Woodlands Area Chamber of Commerce?

The Woodlands Area Chamber publishes levels starting at $500 a year for its Small Business membership, meant for businesses with ten or fewer employees, nonprofits and individuals. Community is $1,000, Professional $1,750 and Premier $3,000, with higher levels up to $15,000. There is a one-time $50 enrollment fee. Check the chamber’s join page for current prices and what each level includes.

Is the Greater Houston Partnership worth it for a small business?

It depends on who you sell to. The Partnership’s 2026 guide starts at $3,000 a year, and its programs focus on regional economic development, policy and access to corporate and institutional decision-makers. It can pay off for B2B firms selling to large companies. Consumer businesses in The Woodlands, Conroe or Katy usually get more from a local chamber where members are also customers.

Chamber of commerce or BNI: which brings more business in Houston?

They do different jobs. A chamber gives you a wide local network, visibility and events, with loose obligations. BNI is a weekly referral group with one member per specialty and an expectation that members pass referrals. Trades and professional services that refer each other often do better in BNI. Businesses that want broad local visibility and B2B contacts usually prefer a chamber.

Are chamber of commerce dues tax deductible?

Generally, dues paid to a chamber of commerce are deductible as an ordinary business expense, except for any portion the chamber reports as going to lobbying or political activity. Chambers that lobby must tell members that nondeductible percentage when dues are paid. Look for it on your invoice and confirm the treatment with your CPA. This is general information, not tax advice.

Which chamber should a Spring or Conroe business join?

In Spring, compare the Houston Northwest Chamber, the Greater Tomball Area Chamber and The Woodlands Area Chamber, since the old Spring-Klein Chamber announced merger talks with Tomball in 2021. In Conroe, Montgomery or around Lake Conroe, the Conroe/Lake Conroe Chamber absorbed the Montgomery Area Chamber in 2024. Visit two events as a guest before joining, and pick the room where your customers are.
