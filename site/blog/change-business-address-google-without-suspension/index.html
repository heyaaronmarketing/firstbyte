A Google Business Profile suspended after changing address usually means Google couldn’t confirm the new details were real. Moving or renaming forces a fresh review, and suspensions tend to follow edits that arrive before the website, signage and paperwork agree with them, or several big edits made at once. Update the real world first, change one thing on the existing profile, re-verify, then leave it alone.

Already suspended? Jump to the second-to-last section, and keep our reinstatement walkthrough open for the appeal itself. Everything else here is about getting through a move or rename without needing it.

Why Google suspends profiles after an address or name change

The rules are written down. Google’s “Edit your Business Profile” page says Google reviews your changes “before we update them live on your profile.” It also says that if you move to a new address after verification, “you must verify your business again,” and that after a name change “you might need to verify your business again.” A re-verification prompt after a move is expected. It doesn’t mean anything is wrong.

A suspension is Google deciding the profile may not represent a real business at a real location. BrightLocal’s suspension guide lists the patterns that raise the odds: an address or hours that don’t match your website, keyword stuffing in the name, switching from a storefront to a service-area business, and lots of edits in a short window. BrightLocal also estimates calls can fall by as much as 90% while a profile is suspended.

The usual ways a move goes wrong:

  • The owner changes the Google address the week the lease is signed, while the new space is still drywall and no sign.
  • The name, address and phone all change in the same sitting. a 2023 Local Search Forum thread describes a tree service that did exactly this to keep roughly 150 reviews and ended up suspended, with a denied first appeal.
  • The new address is a virtual office or an unstaffed coworking desk, which Google’s guidelines for representing your business say isn’t eligible.
  • Someone creates a brand-new profile at the new address, leaving two listings for one business.
  • The website still shows the old suite number for a month after Google has the new one.

Every one of those is a sequencing mistake, which means every one is avoidable.

How to change business address on Google without losing reviews

The short answer: edit the address on the profile you already have. Don’t make a new one.

Google’s duplicate-profile help page is blunt about it: “If your business relocates, do not create a new Business Profile for the new location.” And Google’s page on moving reviews across profiles explains why that matters for your reputation: “If you move to a new address and keep the same business name, Google will transfer your reviews automatically.” The exceptions Google names are categories like hotels, golf courses and scenic attractions, where reviews describe the place itself.

Reviews stay with the profile as long as you move it instead of replacing it. The order we work in:

  1. Before moving day, clean up the new address. Search Google Maps for the new street address and suite. If a previous tenant’s listing is still there, suggest it as closed or moved. Whitespark’s moving guide calls this out, and it’s a step almost everyone skips.
  2. Get the space real. Permanent signage up, the door open during your posted hours, a utility bill or lease in the business name. If Google asks for a video, it wants signage, the business in operation and proof you control the space (our video verification guide has a full shot list). You can’t film what isn’t there yet.
  3. Update your website first. Contact page, footer, location page, embedded map, schema markup, title tags that mention the neighborhood. When Google checks the new address against your site, it should match to the suite number.
  4. Edit the address on the existing profile. One change. Keep the same name, phone number and primary category.
  5. Verify, then stop editing. Google picks the verification method, not you. If it’s a postcard, don’t touch the name, address or category until you enter the code; editing those voids it.
  6. Update citations after the new address is live. Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor, your chamber listing and any industry directories.
  7. Watch for duplicates for a month. Google sometimes auto-creates a listing at a new address from outside data. If one shows up, ask support to merge it into yours.

Will I lose my Google reviews if I move?

Not if you edit the existing profile and keep the same business name. Google moves the reviews with you, per the help page above.

Where owners lose reviews is when they open a second profile at the new location. Then the old reviews sit on the old listing and you’re stuck asking support to move them. another Local Search Forum thread follows a multi-doctor practice that did this in 2023: support closed the old location while the reviews were still waiting to move. The Whitespark account in that thread replied that updating the old profile’s address and re-verifying would have kept the reviews along with the listing’s age and trust.

Distance doesn’t change the process. Google’s review-transfer page lists no distance limit, so a move across town and a move from Spring to Katy work the same way. The competition is what changes. Your reviews come with you, but your map rankings are now measured against a different set of nearby businesses, so expect a few weeks of movement. Whitespark advises waiting a few weeks before judging the effect.

Edit, mark as moved, or create new: which one you need

Most owners only need the first row. The rest cover the messy cases.

Your situationWhat to do on GoogleWhat happens to reviews
Same business, new addressEdit the address on your existing profile, then re-verifyTransfer automatically (same name)
Small name tweak, same services and categoryEdit the name on the existing profile; keep proof readyStay on the profile
Brand-new name with different proper nounsGoogle’s rules treat it as a new business: mark the old profile closed, create a new oneStay with the old, closed profile
Storefront switching to service-areaRemove the address, add service areasStay on the profile
Accidentally made a second profileContact support to move reviews or mergeCan be moved; replies may be lost in a merge
Location closing, business continuing elsewhereEdit the address instead of closing, unless a separate location stays openTransfer with the edit

A word on the “moved” label. Google’s page on marking a business closed only offers “Temporarily closed” and “Permanently closed.” There’s no self-serve “moved” button, because when you edit the address there’s no old listing left to mark. The “moved” status only comes up when you end up with two listings. Then support can mark the old one as moved to the new one, though practitioners on Local Search Forum reported in 2021 and 2022 that support sometimes closes it instead. Another reason to avoid ending up with two.

How to change business name on Google (and stay inside the naming rules)

Name changes are where most of the “google business profile suspended after name change” threads come from. The rules here are stricter than most owners expect.

Google’s guidelines for representing your business include a Rebranding section. A profile can keep its history through “a minor name change, in which the proper nouns and services described in the business name remain unchanged” and “the business category remains unchanged.” Multi-location brands that rename everything may also qualify. If the change doesn’t meet that bar, Google says “it’s considered a new business,” and you “must mark the existing Business Profile as closed” and create a new one.

If you’re planning a full rebrand, that sentence matters more than anything else in this post. Under the written rule, a completely new name means a new profile without your reviews. Plenty of businesses edit the name anyway and pass verification, but know the rule before you pay to wrap new trucks.

Don’t stuff the new name

A rename is when owners are most tempted to slip in keywords. Google’s naming rules ban marketing taglines, phone numbers, URLs, service descriptors and location information. Its own “not acceptable” examples include “Midas Auto Service Experts” and “Holiday Inn (I-93 at Exit 2).” The name on Google should match your sign, your Texas filings and your invoices, character for character.

Get the paperwork changed first

If your LLC or corporation operates under a new name, file an assumed name certificate with the Texas Secretary of State. Since Texas HB 3609 took effect on September 1, 2019, entities like LLCs and corporations no longer also file at the county. Sole proprietors and general partnerships file with the county clerk, which in Conroe means the Montgomery County Clerk. That filing becomes your proof if Google asks.

A hypothetical Conroe rebrand

Say a roofing contractor off Loop 336 in Conroe has run as “Ridgeline Roofing” for nine years with 140 reviews. They add siding and gutters and want to become “Ridgeline Exteriors.” The proper noun stays, but the service word changes, so it sits in a gray zone. Our approach: change the sign, trucks, website and assumed name certificate first, keep the primary category as Roofing contractor, make the name edit alone, and have the filing and sign photos ready for verification. If they wanted a name with no Ridgeline in it at all, we’d talk through the new-profile tradeoff before anything else. Our rebranding guide covers telling customers about the change, which matters as much as Google does.

Moving business: Google Business Profile steps for a Woodlands med spa

To put the whole sequence on a calendar, take a hypothetical med spa that has spent six years in a suite off Research Forest Drive and signed a lease at Hughes Landing on Lake Woodlands Drive. Same name, same phone, same owners, a few miles away.

Six weeks out. List every place the old address lives: Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp, Facebook, the booking software, the Chamber directory. Search Maps for the new suite and flag any old tenant listings. Order permanent signage with the business name for the storefront.

Two weeks out. Draft the website changes, but don’t publish them yet. Write the email and text to clients with the new address, where to park, and the date the old suite closes. The parking note alone saves the front desk a week of calls.

Moving week. Sign goes up. First day treating clients at the new suite, publish the website changes in the morning and edit the Google address that afternoon. If Google asks for a video, the Hughes Landing storefront sign, the lobby and opening the back office with your key, all in one take, cover most of it.

The two weeks after. Hands off the profile until the new address is approved. Then update citations in one batch, add photos of the new space, and post about the move. The old Research Forest suite needs nothing on Google, because the profile moved with you.

Switching from a storefront to a service-area business

This is the change people underestimate. A contractor gives up the shop in Spring and goes mobile, or a mobile IV company stops seeing walk-ins. On Google, that means removing the address and listing service areas instead.

Google’s service-area help page says that if you don’t serve customers at your address, you should “remove your address and only enter your service area.” If customers still come to you and you also travel to them, you’re a hybrid and keep both. Service areas go in by city or ZIP code (Google caps the list and the drive time from your base), and approved edits can take up to 48 hours to show.

BrightLocal lists this switch as a suspension risk factor, so make it a single edit with nothing else changing that week. And be honest about the map. A Conroe crew that lists Willis, Montgomery, Magnolia and The Woodlands looks like a real business. Add Sugar Land and Baytown and it looks like someone padding the list.

Google Business Profile suspended after changing address? Do this next

First, stop editing. Every change now muddies the picture for whoever reviews the appeal.

  • Read the notice in your profile. It usually names the policy involved.
  • Make sure the profile matches reality: real name, real staffed address or service area, matching website.
  • Gather proof that names the business at the new address: lease, utility bill, Texas filing, photos of the permanent sign, a short video from the street to your door.
  • File one appeal through the link in the notice. Don’t file three.
  • Don’t create a fresh profile while you wait. A second profile for the same location is its own violation.

The reinstatement guide covers the appeal, the evidence and what to do after a denial. Reviews usually come back with a reinstated profile (Google notes a few may not), which is another reason to appeal rather than start over. If you run Local Services Ads, the suspension also cuts the profile’s link to your ads and your Google Verified badge, so expect those leads to stop until it’s resolved.

Frequently asked questions

Will I lose my Google reviews if I move my business?

No, as long as you edit the address on your existing profile and keep the same business name. Google says it transfers reviews automatically in that case, except for some categories like hotels or golf courses. You lose them when you create a second profile at the new location, which leaves the reviews on the old listing and forces you to ask support to move them.

How long does Google take to approve a new business address?

It varies. Google’s verification page says verification reviews can take up to 5 business days, and occasionally longer. Postcard verification adds mailing time, which Whitespark says can take 10 business days. Service-area edits can take up to 48 hours to appear once approved. Avoid other edits until the new address is live.

Why was my Google Business Profile suspended after a name change?

Usually because the new name didn’t match anything Google could confirm, or the change went beyond what Google’s rebranding rules allow. Names with added keywords, taglines or locations break the naming guidelines. Change your signage, website and Texas filings first, then edit only the name on Google, and keep proof ready for verification.

Should I create a new Google Business Profile when I move?

No. Google’s help pages say not to create a new profile because of a change in physical location. Edit the address on the profile you already have and re-verify it. That keeps your reviews, photos and the listing’s history. A second profile for the same business creates a duplicate, which can lead to a suspension.

Can I change my business address on Google before I move in?

We wouldn’t. Google may ask you to verify by video, showing exterior signage, your tools or products, and access to staff-only areas. If the space is empty, you can’t pass that check, and an address that doesn’t match your website invites trouble. Make the edit the day you start serving customers there.
