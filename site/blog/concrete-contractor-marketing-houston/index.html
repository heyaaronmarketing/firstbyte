Monday, 5:15 p.m. You’re standing in a backyard in Cypress, measuring for a patio extension. Your phone has a pour scheduled for Wednesday in Spring that the forecast now says is a 70% chance of storms. The ready-mix order needs to move. Somewhere in your truck are four estimates from last week you haven’t followed up on, and one of them was a 1,400-square-foot driveway replacement.

The work isn’t the problem. Most concrete contractors we talk to in Houston are good at the work and don’t have time to sell it.

To get more concrete jobs in Houston, rank in Google Maps for the specific work you want (driveways, patios, slabs, commercial flatwork) in the towns you want it, run search ads on high-intent terms like “driveway replacement” with negatives that filter out DIY and bag-mix shoppers, and use permits, photos and drone shots as proof. Then follow up every estimate on a schedule, and build relationships with builders and GCs so your calendar isn’t all one-off homeowners.

This is the playbook we’d run for a residential and light commercial concrete company working Houston, The Woodlands, Katy, Sugar Land, Pearland and the rest of the metro.

Three kinds of concrete customers, and what each one needs to see

A homeowner replacing a cracked driveway, a builder pouring 30 slabs a year, and a property manager fixing a trip hazard at a strip center all need concrete. They buy it in completely different ways.

Most concrete websites try to talk to all three at once on one page and convince none of them. Give each its own page and its own ad campaign. The homeowner page leads with photos and the estimate process. The builder page leads with capacity, crews, pump access and how fast you return bids. The commercial page leads with scheduling and safety.

Houston clay is your best sales argument

Every Houston homeowner has seen a cracked driveway. Most don’t know why it cracked, and the contractor who explains it gets trusted with the replacement.

The short version: much of Houston sits on the Beaumont Formation, and soils formed from it are highly expansive, according to a Houston Geological Society Bulletin article by a University of Houston–Clear Lake geologist. The same article points to the local cost of pavement damage, from buckled streets to broken curbs. Nationally, a 2023 HUD Cityscape paper cites the American Society of Civil Engineers estimate that one in four homes in the continental U.S. has been affected by expansive soils, with yearly damage above $15 billion.

That’s the setup for your sales pitch. Clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, so what goes under and inside the slab matters. Put your spec on the website and in every estimate:

  • Base preparation and how deep you go.
  • Slab thickness for driveways versus patios and walks.
  • Reinforcement (rebar size and spacing, or wire, or fiber) and why you choose it.
  • Control joint spacing and expansion joints against the house and garage slab.
  • Drainage: slope away from the house, and what you do about downspouts.

A homeowner comparing your $9,800 quote to someone’s $7,200 quote can’t see the difference in the dirt. They can see it on paper. The cheaper quote rarely lists rebar spacing.

One boundary: if a caller is describing a cracked slab under the house, doors sticking or interior cracks, that’s foundation repair territory, which is a different trade with engineers involved. We wrote a separate foundation repair marketing guide for those companies. If you do both, keep the pages and ad groups separate so the leads don’t mix.

Permits as proof: City of Houston driveway and sidewalk rules

Pulling the permit is a cost to you and a trust signal to the customer. Market it.

Per Houston Permitting Center’s residential driveway, sidewalk and curb permit page, a City of Houston permit is required for a new driveway or sidewalk and for removing and replacing an existing one, along with curb and gutter work and driveway extensions. The application needs a project number, two dimensioned site plans and a copy of the survey, plus the city’s sidewalk and driveway application form. The fee starts at a $103 minimum. Inspections are required before and after concrete placement, the culvert must be in and the forms set before you schedule, the bond must be current, inspections are scheduled for the next business day (no same-day), and the permit is valid for one year.

If the property sits in a floodplain, there may be another layer. Houston also has a floodplain permit for single-family driveways, which asks for the site plan to show all proposed flatwork and whether it matches adjacent grade, with a mitigation plan required for fill between natural grade and the 500-year flood elevation, plus pre-construction and final floodplain inspections. After Harvey, plenty of homeowners know their flood zone. Saying “we check floodplain requirements before we quote” on your driveway page is the kind of detail that wins a Meyerland or Kingwood job.

Outside the city, rules vary. In The Woodlands, The Woodlands Township’s permitting page says improvements need prior written approval from the Residential Design Review Committee, and that some paving goes through the City of Shenandoah depending on where the house is. Unincorporated Harris and Montgomery County, Sugar Land, Pearland and Katy all have their own processes.

How to use this in marketing:

  • A line in every ad: “Permits and HOA approvals handled.”
  • A short FAQ on each town page about who issues permits there.
  • A photo of the posted permit and the inspection tag in your job gallery. It looks boring to you. To a homeowner who got burned by a cash-only crew, it looks like insurance.

A website and Google profile that sell flatwork

Concrete is visual and local. Your site should feel like a portfolio organized by job type and town, with an estimate form on every page.

Pages we’d build first: driveway replacement, new driveways and extensions, patios (broom, stamped and exposed aggregate, only the finishes you actually do), sidewalks and walkways, slabs for shops and garages, commercial flatwork, and town pages for the five or six places you most want to work. Each job page gets five to ten photos of real jobs, the spec you used, and roughly how long it took.

Drone proof. An overhead shot of a finished circular driveway or a long commercial pour sells better than any copy. If you fly it yourself for business, the FAA rules apply: you need a Remote Pilot Certificate under Part 107, which means passing the knowledge test at an approved center (age 16 or older) and completing online recurrent training every 24 calendar months. Or hire a licensed pilot for a few hours a month and batch your best jobs.

Google Business Profile. Pick the primary category that matches your main work (concrete contractor for most), add real secondary categories, list each service separately, and set a service area that matches your trucks’ actual range. Post new job photos weekly with the town in the caption. Our local SEO guide for Houston contractors covers the full setup, and our web design and SEO team builds these sites.

Local Services Ads. When we checked Google’s Local Services Ads category list in November 2026, there was no concrete contractor category. Foundations services and General contractor are listed. Foundations services fits foundation repair work, and General contractor fits companies that really manage whole projects, so most flatwork companies should rely on regular search ads and the map pack for now. Check eligibility for your address in the sign-up rather than guessing.

Builders, GCs and bid platforms: the steady side of the business

Homeowner jobs have the best margins. Builder and GC work fills the calendar. You want both, and the marketing for builders looks nothing like the marketing for homeowners.

Start with the paperwork, because that’s what gets you on a bid list:

  • A capabilities sheet: crew count, pump access, typical monthly volume, service area, the kind of work you want.
  • A certificate of insurance and W-9 ready to send the same day.
  • A safety program in writing, even a short one.
  • References from two or three builders or supers who’ll pick up the phone.

Then get on the platforms GCs use to send invites. BuildingConnected, Autodesk’s bid platform, says its network includes over a million construction professionals, and GCs use it to send bid invites to subs. A complete profile with your trades, service area and past projects is what gets you invited. Then bid fast and bid complete: the subs who answer every invite, even with a “no bid this time,” are the ones who get asked next time.

On commercial sites, know the stormwater side. TCEQ’s construction stormwater general permit covers construction that disturbs one acre or more (one to five acres is a small site, five or more is large), and operators must have a stormwater pollution prevention plan before work starts. Your crews’ washout practices fall under the site’s plan, and a GC remembers which sub got them a stormwater complaint.

Relationships still close most builder work. Supplier reps at the ready-mix plants know which builders are growing. Builder association events and the occasional lunch with a superintendent matter. And a job page on your site for every notable project gives the super something to forward to the owner. Our B2B marketing guide for Houston has more on selling to companies rather than homeowners.

Estimate follow-up: where most concrete jobs are lost

Back to those four estimates in the truck. Concrete buyers almost always get multiple quotes. The job often goes to whoever follows up, explains the difference, and makes it easy to say yes.

What a good estimate looks like:

Two rules we push hard. Answer new leads within minutes, because a homeowner who submits three forms usually meets the first contractor who calls back (see speed to lead). And put every estimate in a CRM with a follow-up date, even a simple one. A spreadsheet beats your memory. Tracking close rate by lead source tells you which ads bring buyers and which bring price shoppers.

About lead sellers: platforms like Angi and Thumbtack can fill gaps, but the leads are shared and price-driven. Our Angi vs. Thumbtack breakdown covers when they pay off.

Seasonality, rain delays and the Houston calendar

Houston pours most of the year, which is an advantage over northern markets. The tradeoff is rain. June alone averages 6.09 inches at Hobby, per the National Weather Service (1991 to 2020 normals), and a stalled tropical system can wipe out a week of pours.

Rain delays aren’t just a scheduling problem. They’re a review problem. A customer who was told “Wednesday” and didn’t hear from anyone until Friday leaves a two-star review that says nothing about the quality of the driveway. Build the communication in:

  • Put your rain-delay policy in the estimate and the contract: how you decide, when you’ll notify them, and how rescheduling works.
  • Text the customer the day before every pour with a go or no-go, and again the morning of.
  • Send a photo when forms are set, when the pour is done, and when the job is cleaned up. It cuts the “is anyone coming?” calls and gives you before-after content.

For marketing, run ads with the calendar. Patio searches climb in late winter and spring when people picture summer evenings outside. Driveway replacement runs steadier but slows over the holidays. After a hard freeze or a long drought, cracked-driveway searches tend to rise, so have your driveway ads ready to scale. When you’re booked out six weeks, lower bids rather than pausing, and say “now scheduling for [month]” in the ads so the leads you do get know what to expect.

Service area matters for profit. A driveway job in Pearland is a different day than one in Montgomery if your yard is in Tomball. Most of the companies we’ve seen do best picking a side of town, building pages and ads for it, and saying no to the rest.

Reviews, budget and a first 90 days

Ask for the review when you do the final walkthrough, not when the invoice clears. Text the link with a photo of their finished job. Reply to every review with specifics (“the extension by the side gate”), which helps the next customer picture their own job. The Google reviews guide has templates.

A first 90 days:

  • Days 1 to 30. Build the driveway, patio and commercial pages with real photos and your spec. Fix the Google profile. Set up call tracking and a CRM with follow-up dates. Write the rain-delay policy into your estimate template.

Frequently asked questions

How do concrete contractors get more customers in Houston?

Rank in Google Maps for the specific work you want, such as driveway replacement and patios, in the towns you serve. Build a page for each service with real job photos and your spec for Houston clay. Run search ads on high-intent terms with negatives for DIY and bag-mix shoppers. Follow up every estimate on a schedule, and build relationships with builders and general contractors for steady work.

Do I need a permit to replace a driveway in Houston?

Yes, inside the City of Houston. The Houston Permitting Center requires a residential driveway, sidewalk or curb and gutter permit for new driveways and for removing and replacing existing ones. The application needs two dimensioned site plans and a survey, fees start at a $103 minimum, and inspections are required before and after the pour. Properties in a floodplain may need a separate floodplain permit.

Is there a Google Local Services Ads category for concrete contractors?

Not as of November 2026. Google’s Local Services Ads category list includes Foundations services and General contractor, but no concrete contractor category. Foundations services fits foundation repair companies, and General contractor fits firms that manage whole projects. Most flatwork companies should rely on regular Google search ads and the map pack, and check eligibility for their address in the sign-up.

What keywords should a concrete company use in Google Ads?

Use service and town keywords such as driveway replacement Houston, concrete patio contractor The Woodlands, driveway extension Katy and commercial concrete contractor Houston. Add negatives for products and DIY searches, like Quikrete, bags, mix, calculator, Home Depot, how to and jobs. Avoid blocking the word cement, because many homeowners say cement driveway when they mean concrete.

How do concrete subcontractors get on builder and GC bid lists in Houston?

Have a capabilities sheet, certificate of insurance, W-9, safety program and references ready to send the same day. Build complete profiles on the bid platforms general contractors use, such as BuildingConnected, and answer every invite quickly, even with a no-bid. Ready-mix supplier reps and builder association events are good ways to meet builders who are growing in Houston.
