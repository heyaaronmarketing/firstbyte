Tuesday, 9:15 a.m., at a fabrication shop off the Hardy Toll Road. The bridge saw is cutting a white quartz for a builder’s spec home in Conroe. Two installers are loading a granite vanity bound for Kingwood. Out in the slab yard, a homeowner from The Woodlands is holding her phone up next to a quartzite, comparing it to a photo she saved from Instagram. Her designer is on speakerphone.

Meanwhile the office phone rings with a remodeler asking if you can template Thursday instead of next week.

That’s a fabricator’s marketing problem in one morning. Most of your volume comes from trade accounts that never see an ad, but your best margins often come from homeowners who found you on Google or Instagram, and both groups judge you on the same thing: whether the template, fabrication and install dates you promised actually happen.

Countertop fabricator marketing in Houston works when you keep the builder, remodeler and designer pipeline full with outreach and reliability, and buy homeowner searches with ads that point to a bookable slab yard visit. Show the install on video and publish a timeline you keep. Be open about how you handle silica dust, because more people are asking.

Trade work fills the schedule. Homeowner jobs usually carry better margins, because there’s no middleman markup and you sell edge profiles, backsplashes and sink upgrades directly. Most Houston shops we talk to want more homeowner work and are scared of the hassle. The hassle mostly comes from unclear expectations, which marketing can fix.

Keep the two separate in your tracking. A trade account is measured in jobs per month; a homeowner campaign in cost per signed job.

What Houston homeowners are choosing: quartz, granite and quartzite

Engineered quartz is the default for most kitchens now. In the 2025 U.S. Houzz Kitchen Trends Study, 39% of renovating homeowners chose engineered quartz countertops, against 19% for granite and 11% for quartzite.

Your site should have a page for each material you fabricate, because homeowners search by material: “quartz countertops Katy,” “granite countertops near me,” “quartzite countertops Houston.” Each page should explain the material plainly, show finished local kitchens, and give a price range per square foot installed, even a wide one. If you aren’t comfortable publishing prices, at least publish your price levels (“Group A quartz from [your price] per square foot installed”).

The quartz-versus-quartzite confusion is your friend. Homeowners mix them up constantly, and a short, honest page explaining the difference (one engineered, one natural stone; different care, different price) ranks for a lot of searches and makes you look like the expert. Same for “is granite out of style,” which Houston homeowners ask every year, and which you can answer with photos of the granite kitchens you installed last month.

Remnants deserve their own page. Vanities, laundry rooms, outdoor kitchen tops and bar tops from remnant slabs bring in smaller homeowner jobs that often turn into a kitchen later.

Template to install: sell the timeline in Houston

Ask homeowners what worries them about countertops and you’ll hear the same thing: how long the kitchen is torn up. Sell your timeline, then keep it.

A process page should walk through selection, deposit, template, layout approval, fabrication and install, with the typical days between each step and what the homeowner needs to do (cabinets level and secured, sink and faucet on site, old tops removed or not). Shops that send layout drawings or digital slab layouts for approval before cutting should say so loudly. It’s the single best answer to “will my seams look bad?”

Then use it in follow-up. A text the day before template, a photo of their slab on the saw, a heads-up the morning of install. These cost nothing and get mentioned in reviews more than anything else.

Your timeline is also your trade pitch. Builders along the Grand Parkway and remodelers in Cypress and Tomball will switch fabricators for a reliable template-to-install window. If you can hold one, put it in writing on your trade page.

Silica safety is a marketing question now

Engineered stone has been in the news for years, and homeowners and designers increasingly ask fabricators about it. Have a clear answer.

The facts. Federal OSHA covers private employers in Texas, since Texas isn’t a State Plan state, per OSHA’s State Plans page. Under OSHA’s general industry silica standard, the permissible exposure limit for respirable crystalline silica is 50 micrograms per cubic meter averaged over eight hours, the action level is 25, and employers need a written exposure control plan, exposure assessments, and medical exams every three years for workers exposed at or above the action level for 30 or more days a year.

In 2026, OSHA and NIOSH updated a hazard alert for countertop manufacturing, finishing and installation. Per AIHA’s summary of the 2026 OSHA/NIOSH hazard alert, it names engineered stone and quartzite among the highest-silica materials, notes granite can be up to 50% silica, points to powered hand tools as the biggest exposure source, and recommends wet methods, local exhaust ventilation and doing as much work as possible in the shop.

The news side: a November 2023 CDC report found that at least 7 of 19 confirmed silicosis cases reported in California were tied to engineered stone countertop fabrication, with a median age of 44 for those workers. In August 2024 a Los Angeles County jury awarded $52.4 million to a 34-year-old fabrication worker with silicosis, according to Public Health Watch. Australia banned engineered stone benchtops, panels and slabs from July 1, 2024, per Safe Work Australia.

What to do with that in your marketing:

  • Publish a short safety page about how your shop works: wet cutting, dust collection, in-shop fabrication, training. Only claim what you can back up with your written plan.
  • If you carry low-silica or alternative surfaces, give them their own page with accurate product claims from the manufacturer.
  • Don’t scare homeowners about the countertops already in their kitchens or take shots at competitors. The exposure issue is about cutting and grinding.
  • Give your sales team two calm sentences for when a designer asks.

Shops that handle this openly look like professionals. Shops that dodge it look like they have something to hide.

Trade accounts: winning builders, remodelers and designers

Trade business comes from relationships, and relationships come from showing up with something useful.

  1. A trade page and price sheet. Material groups, edge profiles, cutout fees, typical lead times, and who to call. Refresh it quarterly.
  2. Slab holds that work. Designers and remodelers want to tag a slab for a client and know it’ll be there next week. Make holds easy and confirm them in writing.
  3. A target list. Remodelers in The Woodlands, Kingwood and Memorial, custom builders in Magnolia and Montgomery, kitchen designers in Houston showrooms. Twenty-five names, one visit or call each per month.
  4. Credit on social. Tag the designer and builder (with their permission) on every install post. That’s free marketing for them, which they notice.
  5. Easy client handoff. When a remodeler sends a homeowner to pick a slab, treat that homeowner like gold and report back to the remodeler.

Google Ads for countertop fabricators in Houston

Homeowner search volume is real, and the intent is strong. Someone typing “quartz countertops installed near me” has a kitchen in mind.

Structure we’d start with:

  • One ad group per material (quartz, granite, quartzite, marble, solid surface if you do it), each to its material page.
  • A “countertop replacement” group for people keeping their cabinets, which is a big share of Houston kitchens built in the 1990s and 2000s.
  • A remnants and vanity group for smaller jobs.
  • A slab yard group: “granite slab yard Houston,” “quartz showroom near me.” These people want to visit, so the conversion is a booked visit.

Google’s Local Services Ads category list also includes a Countertop services category. Test it if you have strong reviews. Pay-per-lead pricing can suit fabricators, since one booked kitchen pays for a lot of leads.

Write ads that answer the timeline worry. “Template to install in [your days] days. Visit our slab yard in Houston, book a time online.” Add the starting price per square foot if you can. Price in the ad cuts clicks from people who were never going to buy, which saves money.

Budget honestly: a single-location fabricator testing homeowner search might start around $1,500 to $3,000 a month (an illustrative range, not a benchmark), then scale on cost per signed job. Our Google Ads team sets these up, and the Houston CPC benchmarks help with expectations.

Instagram install videos and the slab yard as content

Countertops are made for short video. A 20-ton slab moving on a forklift is more interesting than almost anything a dentist can post.

Content we’d film every week, on a phone:

  • The slab reveal: a homeowner or designer seeing their slab for the first time.
  • The layout: seam placement and vein matching on a digital or taped layout.
  • Waterjet or saw cuts, sink cutouts and edge profiling (with your dust controls visible, which quietly answers the safety question).
  • The install: tops going in, the final wipe, the faucet hole drilled.
  • The finished kitchen with the neighborhood in the caption: “Calacatta-look quartz, Creekside Park.”

Organic reach builds the trade audience. Paid reach gets homeowners. On Meta, run your best install videos to people within 20 to 30 miles who’ve shown home-improvement interest, then retarget video viewers and site visitors with a slab-yard visit offer. Our paid social team runs these if you’d rather not.

Showroom and slab yard visits in The Woodlands and Houston

The slab yard visit is where homeowner jobs are won, so make it easy to book.

Put a booking link on every material page and in your Google Business Profile. Offer Saturday mornings, when couples can come together. Tell people what to bring: cabinet drawings, a photo of the kitchen, the cabinet color and floor sample. Send a confirmation text with a map pin, because many Houston slab yards sit in industrial parks with confusing entrances.

On the profile itself, upload photos of the yard, the showroom, and the entrance from the street. Answer the common questions in your own posts and website FAQ: do you sell to homeowners directly, do you have remnants, do you remove old tops, do you install backsplashes.

Count visits as a conversion and track which ads and pages produced them. A visit that turns into a signed kitchen is the number your budget should follow.

Reviews from homeowners and trade partners

Ask homeowners the day after install, by text, with a direct link. The installer can mention it on the way out. Ask trade partners too, once a year, since a remodeler’s review carries weight with other remodelers.

The rules, from Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps: no incentives, no asking only happy customers, no discouraging negative reviews. Same link to everyone.

Reply with specifics: the material, the edge, the neighborhood if the customer named it. When a review complains about a seam or a chip, answer calmly and offer a service visit. Read our complete guide to Google reviews for templates.

Frequently asked questions

How do countertop fabricators get more customers in Houston?

Run two pipelines. For trade work, build a trade page and price sheet, make slab holds easy, keep a target list of remodelers, builders and designers, and keep your template-to-install promises. For homeowners, run Google search ads by material that lead to a bookable slab yard visit, post install videos on Instagram, and collect reviews after every install. Track trade jobs per account and homeowner cost per signed job.

What countertop material is most popular right now?

Engineered quartz. In the 2025 U.S. Houzz Kitchen Trends Study, 39% of renovating homeowners chose engineered quartz, 19% chose granite and 11% chose quartzite. In Houston, granite still shows up in plenty of remodels and outdoor kitchens. A fabricator should have a separate web page for each material it cuts, with local photos and an installed price range.

What is the OSHA silica rule for countertop shops in Texas?

Texas private employers fall under federal OSHA. The general industry silica standard sets a permissible exposure limit of 50 micrograms per cubic meter over eight hours and an action level of 25. It requires a written exposure control plan, exposure assessments, dust controls, training and medical exams for workers exposed at or above the action level for 30 or more days a year.

Should a fabricator mention silica safety in its marketing?

Yes, calmly. Homeowners and designers increasingly ask about engineered stone after news coverage of silicosis among fabrication workers. A short page describing your wet cutting, dust collection and training, backed by your written plan, answers the question before it’s asked. Avoid scaring homeowners about installed countertops, avoid health claims you can’t support, and don’t attack competitors.

Are Google Local Services Ads available for countertop companies?

Yes. Google’s Local Services Ads list includes a Countertop services category. You pay per lead rather than per click, and ranking leans on reviews and how quickly you respond. For a fabricator with a strong Google rating, it’s worth testing alongside a regular search campaign. Judge both by booked slab yard visits and signed jobs, not by raw lead counts.
