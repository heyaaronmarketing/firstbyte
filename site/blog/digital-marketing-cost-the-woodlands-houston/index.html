$66.69. That’s the 2026 national median cost of a lead from Google search ads across all industries, per LocaliQ’s 2026 search advertising benchmarks. Attorneys pay about twice that. Restaurants pay less than half.

That spread is why “how much does digital marketing cost?” gets such useless answers. It’s also the question we hear most from owners in The Woodlands, Spring, Conroe and across Greater Houston, so here are real numbers.

Short answer: most single-location businesses around Houston spend roughly $2,500 to $12,000 a month on digital marketing, all in. That covers ad spend plus management, usually $1,500 to $5,000 a month for SEO or ads management, with a website of $3,000 to $15,000 up front. Competitive categories like law, HVAC and med spas spend more.

That range comes from our own proposals and the benchmarks below, and we’ll show the math, by channel, then three sample budgets.

Google Ads costs in Houston: what drives the bill

Search ads are the closest thing to buying customers by the pound. You pay when someone who typed your service clicks. The price depends on your industry, more than anything you do.

Houston tends to run above those national medians in the categories where everyone fights: personal injury, roofing, emergency HVAC and plumbing, foundation repair. A homeowner in Kingwood with no AC in August is worth a lot to a lot of companies, and the auction knows it.

Budget guidance we give most local businesses: $1,500 to $3,000 a month in media is enough to learn what works in a tight service area. Competitive categories in Houston usually need $5,000 or more to stay visible all day. Management on top, typically 10% to 20% of spend or a flat fee, plus a one-time setup of around $1,000 to $2,000 for tracking and structure. Our Google Ads management cost guide and Houston CPC benchmarks go deeper. If your category qualifies, Local Services Ads charge per lead instead of per click, which we compare in LSA vs. Google Ads.

SEO costs: monthly retainers and one-time projects

SEO has no media bill. You’re paying for people’s time: technical fixes, service and city pages, Google Business Profile work, reviews, links and reporting.

Backlinko’s survey of more than 300 SEO professionals, last updated in December 2025, found $1,001 to $2,500 a month is the most common retainer range, with $501 to $1,000 typical for small businesses with simple sites and $2,501 to $5,000 the premium tier. Most one-off projects (audits, content packages, migrations) landed between $1,000 and $5,000, and typical hourly rates ran $50 to $100, with senior specialists in the U.S. higher.

In Houston, a single-location business in a normal category usually lands between $800 and $2,000 a month. Law, dental, HVAC and roofing usually need $2,000 to $5,000 because the competition publishes more and earns more links. SEO is the slowest channel to pay back, often three to six months before meaningful movement, and the cheapest per lead once it works. Full breakdown in what SEO costs in Houston and The Woodlands.

Paid social, websites, streaming TV and mail

Facebook and Instagram. Cheaper per lead than search on paper. WordStream’s 2026 benchmarks put the median lead cost at $27.39 and traffic clicks at $0.60, per WordStream’s 2026 Facebook ads benchmarks, as summarized by PPC Land. The catch is intent: you’re interrupting people, so lead quality varies a lot and creative wears out. Most local businesses start at $1,000 to $5,000 a month in media. Our post on fixing bad Facebook leads explains why the cheap lead isn’t always the cheap customer.

Websites. Most professional small-business sites in The Woodlands land between $3,000 and $15,000. A site with service-area pages, booking and integrations runs higher. Budget domain and hosting yearly, plus some support time.

Streaming TV and audio. CTV CPMs average around $26 per AdWave’s 2026 CTV benchmark, and you can target by ZIP code, which made “TV” realistic for local brands. Production is the other line item. Our Houston radio and TV cost guide has station-level detail.

Billboards. AdQuick’s Houston rate ranges put a static freeway bulletin on I-45, I-10 or the 610 Loop at about $4,000 to $14,000 per four weeks. Full detail in our billboard cost guide.

Direct mail. EDDM postage is $0.260 a piece since July 12, 2026, per MailPro’s July 2026 rate table, so 5,000 homes in Sterling Ridge or Alden Bridge costs $1,300 in postage before printing. See EDDM costs in The Woodlands.

What do marketing agencies in Houston charge?

Four pricing models, often mixed:

  • Monthly retainer. The most common for ongoing work. Small-business retainers in Houston commonly run $1,500 to $5,000 a month; multi-channel programs for growing brands $5,000 to $20,000 and up.
  • Percentage of ad spend. Usually 10% to 20% of media, almost always with a monthly minimum.
  • Project fees. Websites, rebrands, launches, audits.
  • Hourly. Mostly freelancers and consultants.

For a national reference point, SE Ranking’s agency pricing survey found U.S. and Canadian agencies reported a median retainer of $1,863 a month, a median project of $3,625 and a median hourly rate of $112. Small sample (135 agencies), but it lines up with what we see locally.

Be wary of very low monthly prices. A $299 SEO package almost always means automated reports and little real work. Equally, be wary of long lock-in contracts and any setup where the agency owns your Google Ads account. Our posts on agency pricing models and who owns your ad account cover both.

How much should a small business spend on marketing?

The figure everyone quotes comes from big companies. Gartner’s 2026 CMO Spend Survey found marketing budgets averaged 7.8% of company revenue, from a survey of 401 marketing leaders at large organizations. Useful context, not a rule for a three-truck plumbing company in Tomball.

A better method is to work backwards from what a customer is worth:

Run it with real numbers. Say a new HVAC maintenance customer is worth $1,200 in gross profit over two years and you’ll pay up to $300 to win one. Ten new customers a month means a $3,000 acquisition budget. If search leads cost around $90 and one in three becomes a customer, each customer costs about $270, so search fits. If your close rate is one in six, it doesn’t, and the fix is your follow-up, not the budget. Our budget guide and cost per lead explainer go step by step.

Agency, freelancer or in-house: what each option costs

The same $5,000 a month buys very different things depending on who you hand it to.

A freelancer is usually the cheapest per hour and often excellent at one thing, like Google Ads or design. The risk is coverage. When they’re booked, on vacation or gone, your ads keep spending with nobody watching. Ask who has access to your accounts if they disappear for two weeks.

An agency costs more per hour but spreads the work across specialists: someone on search, someone on social, a designer, someone who sets up tracking. You’re paying for the bench as much as the hours. The risk is getting a junior account manager after a senior person sold you, so ask who actually does the work.

An in-house hire makes sense once there’s a full week of marketing work every week. Add salary, payroll taxes, benefits, software seats and training before you compare. One person rarely covers ads, SEO, design and analytics equally well, which is why many Houston companies pair one in-house coordinator with outside specialists. What changed in 2026, and what to budget for 2027

Three shifts are worth a line in next year’s plan.

Search is splitting. BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations jumped from 6% to 45% in a year. Those tools draw on reviews, your Google Business Profile and the content on your site. That makes review generation and solid service pages a budget line rather than an afterthought, typically a few hundred dollars a month of software and staff time.

Social leads got slightly cheaper, search didn’t. WordStream’s median Facebook lead cost dipped from $27.66 to $27.39, and traffic clicks fell from $0.70 to $0.60, per WordStream’s 2026 Facebook ads benchmarks, as summarized by PPC Land. Search remains the more expensive click with the higher intent. Neither number tells you which channel produces customers for you; your own tracking does.

Creative is the new bottleneck. Meta, YouTube and CTV all reward fresh video. If you plan to run those in 2027, budget for a shoot each quarter, not a single brand video you run for a year. Our 2027 marketing plan for Houston businesses lays out the calendar.

Frequently asked questions

How much does digital marketing cost per month for a small business in Houston?

Most single-location small businesses in the Houston area spend roughly $2,500 to $12,000 a month all in, including ad spend, management, creative and tools. Lean starter plans can run near $3,000, while competitive categories like law, HVAC or med spas often spend $10,000 to $20,000 or more. A website is usually a separate one-time cost of $3,000 to $15,000.

What is a good monthly budget for Google Ads in The Woodlands?

Most local businesses in The Woodlands and north Houston can learn what works with $1,500 to $3,000 a month in ad spend in a tight service area, plus management. Competitive categories such as personal injury law, roofing and emergency HVAC usually need $5,000 or more to stay visible. The 2026 national average cost per lead from search ads was $66.69, with wide swings by industry.

How much do marketing agencies charge in Houston?

Houston agencies usually charge a monthly retainer, a percentage of ad spend, project fees, or a mix. Small-business retainers commonly run $1,500 to $5,000 a month, percentage pricing is typically 10% to 20% of ad spend with a minimum, and websites are quoted as projects. An SE Ranking survey put the U.S. and Canada median retainer at $1,863 a month.

Is it cheaper to hire a marketing person or an agency?

For most small businesses an agency costs less than a full-time hire once you add salary, benefits, tools and the range of skills needed: ads, SEO, design and analytics rarely sit in one person. A full-time marketer makes more sense once you have enough steady work to fill their week, and many companies pair one in-house coordinator with an agency for specialist work.

What percentage of revenue should a small business spend on marketing?

Gartner’s 2026 survey of large companies found marketing budgets averaged 7.8% of revenue, which many people quote as a guide. For a small business, a better method is to start with what a new customer is worth, decide what you can afford to pay to win one, and multiply by how many customers you need each month. Growing businesses usually spend a larger share.
