Your agency’s monthly report says ROAS is up, CTR is down, PMax is “still learning” and you picked up 14 key events. You run a roofing company in Spring. Which of those numbers means more roofs?

This glossary is for Houston and The Woodlands business owners who want to read their own reports without a translator. Each term gets a plain-English definition and a local example, and many link to a longer post. Terms are grouped A to Z, and the figures group related terms together, starting with the one that matters most: how the money metrics connect.

If you only learn five terms, make them conversion, CPL, CAC, ROAS and MER. Most of the others explain why those five moved.

A: from A/B test to Average order value

A/B test

Running two versions of an ad, page or email at the same time to see which performs better. A Katy dentist tests “Same-week appointments” against “Most insurance accepted” as a headline, and keeps the winner.

Ad Rank

Google’s calculation of where your ad shows and whether it shows at all, based on your bid, ad quality, landing page and context. It’s why a better ad from a small Humble plumber can sit above a national franchise that bids more.

AEO (answer engine optimization)

Making your content easy for AI tools like ChatGPT and Google’s AI features to quote. A Conroe HVAC company’s page that plainly answers “how much does AC repair cost in Conroe” is AEO. See showing up in AI search.

AI Overviews and AI Mode

Google’s AI-generated answers at the top of some search results (AI Overviews) and its conversational search tab (AI Mode). Google’s page on AI features says there are no special optimizations or schema needed to appear, and the traffic is counted in Search Console under the Web search type. More in AI Overviews for Houston businesses.

Attribution

The rules for giving credit to the ads and channels that led to a sale. If a Sugar Land customer saw your Instagram ad, then searched your name and called, attribution decides which one gets the credit.

Average order value (AOV)

Total revenue divided by number of orders. A bakery in The Woodlands with $18,000 from 600 online orders has a $30 AOV, which caps what it can pay per click.

B and C: Broad match through CTR

Backlink

A link to your website from another site. A mention in Community Impact or a link from the Greater Houston Builders Association counts for far more than 50 links from directories nobody reads.

Broad match

A Google Ads keyword setting that lets your ad show for searches related to your keyword, not just the keyword itself. “Plumber Spring TX” on broad match might also show for “plumbing jobs Spring.” Pair it with negative keywords and smart bidding, or don’t use it.

CAC (customer acquisition cost)

Everything you spent to win a customer, divided by new customers. If a Tomball med spa spends $6,000 in ads and fees and gets 20 new clients, CAC is $300. This is the number to compare against what a customer is worth.

Call tracking

Unique phone numbers that show which ad, page or flyer made the phone ring, often with recordings. Essential for any business where customers call instead of filling out forms. See call tracking explained.

Citation

Any online listing of your business name, address and phone number: Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, your local chamber of commerce directory in The Woodlands or Katy. Consistent citations help Google trust your details.

Consent mode

A Google setting that adjusts how tags behave when a visitor declines cookies. Matters more if you have visitors from places with stricter privacy laws, like a Galveston hotel that books European travelers.

Conversion

The action you count as a win: a call over 60 seconds, a form, a booking, a purchase. In Google Ads, it’s a conversion action. A Woodlands law firm that counts every page view as a conversion is fooling itself.

Conversion rate (CVR)

Conversions divided by clicks. WordStream/LocaliQ’s 2026 Google Ads benchmarks put the all-industry Google Ads average at 8.18%. If 400 people click a Pearland orthodontist’s ad and 32 book a consult, CVR is 8%.

Core Web Vitals

Google’s three page-experience measurements: loading (LCP), responsiveness (INP) and visual stability (CLS). Google’s Core Web Vitals page lists “good” as 2.5 seconds, 200 milliseconds and 0.1. See how fast your website should load.

CPC (cost per click)

What you pay each time someone clicks your ad. The 2026 national average was $5.42, per the same WordStream data, and emergency AC terms in Houston in July run far higher. See Houston CPC benchmarks.

CPL (cost per lead)

Spend divided by leads. The national Google Ads average was $66.69 in 2026. More in understanding cost per lead.

CPM (cost per thousand impressions)

What you pay for 1,000 ad views. The standard price unit for streaming TV, billboards, YouTube and most social ads. A billboard on I-45 near the Woodlands Parkway exit is priced on impressions, too. See billboard costs in Houston.

CRM (customer relationship management)

The software that holds your leads and customers, such as HubSpot or a field-service tool. It’s where you find out which leads actually bought. A Spring HVAC company that logs every call in its CRM can tell which ZIP codes book maintenance plans.

CTR (click-through rate)

Clicks divided by impressions. A high CTR means the ad matches what people searched; it doesn’t mean they bought. A “Free pizza” ad for a Katy restaurant gets a great CTR and terrible margins.

D to G: Display Network through Google Verified

Display Network

Google’s network of websites and apps that show banner and image ads. Cheap reach, low intent. Fine for retargeting, risky as a primary channel for a local service business. A Conroe roofer’s banner on a mobile game app mostly buys accidental taps.

Engagement rate

In GA4, the share of sessions that lasted over 10 seconds, viewed two or more pages or triggered a key event. It replaced bounce rate as the main quality signal. If Facebook traffic to a Sugar Land salon’s site shows 20% engagement and Google traffic shows 65%, that tells you something.

Enhanced conversions

A Google Ads feature that sends hashed customer details (like an email) with a conversion so Google can match it more accurately. Helps when cookies drop off, such as when a Kingwood customer clicks on a phone and fills out the form on a laptop that evening.

Frequency

How many times the average person saw your ad. If people in Kingwood have seen your Facebook ad 11 times this month, they’re tired of it.

Funnel

The path from first seeing you to buying, usually drawn as awareness, consideration and conversion. See from clicks to customers.

GA4 (Google Analytics 4)

Google’s current analytics platform. It tracks events, not page views, as its basic unit. A Tomball landscaper’s GA4 might show a form_submit event, a click_to_call event and a page_view for the pricing page.

GBP (Google Business Profile)

Your free listing on Google Search and Maps: hours, photos, reviews, categories. For most Houston local businesses it’s the single most valuable marketing asset. See the Google Business Profile guide.

GCLID

The Google Click ID added to a URL when someone clicks your ad. Save it with the lead in your CRM and you can tell Google later which clicks became customers. That’s how a Houston law firm teaches Google the difference between a signed case and a wrong number.

Geo-fencing

Showing ads to phones inside a drawn boundary, like the NRG Park area during the rodeo. See geo-fencing ads.

Google Guaranteed and Google Verified

Badges shown on Local Services Ads after Google screens a business, such as license and background checks for a Katy electrician. See Google Guaranteed vs Google Verified.

I to L: Impressions through LSA

Impressions

The number of times your ad or listing was shown. Not the same as people, and not the same as anyone looking. Your Business Profile can rack up thousands of impressions from people searching “coffee Market Street” who never notice you.

Impression share

The percentage of eligible impressions you actually got. A Pearland plumber at 40% search impression share is missing six in ten chances, usually because of budget or ad rank.

Indexing

Google adding a page to its search database. A page that isn’t indexed can’t rank, no matter how good it is. Search Console shows which pages are indexed. A new “AC repair Magnolia” page that isn’t indexed after three weeks needs a look.

Key event

GA4’s name for an important action, like a form submission. Google renamed GA4 conversions to key events in March 2024, and its events and key events page explains how to mark them. A key event becomes a conversion only when you import it into Google Ads.

Keyword

The word or phrase you bid on or optimize for. “Foundation repair Houston” is a keyword. So is “why is my door sticking,” which a smart foundation company in Katy should have a page for.

KPI (key performance indicator)

The few numbers you agree to judge marketing by. Pick three at most: booked jobs, CPL and CAC is a good start.

Landing page

The page someone lands on after clicking an ad. It should match the ad exactly: an ad for “emergency plumber Cypress” lands on an emergency plumbing page that mentions Cypress, not the homepage. See landing pages 101.

Lead

A potential customer who contacted you or gave their details. A lead is not a customer, and a form from a spam bot isn’t a lead. Neither is a call from someone in Dallas asking if you’re hiring.

Lookalike audience

A Meta audience of people similar to a list you upload, such as past customers. Not available for housing, employment or credit ads, which matters to Montgomery County home builders and lenders.

LSA (Local Services Ads)

Google’s pay-per-lead ads at the top of searches for plumbers, roofers, lawyers and other eligible services. You pay per lead, not per click, and can dispute invalid ones. See LSA vs Google Ads.

M to O: Map pack through Organic

Map pack (local pack)

The three business listings with a map that show for local searches. Google’s local ranking help page says rankings come from relevance, distance and prominence. See ranking in the Houston map pack.

Match types

Google Ads settings (broad, phrase, exact) that control how closely a search must match your keyword before your ad can show. Exact match on [roof repair the woodlands] is tight; broad match on the same words can wander into “roof repair jobs Houston.”

MER (marketing efficiency ratio)

Total revenue divided by total marketing spend, across every channel. A Woodlands boutique with $90,000 in monthly sales and $9,000 in total marketing has a MER of 10. It’s harder to game than ROAS because no platform gets to grade its own homework.

Meta Pixel and Conversions API

The code (Pixel) and server connection (Conversions API) that tell Meta what people did on your site after clicking an ad. Without them, a Woodlands med spa can’t tell whether its Instagram ads booked anyone.

NAP

Name, address, phone. It should match exactly on your website, Google Business Profile and citations. “Suite 200” on one and “Ste. 200” is fine; a different phone number is not.

Negative keyword

A word that stops your ad from showing. For most service businesses: jobs, salary, free, DIY, how to. Every Houston account needs a list on day one.

Offline conversion import

Sending sales that happened later, in your CRM or at the counter, back to Google Ads or Meta so their bidding learns which clicks turned into revenue.

Organic

Traffic or results you didn’t pay for per click: SEO rankings, map listings, unpaid social posts. A Pearland bakery ranking for “king cake Pearland” every January is organic.

P to R: PMax through ROI

PMax (Performance Max)

A Google Ads campaign type that its Performance Max help page describes as accessing all Google Ads inventory, including YouTube, Display, Search, Discover, Gmail and Maps, from a single campaign. Powerful with clean conversion data, wasteful without it. See PMax vs Search.

PPC (pay per click)

Any ad model where you pay per click. Usually shorthand for Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search campaigns. When a Houston agency says “we do PPC,” this is what they mean. See whether Microsoft Ads are worth it.

Primary category

The main category on your Google Business Profile, and one of the strongest local ranking signals. A Conroe roofer should pick “Roofing contractor,” not “Contractor.”

Quality Score

A 1-10 Google Ads score per keyword based on expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google’s Quality Score help page calls it a diagnostic tool. A 3 on “dentist The Woodlands” usually means the landing page doesn’t mention The Woodlands or the service. See Quality Score and CPC.

Reach

The number of different people who saw your ad at least once. Pair it with frequency. A streaming TV campaign that reaches 40,000 households in Montgomery County four times each is a reach-and-frequency buy.

Retargeting (remarketing)

Showing ads to people who already visited your site or engaged with you. A Cypress pool builder retargets everyone who looked at the pricing page. See retargeting explained.

ROAS (return on ad spend)

Revenue divided by ad spend for a specific campaign or platform. A ROAS of 4 means $4 back for every $1 spent. Platform-reported ROAS tends to run optimistic, which is why we check it against MER.

ROI (return on investment)

Profit minus cost, divided by cost. Unlike ROAS, it uses profit and includes every cost, so it’s the number your accountant cares about. A Katy remodeler with a 5x ROAS on $40,000 kitchens might still lose money if the jobs run at thin margins.

S and T: Schema through Transcreation

Schema (structured data)

Code that labels your page content so search engines understand it: your hours, address, reviews, FAQs. Google’s LocalBusiness structured data documentation covers the local business version. A Tomball vet clinic’s site should mark up its address, hours and phone number this way.

Search Console

Google’s free tool that shows which searches your site appears for, your clicks and positions, and indexing problems. Every business should have it set up. It’s where a Woodlands CPA finds out people search “tax preparer near me” far more than “accounting firm.”

Search terms report

The list of actual searches that triggered your Google Ads. Reading it weekly is how you find the “plumbing school Houston” clicks you’re paying for.

SERP

Search engine results page. Today it can include ads, LSAs, an AI Overview, the map pack and organic results all on one screen. Search “water heater repair Houston” on a phone and organic results may not appear until the third scroll.

Service area business

A business that serves customers at their location and hides its address on Google, like a mobile groomer in Magnolia. You list up to 20 service areas instead.

Smart Bidding

Google’s automated bidding strategies, such as Maximize Conversions and Target CPA, which set bids per auction using conversion data. They learn from what you count, so a Sugar Land clinic counting page views as conversions trains Google to buy page views.

Speed to lead

How fast you respond to a new inquiry. The difference between five minutes and two hours decides a lot of Houston service jobs. See speed to lead.

Target CPA

A Smart Bidding strategy that aims for an average cost per conversion you set. Set it too low and the campaign stops spending. A Houston pest control company asking for $15 leads in a $60 market will watch impressions dry up.

Transcreation

Rewriting an ad for another language and culture, rather than translating word for word. See marketing to Houston’s Asian American communities and Hispanic marketing in Houston.

U to Z: UTM through Zero-click search

UGC (user-generated content)

Photos, videos and reviews made by customers instead of the brand, like a customer’s video of their new patio cover in Cypress. See UGC vs influencers.

UTM parameters

Tags added to the end of a link (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign) so GA4 knows where a visit came from. Google’s URL builder help explains each one. Put them on every email, social post and QR code. A door hanger in Kingwood with a UTM-tagged QR code is a trackable door hanger.

Zero-click search

A search where the person gets an answer without clicking any result: from the map pack, a phone number or an AI Overview. For a local business, a call from the map pack is a zero-click search that still pays.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ROAS and MER?

ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend for one campaign or platform, usually as reported by that platform. MER is total revenue divided by total marketing spend across every channel. ROAS helps you compare campaigns, while MER tells you whether marketing as a whole is working. Because platforms often over-credit themselves, Houston businesses should check ROAS against MER every month.

What is a good cost per lead for a Houston business?

It depends on your industry and what a customer is worth. National Google Ads averages in 2026 ran about $67 per lead across all industries, about $91 for home services and about $132 for legal. Competitive Houston categories often run higher. A good CPL is one where your close rate and job value still leave a healthy profit after marketing costs.

What is the difference between a GA4 key event and a Google Ads conversion?

A key event is an important action you mark in Google Analytics 4, such as a form submission or phone call click. Google renamed GA4 conversions to key events in 2024. A key event only becomes a Google Ads conversion when you import it into your Google Ads account, or you can track conversions directly in Google Ads with its own tag.

What does NAP mean in local SEO?

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Your NAP should match exactly across your website, Google Business Profile and directory listings like Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing. Consistent details help Google trust that your business is real and located where you say. Mismatched phone numbers or old addresses are a common problem for Houston businesses that have moved.

What are UTM codes and why do they matter?

UTM codes are tags added to the end of a link, such as utm_source and utm_campaign, that tell Google Analytics where a visitor came from. They let you see which email, social post, QR code or print piece drove traffic and leads. Without them, much of that traffic shows up as direct or unassigned, and you can’t tell what worked.
