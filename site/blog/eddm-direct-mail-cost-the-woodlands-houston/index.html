The 2026 EDDM rates and size rules in The Woodlands and Houston

The postage piece is the easy part, because USPS sets one price for everyone. On July 12, 2026, the EDDM Retail rate went from $0.247 to $0.260 per piece, per MailPro’s July 2026 rate table. USPS announced in April that the July changes would cover Marketing Mail, which is the class EDDM travels in.

The physical rules matter more than people expect, because a piece that doesn’t qualify gets turned away at the counter. Under USPS Domestic Mail Manual section 143, an EDDM Retail piece is a flat. It has to be longer than 10-1/2 inches, or taller than 6-1/8 inches, or thicker than 1/4 inch. It can weigh no more than 3.3 ounces. A mailing needs at least 200 pieces (unless the area you’re mailing has fewer than 200 addresses), and you can drop no more than 5,000 pieces per day per 5-digit ZIP Code. On the big end, MailPro’s EDDM guide puts the maximum at 12 by 15 inches and 0.75 inch thick.

A few rules trip up first-timers. EDDM Retail pieces can’t carry personal information, bills or handwritten notes. There’s no forwarding and no return service, so undeliverable pieces just don’t get delivered. And the postage indicia has to sit above and to the right of the simplified address block, per DMM section 144. Your printer will have a template. Use it.

What’s next for prices? In July, USPS asked the Postal Regulatory Commission to move the next market-dominant increase from July 2027 up to January 2027, and estimates at the time pointed to roughly 6.5% for Marketing Mail Flats, according to Quad’s July 2026 postal update. That was a request, not a decision. If you’re planning a spring campaign, price it at today’s rate plus a cushion of about 7% and you won’t be surprised.

EDDM Retail vs. BMEU: which door your mail walks through

There are two ways to send EDDM, and the choice affects cost, volume and how much work lands on your desk.

EDDM Retail is the do-it-yourself version. You pick routes online, pay online, print the pieces with the retail indicia, bundle them by route and carry them to the post office that serves those routes. DMM section 145 spells out the prep: bundles of 50 to 100 pieces, a facing slip on top of each bundle under the straps with the piece count written on it, and a separate stack for each route. Nobody needs a permit. The catch is that 5,000-per-day-per-ZIP cap and the fact that you are now, briefly, a mail clerk.

EDDM BMEU (sometimes called commercial EDDM) goes in through a Business Mail Entry Unit under a mailing permit. Most small businesses do this through a printer or mail house that already holds the permit. If you hold your own, MailPro’s EDDM guide lists a $390 annual mailing fee plus a one-time $390 permit imprint application fee as of July 12, 2026. BMEU removes the daily cap and lets heavier pieces mail at a per-pound price.

BMEU postage is priced by entry point and runs below the retail rate. We couldn’t get the current Notice 123 BMEU figure from USPS directly, so we won’t print one. Ask your mail house to show postage as its own line on the quote. If they won’t, that tells you something.

Our rule of thumb: under roughly 10,000 pieces a drop, with routes inside one or two ZIP Codes, Retail is fine and the savings from BMEU barely cover a mail house’s prep fee. Above that, or if you’re mailing every month, a mail house earns its money. Going through a mail house also makes Informed Delivery campaigns realistic, since USPS’s saturation campaign mailer guide runs through PostalOne! eDoc files that most owners will never touch themselves.

Carrier routes and the EDDM mapping tool, with The Woodlands as the example

EDDM doesn’t mail to neighborhoods. It mails to carrier routes, which are the set of addresses one letter carrier covers. A route might be 300 homes or 700, and it follows streets, not village lines. That’s the first thing to get used to.

The Woodlands Township lists eight village associations: Alden Bridge, Cochran’s Crossing, College Park, Creekside Park, Grogan’s Mill, Indian Springs, Panther Creek and Sterling Ridge, per The Woodlands Township’s village association page. They make sensible units for planning, but a carrier route can cross a village boundary, and Creekside Park sits on the Harris County side of Spring Creek. When you zoom into the mapping tool on USPS’s EDDM page, you’ll see routes as colored shapes with residential and business counts, plus Census-based filters for age, household income and household size.

Targeted lists vs. saturation: when paying more per piece is cheaper

Saturation mail (EDDM) goes to every address on a route with no names. Targeted mail goes to specific addresses from a list, usually with a name, at Marketing Mail or First-Class rates. MailPro’s table shows commercial Marketing Mail letters at $0.395 per piece at 5-digit presort after July 12, 2026, rising to $0.435 and $0.467 at the looser presort tiers, per MailPro’s July 2026 rate table. Your mail house will tell you which tier your list hits.

The trade is simple to state. Saturation is cheaper per piece and wasteful by design. A list is pricier per piece and wastes less. Which wins depends on how much of a route is a real prospect.

  • Saturation tends to win for restaurants, coffee shops, dentists, gyms, pest control, lawn care and anything with broad appeal in a tight radius.
  • Lists tend to win for pool builders, remodelers, solar, financial advisors, senior living and any offer where homeowner status, age or home value decides whether someone can buy.
  • Lists are the only option when you want to mail people by name, follow up with the same people online, or reach new movers.

Lists also open a door that EDDM can’t. Because you have names and addresses, you can match them to Facebook and Instagram audiences or Google Customer Match and show ads to the same households. Google requires 90 days of account history and more than $50,000 in lifetime spend for Customer Match targeting; smaller accounts get observation and exclusions only, per Google’s Customer Match policy. Meta’s customer list audiences have no such spend threshold, though match rates on purchased postal lists are often modest.

What a response costs: break-even math for a Houston mail drop

Response rates for mail vary so much by offer, list and design that we won’t quote a benchmark. The more useful question is how many customers you need for the drop to pay for itself.

Take Example A at about $2,500 all in. If a new customer is worth $250 in gross profit on the first job, you need 10 customers from 5,000 homes, which is one in 500. If a customer is worth $1,000 (an AC tune-up plan that turns into a replacement, say), you need three.

Use gross profit, not revenue. And count lifetime value only if you actually have repeat business you can measure. A lawn care company in Cochran’s Crossing that keeps customers for four seasons can justify a mail program a one-time junk removal job never could. Tracking direct mail responses without fooling yourself

Most mail “doesn’t work” because nobody could see it working. Fix that before the cards are printed.

  1. A unique phone number on the card, forwarding to your main line and recorded. Our call tracking guide covers setup.
  2. A QR code and a short URL that land on a page made for the mailer, with UTM tags (utm_source=mail, utm_medium=eddm, utm_campaign=woodlands-fall) so Google Analytics can separate the traffic.
  3. An offer code that front-desk staff type into the booking system. Short and spoken-friendly: WOODLANDS25, not WD-25-OCT-B.
  4. A matchback after 60 to 90 days. Export new customers’ addresses and compare them to the routes or list you mailed. Many people who respond to mail never scan or quote a code; they just search your name. Matchback catches them.
  5. A holdout group. Skip a few comparable routes on purpose and compare new-customer rates in mailed vs. unmailed routes. It’s the only honest way to see lift.

The matchback is the step people skip, and it often changes the verdict. Branded search and direct traffic usually rise in the week a mailer lands, so watch your Google Business Profile calls and branded search clicks in Search Console for those ZIP Codes too.

Direct mail design that works in a Woodlands mailbox

Your card gets a couple of seconds between the mailbox and the recycling bin. People in The Woodlands get a lot of mail from realtors, roofers and HVAC companies, especially after a storm, so the bar is real.

Two notes from accounts we’ve run. The side with the address block gets read first, so put the offer there, not a logo. And photos of a real local job or a real team beat stock imagery, because people can tell the difference between a Woodlands street and a stock-photo cul-de-sac in Phoenix.

Pairing mail with digital: retargeting, matchback and Informed Delivery

Mail does better when it isn’t alone. Three pairings are worth setting up for any drop over a couple thousand dollars.

Geo-targeted ads on the same streets. EDDM has no names, so you can’t upload it as an audience. You can target the same ZIP Codes or a radius around those routes on Meta and Google from a few days before the in-home date until two weeks after, with creative that matches the card. Our geofencing guide explains what tight radius targeting can and can’t do, and retargeting explained covers following up with people who visited the mailer’s landing page.

Informed Delivery. Households enrolled in USPS Informed Delivery get a morning email with images of the day’s mail. Mailers can add a clickable “ride-along” image and a link to that preview at no charge, and USPS suggests running the campaign from 3 days before to 3 days after the in-home date, per USPS’s Informed Delivery campaign guide. For saturation mail, that generally means going through a mail service provider who submits eDoc files.

Matchback into your ad platforms. If you mailed a named list, upload the list as a custom audience and as a conversion exclusion for existing customers. Then compare cost per new customer for households that got mail plus ads against households that got ads alone.

Frequently asked questions

How much does EDDM cost in The Woodlands and Houston in 2026?

EDDM Retail postage is $0.260 per piece since July 12, 2026, so 5,000 homes costs $1,300 in postage alone. Add printing, which we estimate at roughly $0.09 to $0.17 per piece for a 6.25 by 11 card at that quantity, plus design. A realistic all-in figure for one 5,000-home drop is around $2,000 to $3,000. USPS has asked to move its next increase to January 2027, so check rates before printing.

What size does an EDDM postcard have to be?

An EDDM Retail piece must be a flat, which means it is longer than 10-1/2 inches, taller than 6-1/8 inches or thicker than 1/4 inch. It can weigh up to 3.3 ounces, and the maximum size is generally 12 by 15 inches. Common sizes are 6.25 by 9, 6.25 by 11 and 8.5 by 11. A standard 4 by 6 postcard does not qualify for EDDM.

Is there a limit on how many EDDM pieces I can drop off in Houston?

Yes. EDDM Retail allows no more than 5,000 pieces per day per 5-digit ZIP Code, and each mailing needs at least 200 pieces unless the area has fewer addresses. Pieces go to the post office that serves the routes, bundled in groups of 50 to 100 with facing slips. Larger drops usually go through a mail house using EDDM BMEU, which has no daily cap.

Is EDDM or a targeted mailing list better for a Woodlands business?

EDDM is cheaper per piece and works when nearly every household is a prospect, like restaurants, gyms or pest control. A targeted list costs more per piece but lets you mail only homeowners, certain home ages or new movers, and lets you follow up with the same households in Meta and Google ads. If half of a route could never buy from you, the list is often cheaper per customer.

How do I track whether a direct mail campaign worked?

Put a unique tracked phone number, a QR code with UTM tags and a simple offer code on every piece. After 60 to 90 days, match new customers' addresses against the routes or list you mailed, because many responders just search your business name. Leave a few comparable routes unmailed as a holdout group so you can compare new-customer rates and see the real lift.
