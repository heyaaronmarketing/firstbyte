Why fence demand in Houston comes in waves

Two kinds of fence customers call you. The first has a fence that’s 12 to 15 years old, gray and leaning, and has been meaning to replace it. The second just watched it blow over.

Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 showed how big the second group gets. Sugar Land councilman and fence company owner William Ferguson told FOX 26 his company fielded hundreds of calls in the week after the storm, that some customers would wait six to eight weeks, and that crews couldn’t always get lumber. He also put the average Houston fence at just over $33 a foot. KPRC 2’s explainer noted that most Texas policies cover fences as “other structures,” usually paid at actual cash value prorated for age, and that neighbors often split shared-fence costs 50/50 by custom.

Cities react too. After Beryl, League City waived permit fees for fences 8 feet and over, and Bellaire let fence replacement start without a permit, with a no-fee permit afterward, per Community Impact.

What that means for marketing:

  • Have a storm page ready before hurricane season: how to document damage for insurance, how neighbor splits work, temporary fixes, your current wait time. Turn it on the day after a storm.
  • Have a call plan. A full voicemail box after a storm is lost revenue. Add an answering service or a short text auto-reply with a booking link.
  • Don’t price-gouge, and don’t sound like you are. The same KPRC story quoted a storm attorney warning about contractors charging far more than normal. Homeowners remember.
  • Build the off-season pipeline. The rotted-fence customer is the one who pays your bills in February. Your marketing should speak to them most of the year.

Permits and HOA rules: the Houston and Woodlands specifics

Fence rules in Greater Houston depend on where the house is, and getting them wrong costs you jobs and reviews. Knowing them cold is also great marketing material.

City of Houston. Per the Houston Permitting Center, a residential fence permit is needed for fences more than 8 feet above grade, and for any fence made partly or wholly of masonry or concrete regardless of height. There’s a $33.56 admin fee plus base and plan review fees, Planning and Development review, inspections, and a typical processing time of about 10 days. Most standard 6-foot wood fences inside the city don’t need a city permit, but deed restrictions may still apply.

The Woodlands. The covenant rules are stricter, and they’re specific. The Woodlands Residential Standards require prior approval when more than eight linear feet of fence is replaced, redesigned, rebuilt or relocated. Solid fences top out at 7 feet 6 inches on most lots (8 feet 6 inches for solid wood along the side facing an arterial or major street), transparent fences at 6 feet 6 inches, and the finished side must face out. Acceptable materials are wood, metal, stone and masonry; plastic and fiberglass are on the prohibited list, along with chain link and wire unless screened inside a solid fence. Paint, solid stain or tinted sealant needs approval, while transparent and semi-transparent stains from the Township’s list are pre-approved.

That plastic line matters if you sell vinyl. Check the specific village and lot before quoting vinyl anywhere in The Woodlands. Other master-planned communities around Katy, Cypress and Conroe have their own architectural review, and some sit inside small cities (Bellaire, the Memorial villages, Shenandoah) with their own permit rules.

And everywhere: call before you dig. Texas811 asks for at least two business days’ notice before digging, the service is free, and they say to contact them no matter how small or shallow the project. Put “we call 811 on every job” on your process page. It sounds obvious, and homeowners still notice.

How to use all this in marketing: build one page per major area (“Fence installation in The Woodlands: what the covenants allow”), and offer to handle the HOA application for the customer. A fence company that knows the 7-foot-6 rule without looking it up wins the estimate.

Winning “fence installation near me” in Google Maps

Most fence searches are local and urgent-ish: “fence company near me,” “fence installation near me,” “fence repair Katy,” “iron fence Pearland.” The map pack takes most of those clicks.

  • Primary category: “Fence contractor.” Add secondary categories only for work you really do, such as gates or decks, when Google offers a matching one.
  • Services: List each one separately with a sentence of detail. Cedar privacy fence, pine fence, board-on-board, wrought iron, ornamental aluminum, vinyl, automatic gates, fence repair, fence staining.
  • Photos: Every finished job, ideally with a before shot. Close-ups of post caps, rot boards and gate hardware help homeowners compare quality.
  • Service area: Where your crews actually go. A Tomball shop claiming Pearland and League City confuses Google and customers.
  • Reviews: Ask on the day the gate latches for the first time. More on this below.

Nextdoor and yard signs: the neighbor effect

Fences are public. Every job you finish is visible from the street and from the neighbor’s yard, and a shared fence line means at least one neighbor is already involved. No other trade gets that much free exposure.

Yard signs. Ask permission to leave a sign for two weeks after the install. Use a big phone number, a short URL and one line (“New cedar fence by [company]”). Make sure the HOA allows it. Track signs with their own phone number so you know they work.

Neighbor offers. When you finish a fence, knock on (or mail) the five or six houses that can see it, with a photo of the job and an offer on the shared line. Many fences in a subdivision are the same age and fail together.

Nextdoor. Homeowners ask Nextdoor for fence recommendations all the time. Claim your business page, ask happy customers to recommend you there, and reply when someone tags you. Nextdoor’s Ads Manager page offers image, video, carousel and lead gen ads with homeownership audiences and customer-list lookalikes. In our experience, paid Nextdoor works best for fence companies when it supports organic recommendations in neighborhoods where you already have finished jobs. Lumber prices and quotes that don’t lose you money

Wood fences run on lumber, and lumber prices move. Supply matters more than most homeowners realize: per NAHB, Canada supplies roughly 85% of U.S. softwood lumber imports, and in 2025 antidumping and countervailing duties on Canadian lumber rose from 14.5% to 35%, with an additional 10% Section 232 tariff on top.

That’s a pricing problem and a marketing problem. A quote sent in March and signed in June can lose money if picket and post prices moved in between. What we see fence companies do well:

  • Put an expiration date on every quote, typically 15 to 30 days, and say why in plain language.
  • Show price-per-foot ranges on your website by material, with a “last updated” date, rather than one number you’ll have to defend for a year.
  • Use rising prices as an honest follow-up reason: “Your quote holds through the 30th; after that we’ll re-price based on current lumber.” That’s real urgency, not a fake sale.
  • When lumber gets expensive, push materials with steadier pricing (iron, aluminum, steel-post wood hybrids) in your ads and social.

Estimate-to-close tracking

Fence companies often measure leads and revenue and nothing in between. The middle is where the money is. Track every lead through these stages: lead (call, form or LSA message), estimate scheduled, estimate delivered, signed contract, deposit paid, installed.

Then look at the numbers by source. In accounts we’ve seen, Nextdoor and referral leads often close at higher rates than search leads, and storm leads close fast but cancel more when wait times stretch. Reviews and a first-month plan

Ask for a review when the job is done and the customer has walked the line. Same link to everyone, no discounts for reviews, and reply to every one. A review that says “they handled the HOA application in The Woodlands and finished in two days” is worth more than anything you write. Our complete guide to Google reviews has scripts. A text that works:

“Thanks for choosing us for your new fence. If you’re happy with it, a quick Google review helps your neighbors find us: [link]. And if the gate ever sticks, text me.”

A first-month plan for a fence company that wants steadier work:

  1. Week 1: Fix the Google Business Profile, add services and photos, set up call tracking.
  2. Week 2: Build material pages and area pages for your top three markets, including HOA and permit details.
  3. Week 3: Launch search ads on wood and iron keywords with the negatives above; start the LSA sign-up.
  4. Week 4: Order yard signs, set up the review text and the estimate follow-up sequence, and write the storm page so it’s ready.

Frequently asked questions

How do fence companies get more customers?

Win the Google Maps results for fence searches near you with a complete profile, weekly job photos and steady reviews. Add Local Services Ads under Fencing services and search ads split by wood, iron, vinyl and gates. Use yard signs and neighbor offers after every install, and ask customers to recommend you on Nextdoor. Track every estimate to a signed contract so you know which sources pay off.

Do I need a permit to build a fence in Houston?

Inside the City of Houston, a residential fence permit is required for fences more than 8 feet above grade and for any fence that is partly or wholly masonry or concrete. Most standard 6-foot wood fences don’t need a city permit. Deed restrictions and HOA rules may still apply, and nearby cities and villages set their own rules, so check the address before quoting.

What are the fence rules in The Woodlands?

The Woodlands Residential Standards require prior approval when more than eight linear feet of fence is replaced or changed. Solid fences are generally limited to 7 feet 6 inches, transparent fences to 6 feet 6 inches, and the finished side must face out. Wood, metal, stone and masonry are acceptable, while plastic, fiberglass and unscreened chain link are prohibited. Paint and solid stains need approval.

What keywords should a fence company use in Google Ads?

Use material and service keywords with a location, such as wood fence installation near me, cedar fence Cypress, wrought iron fence Sugar Land, automatic gate installation or fence repair near me. Split them into ad groups with matching landing pages. Add negatives for DIY searches, retail stores, fence materials for sale, jobs, rentals, and electric or invisible dog fences if you don’t install them.

How should fence companies handle lumber price changes in quotes?

Put an expiration date on every quote, often 15 to 30 days, and explain that lumber prices change. Show price-per-foot ranges on your website with a last-updated date instead of a single fixed number. Use the quote expiration as an honest follow-up reason, and promote iron or aluminum when wood prices spike. Duties on Canadian lumber rose sharply in 2025, so price swings are likely to continue.
