Thursday, 4:40 p.m., at a flooring showroom off FM 1960. A woman walks in holding a strip of swollen laminate in a grocery bag. A slab leak under her kitchen, the restoration crew pulled 900 square feet of flooring on Monday, the adjuster’s estimate is in her email, and her in-laws land at Bush Intercontinental in three weeks. She wants to know what’s in stock.

At the same moment your shop-at-home rep is in a living room in Cinco Ranch with a sample case, on an appointment that came from a Google ad. The homeowner is “just getting ideas.”

Both of those people count as one lead in your Google Ads account. They are not the same customer, and treating them the same is where most flooring company marketing goes sideways.

Flooring marketing that works in Houston and The Woodlands sorts customers by what triggered the purchase, then sends each one to the page, the offer and the salesperson that fits. Choice remodelers get inspiration, samples and patience. Water-damage replacements get speed and in-stock inventory. Builders and remodelers get pricing and a crew that shows up. And the whole thing gets judged on measured appointments that turned into signed jobs.

Three flooring customers in Houston, and why they need different marketing

Almost every flooring dealer we talk to describes their customers the same way: homeowners. That’s true and not very useful. The trigger matters more than the demographic.

Choice remodelerTired of carpet, selling the house, a kitchen remodel next door“lvp flooring near me,” “hardwood floors The Woodlands,” “tile showroom Katy”Samples at home, a clear installed price, photos of finished local rooms

The choice remodeler might take two months and visit three showrooms. The water-damage customer often decides in two days, and the first company that answers with a real install date usually wins. The trade account doesn’t care about your Instagram at all.

One website and one campaign can’t talk to all three. You need separate landing pages and ad groups, and ideally separate phone numbers so you can see which trigger is paying the bills.

Showroom, shop-at-home or both: set up Google Business Profile to match

The map pack drives a lot of flooring calls, and the setup depends on how you sell.

If you have a showroom that’s staffed during posted hours, show the address. If you also measure and sell in homes, you’re a hybrid, and Google’s Business Profile guidelines allow you to show the storefront and add a service area. A shop-at-home operation without a staffed showroom should hide its address and list only a service area. Google says the service area shouldn’t extend much past about two hours of driving from your base, which covers the whole Houston metro from almost anywhere, so draw it honestly around where your crews actually go.

Pick the primary category that matches the money. A store that sells and installs might lead with a flooring store category and add flooring contractor, carpet store or tile store as secondary categories if they’re true. Search the category list in your profile and choose the closest match; Google adds and renames categories often enough that we don’t trust anyone’s old list, including our own.

Then do the boring parts well. Photos of the showroom from the parking lot, so people can find the door. A product section for LVP, tile, hardwood and carpet with “from” prices per square foot installed. An appointment link that books a measure or a showroom visit. Saturday hours that are real. Our guide to ranking in the Houston map pack covers the rest.

Shop-at-home companies can win in the map pack without a storefront, but they lean harder on reviews and on the service pages of their website, because there’s no address near the searcher to help them.

Water damage jobs: Harvey, Beryl, slab leaks and the replacement call

Houston generates water-damage flooring work every year, and some years it generates a flood of it.

Hurricane Harvey flooded 154,170 homes in Harris County, just under half of them outside the 100- and 500-year floodplains, and only 36% of those homes had flood insurance, according to Community Impact’s summary of the Flood Control District’s final Harvey report. Hurricane Beryl made landfall near Matagorda as a Category 1 early on July 8, 2024, and CenterPoint faced 2.26 million outages, per The Texas Tribune. Beryl was more of a wind, roof-leak and fallen-tree storm than a flood, but roof leaks ruin floors too, weeks after the storm.

The steady work comes from leaks. Clay soil swells and shrinks, and pipes under slabs pay for it. During the dry summer of 2022, Houston Public Works said water leak calls had doubled in a week and blamed drought-shifted soil, as reported by ABC13. Those were city lines, but plumbers see the same pattern under houses, and the flooring calls follow the plumbing calls by a week or two.

What that means for your marketing:

  • Build one water-damage replacement page. Say what you stock, how fast you can install, that you work from adjuster estimates, and that you check subfloor moisture before installing. Put the phone number at the top.
  • Run a small, always-on ad group for replacement searches, and have a bigger budget ready to switch on after a named storm or a hard freeze. Don’t wait until the day after to write the ads.
  • Partner with restoration companies and plumbers. They pull the wet floor and the homeowner asks them who should put it back. Our water damage restoration marketing post explains how those companies market, which tells you how to pitch them.
  • Keep a short “in stock, installs this week” list on the site and update it on Mondays.

And know the coverage basics so your team doesn’t overpromise. A homeowners policy doesn’t cover flood, flood policies usually carry a 30-day waiting period, and NFIP building coverage tops out at $250,000, per the Texas Department of Insurance. Plumbing leaks are a different claim under the homeowners policy, with its own terms. Your salespeople shouldn’t interpret anyone’s policy. They should know enough to ask the right question.

Insurance jobs without the headaches

Insurance work is good business for flooring companies that handle it cleanly and miserable for the ones that don’t.

The customer has an estimate with line items for removal, material and installation, usually priced per square foot. They want to know if you can do the job for that number, and if not, what the difference buys them. Train one person to read those estimates and answer in a day. That single skill wins more insurance work than any ad.

The legal line is simple. In Texas it’s illegal for a contractor to waive a homeowner’s deductible or offer rebates or credits that cover it, and TDI is blunt about it: offenders can be fined or jailed. So “we’ll cover your deductible” never goes in an ad, a script or a text. “We work directly from your adjuster’s estimate” is fine.

A few other things we’d put on the insurance page:

  • How you handle supplements when the estimate misses something, like stairs or transitions.
  • Whether you’ll match flooring in adjoining rooms, and how you price it if the claim doesn’t cover it.
  • When payment is due and how you handle mortgage-company checks, which can take a while.

Homeowners in this situation are stressed and already tired of phone calls. A page that answers those three things before they ask reads like competence.

Builder, remodeler and property manager accounts

Trade accounts smooth out the months when retail traffic drops. North Houston has plenty of them: production and custom builders along I-45 and the Grand Parkway, kitchen and bath remodelers in The Woodlands and Kingwood, and property managers turning rentals in Spring and Conroe every week.

Ads don’t win these accounts. Outreach and reliability do.

  1. Build a trade page with what you install, crew capacity, typical lead time, how you quote, and a contact who answers.
  2. Make a list of 30 local remodelers and property managers. The kitchen and bath remodelers who don’t install flooring themselves are the easiest yes.
  3. Offer a price sheet for your core LVP and carpet lines, refreshed quarterly, so they can bid without calling you.
  4. Promise a turnaround on rental turns and keep it. Property managers count vacant days.
  5. Send a short monthly email with new lines, discontinued colors and lead times.

Track jobs and revenue per account per month. Two good remodelers can be worth more than your entire Meta budget, and it helps to know which two.

Google Ads for flooring in Houston: search, Shopping and Local Services Ads

Search is where flooring buyers tell you what they want. Split campaigns by material and by trigger, and send each to its own page.

Water damage replacementflooring replacement water damage, flooring after slab leak, flooring in stockReplacement page with phone at top

Negatives matter more than usual, because flooring searches are full of DIY shoppers and job seekers.

Local Services Ads. Google’s Local Services Ads category list includes a Flooring services category, so test it. You pay per lead rather than per click, and you can dispute junk. It tends to suit installers with strong reviews more than showrooms selling material only.

Shopping-style ads. If you sell material online or publish prices for in-stock lines, a Merchant Center feed lets those products appear in Shopping results. It works best for a dealer with real per-box or per-square-foot pricing and a pickup option. For install-only companies, Shopping is the wrong tool, and Performance Max without a feed tends to spend on broad, cheap placements before you’ve learned anything. We’d start with Search.

Settings worth checking: target “people in or regularly in” your locations rather than people interested in them, keep your radius to where crews actually go, and track calls and sample requests as conversions. Sample request funnels that turn browsers into measure appointments

The choice remodeler isn’t ready to book a measure on the first visit. Ask them for one anyway and most will leave. Offer samples instead.

A sample request is a small yes. Five samples mailed or ready for pickup, in exchange for a name, phone, address and a rough project size. Then the follow-up does the selling:

  • Text within an hour confirming the samples and asking which room they’re for.
  • A call when the samples arrive, offering a measure with a specific day.
  • An email a week later with installed photos of the same products in local homes.
  • A retargeting audience of sample requesters on Meta, showing finished rooms and reviews.

Speed matters here more than in most trades. A sample request that sits until Monday competes with every big-box store that already texted them. Our speed-to-lead guide covers the follow-up setup.

On Meta, run sample offers with short videos: a plank click-locking into place, a before-and-after of a dated Kingwood living room, water poured on a waterproof LVP sample. Instant forms bring volume; a website form brings better leads. If the leads are junk, add a qualifying question about timeline. The fixes in our post on bad Facebook leads apply directly.

Financing ads: what you can say and what triggers disclosures

Financing sells flooring. A $9,000 whole-house LVP job is a different conversation at a monthly number. The ads are where companies get sloppy.

Under Regulation Z, section 1026.24, if a closed-end credit ad states certain terms (the down payment, the number of payments or repayment period, a payment amount, or a finance charge), it must also state the down payment, the repayment terms and the APR. So “$129 a month” on a Facebook ad without the rest is a problem. “Financing available” states none of those terms.

Many flooring financing programs are store cards, which are open-end credit with their own advertising section in the same regulation. Your financing provider almost always has approved ad language. Use it word for word and keep a copy.

Meta adds its own layer. Ads promoting credit offers fall under the Financial Products and Services special ad category, which locks age and gender targeting, requires at least a 15-mile radius and removes lookalike audiences, according to Jon Loomer’s guide to Meta’s special ad categories. If financing is the headline, expect to run under it. If financing is a line in the copy of a flooring ad, keep it general. Our kitchen and bath remodeling post has more on financing messages for bigger projects.

Reviews that mention the install, not just the sale

Most flooring companies ask for a review at the wrong moment, which is the sale. The customer hasn’t seen the floor yet.

Ask the day after installation, by text, with a direct link. The installer can mention it as they leave: “The office will text you a link. If we did right by you, a review helps us a lot.” That’s enough.

The rules, from Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps: no discounts or gifts for reviews, no asking only the happy customers, and no discouraging negative reviews. Send every customer the same link.

Reply to reviews with specifics, mention the product and the area when the customer did (“Glad the oak-look LVP worked in the Sterling Ridge house”) and handle the bad ones calmly with an offer to come back out. Prospects read your replies to complaints more closely than the five-star reviews. The complete guide to Google reviews has templates.

Measure appointment-to-sale, then spend

Lead counts lie in flooring. A shop-at-home company can buy 200 Meta leads a month and close nine. A showroom can get 40 search calls and close 15. The useful number is cost per signed job by source.

Mistakes we see most: one landing page for everything, financing ads with a monthly payment and nothing else, sample requests followed up on Monday, review requests at the sale, and a Google Ads account still targeting “people interested in” Houston, which includes people in Ohio.

Frequently asked questions

How do flooring companies get more customers in Houston?

Split your marketing by what triggered the purchase. Choice remodelers respond to samples, local project photos and clear installed prices. Water-damage customers need fast install dates and in-stock material, often found through Google and restoration company referrals. Builders, remodelers and property managers come from outreach and reliable crews. Track each source to measures run and jobs sold, and spend more where the cost per sold job is lowest.

Should a flooring company in The Woodlands show its address on Google?

Show it if you have a showroom staffed during posted hours. If you also sell in homes, Google’s guidelines let you show the storefront and add a service area. A shop-at-home business without a staffed location should hide its address and list a service area, which Google says shouldn’t extend much beyond about two hours of driving from your base.

Are Google Local Services Ads available for flooring?

Yes. Google’s Local Services Ads category list includes Flooring services. You pay per lead instead of per click and can dispute leads that weren’t real customers. LSA ranking leans on reviews and responsiveness, so installers with a strong Google rating tend to do best. Test it beside a regular search campaign and compare cost per sold job, not just cost per lead.

Can a flooring company advertise monthly payments?

Yes, with the full disclosures. Under Regulation Z, an ad that states a payment amount, the number of payments, a down payment or a finance charge must also state the down payment, repayment terms and APR. General phrases like financing available don’t trigger that. On Meta, credit offers fall under the Financial Products and Services category, which limits targeting. Use your financing provider’s approved ad language.

Can a flooring contractor cover my insurance deductible in Texas?

No. The Texas Department of Insurance says it is illegal for contractors to waive a homeowner’s deductible or offer rebates or credits that cover it, and violators can be fined or jailed. A flooring company can work directly from your adjuster’s estimate and explain what upgrades cost beyond the claim, but it can’t absorb the deductible or advertise that it will.
