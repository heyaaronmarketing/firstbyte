Thursday, 4:15 p.m. A listing agent in Kingwood calls. The buyer’s inspection report flagged “indications of adverse foundation performance,” the option period ends Tuesday, and the buyer wants an engineer’s letter and a repair bid before then. Those two leads tell you most of what you need to know about foundation repair marketing in Houston.

To get more foundation repair leads, you need to show up in Google search and Maps when a homeowner first notices a crack, and to be the company local agents and inspectors call when a sale is on the line. Then you have to follow up for weeks, because a $12,000 decision rarely gets made on the first visit. Ads and SEO cover the first part. Relationships and a disciplined follow-up process cover the rest, and most companies only do one of the three well.

This is for owners of pier and slab repair companies, from two-crew shops to outfits running estimators across the whole metro.

Why foundation demand in Houston comes in waves

Houston sits on some of the worst soil in the country for a slab. Much of the city is built on the Beaumont Formation, whose clay-rich soils shrink when dry and swell when wet, according to a Houston Geological Society Bulletin article by a University of Houston-Clear Lake geologist. The same piece notes that about a quarter of the U.S. sits on some kind of expansive soil and calls the Texas Coastal Plain the most notable area for it. Combine that clay with long dry spells followed by downpours and you get slabs that move.

The movement shows up in your phone log. During the summer 2022 drought, the general manager of a large foundation company in Cypress told KPRC 2 in July 2022 that call volume had “almost doubled,” the busy season started about a month early, and crews were booked four weeks out. He put the average repair at about $11,000, with some reaching $30,000, and noted that most insurance policies don’t cover foundation repair. In KPRC 2 this September, a League City repair owner described doing seven evaluations in a single day after six dry weeks, and the station put typical repairs between $3,000 and more than $20,000, with severe cases at $40,000 to $50,000.

Two marketing consequences follow. First, demand spikes after a dry stretch, and a smaller second wave tends to arrive when heavy rain returns and soil swells unevenly. Your budget should be able to move with the weather instead of sitting flat at the same amount every month. Second, the homeowner is facing a five-figure bill that insurance probably won’t touch. That buyer researches, gets several bids and takes their time. Your marketing has to support a long decision, and a lead from the first click is just the start.

The three kinds of foundation leads (and what each one needs)

Most foundation companies lump every inquiry together. They behave very differently.

Lead typeHow it startsUrgencyWhat wins it
Homeowner-noticedA sticking door, a diagonal crack over a window, a gap between brick and trim. Searches “foundation repair near me” or “cracks in wall above door.”Low to medium. Weeks to months.Search and Maps visibility, education, reviews, patient follow-up
Real estate transactionAn inspection report flags the foundation during the buyer’s option period. The agent calls someone they trust.Very high. Days.Agent and inspector relationships, fast bids, engineer coordination, a transferable warranty
Engineer or referralA structural engineer’s report recommends repairs, or a past customer sends a neighbor.Medium. The scope is already defined.Being on the engineer’s short list, clean paperwork, a bid that follows the report

The real estate lead deserves its own section. Texas home inspectors must “render a written opinion as to the performance of the foundation” and report the visible indications behind it, such as doors that bind, sloping floors and cracked masonry, under TREC’s standards of practice. But inspectors can’t give an engineering opinion on the cause or the fix unless they’re also licensed engineers, per a 2018 advisory opinion from the Texas engineering board. So a flagged report usually sends the buyer to an engineer and then to repair companies, all inside a short option period. The company that answers on a Thursday afternoon and has a bid out by Saturday gets the job far more often than the one with the nicest website.

A foundation repair website that earns the call

Foundation buyers are scared and skeptical. They’ve heard stories about companies that “find” $25,000 of work. Your site has to answer the questions they’re too embarrassed to ask on the phone.

Texas doesn’t license foundation repair contractors at the state level. A 2013 bill, House Bill 613, would have created a TDLR license, and the bill analysis for House Bill 613 noted the lack of any state standards for the trade. It didn’t pass. That means your site has to do the trust-building a license would otherwise do. Pages we’d build for a Houston pier company:

  • Warning signs page. Photos of the real symptoms: stair-step cracks in brick, doors that won’t latch, gaps at the frieze board, tile cracking across a room. This page ranks for the searches people make before they know they need you.
  • Repair methods page. Pressed concrete pilings, steel piers, drilled bell-bottom piers, mudjacking or foam for flatwork, pier-and-beam shimming. What you install, why, and when you don’t recommend one.
  • Cost page. Honest ranges by job type, what drives price (number of piers, access, breakouts inside the house, plumbing tests), and what the free inspection includes. Yes, a cost page. People search “foundation repair cost Houston” far more than they search your name.
  • Warranty page. The actual terms in plain English: length, what’s covered, what voids it (no watering, plumbing leaks), and whether and how it transfers to a new owner, with any transfer fee. Real estate agents look for this page.
  • Engineers and process page. Whether you work with independent engineers, who pays for the engineer’s letter, whether you pull permits where required, and how post-repair elevations are documented. Mention that your work follows Texas Section of ASCE’s residential foundation guidelines if it does, because buyers’ engineers often reference them.
  • Real estate page. Turnaround time for bids during an option period, how you coordinate with the buyer’s engineer, sample warranty transfer letter, a direct line for agents.
  • Area pages for the places where you have jobs, reviews and photos: Kingwood and Humble, Katy, Cypress, Sugar Land, Pearland, League City, Spring and The Woodlands. Write about what you actually see there, like older pier-and-beam homes in the Heights versus newer slabs in Cypress subdivisions. Our local landing pages guide covers how to keep these from becoming thin copies of each other.

Every page needs a phone number at the top, a short form that asks for address and what they’re seeing, and a photo upload so the homeowner can send pictures of the crack. That upload alone lets your estimator triage before driving to Tomball.

Google Ads for foundation repair: keywords, negatives, settings

Search ads are where foundation companies spend the most and waste the most. The searches are expensive and full of DIY researchers, renters and people looking for a different kind of foundation.

Structure we’d start with:

  • Core repair campaign: foundation repair near me, foundation repair Houston, foundation repair company, slab repair, house leveling, foundation contractor plus town names (Katy, Pearland, Spring, Kingwood).
  • Symptom campaign: cracks in brick wall house, doors sticking foundation, foundation settling, foundation cracks. Lower intent, cheaper, and best sent to your warning signs page with a “send us photos” form.
  • Pier-and-beam campaign: pier and beam repair, pier and beam leveling, crawl space repair. Only if you do it well.
  • Real estate campaign: foundation inspection for home sale, foundation repair before selling house, foundation engineer report Houston. Small volume, very high value.
  • Brand. Cheap, and it keeps lead sites from sitting on top of your name.

Settings that matter. Set location targeting to “Presence: people in or regularly in your included locations,” and draw it around where your crews will actually drive. Track calls from ads and from the website as conversions, with a minimum call length so wrong numbers don’t count. And because a signed job can come six weeks after the click, import booked inspections and signed contracts from your CRM as offline conversions. Without that, Google optimizes for form fills from people who just wanted a free opinion. Our guide to tracking calls and forms walks through the setup.

Sample responsive search ad headlines (each under 30 characters):

  • Foundation Repair in Katy
  • Free Foundation Inspection
  • Transferable Warranty
  • Bids Fast During Option Period
  • Send Us Photos of the Crack
  • Steel and Concrete Piers

On cost, foundation keywords are among the pricier home services clicks we see, and in accounts we’ve run a qualified inspection often costs several hundred dollars in ad spend. That only makes sense if you know your close rate and average ticket. Local Services Ads and the map pack

“Foundations services” is one of the categories in Google’s Local Services Ads category list, so you can run pay-per-lead ads above the regular search ads in most of Houston. Per Google’s screening requirements page, foundation companies go through business and owner background checks, business registration checks and a general liability insurance check, with state-level license checks applying only where a license exists. That last part mostly doesn’t apply in Texas.

LSA rewards reviews, responsiveness and booking. If your rating is strong and someone answers the phone every time, test it alongside search ads and dispute the leads that aren’t real customers. Our comparison of Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads goes deeper.

The Google Business Profile matters more than most foundation companies treat it. A few specifics:

  • Pick the primary category that most closely describes foundation repair (type “foundation” into the category field and choose the contractor option), and add secondary categories only for work you do in-house, like concrete or drainage.
  • If you don’t meet customers at your yard, set up as a service-area business with your address hidden, and list the areas you serve.
  • Upload job photos weekly. Pier holes, steel going in, the before-and-after crack. Real crews in real Houston yards beat stock photos every time.
  • Answer the Q&A and keep your hours honest. A profile that says “open 24 hours” gets 10 p.m. calls you can’t serve.

Reviews for a business customers only use once

A foundation customer won’t hire you twice, so reviews are your repeat business. They also answer the biggest fear a homeowner has: being upsold.

Two moments to ask. Right after the crew cleans up and the final elevations are done, while the homeowner is watching doors close properly for the first time in a year. And again, gently, if you come back for a warranty adjustment and handle it well. That second review is gold because it proves the warranty means something.

A text that works: “Thanks for trusting us with your home, Ms. Alvarez. If you have two minutes, a Google review helps neighbors in Kingwood find an honest foundation company: [link]. Either way, your warranty paperwork is attached.”

The rules, from Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps: no discounts or gifts for reviews, no discouraging negative ones, and no asking only the happy customers. Google also added language in April 2026 against telling staff to collect a set number of reviews or reviews with specific content, per PPC Land. So skip the “estimator with the most five-star reviews wins a bonus” contests. Our complete guide to Google reviews has more scripts.

Lead-seller platforms and the speed problem

Back to the Pearland homeowner who heard from three companies before you. That’s how most shared lead platforms work: one form, several contractors, and the first one to call usually books the inspection.

We don’t tell foundation companies to quit lead platforms on principle. Some do well with them. But judge them on cost per signed job, not cost per lead, and only after you’ve fixed your response time. If a platform lead sits for two hours, you’re paying to hand it to a competitor. Our breakdown of Angi and Thumbtack leads covers the math, and the speed-to-lead guide covers the process fix.

Over time, the goal is to shift your mix toward leads you own: your own search ads, your Maps profile, agents, engineers and past customers. Those leads aren’t being shopped to four companies at once, and they close at better margins.

Working with realtors, inspectors and engineers

This is the channel almost every foundation company says matters and almost none works on purpose.

  1. Make a list. The 50 agents who list the most homes in your strongest areas (HAR makes this easy), the busiest inspectors, and the independent structural engineers who write foundation letters in your service area.
  2. Give agents a reason to call you. A promise you can keep, like “written bid within 48 hours during an option period,” plus a one-page explainer they can hand a nervous buyer, and a sample warranty transfer letter.
  3. Show up. Brokerage sales meetings in Kingwood, The Woodlands and Katy often welcome a 10-minute talk on reading the foundation section of an inspection report. Bring photos, skip the pitch.
  4. Respect the engineers. Bid to the engineer’s report instead of redesigning it, send post-repair elevations without being asked, and never badmouth a design. Engineers remember which contractors make them look good.
  5. Track it. Put “referring agent” and “referring engineer” fields in your CRM so you can see who actually sends signed work.

Following up on a long, expensive decision

A homeowner facing a $14,000 bid gets two more bids, talks to their brother-in-law, reads about watering their foundation and waits for the next paycheck. The companies that win are the ones still in touch in week five.

A follow-up sequence that doesn’t feel pushy:

  • Same day: the written bid by email, with photos from the inspection and a plain explanation of each line.
  • Day 3: a short text asking if they have questions about the bid or the warranty.
  • Week 2: an email on how to compare foundation bids fairly (pier count, pier type, depth, warranty, transferability). Helpful even if they don’t pick you.
  • Week 4: financing options, if you offer them.
  • Month 2 and 3: a check-in, and a reminder that dry weather makes symptoms worse.

Meanwhile, retarget site visitors with short videos of your crews and testimonials on YouTube and Meta. Retargeting won’t create demand, but it keeps you in the shortlist while they decide. Our paid social team runs these if you’d rather hand it off.

Frequently asked questions

How do foundation repair companies get more leads?

Most foundation leads come from Google searches, the Maps pack, Local Services Ads, lead platforms and referrals from real estate agents, inspectors and engineers. The companies that grow fastest pair search visibility with honest cost and warranty pages, answer calls within minutes, and build relationships with agents who need quick bids during option periods. Track every lead to a signed job so you know which sources pay.

Is Google Ads worth it for foundation repair?

It can be, because people searching foundation repair plus a town name usually have a real problem. Clicks are expensive, though, so use tight location targeting, a long negative keyword list for DIY, jobs and other meanings of foundation, and dedicated landing pages. Import booked inspections and signed contracts as offline conversions so Google learns which clicks become customers, not just form fills.

Do foundation repair contractors need a license in Texas?

Texas does not license foundation repair contractors at the state level. A 2013 bill, House Bill 613, would have created a license through TDLR, but it did not pass. Some cities require permits for certain work, and the engineering side is regulated: Texas inspectors can describe foundation performance but cannot give an engineering opinion on causes or repairs unless they are licensed engineers.

Can foundation companies use Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. Foundations services is one of Google's Local Services Ads categories. Companies go through business and owner background checks, a business registration check and a general liability insurance check, with license checks where a state license exists. You pay per lead rather than per click and can dispute leads that are not real customers. Strong reviews and fast responses help your ranking.

How do I get foundation leads from real estate agents?

Make it easy for agents to send you work during a buyer's option period. Promise and deliver written bids within a day or two, coordinate with the buyer's engineer, explain your warranty transfer process in writing, and give agents a direct phone line. Speak at brokerage meetings about reading the foundation section of an inspection report, and record the referring agent on every job.
