Tuesday, 6:50 a.m. in Cypress. A torsion spring snaps with a bang that wakes the house, and the minivan is stuck behind a door that won’t move. The parent who has to get two kids to school searches “garage door repair near me” on a phone. Near the top of the map is a listing called something like “Garage Door Repair Cypress TX,” no street address, 41 five-star reviews that all read the same. They call. A dispatcher in another state takes the job and sells it to whichever tech picks up.

Your shop is eight minutes away with a truck full of springs. You never got the call.

Garage door company marketing in Houston comes down to winning that 6:50 a.m. search against competitors who aren’t always real businesses. To get more garage door customers, pass Google’s advanced verification so you can run search ads and Local Services Ads, then build a Maps profile and review base that look unmistakably local. Answer the phone every time, and turn repair calls into the install work where the margin is.

The fake listing problem, and why Google treats your category differently

Garage door repair is one of the most abused categories on Google Maps, and Google knows it.

In 2017, after a Dallas Morning News story on door businesses violating Google’s policies, Google started requiring garage door companies in key markets to pass an advanced verification process, according to DASMA’s write-up of the case from the industry’s trade association. A 2019 Wall Street Journal investigation put fake Maps listings in the national spotlight. And in June 2023, Google sued an operator it said had created more than 350 fraudulent Business Profiles backed by over 14,000 fake reviews, per Google’s own announcement. DASMA’s summary says the listings included garage door repair, that one profile went from maid service to garage door company 42 days after it was created, and that the same tool bench appeared in verification photos for two different businesses. The profiles were allegedly sold as lead sources to real companies.

Google said it stopped 20 million attempts to create fake Business Profiles in 2022. Plenty still get through, especially in emergency trades where a single call can be worth several hundred dollars.

For a real Houston company, that means a few practical things:

  • You’re competing with call centers and lead networks as well as the shop down the road. They’re fast and they’re everywhere, and they play by different rules.
  • Google’s systems are suspicious of your whole category, so your own profile gets more scrutiny. Expect verification by video and the occasional suspension. Our post on Business Profile video verification covers how to prepare.
  • Copying the spammers backfires. Google’s Google Business Profile guidelines say unnecessary information in a business name isn’t permitted, virtual offices aren’t eligible, and each business gets one profile. Keyword-stuffed names and extra listings at mailbox stores are how real companies lose their profiles.
  • You can report fake competitors through the guidelines page’s link for reporting third-party policy violations. Document it with screenshots, the phone number, and proof the address is a vacant lot or a UPS Store. It’s slow, and sometimes it works.

Advanced verification: the gate before any Google ad runs

This is the step that trips up the most new garage door advertisers. Per Google’s restricted local services policy, ads for garage door repair services in the U.S. are restricted, and to advertise you must apply for advanced verification.

From Google’s advanced verification page:

  • Passing Business Profile verification doesn’t count. Neither do Local Services background and license checks. Advanced verification is a separate process.
  • You submit a request form with your Google Ads customer ID, business name, owner, address, phone and a working website. If the account is new, build at least one campaign first.
  • You may get a video call with a Google agent, so be at your shop (or near your branded vehicle) when they call.
  • You must hold any licenses your location legally requires.
  • Google says it typically takes six to eight weeks. Until you pass, your ads won’t show on garage door searches.
  • Report changes to your name, address or license promptly, or you can fall out of compliance.

Plan for that six-to-eight-week gap. If you’re opening a new location in Katy or Conroe, start verification before the sign goes up. And if an agency tells you it can “get around” verification for you, find another agency.

Local Services Ads for garage door companies

“Garage door services” is one of the categories in Google’s Local Services Ads list, and for an emergency trade, LSA is often the best-converting paid placement you can buy. You pay per lead rather than per click, the ads sit at the top of the page, and the person who calls already sees your reviews and hours.

The screening is heavier than for most trades. Google’s screening requirements page flags garage door as an urgent category with an extra level of screening that includes service professional background checks, on top of advanced verification. Google’s category list also notes that “pre-badge” ads, which let some newer advertisers start serving early, aren’t available for garage door services. So you won’t get any head start. Budget the onboarding time.

Once you’re live:

  • Set your hours to match when someone really answers. LSA punishes missed calls.
  • Pick job types you actually want. If you don’t do commercial roll-up doors, turn them off.
  • Dispute leads that weren’t real customers (wrong service, spam, out of area). Do it every week.
  • Ask every LSA customer for a review. LSA reviews and responsiveness drive where you rank.

A Business Profile that looks as local as it is

When a homeowner compares your listing to a lead network’s, small signals decide the call. Make yours obviously real.

  • Name: your legal or branded name, exactly as it appears on your trucks and invoices. No “24/7” or city names added.
  • Address: if customers don’t come to your shop, set up as a service-area business with the address hidden, as the guidelines require. If you have a showroom, show it, with hours.
  • Service areas: the towns your trucks really reach in an hour. Cypress, Jersey Village, Tomball, Spring, Klein, The Woodlands and so on. Don’t list the whole metro from one truck in Magnolia.
  • Photos: your branded trucks, your techs in uniform, real installs in real neighborhoods, your warehouse shelves. Upload a few every week. Lead networks don’t have these.
  • Services: spring replacement, opener repair and installation, cable and roller repair, off-track doors, new door installation, insulated doors, commercial if you do it. Each with a sentence in plain English.
  • Website link: a real page with your Texas address, team photos and license or insurance info where it applies.

Reviews that read like real customers

Fake profiles tend to have reviews that sound alike, arrive in bursts and never mention a street or a person. Real reviews mention the tech, the neighborhood, the time of day and the specific part. That texture matters more in this category than in almost any other.

The rules, from Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps: no incentives for reviews, no discouraging negative ones, no asking only the happy customers. In April 2026 Google added language against telling staff to collect a set number of reviews or reviews with specific content, which may include asking customers to name a particular tech, per PPC Land. The scope isn’t settled, so we’d drop tech review-count bonuses and simply ask every customer.

A text that goes out within an hour of the job: “Thanks for having us out today. If the door’s running smoothly, would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It helps people in Spring find a real local company: [link]. If anything’s off, reply here and we’ll come back.”

Reply to every review with something specific. And if a lead network has been collecting reviews under a lookalike name, don’t respond in kind. Report it and keep stacking real ones. Our post on the fake reviews rule covers the legal side.

Repair calls pay the bills. Installs build the business

A spring replacement gets you in the driveway. A new door is where garage door companies make real money, and the best time to sell one is while you’re standing in front of the old one.

Homeowners have a good reason to listen. Garage door replacement took the top spot nationally for the second year running in the 2025 Cost vs. Value report, with an estimated 268% of cost recouped at resale. In the 2025 Cost vs. Value report’s Houston data, the project averaged about $4,607 with an estimated resale value of about $11,307, or roughly 245% recouped. Those are estimates from appraisers and agents, not a promise, but they make a strong talking point on an install page and in a tech’s conversation.

How we’d connect the two:

  • Train techs to give an honest repair-or-replace opinion with a written quote for both, photographed and texted. No pressure. Most owners decide later.
  • Follow up on every replacement quote at 3 days, 2 weeks and 6 weeks, with photos of similar doors you’ve installed in their area.
  • Run Meta and YouTube ads with before-and-after videos of installs in your neighborhoods, aimed at homeowners near your best ZIP codes. Curb appeal is visual, and these ads sell it far better than a search ad can. Our paid social team builds these.
  • Show insulated door options. In a Houston summer, a lot of people use the garage as a workshop or gym, and a door with real insulation is an easy upgrade to explain.

One more thing on price. The category is full of “$29 service call” ads that turn into $900 invoices, and Houston homeowners have learned to distrust them. Publishing honest starting prices for spring replacement and opener installs costs you a few bargain hunters and earns you a lot of trust.

Hurricane season and wind-rated doors on the coast

On the Texas coast, a garage door replacement comes with paperwork. Per the Texas Department of Insurance, properties in 14 coastal counties and in parts of Harris County east of Highway 146 are in a designated catastrophe area, where most new construction, major repairs and other alterations require a windstorm inspection. Most homes need a certificate of compliance (a WPI-8 or WPI-8-E) to be insured through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association.

TDI’s garage door guidance notes that most major garage door manufacturers have product listings with TDI, that published wind ratings matter in high wind areas, and that doors must be installed in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. If you sell in Galveston, League City, Kemah or Brazoria County, build a wind-rated door page that explains product listings, the inspection, and what paperwork the homeowner gets at the end. Have your installers confirm the requirements for each job, and tell homeowners to check with their insurance agent.

Inland in Spring or The Woodlands, wind-rated doors aren’t the same requirement, but plenty of homeowners still want a sturdier door after a bad storm year. Schedule an install promotion before June 1 and keep a storm-damage ad group paused and ready for the morning after.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't my garage door company run Google Ads?

Google restricts ads for garage door repair services in the United States. Before your ads can show on those searches, you must pass advanced verification, which is separate from Business Profile verification and from Local Services Ads screening. You submit a request form, may complete a video call at your business, and must hold any required licenses. Google says it typically takes six to eight weeks.

Are Local Services Ads good for garage door companies?

Often, yes. Garage door services is a Local Services Ads category, and emergency callers like seeing reviews and hours at the top of the page. Screening is heavier than most trades, with service professional background checks plus advanced verification, and pre-badge ads are not available. Answer every call, keep hours accurate, dispute bad leads weekly and ask every customer for a review.

How do I deal with fake garage door listings on Google Maps?

Report them through the Business Profile guidelines page link for reporting third-party policy violations, with screenshots, the phone number and proof the address is fake, like a vacant lot or a mailbox store. Do not copy their tactics, since keyword-stuffed names, virtual offices and duplicate profiles violate Google's guidelines. Focus on real photos, branded trucks and detailed reviews that fake listings cannot match.

What keywords should a garage door company bid on?

Split repair and installation. Repair keywords include garage door repair near me, broken garage door spring, garage door won't open and opener repair. Installation keywords include garage door replacement, new garage door installation plus a town name and insulated garage doors. Add negatives for DIY, parts, jobs, car door and rental searches, and review the search terms report weekly during the first month.

Do I need a wind-rated garage door in Houston?

It depends on location. The Texas Department of Insurance says properties in 14 coastal counties and parts of Harris County east of Highway 146 are in a designated catastrophe area, where most major repairs and alterations need a windstorm inspection, and most homes need a certificate of compliance for windstorm association coverage. Homeowners should confirm with their installer and insurance agent before buying.
