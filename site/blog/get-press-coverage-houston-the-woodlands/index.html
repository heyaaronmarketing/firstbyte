“How do we get Community Impact to write about us?”

Some version of that question lands in our inbox most months. Usually it comes from an owner who just opened a second location in Spring, or hit ten years off Research Forest, or watched a competitor show up in the paper and thought, why them?

The answer is shorter than people expect. Pick the one outlet whose readers are your customers. Give its reporter something that is actually news to those readers. Make the story easy to write, with the facts, the photo and a person to talk to, all in one short email. Do that four or five times a year and you will get covered in Houston and The Woodlands. Blast a release to every newsroom in the metro and you mostly won’t.

This playbook covers who covers what, the hooks that work, a pitch you can copy, when to send it, what gets you ignored, and how the coverage keeps paying off in Google long after the print copy is in the recycling bin. We don’t name reporters on purpose. Beats change and people move. The staff page on the outlet’s own site is the only list worth trusting, so check it the day you pitch.

Who covers what: the Houston and The Woodlands media map

Most pitches fail before anyone reads them because they went to the wrong outlet. A restaurant opening is not a Houston Business Journal story. A $4 million Series A is not a Community Impact story. Here is how we sort them.

A few notes on that table, because the details matter when you pitch.

Community Impact launched its first edition in 2005 and now runs more than 35 editions across more than 65 communities, delivered monthly to mailboxes, per Community Impact’s company page. In the Houston area it publishes 15 editions reaching about 1.15 million mailboxes a month, per Community Impact’s Houston print page. Neighboring editions cover Spring and Klein, Conroe and Montgomery, Tomball and Magnolia, Katy and Fulshear, and New Caney and Porter, so a business near the edge of The Woodlands should know which edition its customers actually get. The paper’s “now open, coming soon” roundups are the easiest door in for a local business. a March 2024 Woodlands roundup listed 26 businesses with an address, a date and a one-paragraph description each, covering everything from a juice bar to a surgery center.

The Houston Chronicle is Hearst’s flagship here, and Hearst bought Houston Community Newspapers in 2016, which brought The Villager (a weekly for The Woodlands, founded in 1977) and The Courier in Conroe under the same roof. Community papers have been cut back everywhere, so confirm the current print and web schedule before you build a plan around one.

InnovationMap comes from Gow Media and the team behind CultureMap and covers “startups, tech, health, energy, social impact, and more,” per InnovationMap’s About page, which also lists a story-idea link and an editor inbox. If you’re a dental office, skip it. If you built software in a Hughes Landing office, it’s your first call.

TV, public radio and the magazines

Houston was the No. 8 TV market in the country in 2022, according to Next TV’s 2022 look at Houston local news. The main English-language news stations are ABC13 (KTRK, owned by ABC), KPRC 2 (NBC, owned by Graham Media Group), KHOU 11 (CBS) and FOX 26 (KRIV, owned by Fox). The same piece reported that Univision 45 topped the 10 p.m. household ratings in January 2022, which surprises a lot of owners. If your customers speak Spanish, Univision and Telemundo belong on your list, and so does our guide to Hispanic marketing in Houston.

TV wants pictures and people. A pitch that says “we can have a technician on a roof in Kingwood at 7 a.m. showing what hail does to shingles” beats a pitch about your company’s values every time. TV also moves fast. When a freeze is coming or a storm is in the Gulf, assignment desks need experts within hours, and the business that already sent a short “here’s who to call” note gets the call.

Houston Public Media is a service of the University of Houston, carrying NPR and PBS programming with a combined monthly audience of more than 2.7 million. It is a poor fit for promotion and a good fit for expertise. A CPA who can explain a property tax change clearly, or an engineer who can talk drainage without jargon, is exactly the kind of source public media uses.

Magazines run on long lead times, often months. Houstonia is Houston’s city magazine, owned by Hour Media, and focuses on the people changing how Houstonians “eat, play, dress, and think.” The Woodlands City Lifestyle is a monthly published by Lifestyle Publications that promotes paid print and digital packages alongside its stories, so ask up front whether a feature is editorial or part of a partner package. And one that trips people up: The Township’s community magazine page says its bimonthly magazine is mailed to every residential address in The Woodlands, carries no advertising and takes content only from Township departments, villages and government partners. You can’t pitch your business into it, though a Township event you support may appear there.

News hooks that work in Houston and The Woodlands

Reporters don’t cover businesses. They cover things that happened, things that are about to happen, and things readers need to know. Your job is to find which of those your business is part of.

A few that work well locally:

  • Openings, moves and expansions with a date and an address. “Opening March 14 at 25 Waterway Ave., hiring 18” is a story. “Exciting new chapter” isn’t.
  • Your own numbers. An HVAC company that counted service calls by ZIP code during an August heat wave has data nobody else has. So does a pet clinic tracking heatstroke cases, or a moving company that knows which suburbs people are leaving for. One clean chart beats ten adjectives.
  • Fast expert reaction. Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. A roofer, a plumber, an insurance agent or a tree service that can explain what to do in two sentences is useful to every newsroom in Houston during that window.
  • Local people. A third-generation owner in Tomball, a veteran who opened a gym in Conroe, a technician who saved a customer’s house during a freeze. Stories need a face.
  • Awards the outlet runs. The Houston Business Journal runs Best Places to Work, and the Houston Chronicle’s Top Workplaces program (run with Energage) recognized 189 employers in 2024. Nominations and employee surveys cost you time, and a win comes with coverage built in.

If you can’t find a hook, wait. Pitching a non-story burns the relationship you need for the real one.

The pitch template

Reporters told Muck Rack’s State of Journalism 2026 that 62% prefer a one-to-one email pitch, 69% want it under 200 words, and 70% value clear relevance to their beat above everything else. Cision’s 2025 State of the Media survey of more than 3,000 journalists found 86% will immediately reject a pitch that isn’t aligned with their beat or audience. Short, specific and on-beat, in other words.

Here is the template we start from. Change every bracket.

Subject: [Business] opening [date] at [street address], [town]

Hi [first name],

[One sentence of news: what, where, when.] [One sentence on why your readers in [area] will care: the gap it fills, the jobs, the change they’ll see.]

Quick facts: [address and cross street], [opening date], [hours], [number of jobs], [price range or service list], [website].

[Owner name] can talk by phone today or tomorrow, and we have high-resolution photos of the space and the team you’re welcome to use.

Thanks,
[Name], [title], [phone]

That’s about 100 words. Notice what isn’t there: no attachment, no history of the company, no adjectives about passion. Paste the press release below your signature if you have one. Don’t attach it.

Address the reporter whose recent stories match your news. Read three of their pieces first, so you know whether they write openings, development or food. And send it from a real person’s inbox with a phone number that gets answered, because a reporter on deadline will call once.

Timing: when to pitch Houston media

The same Muck Rack survey found 78% of journalists prefer pitches before noon. Beyond that, timing in Houston is mostly about lead time and weather.

Outlet typeWhen we pitch (our experience)Why
Monthly community papersThree to six weeks before the datePrint pages are planned well ahead; online can move faster
Daily papers and news sitesOne to two weeks ahead, or same day for breaking newsReporters juggle many stories and need room to fit yours
TV news and morning showsA few days ahead, then a reminder the afternoon beforeAssignment desks plan the next day’s shoots the afternoon before
Weekly business papersTwo to four weeks ahead for features; right away for dealsDeal news goes stale fast; features need reporting time
MagazinesThree to six months aheadIssues are planned seasons in advance

Two Houston-specific rules. First, when a named storm is in the Gulf or the power is out across Harris County, nobody wants your anniversary pitch. Hold it. Second, be ready for those weeks if your business has something useful to say, because that’s when an expert pitch lands. We keep a one-paragraph “storm source” note ready for clients who can genuinely help.

Follow up once, about three business days later, with one new piece of information. Then let it go.

What not to do

Most of the pitches that get ignored are polite and well-meant. They just make the reporter do extra work.

A few that deserve their own warning. Don’t ask to review a story before it runs; reporters won’t agree, and asking marks you as someone who doesn’t understand the job. Don’t offer money, gifts or meals in exchange for coverage. Don’t pitch the same story to two reporters at the same outlet. And don’t demand a link in the story. You can politely ask for your website to be included for readers, but the call is theirs.

Also watch the line between earned and paid. Plenty of local “coverage” is paid. KPRC’s Houston Life, for example, publishes sponsored segments with a disclosure that “the advertiser paid a fee to promote this sponsored article,” as on a KPRC Houston Life segment page. Back in 2017, Talking Biz News reported the Houston Business Journal was charging $250 for expanded People on the Move listings. Paid placements can be worth buying. Just budget them as advertising, judge them like our radio and TV cost guide suggests, and don’t confuse them with earning a reporter’s interest.

How press coverage helps your SEO

Here’s the part most PR advice skips. A story in a Houston outlet keeps working for you in search for years, in three ways.

Links. A link from a real news site is one of the most trusted signals a local business can earn. Many newsrooms mark outbound links with a nofollow attribute, and that’s fine. You still get referral visits and a citation on a site Google knows is real. What doesn’t work is the shortcut. Google’s spam policies name “links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites” as link spam. A wire release stuffed with “best plumber Houston” links won’t help you and can count against you.

Brand searches. In accounts we’ve worked on, a story often shows up as a bump in people searching the business name in the days after it runs, along with more Google Business Profile views and calls. Those searches are the cheapest customers you’ll ever get, because they already want you. Make sure your profile is complete before the story runs. Our map pack guide covers the basics.

AI answers. AI search tools lean on sources that look independent, and local news is about as independent as it gets. Being described accurately in Community Impact or the Chronicle gives those tools something to cite. We cover the rest in how to show up in ChatGPT and AI search.

To capture it, add a “Press” or “In the news” page that links to every story. Quote a line from the article on the relevant service page. Share it on your Google Business Profile and LinkedIn. And watch brand-name searches in Search Console for the two weeks after publication, so you can see what the story was worth. A 60-day PR plan for a Houston or The Woodlands business

If you’ve never pitched anyone, this is a realistic start for a business with one or two locations.

  1. Week 1. List your three best possible stories for the next six months. Be honest about which are news.
  2. Week 2. Match each story to one outlet and one reporter whose recent work fits. Read their last five pieces.
  3. Week 3. Get assets ready: three good photos (owner, team, space), a 100-word company description, one page of facts, and a named spokesperson who will answer the phone.
  4. Week 4. Send the first pitch, using the template above, before noon on a weekday that isn’t a storm day.
  5. Weeks 5 to 6. One follow-up with new information. If it runs, share it everywhere and add it to your press page.
  6. Weeks 7 to 8. Nominate for one award the outlet runs, and send a short “here’s how I can help” introduction to one TV desk for your area of expertise.

Pair the first pitch with a real event if you can. Our 30-day grand opening plan shows how the PR, ads and reviews fit together, and the chamber and networking directory covers the ribbon cuttings that often go with it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my business in Community Impact in The Woodlands?

Send a short email to the reporter who covers business or openings for your edition, with the news in the first sentence, the address, the date and a phone number. Community Impact runs regular roundups of businesses that are new, coming soon, relocating or expanding, so a real opening or move with firm details is the easiest way in. Include good photos and send it several weeks ahead of the date.

Is a press release still worth sending to Houston media?

Yes, as a reference, not as the pitch. Cision’s 2025 survey found 72% of journalists still call press releases the most useful thing PR teams provide. Lead with a short personal email that explains why the story matters to that outlet’s readers, then paste the release below your signature. Skip mass blasts and paid wire releases stuffed with keyword links, which Google treats as link spam.

Does press coverage help SEO for a local business?

It helps in three ways. News sites give you a trusted citation and referral visits, even when the link is nofollow. Stories tend to drive searches for your business name, which convert well. And AI search tools often cite local news when describing businesses. Keep a press page on your site, link each story, and watch branded searches in Search Console after each one runs.

Should I pay for a segment on a Houston morning TV show?

It can be worth it if the audience fits and you treat it as advertising. Some Houston lifestyle shows run sponsored segments with a disclosure that the advertiser paid. Judge them like any ad buy: audience, cost, and a way to track response such as a unique URL or offer code. Don’t confuse a paid spot with earned news coverage, which reporters choose on merit.

How far ahead should I pitch Houston and The Woodlands media?

In our experience, three to six weeks for monthly community papers, one to two weeks for daily papers, a few days for TV with a reminder the afternoon before, and three to six months for magazines. Pitch before noon on a weekday, avoid weeks when a storm or outage dominates the news, and follow up only once with something new.
