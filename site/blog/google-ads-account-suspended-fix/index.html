Google Ads Account Suspended: Causes and How to Appeal

Google Ads account suspended? The suspension types, what Google reviews, how to write an appeal that works, linked accounts, timelines and what never to do.

A new dental office in Conroe opens its first Google Ads account on a Monday. The office manager sets it up on her personal Gmail, enters the doctor’s credit card, and launches a campaign pointing at a website that went live Friday. Wednesday morning the email arrives: “Your Google Ads account has been suspended due to a violation of the Circumventing systems policy.” Nobody circumvented anything. They don’t even know what it means.

This is one of the most stressful emails a business owner gets, because the account goes completely dark and the notice says almost nothing useful. We’ve worked a lot of these for businesses around Houston, and most of them were fixable once someone figured out which kind of suspension it was.

When a Google Ads account is suspended, all ads stop everywhere and the account becomes read-only until an appeal succeeds. To appeal, use the Contact Us link in the in-account notice, fix the actual problem first (payment verification, advertiser verification, a misleading website, a linked account), and submit one thorough, honest appeal per suspended account. Don’t open a new account while you wait. Google treats that as multiple account abuse, which is itself a reason for suspension.

What a suspension actually does

Per Google’s Google Ads account suspensions overview, a suspended account can’t serve ads in any location. You get an in-account notice and an email naming the policy and linking to the appeal. The account goes read-only, but you can still make payments, add a payment method, download tax documents and reports, update security settings, complete advertiser verification, appeal, or cancel and request a refund.

Two lines on that page deserve a slow read. A suspension “is considered permanent” unless an appeal succeeds. And you have at least six months from the suspension date to appeal, which is plenty of time to do it right instead of fast. Suspended accounts may become inaccessible after an extended period, so don’t leave it a year.

Suspended is also different from paused. Google’s account pausing page says an account can be paused for failed or missed verification, a brief investigation, or being linked to a manager account that breaks the third-party policy. Paused accounts can’t run ads either, but a pause usually lifts once you finish the task. Read the notice for which word it uses.

Circumventing systems: the one that confuses everybody

Google’s Circumventing systems policy covers attempts to get around or interfere with its ad systems. The named examples are cloaking (showing Google’s reviewer different content than users see), multiple account abuse (new accounts after a suspension, or spreading violating ads across accounts), resubmitting disapproved ads in slight variations, and false information in verification. With a November 4, 2025 update, Google added an example making clear that false information during Advertiser Verification is a circumventing violation, with no change to enforcement.

Most local businesses that get hit with it did none of that on purpose. The innocent triggers we see most:

  • A landing page or redirect that behaves differently for bots, often from a page builder, tracking redirect or geo-redirect someone forgot about.
  • A domain that was parked or showing “coming soon” when the ad was reviewed.
  • A payment card, phone number or login shared with an account that was suspended before, sometimes a previous owner’s or an old agency’s.
  • Editing a disapproved ad over and over instead of fixing the landing page it was disapproved for.
  • A brand-new account, brand-new website and brand-new card, set up from a VPN or a shared office network.

None of these is a guaranteed suspension, and we can’t see Google’s scoring. They’re just the patterns that keep showing up in the accounts we’re asked to fix.

Misrepresentation and unacceptable business practices

The Misrepresentation policy has ten sub-policies, including misleading representation, dishonest pricing, unreliable claims and unavailable offers. Two of them, Unacceptable business practices and Coordinated deceptive practices, are labeled egregious.

The Unacceptable business practices policy covers implying an affiliation with a brand or government agency you don’t have, offering products or services you can’t deliver (including ones that need a license you lack), phishing, and impersonating public figures, including with AI-generated likenesses.

For Houston-area service businesses, the version that bites is subtler. A “Texas Homeowner Relief Program” headline for a roofing company. A website for an HVAC or electrical business with no license number, no physical address and no real phone. A “Google Partner” badge you aren’t entitled to. Before-and-after photos borrowed from another company. To an automated reviewer, a thin site with no business details and a big promise looks a lot like a scam. Put your legal business name, address, phone, license numbers and real photos on the site before you appeal, and before you advertise at all.

Payment suspensions and suspicious payment activity

Per the billing and payment suspensions page, billing suspensions come from unpaid balances, declined payments, suspicious payment activity, chargebacks against a legitimate Google Ads balance, and promo code abuse (selling codes or using more than one on an account). Often the account is paused first, and only admins can complete the challenge to unpause it.

Google’s page on Ads paused for suspicious payment activity lays out the steps: open the in-account notice, answer questions about your payment history, then confirm the payment method if asked (documents or a code), make a payment if asked, or enter a six-digit code from a temporary $1.95 charge on your card. You may be asked to verify each payment method within 30 days before your appeal is reviewed. The verification task lives under Admin, then Policy, then Account, as “Confirm your payment method.” Uploaded documents must show the same full name, be unexpired and legible, and show all four corners in color.

Don’t file a chargeback because you’re frustrated with a charge. Google lists chargebacks as a suspension reason on their own. Call Google billing support instead.

Advertiser verification, and why the name has to match

Google can ask any advertiser to verify who they are and who pays. The advertiser verification page says tasks depend on your account and billing setup, may include documents, and can come back after big changes like a new payments profile, billing address or legal name. Mismatched organization names and expired documents are named as common reasons tasks fail.

The verification timelines page is blunt: your account will be paused if you aren’t verified by the deadline, the deadline “can’t be extended,” and reviews take up to five business days per task (in rare cases up to 30 days). Google’s suspension page adds that if your identity can’t be verified after three attempts, you can’t appeal.

The Texas wrinkle is DBAs. If your payments profile says “Smith Plumbing” but your documents say “JS Mechanical LLC,” that’s a mismatch. Harris County’s business roadmap notes that an assumed name certificate gets filed with the county clerk where the business operates. Keep a copy of the certificate of formation and any assumed name filing ready, and make the payments profile’s legal name match them exactly.

Strikes: the slow path to suspension

For policies on Google’s strike system list, you get a warning first. Keep violating the same policy and the strikes escalate:

To clear a hold, remove or fix every violating ad and asset and submit the acknowledgement form. New violating ads during a hold earn another strike. If the strike itself was wrong, appeal it; a successful appeal removes the strike and releases the hold immediately.

Why new accounts get flagged

The Conroe dental office from the opening isn’t unusual. New accounts have no history, so Google’s systems lean hard on everything else: does the payment method’s name match the business, does the website look like a real business, is the account connected to anything that was suspended before.

The scale is large. In 2024 Google suspended about 39.2 million advertiser accounts worldwide, per Search Engine Roundtable. For 2025, per TechCrunch’s coverage of Google’s 2025 Ads Safety Report, Google blocked 8.3 billion ads and suspended 3.3 million advertiser accounts in the United States alone, while saying its AI-driven enforcement cut incorrect suspensions by 80% year over year. That’s Google’s claim, not an independent number, and it still leaves plenty of real businesses caught by mistake.

Before launching a new account, we’d have the website fully live with business details, set the account up on a business Google login (not someone’s personal Gmail), use a card in the business’s name, complete advertiser verification promptly when asked, and start with a modest budget. Our small business Google Ads guide walks through the setup.

Linked and related accounts

Google connects accounts that share payment methods, identity documents, logins, manager accounts and websites. Its suspension page says related accounts may also be suspended, and that before appealing you should make sure all linked accounts, current and previous, comply with policy. The account pausing page adds that accounts sharing a payment method may be paused together, and linked Merchant Center accounts can be paused too.

  • Submit a separate appeal for each suspended account.
  • If your account was suspended because other accounts using the same identity documents were suspended, Google says yours is reinstated automatically once those are successfully appealed. Appeal the root account first.
  • If you’re linked to an agency’s manager account and the agency’s other clients are the problem, unlinking may be the fix. This is one more reason the business should own its account outright, which we cover in who owns your Google Ads account.

How to write a Google Ads appeal that works

Google says reinstatement happens only in compelling circumstances, such as a mistake, and asks appeals to be “thorough, accurate, and honest.” Here’s our process:

Evidence that helps, depending on the policy: business registration and any assumed name filing, a Texas license number with a link to the state lookup (TDLR, TSBDE, the Texas Medical Board), a utility bill or lease for the address, screenshots showing the landing page as users see it, proof the payment card belongs to the business, and a plain list of what you changed. Here’s a sample for a circumventing-systems suspension:

We are a family dental practice in Conroe, Texas, operating as [legal name] (Texas SOS file no. …). Our account was suspended on March 4 under Circumventing systems. We believe this was triggered by our website, which launched March 1 and briefly redirected mobile visitors to a scheduling page on a different domain. We removed that redirect on March 5. The account was created on a personal Gmail; we have since added a business login with 2FA. The card on file belongs to the practice. We have no other Google Ads accounts. Attached: registration, dental license lookup, website screenshots.

Submit one appeal at a time. Google says excessive appeals may not be processed and suspected misuse can pause appeal processing for seven days. If it’s rejected, you can appeal again, but only once you’ve changed something real.

What never to do while suspended

Opening a new account is the big one. Google’s Circumventing systems policy names creating new accounts after a suspension as multiple account abuse, and the suspension page says new accounts you create may also be suspended, recommending you reinstate the existing account first. A new account opened the same day almost always gets linked by card, login, domain or address, and now you have two suspensions, one of them for an egregious policy.

The other one: hiring a “reinstatement guaranteed” service that asks for your login, or offers to submit fresh documents for you. Verification fraud is itself a circumventing violation. If you want help, hire someone who will tell you what’s wrong with your account and your website, and let you submit truthful information yourself. If you think the account was hacked, use Google’s compromised account form and secure the login before anything else.

Timelines Google actually publishes

Google doesn’t publish a standard turnaround for suspension appeals. It does publish timelines for the steps around them:

Its pausing page says pause-related appeals typically take five to seven business days, longer for complex cases. In our experience, payment and verification cases often resolve in a week or two, and egregious-policy appeals can stretch to several weeks. Plan your marketing for at least a month without Google Ads while an egregious appeal is pending: lean on your Google Business Profile, Local Services Ads if you qualify (see LSA vs. Google Ads), Microsoft Ads and Meta ads, as long as that account is healthy.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Google Ads suspension appeal take?

Google doesn’t publish a standard turnaround for suspension appeals. Its pausing page says pause-related appeals typically take five to seven business days, verification tasks take up to five business days each, and suspicious payment reviews usually get a decision within five business days. In our experience, egregious-policy appeals such as circumventing systems can take several weeks. You have at least six months from the suspension date to appeal.

Can I open a new Google Ads account if mine is suspended?

No. Google’s Circumventing systems policy treats creating new accounts after a suspension as multiple account abuse, an egregious violation. Google says new accounts you create may also be suspended and recommends reinstating the existing suspended account first. New accounts usually get linked through the payment card, login, domain or address, which turns one suspension into two and makes the original appeal harder.

What does circumventing systems mean in Google Ads?

It means Google believes the advertiser tried to get around or interfere with its ad review systems. Examples include cloaking, where reviewers see different content than users, creating new accounts after a suspension, resubmitting disapproved ads in variations, and giving false information during advertiser verification. Innocent triggers include unexpected redirects, parked or unfinished websites and payment methods shared with previously suspended accounts. The account is suspended without prior warning.

How many times can I appeal a Google Ads suspension?

You can appeal again after a rejection, but submit one appeal at a time. Google says excessive appeals may not be processed and suspected misuse of the appeals function can pause appeal processing for seven days. Each new appeal should follow a real change, such as fixing the website or completing verification. If advertiser verification fails three times, Google says you can no longer appeal.

Why was my brand new Google Ads account suspended?

New accounts have no history, so Google weighs other signals: whether the payment method matches the business, whether the website shows real business details, and whether the account links to anything previously suspended. Common triggers are a parked or unfinished website, redirects, a personal card or Gmail, and shared payment methods. Fix those, complete any requested verification, then file one detailed appeal through the in-account notice.
