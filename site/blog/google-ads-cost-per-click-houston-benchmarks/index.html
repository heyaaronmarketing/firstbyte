$5.42. That’s the average cost per click on search ads across all industries in the latest data, from LocaliQ’s 2026 search advertising benchmarks. It’s also close to useless for planning a budget in Houston, because nobody pays the average.

A lawyer bidding on “18 wheeler accident attorney Houston” and a bakery in Tomball bidding on “custom birthday cakes” are in completely different auctions. One might pay more for a single click than the other spends in a week.

This guide gives you the 2026 benchmarks by industry, what we see Houston and The Woodlands advertisers actually pay, the math to turn a click price into a budget, and the levers that push your cost down.

How much does Google Ads cost per click in Houston?

Short answer: most Houston-area businesses pay somewhere between $2 and $12 per click on Google search ads in 2026, with the national average at $5.42. Legal, home services and dental sit at the top; restaurants and travel near the bottom. Competitive Houston terms like personal injury or emergency HVAC can run far higher.

The national numbers come from LocaliQ’s analysis of 13,474 U.S. search campaigns running from April 1, 2025, to March 31, 2026 (the figures are medians, which keeps a few extreme accounts from skewing them), per WordStream’s version of the same report. Across all industries: $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate, an 8.18% conversion rate and $66.69 per lead.

The direction is worth noting. Clicks got a bit more expensive again, but cost per lead fell overall for the first time in five years, because conversion rates improved. Better-built campaigns are absorbing the price increases. Sloppy ones aren’t.

Why Google Ads cost more in some parts of Houston

Google doesn’t publish CPCs by city. What we can tell you from inside accounts is where competition stacks up.

Clicks get pricier where three things overlap: high household incomes, lots of new homes, and lots of advertisers in the same trades. The Woodlands, Katy, Cypress, Sugar Land and the Memorial and Galleria corridor all tick those boxes. Home services, med spas, dentists and orthodontists in those areas compete with each other and with metro-wide companies bidding across all of Harris, Montgomery and Fort Bend counties.

Growth adds bidders. The Houston metro gained 126,720 residents in the year to July 2025, the most of any U.S. metro, per ABC13’s report on the Census estimates. New subdivisions along the Grand Parkway, in Conroe and out past Cypress bring new customers and, a few months later, new competitors chasing them.

Weather is the other Houston multiplier. In the HVAC accounts we’ve run, the first week of real heat in May or June and any power outage during a storm push emergency search volume and click prices up at the same time. Roofing and water damage restoration see the same thing after hail or a hurricane. Our hurricane season playbook covers how to budget for those weeks.

What actually decides your cost per click

Google Ads is an auction, but you don’t pay your bid. Per Google’s explanation of actual CPC, your actual CPC is the minimum needed to clear the Ad Rank thresholds and beat the advertiser below you. Ad Rank combines your bid with ad quality (expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience), the competitiveness of the auction, the context of the search, and the expected impact of your assets.

Translated into things you can change:

  • Keyword intent. “Emergency AC repair Spring TX” costs more than “how does an AC work,” and is worth far more. Pay for the first kind.
  • Ad quality. A relevant ad sending people to a matching page lowers what you pay for the same position. Our Quality Score guide goes deep on this.
  • Location settings. Google’s default option is “Presence or interest,” which can show your ads to people who are only interested in Houston, per Google’s location options help page. For most local businesses we switch it to “Presence” so you’re not paying for a click from someone in Ohio.
  • Schedule. Running 24/7 when nobody answers the phone after 6 p.m. pays for clicks you can’t convert.
  • Campaign type. Search, Performance Max and Local Services Ads all price differently. See Performance Max vs. Search and Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads.

To estimate your own CPCs before spending, use Keyword Planner. Its “top of page bid” columns show roughly the 20th and 80th percentile of past top-of-page bids for a keyword, filtered to your location, per Google’s Keyword Planner help. Set the location to your actual service area, not all of Texas. Expect to pay toward the high end in your first few weeks while the account builds history.

How much should a small business spend on Google Ads in Houston?

Work backward from customers, not forward from a number that feels comfortable.

The funnel above uses the home improvement averages: $8.33 per click and an 8.05% conversion rate. At $3,000 a month, that’s about 360 clicks and 29 leads. If your team closes 40% of leads, that’s roughly 11 or 12 jobs. Whether that’s a good deal depends entirely on what a job is worth to you. For a $9,000 AC replacement, it’s great. For a $150 service call with no follow-on work, it’s tight.

Rough monthly ad spend ranges we see for single-location Houston businesses (ad spend only, not management):

  • $1,500-$3,000: one service, one area, tight keyword list. Enough to learn, not enough to cover the metro.
  • $3,000-$7,500: two or three service campaigns across your core towns. Where most home service and dental accounts we look at sit.
  • $7,500 and up: competitive categories (legal, roofing, HVAC in summer) or multiple locations.

Below about $1,000 a month in a category with $8-10 clicks, you’re buying around 100 clicks, which isn’t enough data for Google’s bidding to learn or for you to judge. Put that money into your Business Profile and reviews first. Our marketing budget guide and what Google Ads management costs in Houston cover the rest of the math.

Which Houston keywords are worth the click (and which to block)?

The cheapest way to lower your average CPC is to stop paying for searches that were never going to become customers. The most expensive way is to chase low-intent keywords because they look cheap.

Here’s how we’d sort keywords for a Houston plumbing company, as an example. The pattern holds for most local services.

  • Bid hard: “emergency plumber near me,” “slab leak repair Spring TX,” “water heater replacement The Woodlands,” “burst pipe repair Houston.” Urgent, specific, local. These are the clicks you can afford to pay $15 or more for if your jobs are worth it.
  • Bid carefully: “plumber Houston,” “plumbing company Katy.” Broad but local. Worth it with tight ad copy and a page per town.
  • Usually skip: “how to fix a running toilet,” “plumbing cost,” “water heater sizes.” Research searches. Content on your website can win these for free.
  • Block with negatives: jobs, hiring, salary, apprenticeship, license test, DIY, YouTube, parts, supply house names, free, and any town you don’t serve (Galveston, Beaumont, Huntsville if you stop at Willis).

Write ads that sort people before they click. “Licensed Houston plumbers. Slab leak detection from [your price]. Same-day in Spring, The Woodlands and Conroe.” A price, a service and a set of towns will scare off some clicks, and they’re the ones you didn’t want to pay for.

And judge match types by results. Broad match paired with smart bidding can work well once you have 30 or more tracked conversions a month. Before that, phrase and exact match on a short list give you control while the account learns. Check the search terms report every week either way.

Track five numbers monthly: cost per click, conversion rate, cost per lead, cost per booked job, and search impression share lost to budget. That last one tells you whether more budget would buy more leads or just more expensive clicks.

Cost per lead matters more than cost per click

A $12 click that converts at 20% costs $60 per lead. A $3 click that converts at 2% costs $150. Owners who fixate on CPC tend to buy cheap, broad keywords and wonder why the phone doesn’t ring.

The two numbers to watch every month are cost per lead and cost per customer. You can only see them if calls and forms are tracked as conversions, so start with tracking calls and form submissions. How to lower your Google Ads cost per click

A few notes on those steps from accounts we’ve run.

Negatives come first because they’re the fastest money. In a typical Houston home services account, the first search terms report shows clicks on “jobs,” “salary,” “how to,” “DIY,” competitor supply houses and towns you don’t serve. Add negatives weekly for the first two months, then monthly.

Splitting campaigns by service lets you write an ad that matches the search and send it to a page that matches the ad. That’s the main thing that moves ad relevance and landing page experience, two of the three parts of Quality Score. The page matters more than people expect; see landing pages 101.

Don’t forget Microsoft Ads. The LocaliQ figures blend Google and Microsoft campaigns, and in our experience Bing clicks for local services often cost less, with an older audience that converts well for some trades. Our post on whether Microsoft Ads are worth it has the trade-offs.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good cost per click for Google Ads in Houston?

It depends on your industry. In 2026 the national average is $5.42, with legal around $9.87, home improvement $8.33, dental $8.00 and restaurants $2.05, per LocaliQ. A good CPC in Houston is one that produces leads at a cost you can afford, so judge it against cost per lead and cost per customer. A higher click price is fine if those clicks convert well.

How much does a Google Ads click cost for HVAC companies in Houston?

There’s no published Houston HVAC average. Nationally, home and home improvement clicks average $8.33 in 2026. In Houston HVAC accounts we’ve run, emergency and repair terms run above that during the first heat waves and storm outages, while maintenance and tune-up terms cost less. Use Keyword Planner with your exact service area to see current top-of-page bid ranges before setting a budget.

Why are my Google Ads so expensive in The Woodlands?

The Woodlands has high household incomes, many new homes and lots of advertisers in home services, healthcare and med spas, so auctions are competitive. Costs also rise when ads are broad, run 24/7, target interest in the area instead of presence, or send clicks to a generic homepage. Tightening keywords, negatives, location settings and landing pages usually lowers costs within weeks.

Is $1,000 a month enough for Google Ads for a small business in Houston?

In low-cost categories like restaurants, auto repair or beauty, $1,000 can produce useful leads with a tight radius and narrow keyword list. In categories averaging $8 to $10 per click, it buys only about 100 to 125 clicks a month, which is thin for learning. In that case, start with your Business Profile and reviews, or focus on one high-value service.

Do Google Ads cost more during hurricane season in Houston?

For some trades, yes. Roofing, water damage restoration, generator and tree service searches surge after storms, and so do competing advertisers, which pushes click prices up for days or weeks. Many other categories see little change. Set aside budget for storm weeks, prepare ads and landing pages before June, and watch search terms closely when demand spikes so you don’t pay for irrelevant clicks.
