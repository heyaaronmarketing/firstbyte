Google Business Profile video verification is one unedited clip, at least 30 seconds long, recorded live inside your Business Profile on a phone. It has to show where the business is, that the business exists, and that you’re the one in charge of it. Google says it reviews the video within up to 5 business days and tells you what was missing if it turns you down.

Plenty of owners are on their third or fourth take before one sticks.

The trouble is that Google wrote the instructions for a shop with a sign over the door. A plumber working out of a house in Spring, or a mobile groomer in Cypress with a van and no lobby, has to guess what “show your location” means when the location is a cul-de-sac. What follows is the version we’d hand a client before they hit record.

Google Business Profile video verification requirements

Google’s video verification help page breaks the video into separate proofs, worded a little differently for storefronts than for service-area businesses.

ProofStorefront or hybrid businessService-area business (no customer-facing location)
LocationStreet signs, building numbers or nearby familiar places that match your spot on Google MapsStreet signs, landmarks or other identifiers at your business address; home-based businesses can film nearby landmarks and signs
Business existsStorefront, showroom or signage with the business name on a permanent fixture, matching your profileProfessional tools, equipment, workspace, business cards or branded apparel
Proof of managementThings only staff can do: opening the register, entering the kitchen or stockroom, using the point-of-sale systemPerforming a service, accessing business-only assets like a branded van, or showing a permit, invoice or utility bill that matches your profile name

The clip itself has its own rules. It must be recorded live (no pre-recorded or offline clips), unedited, unique and complete with no breaks, at least 30 seconds long, and recorded and uploaded from a mobile device through your Business Profile. It must not show sensitive information such as bank account, tax or ID numbers, private information about you or others, or other people’s faces.

“No breaks” means you can’t pause, walk out to the truck and resume. “Other people’s faces” means a customer at the counter can sink an otherwise perfect take.

One more line on that page catches people out: online-only businesses aren’t eligible for a Business Profile at all.

How video verification works in the app and on desktop

The recording has to happen on a phone. Google’s steps, from the same help page:

  1. On your mobile device, open your Business Profile.
  2. Tap Get verified. You may need to enter your phone number first.
  3. Tap Business video, then Next.
  4. Read the requirements and allow access to location, camera and microphone.
  5. Tap Next, then Start recording. Tap stop when you’re done.
  6. Watch it back. Tap Try again to re-record if anything is missing.
  7. Tap Upload video.

To find your profile on the phone, Google’s editing instructions say to open the Google Maps app and tap Business at the bottom right, or use business.google.com in the phone’s browser. Sign in with the account that owns the profile. A lot of first attempts die right here, on a personal Gmail, while the profile actually lives under the office manager’s account.

If you start on a computer, you’ll get as far as choosing the method and then need your phone. UENI’s help doc describes scanning a QR code from the desktop screen with a phone signed in to the same account. Google reshuffles this screen often, so go with whatever it shows you.

Google added a preview before submitting, reported by Search Engine Land. Use it. If you can’t read your own van lettering on playback, neither can the reviewer.

A video verification example: the Spring plumber with a wrapped van

Say you run a one-truck plumbing company out of your house in a Spring subdivision off Kuykendahl. No storefront. Your address is hidden on your profile, as it should be (more on that below). Your van is wrapped with the business name and phone number, and you keep your tools in the van and the garage.

We’d script about 90 seconds. That’s three times the minimum, and it gives you room to move slowly.

TimeShotProves
0:00-0:15Start at the corner street sign nearest your house, then pan to the subdivision entrance or another fixed landmark. Walk toward the house.Location
0:15-0:30Walk up the driveway to the van. Hold steady on the business name on both sides and the back doors for a few seconds each.Business exists
0:30-0:50Open the van with your keys. Show organized tools, a pipe threader, fittings bins, the water heater dolly.Management and tools of the trade
0:50-1:10Pull a blank invoice or business card from the console and a utility bill or permit with the business name on it. Keep account numbers covered with your thumb.Management (documents)
1:10-1:30Open the garage with the remote, show stock and equipment, and finish on a branded shirt or hat hanging up (or a quick mirror shot of you in it).Business exists, workspace

Look at what we left out. No customer’s house, which films someone else’s property and maybe their face. No neighbor waving from the next driveway. No driver’s license close-up.

Film in daylight, early, before the July heat and before the neighbors are out walking dogs. Wash the van first. A wrap under a coat of yellow pine pollen is hard to read on a phone screen.

If Google comes back saying the location was weak, start wider next time: the subdivision monument sign, two street signs instead of one, a longer walk to the house.

Video verification guidelines for home-based, co-working and multi-location businesses

If the listing type is wrong for how you operate, no video will save it.

Home-based and service-area businesses

Google’s guidelines for representing your business uses a plumber as the example: if you run the business from home and don’t serve customers there, clear the address from the profile and list service areas instead (the service-area business help page has the limits). Google still sees the address during verification. Customers don’t.

For the video, that means the van, the garage and the paperwork carry the weight. If you’re converting an old storefront listing to service-area at the same time, do that edit first and let it settle; our guide to changing your address on Google without a suspension explains why stacking changes is risky.

Co-working spaces and virtual offices

No setup causes more grief than this one. Google’s guidelines say a rented mailing address you don’t operate from isn’t eligible, that P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations aren’t acceptable, and that a co-working office only qualifies if it maintains clear signage and receives customers there during business hours. Service-area businesses can’t list a virtual office unless it’s staffed during business hours.

In practice, a co-working office off Six Pines Drive can work if your name is on the door, you’re there during listed hours and clients meet you there. A mail suite on FM 1960 won’t, and putting it on camera invites a closer look at everything else on the profile.

Multi-location businesses

Google’s bulk verification page allows bulk verification for 10 or more locations of the same business, through Business Profile Manager under Verifications, then Chain. Service-area businesses don’t qualify. A three-office dental group in The Woodlands, Conroe and Kingwood verifies each office separately, usually with a video at each, filmed by someone with keys.

Why verification videos get rejected (and what “couldn’t verify” means)

Google doesn’t publish rejection statistics. This list is built from its stated requirements plus the failure patterns practitioners report.

  • The name doesn’t match. The van says “Gulf Coast Plumbing,” the profile adds “& Drain Services Spring TX.” Signage, documents and profile name should agree.
  • The location can’t be tied to the map. Close-ups of a garage door could be anywhere. Wide shots of street signs can’t.
  • No proof you’re in charge. Walking past a building is not the same as opening it with your own keys.
  • It was cut, paused or uploaded from the camera roll. Google wants one live take recorded in the profile.
  • Private details on screen. Account numbers, ID cards, customer names on a work order, a customer’s face.
  • Shaky, dark or rushed. Signs filmed at walking pace blur.

What the messages mean, using Google’s wording. A Review issues notice means the video wasn’t accepted. Google says it shares the reasons, and you fix them by tapping Review issues, then recording a new video that includes everything from the first one plus what was missing. If the Get verified button reappears, the verification status page says Google couldn’t completely verify the business, and you can retry or pick a different method if one is offered.

Neither is a suspension. That’s a separate process, covered further down.

Verification video not uploading? Try this first

Google’s advice for a failed upload is one line: try again. Here’s what we’d check, roughly in this order:

  1. Don’t cancel. Google’s help page says if you cancel the upload, you and Google both lose access to the video. Let it sit.
  2. Get on solid Wi-Fi. A 90-second video is a large file, and one bar of LTE at the end of a cul-de-sac is a common culprit.
  3. Keep the screen on and the app in front. Switching apps or letting the phone lock can stall the upload.
  4. Update the app and check permissions. Free some storage, and confirm camera, microphone and location are allowed.
  5. Re-record if the upload truly dies. You can’t attach a saved file, so a new live take is the only way back.

If the app crashes on Business video every time, switch routes: Maps app instead of the browser, or the reverse.

Google business video verification not working? What to do next

After two rejections, stop re-shooting the same video. Read the reasons. If Google flagged location, a third lap around the van won’t change anything. If you keep failing, or the screen says “No more ways to verify,” work down this list:

Google’s Google’s main verification page says live video calls aren’t available for every business and are only offered during local business hours. A rep has you show location and proof of management in real time, which is often easier for home-based owners because you can answer questions.

When the profile shows no options at all, a Local Search Forum moderator reported in November 2025 that Google’s verification status tool has become the main way in: click through to the end and choose Contact us, and you may be offered a live video call. In another thread, the same moderator suggested having signage photos, a utility bill, LLC or DBA paperwork, a license, and an email address on your business domain ready. That’s practitioner advice, not Google policy, but it’s the same pile of documents we’d collect. In Texas, a sole proprietor’s assumed name certificate from the county clerk is a good document to have scanned.

Whatever you do, don’t open a second profile to start over. Duplicates are a fast route to a suspension.

If the profile is suspended, not just unverified

That goes through Google’s appeals tool instead. Per the appeals help page, decisions can take up to five working days, you shouldn’t file multiple appeals for the same issue, and evidence must be uploaded within 60 minutes of submitting. Our suspension and reinstatement guide walks through it step by step.

While you’re stuck, expect “Google verification” sales calls. Google doesn’t charge to verify. See Google Business listing scam calls.

Google Business Profile verification without video

You can’t choose your method. Google’s main verification page says the methods “are automatically determined by Google and can’t be changed,” based on business type, public information, region and hours, and you may need more than one. If video is the only option on your screen, that’s the option.

If other options do show up, this is what each one involves:

  • Phone or text: a code by call or SMS. Phone trees (IVR) can’t receive it, so use a number that rings a person.
  • Email: sent to the address shown on the verification screen, which you need access to.
  • Postcard: a 5-digit code by mail. Google says it usually arrives within 14 days and expires after 30. Requesting another one cancels the first, so be patient.
  • Live video call: covered above, not always offered.
  • Instant verification: sometimes available if you’ve already verified your website in Google Search Console, or through bulk verification.

Verify your site in Search Console regardless. It costs nothing and it’s part of the basic setup in why your Google Business Profile is your number one asset.

After you’re verified, don’t trip it again

Google warns that changes to business info may trigger re-verification, so if a move or rebrand is coming, read our guide to changing your address on Google without a suspension first. Day to day, add managers through the profile’s user settings instead of sharing the owner login, and keep the shot list. You’ll want it again.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Google Business Profile video verification take?

Google says it reviews verification videos within up to 5 business days, and occasionally longer. You'll get a notification and email when the business is verified. If the video doesn't meet the requirements, you'll see a Review issues notice with the reasons, and you can record and upload a new video that covers everything the first one did plus what was missing.

Can I verify a home-based business without showing my home address?

Yes. A home-based service business should remove its address from the profile and list service areas instead. The verification video still needs to show where you operate, so start at nearby street signs and landmarks, then show your branded vehicle, tools and a document with the business name. The address stays hidden from customers on Google even though Google sees it during verification.

Can I upload a pre-recorded video for Google verification?

No. Google requires the video to be recorded live through your Business Profile on a mobile device, in one continuous take with no edits or breaks, and at least 30 seconds long. Clips from your camera roll, edited compilations and screen recordings aren't accepted. You can preview the recording before uploading and tap Try again to re-record if something is missing.

Does Google allow a virtual office or co-working space on a Business Profile?

Only in limited cases. Google says a rented mailing address you don't operate from isn't eligible, and remote mailboxes aren't acceptable. A co-working office can qualify if it has clear signage and you receive customers there during business hours. Service-area businesses can't list a virtual office unless it's staffed during business hours.

What if video verification is the only option and it keeps failing?

Read the rejection reasons and fix that specific gap, usually location shots or proof of management, then record a fresh take. If it still fails or no options remain, use Google's verification status tool and its Contact us option, where you may be offered a live video call. Have a utility bill, business registration and signage photos ready. Don't create a duplicate profile.
