Google Guaranteed vs Google Verified: there’s no longer a choice to make. On October 20, 2025, Google retired the Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified by Google badges on Local Services Ads and replaced all of them with one badge, Google Verified. The money-back guarantee that came with Google Guaranteed was discontinued too, and the badge itself still costs nothing. You earn it by passing Google’s screening, and you pay only for the leads your ad brings in.

If you searched your own trade recently, saw the green check missing and assumed you’d been dropped, you probably weren’t. Occasionally the cause is a lapsed insurance certificate. For Texas contractors the checks behind the badge also depend on the trade, because the state licenses some trades and not others.

What changed: Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified are now one badge

Before October 2025, a Local Services Ad carried one of several trust marks, depending on category:

  • Google Guaranteed for home services like HVAC, plumbing, cleaning and locksmiths. It came with a money-back guarantee for customers who booked through the ad.
  • Google Screened for professional services such as lawyers, financial planners and real estate agents. No money-back guarantee. Per Search Engine Land’s 2019 explainer, Houston and San Diego were the two test markets when Google Screened launched.
  • License Verified by Google, a lighter mark showing Google had checked a license.

Google announced the change in August 2025. Search Engine Roundtable published the advertiser email, which said the old badges end “as of 10/20/2025” and that “the Money Back Guarantee associated with the Google Guarantee badge will also be discontinued.” Google’s “About Google Verified badge” page describes the new approach as “launching a single badge for all advertisers.”

Customers now see one Google Verified badge on your LSA profile, with the specific checks you passed listed. Search Engine Land adds that the icon “may also display dynamically” on ads when Google thinks it will influence a decision, which is why it comes and goes on individual searches.

What does the Google Verified badge mean now?

It means your business passed every screening step Google requires for your category and location. Depending on the trade, the steps Google lists on Google’s screening and verification process page include:

  • A Google Business Profile matched to the ad that you own or manage.
  • Business registration, usually through a D-U-N-S number in the U.S. (Google also accepts Secretary of State registration, an EIN or a state revenue registration).
  • A business representative check through Dun & Bradstreet, plus an identity check with a government photo ID.
  • Criminal background checks on all business owners, and in some categories on the field techs who do the work. Office and customer support staff are excluded.
  • License verification against state databases where a license exists, and a certificate of insurance.
  • Ongoing re-checks. Google can ask you to repeat steps at any time.

It does not mean Google inspected your work or will pay a customer if a job goes wrong. Under Google Guaranteed a homeowner could at least file a claim. Google Verified is a screening signal and nothing more.

If your office still answers the phone with “we’re Google Guaranteed,” retire the line. It hasn’t been true since October 2025.

Google guaranteed badge gone? What happened to the money-back guarantee

It’s gone, with a short wind-down. Google’s Guarantee help page says consumers can still request reimbursement for services booked through Local Services Ads before December 7, 2025, and that requests must be filed “within 30 days of the initial service completion date.” By now, in late 2026, that window has closed for practically every job.

For reference, the old guarantee covered up to $2,000 over a customer’s lifetime, according to that same Search Engine Land explainer. Google reimbursed the customer, not the contractor, and Location3 noted that most consumers weren’t even aware of it and refund claims were rare.

There’s nothing to cancel in your account; existing verified advertisers got the new badge automatically. The cleanup is in your own marketing. Pull “Google Guaranteed” logos and “backed by Google’s guarantee” lines off the website, truck wraps, yard signs and quote templates. If you want to make a promise, make it yours. A written workmanship warranty says more than the badge ever did.

Google verified badge cost: is it free?

Yes. There’s no fee for the badge, and Google’s screening and verification process page says businesses aren’t charged for the background checks either. What you pay for is Local Services Ads leads: calls, messages and booking requests from customers in your service area. You set a budget and a bid, and Google charges per valid lead.

The real costs of getting verified are indirect. You need a certificate of insurance valid for at least 14 days after you submit it, with the policy number visible. Someone has to round up the license number, the COI and IDs for every owner (and for some trades, the techs). If you pay an agency, you’re paying for the paperwork chase, since the process itself is free.

Lead prices vary by trade, ZIP code and lead type, with no public rate card. And Search Engine Roundtable reported that since October 1, 2026, some unanswered business-hours calls count as charged leads. We went through what is and isn’t billed in our Angi vs Thumbtack comparison, and how LSA stacks up against regular search ads in Local Services Ads vs Google Ads.

What Google checks for Texas contractors, trade by trade

Google’s business screening and verification requirements lists the U.S. checks by category, and every license line carries the qualifier “where applicable by local law.” In Texas that qualifier does a lot of work.

Every row requires general and professional liability insurance. Google doesn’t publish dollar minimums, only that coverage must meet the requirement for your category and location.

Licensed trades: TDLR electricians and HVAC

For electricians, TDLR’s electrical contractor requirements say you must employ a licensed master electrician and carry liability insurance of at least $300,000 per occurrence, $600,000 aggregate and $300,000 for products and completed operations. A/C contractors carry the same minimums for Class A and lower ones for Class B, per TDLR’s A/C contractor license page. Google checks licenses against state databases where it can, so the business name on your TDLR record should match your Business Profile.

Unlicensed trades: roofing in Texas

Jobber’s Texas roofing license guide states it plainly: Texas does not require a state roofing license, and TDLR doesn’t regulate roofers. With no state license to check, a Montgomery County roofer’s badge rests on business registration, insurance and any required background checks. A newer crew from outside the area can, in principle, clear the same bar, so in roofing the badge separates you less than reviews and local job photos do. How to get the Google verified badge (and how long it takes)

You get it by signing up for Local Services Ads and finishing every screening step for your category. There’s no separate application for the badge. Google says on Google’s business screening and verification requirements that the process takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. Two things can stretch it: a D-U-N-S number application, which Google says can take up to 30 days, and a Business Profile that isn’t verified yet.

Some U.S. advertisers get a shortcut: Google’s screening and verification process page describes a risk-based review that can grant the badge right away, followed by 14 days to finish the remaining steps. Don’t let that deadline slide.

A hypothetical: a two-truck electrician in Tomball

Say you own a residential electrical shop off FM 2920 in Tomball, with a current TDLR contractor license, a master electrician on staff and two trucks covering Cypress, Magnolia, Spring and The Woodlands. A realistic path:

  1. Day 1: Confirm your Google Business Profile is verified and the name matches your TDLR record exactly. If it isn’t verified yet, start there; our Business Profile video verification guide covers the methods and timing.
  2. Day 1: Look up your D-U-N-S number. If you don’t have one, apply now, because that’s your longest wait.
  3. Day 2: Create the LSA account, pick the job types you want (panel upgrades, maybe not doorbell swaps) and your ZIP codes.
  4. Day 2: Upload the COI. If the policy renews next week, wait for the new one.
  5. Days 3 to 10: Each owner completes the background check invitation the day it arrives.
  6. Weeks 2 to 4: Google checks your license against the state database and reviews everything. Your ad goes live with the badge once it clears.

When this drags past six weeks, the hold-up is usually on the shop’s side: an unopened background check email, or a COI with the wrong business name. If Google denies a background check, Google’s screening and verification process page says you wait 30 days to reapply, and 180 days after a second denial.

How the badge affects LSA ranking and clicks

Google says two things that sound contradictory. Google’s “About Google Verified badge” page says “Local Services Ads ranking won’t be affected by this change,” meaning the switch from the old badges didn’t reshuffle anyone. Meanwhile, Google’s “About ad rankings” page lists profile quality as an auction factor, and profile quality includes “the verification checks you have completed,” along with your rating, review count, response time and photos. Higher-quality profiles, Google says, may rank higher and pay less per lead.

Both are true. The rename itself was neutral, and completed checks still feed profile quality. Since you can’t run an LSA without passing the required checks anyway, the practical ranking levers are the other ones Google lists: your bid, reviews and responsiveness (“missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness”). Our guide to Google reviews covers that side.

As for clicks, we haven’t found a trustworthy public study isolating the badge’s effect, so we won’t quote one. What we can say is that a line like “background-checked technicians” speaks directly to what a Kingwood homeowner worries about before letting a stranger into the attic.

Google verified badge missing? What to check first

Work down this list before you call Google support:

  1. Look at your profile, not one search result. The badge shows on ads dynamically, so one search from your own phone proves little.
  2. Check the LSA dashboard for verification tasks. Google re-evaluates advertisers and can ask you to repeat checks. Leave one unfinished and the badge, or the ad, is at risk.
  3. Check your insurance dates. An expired certificate is a frequent culprit. Upload the renewal before the old policy ends.
  4. Check your license renewal. TDLR electrical contractor licenses renew every year. A lapse in the state database can fail Google’s check.
  5. Did ownership change? A new partner or owner means a new background check.
  6. Is your Business Profile healthy? A suspended or unverified profile breaks the link to your ad. A recent move or name change is a common trigger; see our guides on moving or renaming without a suspension and getting a suspended profile reinstated.
  7. Are you on a partner program? Per Google’s “How providers qualify” page, some providers come through partners like Networx or Top Rated Local, which screen under their own rules and show the partner’s logo instead.

Still missing from your profile? Open a case from the LSA account, ask which step is incomplete, and keep the case number.

Is Google Guaranteed (now Google Verified) worth it?

For licensed trades that answer the phone, yes. The badge is free, and it comes attached to the most prominent paid placement a home service company can buy on Google.

We’d hesitate in two cases. If nobody can pick up during business hours, you’ll pay for calls you never speak to. And if your best work is large, planned projects, LSA leans toward urgent jobs; a search campaign with your own landing pages usually does better for a $30,000 remodel.

For HVAC specifically, LSA plus a well-built search campaign is the setup we usually recommend; see HVAC marketing in Houston and The Woodlands. And if you want someone to manage both, that’s our search marketing team’s daily work for home service companies from Tomball to Pearland.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Guaranteed still a thing?

No. Google retired the Google Guaranteed badge on October 20, 2025, along with Google Screened and License Verified by Google. All Local Services Ads advertisers who pass screening now get one Google Verified badge. The money-back guarantee was discontinued, with reimbursement requests accepted only for services booked before December 7, 2025 and filed within 30 days of the job being completed.

Google Guaranteed vs Google Screened: what was the difference?

Google Guaranteed covered home services like HVAC, plumbing and cleaning, and came with a money-back guarantee of up to $2,000 per customer lifetime. Google Screened covered professional services like lawyers, financial planners and real estate agents, and had no guarantee. Both were replaced by Google Verified in October 2025.

How much does the Google Verified badge cost?

The badge is free, and Google does not charge for the background checks. You pay for Local Services Ads leads, which are priced by trade, location and lead type. Indirect costs are the liability insurance Google requires, the time to gather licenses and IDs, and any agency fee for setup.

How long does it take to get Google Verified?

Google says screening takes three to four weeks on average after you submit documents. A new D-U-N-S number can add up to 30 days, and an unverified Business Profile adds more. Some U.S. advertisers get accelerated verification after a risk review and receive the badge sooner, but then have 14 days to finish the remaining steps.

Do roofers in Texas need a license to get Google Verified?

Texas has no state roofing license, so there is no state license for Google to check. Roofers still go through Google's business and owner checks where required, business registration, and a certificate of general and professional liability insurance. Some cities have their own contractor registration rules, so check where you work.

Why did my Google Verified badge disappear?

Start with your LSA profile, since the badge shows on ads only when Google expects it to help. If it is missing from the profile, look for open verification tasks, an expired insurance certificate, a lapsed state license, an ownership change that needs a new background check, or a suspended Business Profile. Fix the item and ask support which step is incomplete.
