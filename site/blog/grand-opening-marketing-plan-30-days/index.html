The 30-Day Grand Opening Marketing Plan for Houston and The Woodlands Businesses

A day-by-day 30-day grand opening marketing plan for Houston and The Woodlands: Google profile, waitlist, local ads, banners, ribbon cutting and reviews.

Thirty days is enough time to open busy. It is not enough time to get a Google Business Profile verified if you start on day 29 and the video gets rejected twice.

That’s the pattern we see with most openings around Houston and The Woodlands. The build-out runs late, the owner is buried in inspections and hiring, and marketing starts when the sign goes up. By then the cheap wins are gone.

The short answer: a 30-day grand opening marketing plan gets your Google Business Profile verified with an opening date, puts a location page live, builds a waitlist with a real reason to join, runs tight-radius Meta and Google ads, hosts a soft opening for neighbors and creators, makes opening week an event, and plans for the dip in week three.

Below is the countdown we’d run for a café, salon, clinic, gym or shop opening anywhere from Market Street to Katy. Every step has a day number. Steal the table, put your opening date at the top, and work backward.

What should you do before the 30-day countdown starts?

Three things take longer than owners expect, so start them as soon as you have a signed lease and a realistic opening month.

Your Google Business Profile. Google lets you enter an opening date up to one year ahead, and only the year and month are required. Once the profile is verified, it shows on Google 90 days before that date, and after you open it carries a “Recently opened” tag for 90 days, per Google’s help page on opening dates. Before opening, you can post announcements and opening offers and add construction photos. Customers can’t leave reviews until you’re open. Verification is often a video now, and our video verification shot list covers what Google wants on camera (hard when the storefront is still drywall).

Signs and banners. In The Woodlands, a grand opening banner needs approval before it goes up. Under Section 6.7.H of The Woodlands Commercial Planning and Design Standards, it can be up to 3 by 8 feet on a neutral background, displayed from four weeks before opening to two weeks after. Inside the City of Houston, the closest thing is a Limited Use Banner: seven consecutive days in any 30, up to 40 square feet, flush on the wall, with a permit each time, per the city’s Limited Use Banner application. Our sign rules guide has the rest, including unincorporated Harris County.

A phone number and an inbox someone actually answers. Get the permanent number before anything prints. Changing it later splits your listings.

If you’re reading this with fewer than 30 days left, do these first anyway and accept that the profile may go live late. Everything else in the plan still works.

Days 30 to 22: get found and start the list

Before anyone sees an ad, three pages need to exist. People will look you up the second they hear about you.

A location page on your own site with the address, a map, hours (opening week and normal), parking, the opening date and the offer. If you’re in a center with a parking garage or a confusing entrance, say which entrance. If you’re off Research Forest or Woodlands Parkway, say which side of the road. Small stuff, and it saves you from a lot of “where are you?” calls. Our guide to local landing pages goes deeper.

The Google Business Profile filled out the way you’d fill out a lease application: primary category chosen carefully, services listed, a description that says what you sell and where, the opening date, exterior photos so people recognize the building.

A waitlist form with a reason to join. “Sign up for updates” gets you your mom and two employees. Something with a limit works better:

  • A café: free drip coffee for the first week for the first 300 on the list.
  • A salon or med spa: founding-client pricing on one service, good through the first 60 days.
  • A gym or studio: a founding rate with a hard cap. Our gym presale guide covers this model end to end.
  • A shop: an invite to a preview night plus a small credit.

Collect name, email and mobile, and add a separate unchecked box for text messages. Texas SB 140 brought marketing texts under the state’s telephone solicitation law in September 2025; after a November 2025 court order, the Texas Secretary of State’s FAQ says a business texting with prior consent doesn’t need to file the registration statement. National Law Review is clear that cold texting and purchased lists are still exposed. So text people who opted in, and nobody else. Our Texas SMS rules post has the details.

Start the waitlist ads around day 25, once tracking fires on a test sign-up. Before that you’re paying for clicks you can’t count.

Days 21 to 8: build local buzz without burning the budget

This is where most of the money goes, and where most of it gets wasted on people who will never drive to you.

Keep the radius honest. People in The Woodlands rarely cross I-45 for a coffee. They’ll drive 15 minutes for a dentist and 25 for a specialist. Set your Meta and Google radius by category, and exclude what doesn’t fit. A Hughes Landing café might target three to five miles. A clinic in Spring might reach Klein, Tomball and The Woodlands. Don’t pick “Houston” as the target, ever. Houston is 600-plus square miles.

Creator preview. Invite six to ten local creators who actually live here (check their audience location if they’ll share it) and a few nearby business owners. Give them the real experience, not a special one. They post if they want to, and any free product or service gets disclosed as a paid or gifted partnership. the FTC’s Disclosures 101 guide spells out what “clear and conspicuous” means. Ask permission to run their best clip as an ad from their handle in opening week; it tends to beat anything your brand account posts. See our influencer disclosure guide for the paperwork.

Local press. Community Impact runs “now open, coming soon” roundups; a March 2024 Woodlands roundup listed 26 businesses, each with an address, a date and a short description. That’s the easiest press slot in town. Send the reporter covering your edition a short email three to four weeks out with the name, address, date, what you sell and one good photo. Our press coverage guide lists the other outlets.

The ribbon cutting. Per The Woodlands Area Chamber’s join page, the $500 Small Business membership includes a ribbon cutting at the Chamber office, and the $1,000 Community tier includes one within 12 miles, which means at your door. If your customers are local, that photo and the chamber’s social post are worth more than the scissors suggest. The chamber directory compares the Spring, Tomball, Conroe and Houston options.

Neighbors. Walk the center with a flyer and a free-item card for the staff of every business near you. Email the property manager. If you’re near a village center, reach the village association; The Woodlands Township’s village association page lists them. Office parks off Research Forest and Lake Robbins have hundreds of people who eat lunch somewhere.

How much should a grand opening marketing budget be in Houston?

Honest answer: it depends on what one customer is worth to you over a year, and there isn’t a published Houston benchmark worth trusting. What we can give you is how we’d split it.

For a small café, salon or shop, we’d plan somewhere around $1,500 to $5,000 in ad spend across the six weeks around opening, plus the cost of the offer itself. A clinic, gym or anything with a high lifetime value can justify more, and often has to, because the competitors bidding on “near me” searches are established. Treat those ranges as our experience, not a rule. Our budget guide shows how to work it from your own numbers.

Two things we’d keep out of the opening budget. A billboard, because one opening week isn’t long enough for outdoor to work. And a big print run of flyers with a QR code nobody tracks. If you want mail, our EDDM cost guide shows how to do it with routes you can measure.

Days 7 to 1: turn interest into plans

A week out, the ads change jobs. Awareness is done. Now each ad should ask for one action: RSVP for the ribbon cutting, book an appointment, order ahead, claim the founding rate.

  • Retarget everyone who watched your videos, visited the location page or joined the list. These are small audiences, so cap frequency or they’ll see you six times a day.
  • Run the creator clips you got permission for as ads.
  • Email the waitlist twice: once with the date and offer, once the day before. Text the people who opted in, between normal hours.
  • Answer every comment and DM within the hour. New businesses get questions about parking, pricing and whether you take insurance, and slow answers look like a business that isn’t ready. Our speed-to-lead guide has the math on response time.

Then train the staff. Every person who touches a customer should be able to say the opening offer in one sentence and ask for a review without sounding like a script. A version that works:

“We just opened and Google reviews make a huge difference for us. If you had a good time, would you leave one? The QR code on the counter goes straight there.”

Opening week: make it an event people can plan around

A ribbon cutting at 10 a.m. on a Tuesday gets the chamber photo and not much else. Pick the time your customers are free. For most Woodlands consumer businesses that means Saturday morning, or a weekday evening if you sell to people who work.

Make the offer time-boxed and simple. “Free kids’ meal with any entrée through Sunday” beats “20% off” because it’s easier to repeat and doesn’t train people to wait for a discount. Turn on Google Ads for your brand name and your main category plus your area (“brunch The Woodlands,” “nail salon Spring TX”) so the people who heard about you find you instead of the competitor bidding on their way in.

Capture content all week: the line, the first customer, staff in action, the ribbon. Vertical video, natural light. That footage feeds your ads and posts for the next month.

And ask for reviews, in person, all week. Don’t trade anything for them. Google’s prohibited content policy for Maps bans offering incentives for reviews, and the FTC’s consumer reviews rule (in effect since October 2024) prohibits buying reviews conditioned on what they say. A free cookie for a five-star review breaks both. Our Google reviews guide covers the ask and the replies.

Days 8 to 30 after opening: plan for the dip

Almost every opening has the same shape. A big first week, a decent second week, and then a third week that makes the owner nervous. The excitement wears off and the regular habits haven’t formed yet.

The fix is boring and it works:

  • Send a second-visit offer to everyone who came during opening week, good for the next 21 days.
  • Follow up with waitlist sign-ups who never showed. A short “we saved your spot” note converts more than you’d think.
  • Keep search ads on for your category and area. Pull back on awareness ads; keep retargeting.
  • Post on your Business Profile weekly and reply to every review the same day.
  • Keep asking for reviews. BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found 47% of consumers won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, 74% only consider reviews from the last three months, and 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher. A brand-new profile with six reviews is losing customers it never hears from.

Review velocity in the first month also helps your map pack position. Our guide on ranking in the Houston map pack explains the rest of that picture.

What does a grand opening look like when it works?

Two openings we’ve run show the range. For The Toasted Yolk Cafe, location pages and Business Profiles went live before the doors opened, then grand-opening paid social, creator tastings and RSVP campaigns ran around each launch. Across seven new locations, the case study reports 21 days to No. 1 in the local map pack and 3,200 grand-opening week guests. For Equinox, geo-fenced social and search started 90 days before each new club, with a founding-member offer and an SMS and email nurture; the case study reports 94% capacity on opening day.

The common thread isn’t budget. In both cases the search listings were ready before the hype started, and the follow-up kept going after the ribbon.

What to track, and the grand opening mistakes we see most

Keep a one-page scorecard. Before opening: waitlist sign-ups, cost per sign-up, RSVPs. Opening week: visitors (count them, even with a clicker), bookings or covers, Business Profile calls and direction requests, reviews collected. Days 8 to 30: repeat visits from the opening-week list, cost per new customer by channel, average rating and review count. If you take calls, give each channel its own tracked number; our GA4 and Google Ads tracking guide covers forms and calls.

The mistakes we see most:

  • Starting the Google profile last. No profile, no map listing, no reviews during the one week people are most willing to leave them.
  • Targeting all of Houston. Spend gets spread across Pearland and Baytown when your customers are within ten minutes.
  • A deep discount as the whole plan. You fill the room with deal hunters and teach everyone your real price is 40% off.
  • No one assigned to the phone. Opening week is when calls spike and voicemail fills up.
  • Stopping everything in week two. The dip comes either way. It’s shallower if search and retargeting stay on.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I start marketing a grand opening?

Start the Google Business Profile as soon as you have a lease and an opening month, because Google lets you set an opening date up to a year ahead and shows a verified profile 90 days before it. Paid ads and the waitlist usually start 25 to 30 days out. Banner approvals, chamber ribbon cuttings and press pitches need three to four weeks of lead time in The Woodlands and Houston.

Can I put up a grand opening banner in The Woodlands?

Yes, with approval first. The Woodlands commercial standards allow a grand opening banner up to 3 by 8 feet on a neutral background, from four weeks before opening to two weeks after, and it must be approved by the Township’s review committee before you install it. Inside the City of Houston, temporary banners go through a Limited Use Banner permit with its own size and time limits.

Can I give customers a discount for leaving a Google review at my grand opening?

No. Google’s Maps content policy bans offering incentives in exchange for reviews, and the FTC’s consumer reviews rule prohibits paying for reviews that express a particular sentiment. A discount for a five-star review breaks both. Ask every happy customer in person, put a QR code at the counter and send a link afterward, but keep the offer separate from the review ask.

How do I get my new business in Community Impact in The Woodlands?

Email the reporter who covers business for your edition three to four weeks before you open. Put the news in the first sentence, then the business name, address, opening date, what you sell, a phone number and one good photo. Community Impact runs regular roundups of businesses that are new or coming soon, and a firm opening date with real details is the easiest way into one.

How do I get a new business on Google Maps before it opens?

Create your Google Business Profile, add the opening date under Edit profile, and complete verification, which is often a short video. Once verified, the profile appears on Google up to 90 days before the date you entered. You can post announcements, opening offers and construction photos before you open, but customers can’t leave reviews until the business is officially open.
