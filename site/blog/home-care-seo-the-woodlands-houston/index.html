Home care SEO is the work of showing up when a family searches “home care near me” or “in-home care in Spring TX,” in both the map pack and the regular results. For an agency in The Woodlands or Greater Houston, most of it comes down to a correctly set-up Google Business Profile, one useful page for each community you actually staff, steady reviews from families, and pages that answer cost and payment questions honestly.

Claiming the profile takes ten minutes. The harder calls come after: which category fits your Texas license, what to do when your office is a suite off Sawdust Road and your caregivers cover six towns, and how to compete when a family in Kingwood is comparing you with a franchise that has three listings.

That’s what this post covers. It’s the companion to our referral playbook for home care agencies; that one covers hospitals and attorneys, this one covers Google.

What home care SEO has to win

A daughter searching for help for her father sees two kinds of results, and they’re ranked differently.

  • The map pack. Three Business Profiles with stars and a call button. Distance from the searcher weighs heavily, along with your category, your reviews and how well your profile matches the search.
  • Organic results. Websites. Here a page about “home care in Conroe” with real Conroe details beats a homepage that lists 14 towns in the footer.

For “near me” searches the map pack dominates on phones. For longer searches like “24 hour home care for dementia The Woodlands” or “how much does in-home care cost in Houston,” organic pages and AI answers carry more weight, and those are the searches adult children type once they’re past the panic stage.

Our guide to ranking in the Google map pack in Houston covers the general mechanics. Below is what’s specific to home care.

Home care Google Business Profile: categories and setup

Your primary category is one of the strongest signals your profile has, and home care is a category mess.

Many non-medical agencies use Home health care service. Google also has a newer category, Home help service agency, aimed at non-medical in-home help, which few agencies were using when local SEO practitioners discussed it in a Local Search Forum thread. The same thread is a good reminder not to go “fixing” competitors’ categories through public edits; it’s an easy way to draw attention to your own profile.

In Texas there’s a wrinkle worth raising with your attorney. Health and Safety Code §142.002 ties licensing to what an agency represents to the public, and home health is a different license category from personal assistance services. If your HCSSA license covers personal assistance only, a primary category that says “home health” is worth a question to your attorney before you pick it. Our usual setup for a personal-assistance-only agency:

  • Primary: Home help service agency, if it fits your services.
  • Secondary: other categories only where they accurately describe a service you provide. Fewer, accurate categories beat a long list of hopeful ones.
  • Business name: your real name only. No “Home Care The Woodlands” tacked on; Google’s Business Profile guidelines don’t allow extra information in the name.
  • Services: companion care, personal care, respite, dementia and Alzheimer’s care, post-hospital care, 24-hour and live-in care, each with a plain description.
  • Photos: real staff, your office and training, shot with permission. Never a client’s face or home without written consent.

One outdated tip to ignore: plenty of articles still recommend Business Profile messaging. Google removed chat from Business Profiles on July 31, 2024. Put the effort into answering calls instead.

Service-area rules when you run the agency from an office or a home

Most home care agencies are service-area businesses. Caregivers go to the client; families rarely visit the office. Google’s Business Profile guidelines say a service-area business should hide its address, keep its service area within about two hours’ driving time of its base, and never list a virtual office or a rented mailbox unless it’s staffed during business hours.

What that means in practice:

  • Run from home? Hide the address and list the towns or ZIP codes you staff. Your license address and your Google address should still be the same real place.
  • Real office where families come for consultations during posted hours? You can show the address and add a service area.
  • Thinking about a second listing in Kingwood? Only if it’s a real, staffed office. Texas also requires an HCSSA license for each place of business that directs services, so a fake branch is a problem with Google and with the state.
  • Moving offices? Change it carefully. Our guide to changing a business address without a Google suspension walks through the order of operations.

Service area settings don’t make you rank in a town 25 miles away. They tell Google where you’re willing to work. Ranking in those towns comes from your website, which is the next section.

Content that answers adult children’s questions

Location pages catch “near me.” Answer pages catch the questions that come before the call. The adult child searching at 11 p.m. wants to know what this will cost, what a caregiver will and won’t do, and how to start.

Cost

Publish your rates, or at least a range and your minimum shift. CareScout’s 2025 Cost of Care Survey put the 2025 national median for a non-medical caregiver at $35 an hour, and CareScout’s Texas release put the Texas median at $68,640 a year for 44 hours a week, which works out to about $30 an hour. Houston rates vary by agency and schedule, so show yours and explain what changes it: overnight, weekends, live-in and two-person transfers.

Pages worth writing

  • How much does in-home care cost in Houston, with your own rate table.
  • Live-in vs 24-hour care: the difference and the cost.
  • What a non-medical caregiver can and can’t do under your Texas license.
  • Signs a parent needs help at home, written by your care coordinator.
  • Bringing a parent home from the hospital: the first 72 hours.
  • Respite care for family caregivers, including short weekend blocks.

Write them in plain language, sign them with a real name and title, and link each one to the matching town pages. That’s also what AI answers tend to quote: a clear, specific answer from a named local source.

Medicaid, VA and long-term care insurance pages

Only build these if you actually accept the payment source, and keep them accurate.

  • Medicaid. Texas Medicaid pays for attendant care for some people through STAR+PLUS health plans and programs like Community First Choice, but agencies need contracts with the plans and the rules are detailed. If you’re contracted, say which plans. If you’re not, say so and point families to the H-GAC or Harris County Area Agency on Aging.
  • VA. Veterans may get in-home help through VA community care programs or use a VA pension with Aid and Attendance to pay privately. Explain which applies to your agency and suggest families confirm eligibility with the VA or an accredited representative.
  • Long-term care insurance. Explain how you help with claim paperwork, caregiver timesheets and the waiting period before benefits start. Families often don’t know their policy covers home care until someone asks.

Don’t promise coverage. A page that says “VA pays for everything” will produce angry calls, and possibly a complaint.

Reviews from families without oversharing

Reviews matter for the map pack, and families read them closely. Home care reviews come with privacy problems most businesses don’t have.

Google’s review content policy prohibits incentives, asking only happy families, and asking staff to collect reviews that mention specific content or name a staff member. So no “please mention Maria!” in your review request, even though families often will on their own.

Our rules for home care clients:

  • Ask the adult child who hired you, around the 30-day mark, with a plain link.
  • Never confirm in a reply that the reviewer’s parent is a client. Thank them, keep it general and invite them to call.
  • Don’t mention diagnoses, addresses, schedules or hospital stays in replies, even if the family did.
  • Don’t ask caregivers to review the agency. Employment is a conflict of interest under Google’s policy, and the FTC’s rules apply too; see our post on the fake reviews rule.

The complete guide to Google reviews has request templates, and the medical practice post covers privacy-safe replies in more depth.

Measuring home care SEO with Search Console

A rank check shows where you appeared from one spot on one day. Search Console shows what families actually typed, across every town, for months.

In the Performance report, use the Queries tab with a filter for “home care,” “in home care” and each town name. Then switch to Pages and check each location page’s impressions, clicks and average position. Do it monthly and write down three numbers per town page.

Search Console stops at the click, so close the loop:

  • Use a tracking number on the Business Profile and another on the website, so you can see which produces calls.
  • Mark contact form submissions and call clicks as conversions in Google Analytics.
  • Ask every new family how they found you and log it next to the source in your intake sheet.

The measure that matters is new weekly care hours by channel, not traffic.

Home care marketing services: what to expect and ask

If you hire help, most single-location agencies fall in the single-location band in our SEO cost guide for Houston and The Woodlands. Before you sign with anyone, ask:

  • Which Business Profile category will you use, and why, given our license?
  • Which towns get pages, and what will make each one different?
  • How will you handle reviews and photos without client privacy problems?
  • Will you report calls and inquiries, or only rankings?
  • Who owns the website, the profile and the tracking numbers if we part ways?

Be wary of anyone selling a fake address in each suburb or a 50-town page package. Those get profiles suspended and waste money. Our web design and SEO team builds town pages and profile setups for agencies in Spring, Tomball and around The Woodlands.

Today, before anything else: open your Business Profile, read the primary category next to your HCSSA license, and write down the towns you could staff tomorrow morning. Those two lists set the scope of everything above. Frequently asked questions

What Google Business Profile category should a home care agency use?

Pick the category that most accurately describes your licensed services. Many non-medical agencies use Home health care service, but Google also offers Home help service agency for non-medical in-home help. In Texas, an agency without a home health license should be careful about any wording that implies one, so check your category choice against your HCSSA license.

Can I list my home care agency in several cities on Google?

You can show one Business Profile per real, staffed location, and set a service area listing the towns you cover within about two hours' drive. You can't create profiles for virtual offices or mailboxes. To appear in other communities, build a genuinely local page on your website for each town you staff, and let the profile point to your main location.

How long does home care SEO take?

Profile fixes such as a better category, services and fresh photos can change map visibility within weeks. New town pages usually take two to four months to settle in the organic results, longer in competitive areas like The Woodlands and Kingwood where franchises have been publishing for years. Steady reviews from families keep improving results over time.

How much does in-home care cost in Houston?

CareScout's 2025 survey put the Texas median for a non-medical caregiver at $68,640 a year for 44 hours a week, roughly $30 an hour, and the national median at $35 an hour. Houston agency rates vary with overnight, weekend and live-in schedules and minimum shift length, so families should compare written rate sheets from two or three licensed agencies.

Should my home care agency show its address on Google?

Only if families can visit you there during posted hours, such as for consultations at a staffed office. If you run the agency from home or a back office that clients never visit, Google's guidelines say to hide the address and set a service area instead. Either way, the address must be a real place that matches your license.
