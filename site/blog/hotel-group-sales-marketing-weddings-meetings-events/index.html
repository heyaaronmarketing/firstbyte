Monday, 8:12 a.m. The director of sales at a 250-room hotel in The Woodlands opens Cvent and finds 14 RFPs from the weekend. One is a 60-person leadership offsite for an energy company in the Energy Corridor, two nights in April. One is a 150-guest October wedding that came through The Knot. There’s a church conference, a youth volleyball team block and a pharmaceutical dinner that may or may not be real.

She answers two before lunch. The rest wait until Wednesday, after the BEO meeting, a site visit and a revenue call. By Wednesday the energy offsite has a proposal from a resort on Lake Conroe and a hotel in Sugar Land. Nobody calls her back.

Group business rarely gets lost in the ad account. It gets lost between the RFP and the reply. This post is about filling weddings, corporate meetings and social events at hotels and resorts: where planners and couples look, what they want to see before they ask, how fast you need to answer, and how to tell which marketing turns into signed contracts.

If you’re after transient direct bookings, our hotel direct bookings guide covers that side. This one is the sales office.

How meeting planners actually find a venue

A planner sourcing a 60-person offsite isn’t browsing. She has a date range, a budget, a headcount and an approval chain, and she wants three to five credible options fast.

Cvent’s 2026 Planner Sourcing Report surveyed more than 1,650 planners in July 2025. A few findings that should change how you market:

  • Attendee engagement and satisfaction now rank above cost management as the top priority.
  • Meeting room specifications (50%), photos and video (48%) and floor plans or diagrams (46%) most influence whether a planner sends an RFP at all.
  • Almost half (48%) are sourcing non-hotel venues like restaurants, galleries and wineries, so your competition includes the event space down the street.
  • Most want answers within four business days, and 24% call slow replies one of the most frustrating parts of the process.

Two more from Hotel Online’s roundup of group sales stats: CoStar has seen some group booking windows that used to run 30 to 60 days shrink to two or three weeks, and nearly 70% of planners in a 2026 industry study use AI tools like ChatGPT when sourcing destinations. So a planner may never type “meeting space The Woodlands” into Google. She may ask an assistant for “a hotel north of Houston with a 70-person boardroom setup and breakout rooms,” and the answer depends on what your website, Cvent profile and listings actually say. Cvent Supplier Network: fix the profile before you buy ads

For corporate and association business, Cvent is the marketplace. Group business sourced through the Cvent Supplier Network hit about $16.5 billion in 2024, a record, with room nights up 16% over 2019, per Cvent’s 2024 sourcing release. Cvent’s own page cites 155,000-plus sourcing planners and nearly 340,000 hotels and venues on the network, per Cvent’s advertising page.

That second number is the problem. A planner searching Houston north sees dozens of properties, and the profile is your first sales call. Before spending anything on Cvent advertising, make sure the free profile has:

  • Every meeting room with dimensions, ceiling height and capacity by setup (theater, classroom, banquet rounds, U-shape, reception).
  • Current floor plans, real photos of each room set for an event, and a short video walkthrough.
  • Guest room count, the number you’ll commit to a group block, and airport drive times to IAH and Hobby.
  • F&B minimums or a sample menu range, AV options, and outdoor or unique spaces (a lawn, a rooftop, a lakeside deck).
  • Recent renovations, with dates.

Then look at paid options. Cvent sells Diamond Listings for premium placement and richer content in search results, and competitive ads that show your property to planners looking at a competitor set you choose, plus off-platform retargeting. We’d test competitive ads against the two or three hotels you lose to most often. Judge it on what Cvent itself reports: RFP volume, RFP value, room nights requested and business awarded. Leads alone will flatter it.

Venue pages, floor plans and virtual tours on your own site

Most hotel websites are built for the leisure guest, with a meetings page tacked on: one stock photo of a ballroom and a form. Planners and couples go looking for specifics and leave when they don’t find them.

Meetings and events hubCorporate and association plannersCapacity chart for every room, downloadable floor plans and fact sheet, guest room count, AV, parking, airport times, RFP form and a direct phone number

The small meetings page is underused. Per Hotel Online’s roundup of group sales stats, 86% of planners in Cvent’s 2026 report are more likely to choose a venue for a simple meeting when online booking is available. An executive assistant booking a 16-person board meeting doesn’t want to wait three days for a proposal. Give her a package price and a calendar.

On virtual tours: a 360 tour is nice. A two-minute phone video of the ballroom set for 200 rounds, walking from the pre-function space to the restrooms, is often more useful, and it takes an afternoon. Put both on the space pages and in your Cvent profile. Our web design and SEO team builds these, and the landing pages primer covers the form side.

Response time: four days is the floor

Back to Monday morning. The 2025 Cvent report found 80% of planners expect an RFP response within four days, per Cvent’s 2024 sourcing release. For meetings of 50 attendees or fewer, 39% will drop a venue that hasn’t replied within two days, per Hotel Online’s roundup of group sales stats.

Four days is what planners tolerate. The hotel that answers the same day with real dates and a real rate usually sets the frame that everyone else is compared to. A standard we’d hold a sales team to:

The fix is mostly process. Route RFPs by segment and size so small meetings don’t sit in the same queue as a 400-room citywide. Build proposal templates by room and package so a manager can turn one around in 20 minutes. Give someone backup coverage for days when the DOS is on a site visit. And answer wedding inquiries by phone or text within the hour if you can, because couples often email five venues on the same night.

We cover the follow-up side in our speed-to-lead post. The short version: the second and third touches win almost as much business as the first.

Google Ads for group business

Search ads work for group business when the keywords say “event,” not “room.” A campaign that mixes “hotels in The Woodlands” with “conference venue The Woodlands” will spend its budget on transient travelers and send them to an RFP form.

Build separate campaigns:

  • Meetings: meeting space The Woodlands, conference venue north Houston, corporate retreat near Houston, offsite venue Houston, hotel with meeting rooms Spring TX, board meeting room rental.
  • Weddings: wedding venue The Woodlands, hotel wedding venue Houston, wedding reception venue with hotel rooms, Lake Conroe wedding venue.
  • Social: holiday party venue The Woodlands, rehearsal dinner venue, quinceañera venue Houston, reunion hotel Houston.

Negatives for the meetings campaign: jobs, careers, hiring, salary, apartments, tonight, cheap, “near me now,” free, virtual, Zoom, and your competitors’ room-booking terms. For weddings, add “courthouse,” “elopement” (unless you sell it), “DIY” and “backyard.”

Sample responsive search ad copy for meetings: “Meeting Space in The Woodlands” / “Breakouts for 10 to 300” / “Reply With a Proposal in 24 Hours.” Description: “Board rooms to ballroom, 250 guest rooms on site, 35 min to IAH. Send your dates and get a proposal fast.” Land it on the meetings hub, not the homepage.

An illustrative test budget is $1,500 to $4,000 a month across meetings and weddings for a single full-service property, more around peak wedding planning in January and February. It’s an example, not a benchmark. Track RFP form fills, wedding inquiries and calls as separate conversions, then import signed contracts as offline conversions so Google learns which clicks became business. LinkedIn for corporate meetings

LinkedIn is expensive per click and still the best way to reach the people who book corporate meetings before they open an RFP. LinkedIn’s targeting options include job title, company, industry and seniority, plus matched audiences from your website visitors, contact lists and target account lists.

How we’d set it up for a hotel north of Houston:

  • Audience one, titles. Executive assistants, event and meeting planners, HR and training managers, sales operations and sales enablement leaders, in Houston-area companies with 200 or more employees.
  • Audience two, accounts. A list of 100 to 300 companies within an hour’s drive that hold offsites, sales meetings and training: energy, engineering, healthcare systems, chemical and industrial firms, and law and accounting offices.
  • Audience three, retargeting. Visitors to your meetings pages.

Creative that works: a short video walkthrough of the boardroom, a “Q3 offsite package from $X per person” offer, and a lead gen form with three questions (dates, headcount, overnight or day only). We’d budget LinkedIn as the slow, steady pipeline builder and judge it over a full quarter. Our B2B marketing guide for Houston energy and industrial has more on reaching that audience.

Weddings: marketplaces, Instagram and the room block

Weddings bring a different buyer and a different clock. The Knot’s 2026 Real Weddings Study put the 2025 average wedding cost at $34,000 with 117 guests, or $292 per guest, and found couples hire an average of 13 vendors. Thirty-seven percent reached out to more vendors than they planned to, looking for budget-friendly options. So your inquiry is one of many sent the same evening.

The Knot and WeddingWire are still where many Houston couples shortlist venues, and Zola lists venues too. A listing works when it has real photos from weddings at your property, starting package prices, a capacity range and fast replies. Our wedding venue marketing guide goes deep on marketplaces, Instagram and tours.

A hotel has one advantage a barn venue doesn’t: guest rooms. Sell it. A 150-guest wedding often brings a block of 30 to 50 room nights across Friday and Saturday, plus a rehearsal dinner and a farewell brunch. Put that on the wedding page in plain terms: room block on site, no shuttles, a suite for getting ready, late checkout for the couple. Then make the block terms simple, whether courtesy or contracted, and explain attrition in one sentence before anyone signs.

Need periods and the Houston group calendar

Group marketing should follow your need periods, not the calendar the brand sends you. Houston’s is unusual.

The city hosts big compression events. The Offshore Technology Conference drew nearly 25,000 people to NRG Park May 4 to 7, 2026, per OTC’s closing release. RODEOHOUSTON ran March 2 to 22 in 2026, per RODEOHOUSTON. Houston ranked No. 5 on U.S. News’ first Best Conference Cities list, and Houston First’s sales team booked 517 future meetings and conventions worth 881,737 hotel room nights in 2025, a record, per Houston First. The George R. Brown expansion adds 700,000 square feet, including a 60,000-square-foot ballroom, with a target opening of May 2028, per Axios Houston.

For a hotel in The Woodlands, Spring or Katy, compression downtown means overflow and higher rates on those dates. Don’t spend group marketing dollars chasing business into weeks you’d sell out anyway. Spend them on the gaps:

  • July and August. The heat keeps leisure and weddings down. Market indoor corporate training, summer sales meetings and youth sports blocks.
  • Thanksgiving to New Year’s. Holiday parties fill evenings in early December, then the last two weeks go quiet. Sell holiday parties in August and September.
  • Sunday to Tuesday nights. Pitch day meetings and short corporate stays to companies within 30 minutes.
  • Hurricane season, June 1 to November 30. Have flexible terms ready. Planners remember who was reasonable after the last storm.

In The Woodlands, you’re also competing with the Waterway Marriott and its 65,000 square feet of meeting space, per Community Impact, plus resorts and conference centers around Lake Conroe. A smaller hotel wins on the 20-to-80-person meeting with a quick answer and a fair package.

The group sales funnel and the numbers to watch

Most sales offices track leads and revenue and nothing in between. You need the middle to know which marketing works.

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Response timeMedian hours from RFP received to proposal sentThe number planners feel first
Lead-to-proposal rateProposals sent ÷ qualified leadsIf good business is being declined or ignored
Proposal-to-contract rateContracts signed ÷ proposals sentPrice, package and follow-up quality
Lead-to-contract rateContracts signed ÷ qualified leads, by sourceWhich channel actually sells
Group paceGroup revenue on the books for a future month vs. same point last yearWhether you need more demand for that month
Pickup (actualization)Room nights used ÷ room nights contractedBlock sizing and attrition risk

Tag every lead with its source (Cvent, The Knot, Google Ads, LinkedIn, website organic, referral, walk-in) in your sales system, and keep that tag through to the signed contract. Then cost per contract by source becomes a number you can act on.

We built the same discipline on the transient side for Viceroy Los Cabos, where booking-engine tracking let us measure true room-night return: 11.4x return on ad spend and a 212% increase in direct bookings. Group is harder to track because contracts close weeks later and offline, which is exactly why the source tag matters.

Frequently asked questions

How do hotels get more group business?

Start with your listings and website: a complete Cvent profile with room specs, floor plans, photos and video, and venue pages on your own site with capacity charts and package prices. Then answer RFPs fast, ideally the same business day. Add Google Ads on event keywords, LinkedIn for corporate planners and wedding marketplaces for couples, and track every lead to a signed contract by source.

How fast should a hotel respond to an RFP?

Cvent’s planner research found 80% of planners expect a reply within four days, and for meetings of 50 or fewer attendees, 39% will drop a venue that hasn’t responded within two days. We recommend acknowledging within an hour during business hours and sending a real proposal within 24 hours. The first credible proposal often sets the terms the others get compared to.

Is Cvent advertising worth it for a hotel?

It can be, once your free Cvent Supplier Network profile is complete. Planners decide whether to send an RFP based largely on room specs, photos and video, and floor plans, so fix those first. Then test competitive ads against the two or three properties you lose to most and judge results on RFP value and awarded business, not lead count.

Should hotels use LinkedIn ads for meetings and events?

LinkedIn is useful for corporate meetings because you can target by job title, company, industry and seniority, and upload lists of target companies. Reach executive assistants, meeting planners, HR and sales operations leaders at companies within driving distance. Clicks are expensive, so judge LinkedIn over a full quarter on proposals and contracts rather than on cost per click.

When should a Houston-area hotel market group business?

Market into your need periods, not your busy weeks. In Greater Houston that often means July and August, the last two weeks of December, and Sunday through Tuesday nights. Compression events like OTC in May and the rodeo in March fill rooms on their own. Sell holiday parties in late summer, and plan wedding pushes for January and February when couples start booking.
