Where Houston Is Growing: The Corridors Bringing New Customers in 2027

Census, school and home sales data on where Houston and The Woodlands area growth is landing in 2027, and what it means for ad targeting and new locations.

126,720. That’s how many people the Houston metro added between July 2024 and July 2025, the largest numeric gain of any metro in the country, per the Census Bureau’s March 2026 county and metro release. The metro passed 7.9 million.

The bigger story for a business owner is where those people went. Harris County still added the most of any U.S. county. But look at the map of who’s moving in, and the growth sits in a ring 25 to 45 miles out: Fulshear and west Katy, the Waller County prairie, the SH 249 corridor through Tomball and Magnolia, Conroe and Willis on I-45, and the Grand Parkway’s newer eastern arc around New Caney.

This is a data story with maps. We pulled the latest Census estimates, school enrollment, master-planned community sales and road projects, then translated them into the two questions we get from clients most: where should we advertise, and where should the next location go?

County growth around Houston: the 2025 Census numbers

Here’s what the Census Bureau’s March 2026 release shows for the Houston-area counties, July 2024 to July 2025.

Rates for Harris, Montgomery and Fort Bend are our arithmetic from the release’s 2024 and 2025 figures. Waller grew 5.7%, second-fastest of any U.S. county with at least 20,000 people, and was the fastest-growing county in Texas, per The Texas Tribune. Brazoria County didn’t make either top-10 table, though it keeps growing along SH 288, and we come back to it with the master-planned communities.

Two caveats from the reporting. Harris County’s gain was less than half of its 2024 gain, and its domestic migration has been negative for roughly a decade as people move to the surrounding suburbs, per Axios Houston. International migration fell in nine of ten U.S. counties over the same period. In plain terms, the core is still growing, mostly from births and immigration, while people moving from elsewhere in the U.S. are landing in the outer counties.

For marketing, that means a Montgomery County business is sitting in one of the five fastest-growing counties in America by headcount. Roughly 30,000 new neighbors a year need a dentist, a mechanic, a gym and a favorite taco place.

Fast-growing cities: Fulshear, Conroe, and the places that aren’t really cities

City estimates tell a sharper story, with one trap.

Fulshear grew 21.0% in a year to 64,630 people, adding 11,196, per the Census Bureau’s May 2026 city release. It was the second-fastest growing U.S. city with 20,000+ people, and seventh in the country by raw numbers, just behind the City of Houston’s 11,515. Axios noted it was Fulshear’s third straight year at No. 2.

Conroe has more than doubled since 2010, from 56,207 residents to roughly 115,000 by July 2024, per the Houston Chronicle, which also reported Conroe ranked No. 20 nationally on U-Haul’s 2025 growth index and first among Houston-area suburbs. The I-45 frontage from League Line to Loop 336 shows it.

Now the trap. Katy and Magnolia grow fast in real life, but much of that growth happens outside their city limits, in unincorporated subdivisions with a Katy or Magnolia mailing address. City population estimates undercount what you see on FM 1488 or along the Grand Parkway west of I-10. School districts are a better yardstick for those areas.

For ad targeting, that trap matters. If you target the “City of Magnolia” in Google Ads, you’re aiming at a small town footprint. Target by radius or ZIP code around where the rooftops actually are, and check the location report after two weeks to see where clicks really came from.

School enrollment: the growth signal that leads everything else

Families with kids drive a huge share of local spending, and school enrollment is the cleanest public count of where they’re going. The Texas Education Agency numbers, as reported by Community Impact’s look at TEA enrollment data, show two very different metros.

The same report showed Region 6, which includes Montgomery County, up 3.8% while Region 4, which covers Harris County, fell 0.9%.

Waller ISD makes the newest point. Enrollment went from 9,894 in August 2025 to 11,467 in August 2026, and the district was processing about 60 new applications a day, per ABC13. Lamar CISD says it has 94 active subdivisions and more than 55,000 future lots in development, and it’s planning for nearly 2,000 new students a year by 2034, per Community Impact. Those future lots are your future customers, and they show up as households before they show up in any city estimate.

Master-planned community sales: where the rooftops are going up

RCLCO ranks the top-selling master-planned communities in the country twice a year, and Houston dominates the list.

In RCLCO’s mid-year 2026 ranking, nine Houston-area communities made the national top 50 for the first half of 2026, led by Tamarron at No. 6 with 594 sales, Bridgeland at No. 9 with 500 and Sunterra at No. 10 with 450. For all of 2025, ten Houston communities made the top 50, with Sunterra fifth at 1,024 sales and Tamarron ninth at 974, per Houston Agent Magazine’s summary of RCLCO’s 2025 list.

Look at where those nine sit. Tamarron, Sunterra, Anniston, Elyson and Jordan Ranch are on the Katy-Fulshear side, with Tamarron and Sunterra straddling into Waller County. Bridgeland and The Grand Prairie are in the Cypress-Hockley stretch along US 290. Sienna is in Missouri City, and Meridiana is in Brazoria County off SH 288. Every one of them is west or south of the city.

Montgomery County’s big communities (Woodforest, Grand Central Park, The Woodlands Hills, Artavia) grow too, and the county’s 30,011-person gain proves people are arriving. They just didn’t crack the national top 50 in that half-year. A business on the north side shouldn’t read that as slowdown. It reads as the growth being spread across more, smaller subdivisions, which actually makes targeting by radius more important. The road corridors: Grand Parkway, SH 249 and I-69

Houston growth follows pavement. Three corridors explain most of what’s coming in 2027 and after.

Grand Parkway (SH 99). The planned loop is 184 miles, per TxDOT. Segments H, I-1 and I-2 opened in May 2022, adding 52.5 miles from I-69 in New Caney to I-10 near Baytown through Harris, Montgomery, Liberty and Chambers counties, per Community Impact. That stretch explains a lot of the New Caney, Splendora and Liberty County numbers above. On the south side, Segment B runs 28 miles from I-45 in League City to SH 288 near Rosharon and is environmentally approved but not built, per TxDOT’s Segment B page. The first piece, B-1, is about 14 miles from League City to south of FM 2403 in Alvin. TxDOT expects construction to start in early 2027 and finish by 2032.

SH 249 and the Aggie Expressway. The tolled extension northwest of Tomball, which TxDOT’s project sheet calls the Aggie Expressway, runs about 14.8 miles from Pinehurst to Todd Mission, per TxDOT’s project sheet. Segment 1B from FM 1488 in Magnolia to FM 1774 opened in March 2021, and the final segment opened in fall 2022, per Community Impact. That corridor is why Tomball ISD grew 14.9% in five years and why Magnolia-area rooftops keep climbing.

I-69 (US 59). It’s the spine of two growth stories at once. Southwest, it runs through Sugar Land and Rosenberg into Fort Bend’s Lamar CISD territory. Northeast, it runs through Humble and Kingwood to New Caney and Splendora, where it meets the newer Grand Parkway segments.

A business deciding where to open should drive these corridors at 5:30 p.m. on a Tuesday. Where the traffic backs up on the way home, rooftops are already there and services haven’t caught up yet.

What the growth data means for where you advertise

Now the practical part. Here’s how we’d use this data in a campaign for a Houston or The Woodlands business.

Target the rings, not just the city. A Woodlands business with a 10-mile radius is missing most of Conroe’s growth and all of Magnolia’s. Extend radius ads to 15 or 20 miles if you can serve those customers, or build separate campaigns for the growth ZIPs so you can see what they cost. Use “presence” targeting (people in or regularly in your locations) so you’re not paying for people merely interested in the area.

Reach new movers early. People pick a new dentist, vet, gym and pediatrician in their first few months. Google Business Profile visibility in the new area, local search ads on “near me” terms and Meta ads within a few miles of new subdivisions do most of that work. Our guide to geofencing ads covers the tighter targeting options and their limits.

Make location pages for growth areas. If you serve Fulshear, Magnolia or New Caney, give each a real page with drive times, landmarks and reviews from customers there. Our local landing pages guide shows the format.

Watch your location reports. Google Ads and GA4 both show where clicks and conversions come from. After a month, compare cost per lead in growth ZIPs against your established ones. Newer areas are often cheaper to reach because fewer competitors bother.

Speak to newcomers. A family that moved from Chicago to Fulshear last spring doesn’t know which roads flood, what a MUD tax is, or that you’ve been fixing AC in Fort Bend since 2009. Ads and pages that say “new to Fulshear?” or answer newcomer questions tend to earn clicks that generic copy doesn’t. Spanish-language ads are worth testing in many of these corridors too; our Spanish-language marketing guide covers the setup.

Expect thinner competition and thinner data. In a new subdivision, there may be only a handful of reviews for anyone in your category, so 20 good reviews can put you near the top of the map pack. The flip side is that keyword planners show low volumes for brand-new areas. Don’t let a small search volume number talk you out of a ZIP code where 2,000 homes were sold last year.

Where to open a new location in Greater Houston

Ads can reach anyone. A building can’t move. Opening a second location is where this data earns its keep.

What we’d look at, in order:

  1. School enrollment trend for the district around the site. Growth of 10% or more over five years means families are arriving faster than most service businesses can open.
  2. Active subdivisions and lots within a 10-minute drive. Lamar CISD’s 55,000 future lots are an extreme example. Your local MUD or county permit data gives a closer read.
  3. Road timing. A site that’s quiet today may sit next to a Grand Parkway segment opening in five years. Segment B-1 near Alvin and League City is that kind of bet.
  4. Competitor density. Search your category in Google Maps from the site and count the results with more than 50 reviews. Growth areas often have one or two established players and a lot of open room.
  5. Your own customer data. Map where your current customers already drive from. If 20% of them come from Fulshear to your Katy location, that’s your answer.

Frequently asked questions

What are the fastest-growing areas around Houston in 2027?

The latest Census estimates show Waller County growing 5.7% in a year and Liberty County 4.4%, while Harris, Montgomery and Fort Bend added the most people. Fulshear was the nation’s second-fastest growing city of its size. School data points to Lamar CISD, Waller ISD, Tomball ISD, New Caney ISD and Splendora ISD, along the Grand Parkway, SH 249 and I-69 corridors.

How fast is Montgomery County growing near The Woodlands?

Montgomery County added 30,011 people between July 2024 and July 2025, the fourth-largest numeric gain of any U.S. county, reaching 781,194 residents. That’s roughly 4% growth in a year. Conroe has more than doubled since 2010 to about 115,000, and Conroe ISD added nearly 5,000 students from 2021 to 2026. Growth spreads across Conroe, Willis, Magnolia and New Caney.

Which Houston master-planned communities sell the most homes?

In RCLCO’s mid-2026 ranking, nine Houston-area communities made the national top 50, led by Tamarron, Bridgeland and Sunterra. For all of 2025, Sunterra ranked fifth nationally with 1,024 sales and Tamarron ninth with 974. Most top sellers are on the Katy, Fulshear, Cypress and Hockley side, with Sienna in Missouri City and Meridiana in Brazoria County.

How should a Houston business change its ad targeting for growth areas?

Target by radius or ZIP code around where new rooftops are, not by small city names like Magnolia or Katy whose limits miss most of the growth. Build separate campaigns for growth ZIPs so you can compare costs, use presence-only location targeting, and add location pages for each growth area. Check Google Ads and GA4 location reports after a month and shift budget toward what converts.

When will the Grand Parkway Segment B-1 be built?

TxDOT expects construction on Segment B-1 of the Grand Parkway, about 14 miles from League City to south of FM 2403 in Alvin, to start in early 2027 and be completed by 2032. It’s the first piece of Segment B, which will eventually connect I-45 to SH 288 near Rosharon. Businesses in Alvin, League City and Pearland should watch that corridor.
