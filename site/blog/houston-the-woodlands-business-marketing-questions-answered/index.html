Budget and cost questions from Houston owners

1. How much should a small business in Houston spend on marketing?

There’s no universal percentage that holds up. Work backward instead: what’s a customer worth to you, how many more do you want a month, and what does one cost to win in your category? A plumber who nets $400 a job can afford a very different cost per lead than a med spa selling $3,000 packages. Our budget-setting guide walks through the math, and what digital marketing costs in The Woodlands and Houston covers typical ranges.

One more piece most owners skip: budget for the agency or the person running it separately from the media. A $2,000 ad budget with nobody reading the search terms report is usually worse than $1,200 with someone who does.

2. What does a lead actually cost on Google Ads?

Nationally, WordStream/LocaliQ’s 2026 Google Ads benchmarks put the all-industry averages at $5.42 per click, an 8.18% conversion rate and $66.69 per lead. Home services ran $8.33 a click and $90.92 a lead; attorneys, $9.87 and $131.63. Competitive Houston categories like roofing, personal injury and emergency AC in August often run above those averages. See our Houston CPC benchmarks and cost per lead explainer.

3. Is marketing supposed to pay for itself right away?

Paid search often can within a couple of months, if tracking is right. SEO and brand work usually can’t, and judging them on 30-day results kills them early. We’d hold each channel to a payback window that fits it. A rough version: search ads judged at 60 to 90 days, Meta at 90, SEO at six to twelve months, streaming TV and billboards on whether branded searches and direct calls rise over a quarter. Marketing that pays for itself has the framework.

SEO and Google Maps questions

4. How long does SEO take to work?

For most local businesses in Houston and The Woodlands, first movement shows up in one to three months, steady calls around months four to six, and gains keep compounding through month twelve. Google’s own SEO Starter Guide says some changes “could take several months” and that you “likely want to wait a few weeks” before judging a change. Full breakdown in how long SEO takes in Houston.

5. Why don’t I show up in the Google map pack?

Google’s local ranking help page says local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that you can’t pay for a better local ranking. In practice that means the right primary category, a complete profile, steady reviews and a website page for each service. Distance you can’t change, which is why a business in Conroe rarely ranks in Sugar Land. It’s also why your ranking looks great from your office and mediocre from a customer’s kitchen in Magnolia; check it from several spots across your service area. Start with ranking in the Houston map pack.

6. What does SEO cost?

For a single-location local business, monthly retainers usually run $800 to $2,000; competitive categories like law, HVAC and dental often run $2,000 to $5,000. Our SEO cost guide explains what should be included at each level.

Google Ads vs. Local Services Ads

7. Should I run Google Ads or Local Services Ads?

If your category qualifies for LSA (most home services, many legal and some auto categories), run both and compare cost per booked job. LSA charges per lead instead of per click, and Google’s “How leads work” page explains what counts as a charged lead and what gets credited. Search ads give you control over keywords, copy, landing pages and schedule. LSA ranks heavily on reviews and how quickly you answer, so a company with 15 reviews that lets calls roll to voicemail will struggle there no matter what it spends. Details in LSA vs Google Ads.

8. Why am I getting clicks but no calls?

Usually it’s one of four things: the keywords are too broad, the ad sends people to the homepage, the landing page is slow on a phone, or calls aren’t being tracked so you can’t see the ones you did get. Google Ads not converting has the full checklist.

9. Is Performance Max right for a local business?

Sometimes. Google’s Performance Max help page describes it as a goal-based campaign that runs across YouTube, Display, Search, Discover, Gmail and Maps from one campaign. That reach is useful when your conversion tracking is clean. When it isn’t, PMax happily optimizes toward junk. We usually start local service businesses on Search, then add PMax once calls and booked jobs are flowing back into the account as conversions. Compare them in PMax vs Search for local businesses.

Social media questions

10. Which social platform should a local business use?

The one your customers already scroll, which for most Houston adults means Facebook, Instagram and YouTube. LinkedIn earns a spot only if you sell to other businesses. Pew Research Center’s 2025 survey found 84% of U.S. adults use YouTube, 71% use Facebook, 50% use Instagram and 37% use TikTok. A restaurant in The Woodlands and a B2B supplier in the Energy Corridor will answer this differently. Facebook vs Instagram vs TikTok compares them.

11. Are Facebook leads any good?

They can be, but instant forms make it easy for people to submit without meaning it. Someone in Humble scrolling at 11 p.m. taps a pre-filled form and forgets about it by breakfast. Add a qualifying question or two, turn on higher-intent form settings, and call within minutes. Fixing bad Facebook leads goes step by step.

12. How often should we post?

Less often than you think, if the posts are good. Two or three useful posts a week beats a daily stream of stock graphics. For most local service businesses, organic social builds trust, and paid social is what actually reaches new people.

What counts as useful: a 30-second video of your tech explaining why Houston clay soil cracks slabs, a before-and-after from a job in Kingwood, your crew at a Township event. In most accounts we’ve run, real people and real places beat stock photos by a wide margin.

13. Is Nextdoor or Yelp advertising worth it?

Sometimes, for specific categories. Nextdoor can work for home services in neighborhoods where people actively ask for referrals, which describes a lot of The Woodlands villages and Cypress subdivisions. Yelp matters more for restaurants and some personal services than for contractors. Test either one with a small budget and its own tracking number before you sign a long contract. We break them down in is Nextdoor advertising worth it and is Yelp advertising worth it.

Website questions

14. Do I need a new website?

Not always. If it loads fast on a phone, has a page for each service, shows your phone number at the top and tracks calls and forms, fix the content before you rebuild. Google’s Core Web Vitals page sets the “good” thresholds at 2.5 seconds for Largest Contentful Paint, 200 milliseconds for Interaction to Next Paint and 0.1 for Cumulative Layout Shift. Signs your website needs a redesign lists the real triggers.

15. How much does a website cost?

For a small business in The Woodlands or Houston, a professional build usually runs from a few thousand dollars for a simple service site to well above that for custom design, e-commerce or many locations. Website cost in The Woodlands has the breakdown. Ask who owns the domain and the hosting account before you sign; it saves a painful conversation later.

16. Do we need a blog?

You need pages that answer the questions your customers search, and a blog is one place to put them. A Spring foundation repair company doesn’t need “5 Fun Facts About Concrete.” It needs a page on why slabs crack after a dry Houston summer, one on what a repair costs, and one on how to read an engineer’s report. Two strong posts a month beat eight thin ones. See blogging for local SEO.

Reviews and reputation

17. How many Google reviews do we need?

Enough to look current and credible next to your competitors. BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found 74% of consumers only consider reviews from the last three months, 47% won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher. A steady trickle matters more than a big old total. A Cypress HVAC company with 300 reviews, the latest from 2023, looks closed next to a competitor with 90 and three from last week.

Reply to every one, good and bad. People read the replies to the one-star reviews more carefully than the reviews themselves.

18. Can we offer a discount for reviews?

No. Google’s prohibited content policy bans incentives for reviews and bans asking only happy customers. Ask every customer the same way, by text, soon after the job. The complete guide to Google reviews has scripts, and the FTC fake reviews rule covers the federal side.

Agencies and AI search

19. How do I choose a marketing agency?

Write a one-page brief first: the outcome you’re buying, your budget split into fees and ad spend, and who owns your logins. Send the same brief to every agency. Insist that you own your Google Ads account, analytics and domain. Ask who will actually do the work each week, and whether you’ll ever talk to that person. A senior strategist on the sales call and a rotating junior team after signing is a common pattern, in Houston and everywhere else. Choosing an agency in The Woodlands and contract red flags cover the rest.

20. How do I show up in ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overviews?

Mostly by doing good SEO. Google’s page on AI features says there are no extra requirements or special schema to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode; a page needs to be indexed and eligible for a snippet. Clear answers to real questions, consistent business details everywhere, and reviews all help. A page that plainly answers “how much does a water heater replacement cost in Houston” is the kind of thing AI tools quote, while vague marketing copy gets skipped. It’s becoming a real channel, too: BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found 45% of consumers use ChatGPT or other AI tools for local recommendations. Frequently asked questions

How much does marketing cost for a small business in The Woodlands?

Most small local businesses we see in The Woodlands and Houston spend somewhere between $1,000 and $10,000 a month on ads, plus management or agency fees, but the right number depends on what a customer is worth to you and what one costs to win in your category. Work backward from your goals and your measured cost per customer instead of picking a percentage of revenue.

How long does it take to see results from Google Ads in Houston?

Clicks start the day a campaign goes live, and with good tracking you can usually judge lead quality within four to eight weeks. Smart bidding needs conversion data to learn, so the first month is often the most expensive. Plan to review search terms weekly, add negative keywords, and evaluate the account on cost per booked job rather than cost per click.

Is SEO worth it for a local Houston business?

Usually, if you can give it six to twelve months. SEO builds rankings in the map pack and organic results that keep producing calls without paying per click. It works best alongside paid search, which covers the months while SEO builds. Categories with very high click costs, like roofing and legal, tend to get the most value from it.

Do Google reviews help my business rank higher?

Reviews are part of what Google calls prominence, one of the three factors behind local rankings along with relevance and distance. They also decide whether people call. Surveys show most consumers focus on reviews from the last three months, so ask every customer consistently after each job, reply to every review, and never offer incentives for them.

Should a Houston business hire an agency or do marketing in-house?

It depends on your budget and how much time you have. One in-house marketer can rarely cover ads, SEO, design and analytics well, while an agency brings specialists for less than several hires. Whatever you choose, keep ownership of your ad accounts, analytics, website and domain, and make sure reporting shows cost per lead and cost per customer.
