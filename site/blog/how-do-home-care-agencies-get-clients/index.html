How do home care agencies get clients? Mostly through the people who meet a family on its worst week: hospital discharge planners and case managers, skilled nursing and rehab staff, senior living directors, elder-law attorneys, geriatric care managers and physicians’ offices. The rest come from adult children searching Google, usually at night, right after a fall or a worrying phone call with Mom.

The agencies that grow steadily work both channels on a fixed schedule, and they track every referral until it becomes a signed client or a clear no.

Say you own a licensed personal assistance agency in Conroe with 25 caregivers and room for 400 more weekly hours. You need about 30 referral contacts, a calendar that puts you in front of each one at the right rhythm, and a phone that answers when a case manager calls at 4:40 on a Friday.

How do home care agencies get clients around The Woodlands?

Start with the local math, because it decides where you spend your week.

Census Bureau QuickFacts puts 14.9% of Montgomery County residents at 65 or older. In a county of more than three-quarters of a million people, that’s well over 110,000 older adults within a short drive of The Woodlands, and the county is still adding rooftops. Most of them want to stay put: AARP’s 2024 Home and Community Preferences survey found 75% of adults 50 and older want to keep living in their current homes as they age.

The referral side is concentrated too. Community Impact counted ten hospitals in The Woodlands area, including Memorial Hermann The Woodlands Medical Center, St. Luke’s Health-The Woodlands Hospital, Houston Methodist The Woodlands Hospital and the HCA Houston Healthcare campuses in Conroe and Tomball. Each one sends older patients home every day, many of them weaker than when they arrived and living with a spouse who can’t lift them.

That gives you two jobs: become the agency those hospitals’ professionals trust with a same-week start, and be easy to find and call for the daughter in Spring who has 48 hours to sort out Dad’s discharge.

One more local resource worth knowing: H-GAC’s Area Agency on Aging covers Montgomery and 11 other counties around Houston, while Harris County has its own Area Agency on Aging. Their benefits counselors and care coordinators talk to families constantly. Know them, even though they won’t steer families to one private agency.

Hospital discharge planners and case managers

This is the source most owners chase first and the one that takes longest to earn. Federal rules explain part of why. Under 42 CFR 482.43, a hospital discharging a Medicare patient to home health or a skilled nursing facility has to give the patient a list of available providers and “must not specify or otherwise limit” the choice. Private-pay non-medical agencies aren’t on that required list, but most case management teams keep a resource sheet of agencies they’ve found reliable. Your goal is to get on that sheet and stay near the top.

What gets you there is rarely a brochure. It’s answering the phone on a Friday afternoon, getting a caregiver to a house in Magnolia by Saturday morning, and calling the case manager back to say it happened.

A few practical notes for the hospitals around The Woodlands and Conroe:

  • Register before you visit. Most health systems require vendor check-in. Call case management and ask how they handle outside agencies.
  • Bring the boring proof. Your HCSSA license, liability coverage, how you screen and train caregivers, and your real minimum shift. Case managers are protecting their patients and their own credibility.
  • Know your limits out loud. If your license covers personal assistance services only, say so. Planners remember the agency that said “that’s skilled care, call a home health agency” and pointed them to one.
  • Mind privacy. Updates about a patient need the family’s permission. Our medical practice marketing guide covers HIPAA and tracking in detail; if your agency bills Medicaid or another payer electronically, have counsel confirm where HIPAA applies to you.

Skilled nursing, rehab and senior living communities

A two-week rehab stay after a hip replacement ends with a discharge meeting. The social services director in that meeting is deciding whether the patient can safely go home, and a reliable agency can tip the answer toward yes. That makes skilled nursing and inpatient rehab one of the best home care referral sources in Montgomery County, especially for agencies that can start the same day the patient leaves.

Senior living is the fastest win for most agencies. Independent living residents often need a few hours of help a week, and assisted living residents sometimes need more one-on-one time than the community’s staff can give. The executive director wants an outside agency that:

  • Checks in at the front desk, wears ID and follows the community’s rules.
  • Can cover a four-hour shift on short notice.
  • Never poaches residents toward moving out.
  • Tells the community’s wellness nurse when something changes, with the family’s permission.

Pick three or four communities within 15 minutes of your office and become the agency their staff knows by name. Offer a short family session on fall prevention, with no pitch.

Home health and hospice agencies are the other quick source. Their nurses visit for an hour; the family still needs someone for the other 23. An intake coordinator who knows you can fill non-medical hours will call you often.

Elder-law attorneys, care managers and physicians

These three take longer to build, but a single good relationship can send you clients for years.

Elder-law and estate attorneys

Attorneys in The Woodlands and Conroe handle guardianships, trusts, powers of attorney, Medicaid planning and VA benefits. Their clients often need care right now while the paperwork catches up. The National Academy of Elder Law Attorneys has a member directory, which is a good place to build your list. What attorneys want: clear rates in writing, caregiver timesheets that would hold up if anyone asked, and an agency that understands what a VA pension application or long-term care insurance claim needs.

Geriatric care managers

Aging life care professionals (the Aging Life Care Association is their professional group) are hired by families to manage everything. They’re demanding, they check your work and they’re fiercely loyal to agencies that keep the same caregiver on a case. Ask one what agencies get wrong. Then don’t do that.

Physicians’ offices

Geriatricians, primary care, neurology and cardiology offices see the decline first. The doctor rarely has time for you; the office manager and the nurse do. Leave a one-page sheet that explains what non-medical home care covers, what it doesn’t, how to refer and how fast you can start. Come back every month or two with something useful.

How to get more clients for my home care business online

The other half of your clients never come through a professional. They come from an adult child who searches “home care near me” or “in-home care for elderly parents Spring TX” and calls the first two agencies that look trustworthy.

That person is busy. AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving counted 63 million family caregivers in the U.S. in 2025, six in ten of them employed, and nearly one in four providing 40 or more hours of care a week. They are not reading your mission statement. They want to know:

  • Do you serve my parent’s town, and can you start this week?
  • What does it cost per hour, and what’s the minimum shift?
  • Are you licensed by the state, and are caregivers background-checked and trained?
  • Will a person answer if I call right now?

Put those answers on your homepage and every town page. Then make the phone fast. If a call at 8:30 p.m. goes to voicemail, that family calls the next agency, and the Google ad or referral that produced the call was wasted. We track this with call tracking for exactly that reason.

For paid search, start small: brand terms, “home care” plus your towns, and “in home care near me,” with ads scheduled for when someone actually answers. Marketing plan for home care agency: the monthly outreach cadence

A marketing plan for a home care agency is mostly a calendar. Split your referral list into tiers and give each tier a rhythm you can keep, even in a week when two caregivers call out.

  • Tier A (8 to 10 contacts): sources that already sent you a client or clearly could. In person every two weeks.
  • Tier B (10 to 15): good fits you’re still earning. In person monthly, plus one useful email.
  • Tier C (the rest): quarterly touch, mostly by email or a quick call.

Promising a hospital next-day starts you can’t staff burns the relationship faster than never visiting. When recruiting is behind, slow the outreach and say why.

Home care agency marketing ideas that stay inside Texas rules

A few rules shape what you can say and do, and competitors break them all the time.

The license comes first. Under Health and Safety Code §142.002, nobody may provide personal assistance services for pay in Texas, or tell the public they do, without a home and community support services agency (HCSSA) license for each place of business that directs those services. Texas HHSC’s HCSSA licensing page lists the categories: Licensed Home Health, Licensed and Certified Home Health, Hospice, Personal Assistance Services, plus home dialysis designations. If you hold a personal assistance license only, keep “home health” out of your ads, site and profile; home health is a separate license category, and the statute is written around what you represent to the public. Put your license number on your site. Families and planners notice.

Paying for referrals is a question for a lawyer. Texas has a patient-solicitation law, Texas Occupations Code §102.001, that deals with paying or accepting something of value for securing patients for a licensed provider, and federal anti-kickback rules can come into play when Medicaid or another federal program pays for care. We’re marketers, so we won’t tell you where the lines sit. Before you sign with a placement service, offer anyone a referral bonus or set a budget for gifts and meals for referral sources, talk to a healthcare attorney.

Reviews have rules. Google’s review content policy bans incentives, asking only happy families, and asking staff to collect reviews that name a specific caregiver. Our guide to Google reviews has a request script that stays inside the lines.

Inside those limits, plenty works:

  • A family guide to the first week home from the hospital, printed for discharge planners and posted on your site.
  • Respite care packages for family caregivers, promoted to churches and caregiver support groups.
  • A LinkedIn presence for the owner, since case managers and attorneys check who they’re referring to.

Score your referral sources every 90 days

Most agencies know how many clients they have. Few know which source sent them, or which sources send inquiries that never turn into care. Without that, you keep buying lunch for the friendliest office instead of the most productive one.

A spreadsheet works fine at first. Log every inquiry with the fields above. After 90 days you’ll see which three or four sources produce most of your hours. Those move to Tier A. Sources that refer but never convert may be sending clients who need skilled care, which is a conversation worth having.

For online inquiries, separate calls from Google Business Profile, organic search and ads with tracking numbers, so the review push and the ad spend each get credit for what they actually do.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best referral sources for home care?

For most non-medical agencies around Houston, the fastest sources are senior living communities, home health and hospice intake teams, and past clients' families. Hospital case managers, skilled nursing and rehab social workers, elder-law attorneys, geriatric care managers and physicians' offices take longer to earn but send steady, high-value clients once they trust that you start quickly and staff reliably.

How long does it take a new home care agency to get clients?

Expect your first clients from personal networks, senior living and online search within the first few months, and hospital or physician referrals to build more slowly as you prove reliability. The agencies that speed this up answer every call live, start care within a day or two, and visit their best referral contacts on a fixed schedule instead of when things get slow.

Can a home care agency pay for referrals in Texas?

Ask a healthcare attorney before you pay anyone for a referral. Texas has a patient-solicitation law in Occupations Code chapter 102, and federal anti-kickback rules can apply when Medicaid or other federal programs pay for care. Placement-agency fees, referral bonuses and gifts to referral sources can all raise questions, so get advice on your specific arrangement first. Referrals earned through reliable service don't involve paying anyone.

Do non-medical home care agencies need a license in Texas?

Yes. Texas Health and Safety Code section 142.002 requires a home and community support services agency license to provide personal assistance services for pay or to advertise them, and the license applies to each place of business that directs those services. Texas HHSC lists Personal Assistance Services as its own license category, separate from home health and hospice.

Should a home care agency use Google Ads?

Usually yes, once your phones are answered and your website shows rates, service towns and your license. Start with branded searches and home care searches in the towns you staff, schedule ads for staffed hours, and track calls. Health-related advertising has audience and remarketing restrictions, so check Google's healthcare and personalized advertising policies before building campaigns.
