To get more plumbing service calls in Houston, answer more of the calls you already get, then make sure Google shows you first when the next one happens. That means a text back within a minute on every missed call, a Business Profile with honest service areas, Local Services Ads raised before the weather turns, and new reviews every week. After that, build offers around the work this region sends you on a schedule: burst pipes in a hard freeze, slab leaks in clay that shrinks and swells, and water heaters at the end of their life.

We’d hand this to a two- or three-truck shop in The Woodlands in exactly this order. The first fix costs almost nothing, and it isn’t really marketing.

Fix the phone before you buy more calls

Here’s the part most shops get wrong. They spend real money to make the phone ring, then let a big share of those calls hit voicemail while the owner is under a sink in Oak Ridge North. A homeowner with water on the floor won’t leave a message. They call the next plumber on the list.

Missed-call text-back fixes most of that for very little money. When a call goes unanswered, your phone system sends a text within seconds: “Sorry we missed you, this is Smith Plumbing. Is this an active leak? Reply with your address and we’ll call you right back.” A lot of people reply, and every reply is a lead you already paid for.

Before you switch it on, ask your phone or CRM vendor to confirm your number is registered for A2P 10DLC, the carrier system for business texting from 10-digit numbers. Per Twilio’s carrier notice, unregistered traffic to US numbers has been blocked since September 1, 2023. And give each marketing channel its own tracking number so you can see which one drove the call; our guide to call tracking walks through the setup.

Plan for the freeze before the forecast

Winter Storm Uri is the reference point for every plumber here. According to the National Weather Service’s February 2021 climate summary, Bush Intercontinental hit 13°F on February 16, 2021, tying a record from 1895, and Hobby dropped to 15°F. The NWS Houston/Galveston’s 2021 annual summary lists “broken pipes” among the impacts and says the event may have been the costliest weather disaster in Texas history, ahead of Harvey.

The shops that did well that week were already visible, already answering and honest about wait times. Freezes that size are rare, but shorter hard freezes come often enough that prep pays. Our November checklist:

  • Build a freeze page now. Burst pipe repair, frozen pipes, how to find the main shutoff, what to do while you wait. Homeowners search for help before they search for a plumber.
  • Write a paused “freeze” campaign in Google Ads. Terms like burst pipe repair, frozen pipe repair and emergency plumber plus your towns, with ads that state real wait times. Turn it on when the NWS issues a hard freeze watch.
  • Raise your LSA budget a few days ahead, then cap it at what your trucks can actually run. Calls you can’t serve still cost money and reviews.
  • Line up overflow answering and a triage script: active leak first, no water second, everything else on a callback list by text.
  • Plan the follow-up. Two weeks after a freeze, the calls change to pipe insulation, rerouting exposed lines and repipes after damage.

Slab leaks, clay soil and the calls nobody plans for

Most Houston-area homes sit on concrete slabs, and much of the metro sits on clay that moves. The USDA’s official description of Lake Charles clay, a soil mapped across Southeast Texas between the Colorado and Trinity rivers, says it cracks 1 to 2 inches wide at the surface when dry, and the cracks stay open 60 to 90 days most years. Soil that opens and closes like that puts stress on everything buried in it, including supply lines under the slab.

Homeowners rarely search “slab leak” first. They search the symptom: a water bill that doubled, a warm spot on the tile, water running with every tap off.

  • Build one page for slab leak detection and one for slab leak repair, with your options explained plainly (spot repair, reroute, repipe).
  • Write a short guide on reading your water meter to check for a leak. It earns links and calls from people who aren’t sure yet.
  • Offer leak detection as a flat-fee entry job. It’s a smaller ticket that opens the door to a reroute or a repipe.
  • Trade referrals with a foundation repair company. Their inspectors find plumbing problems and your techs find cracks.

Soil varies across the metro, so check where your slab jobs actually cluster by ZIP before you target a town. We cover which slab, repipe and leak phrases deserve their own page in our plumbing SEO keyword map for Houston.

How to get more plumbing leads from your Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile is the free version of the ad, and for emergency work it often beats the paid one. The settings that matter most:

  • Service areas. Google’s service area help page allows up to 20, defined by city or ZIP code, and says your overall area shouldn’t run more than about 2 hours of driving from your base. For a shop based off Research Forest Drive, that might be The Woodlands, Spring, Conroe, Shenandoah, Oak Ridge North, Tomball and Magnolia, plus ZIP codes like 77380, 77381, 77382, 77373 and 77379 where you want the work.
  • Address. If customers don’t come to you, remove the address and run as a service-area business. Most plumbers working from a house or a yard fall in this group.
  • Name. The Business Profile guidelines prohibit adding service or location words to your name. “Smith Plumbing” is fine; “Smith Plumbing Emergency Plumber The Woodlands” is a suspension waiting to happen.
  • Category and services. Choose Plumber as the primary category and add each service you actually sell with a short description: water heater replacement, slab leak detection, hydro jetting, gas line repair.
  • Photos and posts. Real trucks and real jobs. Set special hours and post shutoff tips before a freeze.

One honest limit: service areas tell Google where you work, but your distance from the searcher still counts. A shop in Conroe won’t own Katy by listing it. For the full ranking picture, see our guide to local SEO for Houston contractors.

How to get more plumbing customers with Local Services Ads

Local Services Ads sit above everything else on the page and charge per lead instead of per click. For an emergency trade that’s usually the first paid channel we’d turn on. If you’re weighing it against regular search ads, our breakdown of LSA vs Google Ads for home services covers the trade-offs, and Google Guaranteed vs Google Verified explains the badge.

Know what you’re paying for. Google’s Local Services Ads billing page counts answered calls, texts, voicemails, booking requests and even some missed calls as charged leads. Follow-ups from the same customer within 15 days aren’t charged again, and unanswered calls are only charged during your ad schedule and business hours. Google keeps checking lead quality for 30 days and can credit bad ones automatically.

The LSA ranking page lists your bid, how likely your ad is to produce a lead, and your profile quality as the placement factors, with responsiveness to customer inquiries counted under the second one. So a shop that lets calls ring out ranks lower and still pays for some of the missed calls. That’s one more reason the phone section came first.

How we run LSA for plumbers:

  • Turn off job types you don’t want, so a drain-only crew doesn’t pay for water softener shoppers.
  • Set your schedule to the hours someone really answers.
  • Dispute junk leads every week; don’t wait for the automatic review.
  • Run regular search ads alongside LSA for high-ticket work like repipes and tankless installs, where a landing page sells better than a phone call. That’s the work our search marketing team does every day.

How to get more plumbing jobs from the reviews you already earn

Google’s own local ranking page says review count and rating feed into how prominent Google considers a business. It doesn’t talk about recency, but we keep seeing it in map pack audits: a shop with 400 reviews and nothing new since spring tends to slip behind a shop with 120 and a few fresh ones every week.

So make the review request part of closing the ticket:

  1. The tech finishes, walks the customer through the work and asks in person.
  2. Your system sends a text with the direct review link within the hour, while the customer still has hot water and good feelings.
  3. One reminder two days later. No more.
  4. The office replies to every review within a few days, naming the service (“glad the new water heater is working”) without sharing private details.

Ask every customer, not only the happy ones, and never offer anything for a review. The FTC rule on fake and incentivized reviews applies to small shops too; our explainer on the fake reviews rule covers the lines. Techs named in reviews should hear about it at the Monday meeting.

Water heater replacement offers that don’t race to the bottom

Water heaters are the most predictable revenue in residential plumbing. The Department of Energy says conventional storage tanks last about 10 to 15 years and tankless units about 20. Run that against your own map. A Spring or Conroe subdivision that went up in 2012 is full of original tanks that are now 14 years old, and the builder probably installed the same model in every garage on the street.

For pricing, Fixr’s January 2026 cost guide puts a typical national install at about $1,400, within a $900 to $3,000 range, and tankless at roughly $1,000 to $4,000. Houston prices vary with permits, venting and attic installs, so treat those as a sanity check.

Offers that work without wrecking your margin:

  • Free age check on every service call. Techs read the serial number, note the year and tell the homeowner where the tank stands. It’s honest and it fills next year’s pipeline.
  • “Replace before it floods the garage.” Upfront, all-in price including haul-away, permit and expansion tank if needed.
  • Financing shown on the page, since a $2,500 surprise is easier as a monthly number.
  • A tankless consult for homeowners who are planning, not panicking. Different page, different ad, different conversation.

How to grow a plumbing business with memberships

Emergency calls are lumpy. A membership program smooths them out and builds a list of homeowners who already trust you.

A plumbing membership in Houston can be simple: an annual water heater flush and inspection, a whole-house plumbing check, no trip fee, a small repair discount and priority scheduling. Priority is the part people here actually buy. In a freeze, members go to the front of the line, and you can say so plainly when you sell the plan.

A hypothetical: a three-truck shop in Spring signs 250 members at $15 a month. That’s $45,000 a year before a single repair, plus 250 scheduled visits where techs spot aging tanks, corroded shutoffs and slow drains. Nobody charged you a lead fee for those jobs. Your numbers will differ, but the visit is the marketing.

Don’t promise priority you can’t deliver in a freeze week. And text members in November with shutoff and insulation tips so they remember why they pay. More on how this fits the rest of the funnel is on our home services marketing page.

Put your license number on everything

Texas makes license display a rule, and it doubles as a trust signal. Under 22 TAC §367.10, the responsible master plumber’s license number and the company name have to appear on both sides of every service vehicle, in contrasting letters at least two inches tall, preceded by “M,” “MPL” or “RMP.” Advertising that solicits plumbing work has to show the license number too, and the first page of every proposal, invoice and contract has to say the company is “regulated by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.”

Licensing is handled by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, which was still the agency in charge when we checked in October 2026. The board’s future has been debated before (a 2018 Sunset staff report recommended moving plumbing into TDLR), so confirm the current rules on the state’s site before you print new wraps.

From a marketing view, put the license number in your site footer, on every landing page, in your Business Profile description and on the truck. Homeowners who have been burned by an unlicensed handyman look for it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more plumbing calls?

Start with the basics that decide who Google shows and who gets hired: a complete Business Profile with real service areas, Local Services Ads, fast answers on every call, and a text back on every missed one. Then add steady review requests and offers built around predictable work like water heater replacement. Most shops gain more from answering faster than from spending more.

How do I get more plumbing leads without paying for them?

Your Business Profile, reviews, past customers and memberships are the free channels. Keep the profile complete, post before freezes, ask every customer for a review by text, and contact past customers when their water heater reaches 10 years. A short guide on checking your water meter for leaks can also earn search traffic and calls over time.

What are good plumbing marketing ideas for a small shop?

Pick ideas tied to local demand: a freeze-prep page and paused freeze ad campaign, a slab leak detection page, free water heater age checks on every call, a simple membership with priority scheduling, referral swaps with foundation repair companies, and missed-call text-back. Each one is cheap to set up and easy to measure with call tracking.

Are Local Services Ads worth it for plumbers in Houston?

For most residential plumbers, yes, as long as someone answers the phone. You pay per lead rather than per click, and LSA appears above regular ads. Google charges for answered calls, texts and some missed calls, so a shop that lets calls ring out pays for leads it never books. Dispute junk leads weekly and turn off job types you don't want.

How long does it take to see more plumbing service calls?

Paid channels like Local Services Ads and search ads can add calls within days of launch. Missed-call text-back helps the first week. Business Profile improvements, reviews and SEO pages usually take a few months to show up in rankings, and memberships build over a year. Track calls by source from day one so you can see which change actually worked.
