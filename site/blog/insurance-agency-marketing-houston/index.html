Tuesday at an independent agency in Kingwood. One client forwards a renewal with a number she doesn’t believe. Another has a nonrenewal letter from a carrier that’s pulling back in Texas. Insurance agency marketing in Houston works when it puts you in front of homeowners at the exact moments they shop: a renewal shock, a nonrenewal, a home purchase, a flood scare. It has to stay inside Texas Department of Insurance advertising rules and federal telemarketing law, and it builds referral pipelines with the realtors and lenders who control the timing. Then you round out every account so one carrier’s rate filing doesn’t take your book with it.

This is written for independent property and casualty agencies with one to a few offices across Greater Houston.

The Texas home insurance market you’re selling into

The last few years made home insurance a kitchen-table topic in Houston. The average Texas homeowners premium was $3,489 in 2025, up from $1,791 in 2016, and average rate changes ran 21.1% in 2023 and 18.7% in 2024 before slowing to 4.3% in 2025, according to TDI’s homeowners market overview. The three biggest groups, State Farm, Allstate and USAA, wrote about 47% of the market between them in 2024.

Carriers pulled back too. Community Impact’s Cy-Fair report described Hippo nonrenewing a Cypress-area homebuilder’s policy despite no claims, noted USAA’s first loss in its 102-year history in 2022, and cited a Kinder Institute finding that more than 20% of Harris County housing units sit in major flood areas.

Now the market is shifting. Over the 90 days through October 2026, filed homeowners rate changes averaged a 4.0% decrease, per the same TDI page, and Carrier Management reported that filings since July 1, 2026 would cut rates for about 700,000 Texas homeowners policyholders.

For an independent agency, both halves of that story are marketing. Homeowners who got hit with two years of increases are still angry and still shopping. Homeowners whose captive carrier is now cutting rates may not hear about better options unless someone tells them. Your pitch, “we shop multiple carriers every renewal so you don’t have to,” has rarely been easier to explain.

TWIA, flood and the coastal side of Houston

If you write in Clear Lake, League City, Seabrook, La Porte, Galveston or Brazoria County, windstorm coverage is part of nearly every conversation. The Texas Windstorm Insurance Association covers the 14 first-tier coastal counties, including Galveston, Brazoria and Chambers, plus parts of Harris County east of Highway 146 such as La Porte, Morgan’s Point, Pasadena, Seabrook and Shore Acres, per TWIA’s own overview. TWIA calls itself a residual insurer of last resort, applications are submitted through TDI-licensed agents, and applicable structures need a WPI-8 certificate of compliance.

That’s a content opportunity most agencies ignore. Pages like “TWIA windstorm insurance in League City: what you need” and “Do I need a WPI-8 to sell my house in Seabrook?” answer questions homeowners and realtors type into Google.

Flood is the cross-sell almost every Houston household needs and many don’t have. It typically takes 30 days for a new flood policy to go into effect, per FloodSmart’s agent resources, so the marketing window is before a storm is in the forecast. Send flood reminders in April and May, and again when the season’s first storm forms in the Gulf. “Your homeowners policy doesn’t cover flooding, and a new flood policy usually takes 30 days” is a message that works, as long as it’s accurate for each client.

Texas Department of Insurance advertising rules, in plain terms

Insurance ads in Texas fall under 28 TAC Chapter 21, Subchapter B. Read the rule itself or ask your carriers’ compliance teams before anything goes live, but the main points, as summarized by IIAT’s summary of the Texas advertising rules, are these:

  • Every ad must identify who’s responsible for it, through the full licensed name, a registered assumed name or the agent’s Texas license number.
  • Ads must be truthful and not misleading in fact or implication, can’t make unfair or incomplete comparisons of policies or rates, and can’t disparage competitors.
  • An ad about a specific policy must state the insurer’s full licensed name, and an agent must file an ad that mentions an insurer or its policy with that insurer for approval before using it.
  • Testimonials must be genuine, reflect the author’s current opinion and apply to the advertised policy.
  • Online, required disclosures can sit behind a conspicuous, clearly labeled link next to the relevant information.

Gifts and incentives are tightly limited. Promotional or educational items of $25 or less are allowed, per IIAT’s summary of rebating and referral rules, and anything bigger tied to buying insurance can become an illegal rebate.

The practical takeaway: write about your service and process, which you control, rather than about price promises you can’t keep for every household.

Google Ads for “home insurance quote” searches

Search is where shopping intent lives, and it isn’t cheap. WordStream’s 2025 benchmarks put the median Finance & Insurance cost per click at $3.46 and cost per lead at $83.93, per WordStream’s 2025 benchmarks. That category blends banks, lenders and every kind of insurance, and in accounts we’ve run, home insurance quote terms in Houston often cost well above that blended figure. Expect to pay more for the click and to win on conversion rate.

Structure we’d use:

  • Home by town: “home insurance quote Katy,” “homeowners insurance The Woodlands,” “home insurance Pearland,” landing on a town page that mentions local issues (flood zones, MUD-heavy new construction, hail in Cypress).
  • Moment-of-need: “home insurance non renewal Texas,” “dropped by home insurance,” “home insurance went up,” “new home insurance before closing.” These are often the best-converting ad groups.
  • Coastal and flood: “TWIA agent League City,” “windstorm insurance Galveston,” “flood insurance quote Houston.”
  • Independent agent: “independent insurance agent near me,” “insurance agency Spring TX,” “compare home insurance quotes.”
  • Bundles and specialty: “home and auto insurance quote,” “landlord insurance Houston,” “umbrella insurance.”

Two policy notes. Google’s personalized ad restrictions cover housing, employment and credit, and insurance isn’t on that list, per Google’s personalized ads policy FAQ, so you keep normal targeting. Carrier names are trademarks; bidding on them is a gray area and using them in ad text usually needs permission, which your appointment may or may not give. Ask the carrier.

Send calls to a licensed person during business hours and use a short quote form with a callback promise after hours. Our search marketing team runs this setup, and our Houston CPC benchmarks give more local context.

Meta ads: insurance is a special ad category now

A lot of agents still think the special ad category rules on Facebook and Instagram are only about credit. They aren’t. Meta’s Financial Products and Services category replaced the old Credit category and covers banking, investment and insurance ads as well as loans, according to Jon Loomer’s guide to Meta’s special ad categories. In the U.S., that means age locked to 18 to 65+, all genders, no ZIP code targeting, a minimum 15-mile radius around any location, limited detailed targeting with no exclusions, and no lookalike audiences.

So on Meta, the creative has to do the targeting. A stock photo of a smiling family and “Save on insurance!” reaches everyone and converts no one. What works better:

  • A 30-second phone video of the agent saying “If your home insurance renewal jumped this year, bring it to us. We’ll shop it with several carriers and tell you honestly if you’re already in a good spot.”
  • Nonrenewal and new-homeowner messages, which self-select the right people.
  • Lead forms with a qualifying question or two (renewal date, ZIP, whether they own or rent) and a higher-intent setting. Fewer leads, better ones. Our guide to fixing bad Facebook leads goes into detail.
  • Retargeting website visitors and video viewers, which still works inside the category.

Meta is a good place to build familiarity before renewal season. Search does the closing. If you’d rather hand this off, see our paid social service.

Buying leads: TCPA consent and Texas telemarketing rules

Shared and “exclusive” internet leads are a big part of how many agencies grow, and the compliance ground moved recently.

The FCC’s one-to-one consent rule, which would have required a consumer’s consent to name a single seller, was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC just days before its January 27, 2025 effective date, along with the requirement that calls be “logically and topically” related to the original interaction, per Morrison Foerster. The FCC then formally removed the rule in 2025, per Goodwin. Prior express written consent is still required for autodialed or prerecorded marketing calls and texts, and the consent still has to clearly tell the consumer they may hear from the sellers in question.

Texas added its own layer. SB 140, effective September 1, 2025, brought marketing texts under the state’s telephone solicitation law, with a registration requirement and a private right of action, per Morgan Lewis’s summary of SB 140. Persons licensed under the Texas Insurance Code are exempt from that registration, per a Mondaq review of the exemptions, but the lead vendors and call centers working for you may not be. And the federal rules still apply to you either way.

Our view: the cheapest lead is usually the most expensive one once you count the agent time spent chasing people who don’t remember filling out a form. Lawsuits cost more than that. Ask your attorney before you buy, and read our Texas SMS rules post before you text anyone.

Realtors, mortgage lenders and builders: the referral engine

Every home purchase in Houston needs a policy before closing, which makes realtors, loan officers and title companies the best referral sources an agency has. They don’t care about your logo. They care whether you’ll get a binder to the lender on time and keep their closing from slipping.

  • Make a closing promise. “Quote within one business day, binder to the lender within 24 hours of approval.” Then keep it, every time.
  • Give them a dedicated line that reaches a licensed person, plus a simple intake form they can send buyers.
  • Educate, don’t pitch. A one-page “Insurance questions to ask before you buy in Houston” covering flood zones, roof age, WPI-8 on the coast and TWIA is something agents happily hand to clients. Keep it inside the advertising rules above.
  • Show up where they are. HAR events, lender lunch-and-learns, builder sales offices in Bridgeland, Sienna or The Woodlands Hills.

On paying for referrals, Texas generally allows a licensed agent to pay an unlicensed person a referral fee as long as the fee isn’t based on whether the customer buys insurance and the referrer doesn’t discuss specific policy terms, per IIAT’s summary of rebating and referral rules. Lenders and realtors have their own rules about what they can accept, so many agencies stick to reciprocal referrals and great service. Google Business Profile, reviews and why LSA isn’t an option here

Local Services Ads might seem like an obvious fit, but Google lists the Insurance agency category as available in California and Florida only, per Google’s Local Services Ads category list. Houston agencies have to win with the Business Profile, organic search and regular ads.

  • Primary category Insurance agency, with secondary categories only for real specialties.
  • One profile per physical office with staff during posted hours. Don’t create profiles for home addresses or virtual offices.
  • Photos of the actual office and team. Insurance is a trust purchase, and faces help.
  • Services listed by line: home, auto, flood, windstorm, landlord, umbrella, commercial.

Ask for reviews at two moments: right after a client binds a policy that saves them money or solves a nonrenewal, and after a claim goes well. Same link to everyone, no gift cards (that runs into both Google’s rules and Texas rebating limits), and reply to every review without discussing policy or claim details in public. Retention and cross-sell: rounding out the account

In a market where a carrier’s rate filing can move your whole book, the agencies that grow are the ones whose clients hold several policies with them. A household with home, auto, umbrella and flood is far harder to poach than a single auto policy.

  • Track policies per household as a headline number, and set a target for it.
  • Run a renewal review 45 to 60 days before every home renewal, before the client gets the notice and starts shopping without you.
  • Run flood and umbrella campaigns to your own book. Your best prospects are already clients.
  • Send a short monthly email: storm prep, what changed in Texas rates, a claim story with names removed. Our email marketing basics post covers getting started.

Track new households, policies per household, retention rate and cost per bound policy by source. Lead counts are the least useful number you’ll see.

A first 90 days for a Houston agency

Here’s a plan we’d use for a one-to-three-office independent agency. Spend ranges are illustrative.

  1. Days 1 to 30: tighten the Business Profile, build town pages and moment-of-need pages (nonrenewal, new purchase, flood, TWIA), run the ad copy past your carriers, set up call tracking and a CRM source field.
  2. Days 31 to 60: launch Google Ads at roughly $2,000 to $5,000 a month across home-by-town and moment-of-need ad groups, start renewal reviews and flood campaigns to the existing book.
  3. Days 61 to 90: build a referral list of 25 realtors and 10 loan officers you’ll actually call on, test Meta video under the Financial Products and Services category, and decide on lead vendors only after checking their consent records.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more home insurance clients in Houston?

Show up where homeowners shop: Google searches for home insurance quotes by town, nonrenewal and new-purchase searches, and referrals from realtors and mortgage lenders who need binders before closing. Build pages for each of those moments, keep a strong Google Business Profile with steady reviews, and run renewal reviews for your own book. Rounding out accounts with flood, auto and umbrella keeps clients longer.

Are insurance ads a special ad category on Facebook?

Yes. Meta’s Financial Products and Services special ad category replaced the old Credit category and includes insurance ads. In the U.S., age is locked to 18 to 65+, all genders are included, ZIP code targeting is unavailable, locations need at least a 15-mile radius, detailed targeting is limited and lookalike audiences are off. Specific creative and retargeting work best under these limits.

Can Houston insurance agencies use Google Local Services Ads?

Not currently. Google lists the Insurance agency category for Local Services Ads as available in California and Florida only, so Texas agencies can’t run them. Houston agencies should focus on their Google Business Profile, reviews, local landing pages and regular Google search ads targeting home insurance quote and nonrenewal searches.

Is the FCC one-to-one consent rule in effect for insurance leads?

No. The Eleventh Circuit vacated the FCC’s one-to-one consent rule in Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC in January 2025, days before it was set to take effect, and the FCC formally removed it later in 2025. Prior express written consent is still required for autodialed or prerecorded marketing calls and texts, and Texas SB 140 now covers marketing texts. Check with an attorney before buying leads.

What are the Texas insurance advertising rules for agents?

Under 28 TAC Chapter 21, an ad must identify the responsible agent or agency by licensed name, registered assumed name or license number, be truthful and not misleading, avoid unfair comparisons and disparaging competitors, and name the insurer when promoting a specific policy. Agents must get insurer approval for ads that mention the insurer. Promotional gifts are limited to $25 or less.
