Is Nextdoor advertising worth it? For a local business that serves homeowners in a defined area, a small, tracked test usually is, and the free Business Page is worth claiming either way. Paid Nextdoor ads do best for home services, pet care, neighborhood retail and listing agents in owner-heavy suburbs. For emergency trades, B2B companies or anyone selling statewide, we’d keep the money on Google and Meta.

Most of what ranks for this question is out of date. Between 2024 and 2026, Local Deals was folded into a new ad flow, a paid lead product called Opportunity Alerts appeared, and in August 2026 Nextdoor stopped letting owners promote their businesses from personal accounts. This is how it works now for a business in The Woodlands, Spring, Conroe or elsewhere around Houston.

How Nextdoor for Business works in 2026

Nextdoor sells four separate things to local businesses, and owners tend to blur them together.

  • The free Business Page. Nextdoor’s small business page lists a Business Page, free Business Posts that appear in the neighborhood newsfeed, visibility in Search and Discover, and customer recommendations, all marked free.
  • Recommendations and Faves. When a neighbor who has hired you vouches for you, that shows on your page as a Fave. Nextdoor’s 2026 Fave Awards announcement says more Faves raise a business’s position in search, and that neighbor-to-neighbor recommendations make up nearly 30% of all content on the platform.
  • Nextdoor Ads. Self-serve feed ads, launched for small businesses in 2022. Local Deals now lives here as the “Promote a sale or discount” goal.
  • Opportunity Alerts. A monthly subscription for home-service categories that texts or emails you when a nearby neighbor posts asking for your kind of service.

One rule change shapes everything else. In its self-promotion policy update dated August 19, 2026, Nextdoor said promotion “must be done from a free Business Page, not a personal neighbor account.” Plug your company in a “who does good fence work?” thread from your personal profile and the post can get flagged, which means fewer neighbors see it.

Is a Nextdoor business page free?

Yes. Claiming, verifying and posting from a Business Page costs nothing. Nextdoor’s self-promotion update says verification takes about a minute, and freelancers and home-based businesses are welcome to set one up. Nextdoor’s Fave Awards release says nearly 6.6 million businesses have claimed a free page.

A few setup details that matter more than they look:

  1. Claim the existing page first. Neighbors may have created one when they recommended you, and a second page splits your recommendations.
  2. Pick categories the way a neighbor would describe you. “Pool service” beats “aquatic maintenance solutions.”
  3. Set a real service area. Business Posts use a radius you can widen or shrink, plus neighborhoods you pick. Include your own, or you won’t see your posts from your member account.
  4. Upload photos of actual jobs in recognizable settings. A finished patio with pine trees behind it reads as local in The Woodlands in a way a stock photo never will.
  5. Casual services such as dog walking need business documents to verify before posting to the nearby feed.

Then ask 20 recent nearby customers for a recommendation. In month one, that does more than any ad budget will.

How much does Nextdoor advertising cost?

Nextdoor doesn’t publish a rate card. You set a daily budget, and what you pay depends on reach and competition. Nextdoor’s ad setup page advertises budgets of “as little as $5/day,” and turning ads off means you’re only charged for impressions already served. The Ads Manager quickstart says bids are CPM or CPC depending on the objective you choose.

The billing setup depends on which door you walk through, which trips up a lot of owners:

Watch the 30-day prepay on the simple flow. Set $15 a day there and $450 leaves your card on day one.

Ignore the old numbers floating around, like Local Deals “from $1” or Neighborhood Sponsorships at $32 a month. Those products were renamed or retired, and the per-lead ranges we’ve seen published cite no data.

For planning, we use $10 to $20 a day for one service in a handful of ZIP codes, or $600 to $1,200 over 60 days. That’s our working range from setting these up, not a Nextdoor figure.

Is Nextdoor advertising worth it for your business? Who it fits

Nextdoor ads work when the buyer owns the house, plans a few days out and cares what the neighbors think. They fall flat when the buyer is in a hurry.

Nextdoor’s own products point the same way. Its Nextdoor’s ad how-to page lists a Real Estate section placement for real estate and home services advertisers, and Opportunity Alerts covers 33 service categories from plumbing and roofing to movers and pet sitting.

Real estate needs one extra check. Nextdoor’s Nextdoor’s ad policy says housing ads must not assert or imply that ZIP code, age, familial status or several other traits are relevant. Write about the home, never about who belongs there.

Nextdoor ads vs Facebook ads (and Google LSA)

This one comes down to reach against context. Facebook and Instagram reach far more of your neighbors. Nextdoor reaches fewer of them, in a feed where people are already talking about their houses and asking who to hire.

Pew Research Center found 71% of U.S. adults use Facebook and 50% use Instagram in 2025. Its latest Nextdoor figure is 13%, from 2021. Nextdoor reports 22.9 million platform weekly active users in its Q2 2026 results, and its business pages claim one in three U.S. households is on the platform, with no footnote.

Why Nextdoor may be unusually active in The Woodlands

This part is our hypothesis. Nextdoor doesn’t publish usage by city, and we haven’t found anyone who has for this area.

The reasoning goes like this. Nextdoor organizes around neighborhoods, and The Woodlands is already divided into named villages with their own associations: Grogan’s Mill, Panther Creek, Cochran’s Crossing and Indian Springs on the older east side, Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge and Creekside Park to the west, per a local real estate guide. People here say “I live in Sterling Ridge” the way people elsewhere name a street. Strong shared identity, lots of single-family owners and steady new arrivals are the conditions that produce “who do you use for…” posts.

The same pattern shows up in master-planned communities nearby. Grand Central Park in Conroe, for example, is a Johnson Development community of about 2,000 acres with more than 1,500 homes so far, and its final lots went to builders in early 2026, according to Houston Agent Magazine. New arrivals need a pest service, a pool company, a vet and a handyman, and they ask their neighbors first.

You can check it for your own category in ten minutes. Search the app for your category plus “recommend” and count the asks in your service area over the last 90 days. If you find several a week, Nextdoor deserves a test. If you find two all year, keep the free page and spend the money elsewhere. For businesses north of the county line, our Conroe marketing page covers the wider Montgomery County picture.

A 60-day Nextdoor test plan with budget and tracking

Nextdoor’s dashboard reports impressions, clicks and messages, not booked jobs. Set up tracking before the first dollar goes out.

The setup

  • A dedicated call tracking number on the ad and on your Business Page. Our guide to call tracking explains how to do this without hurting your Google listing.
  • UTM tags on every link, for example ?utm_source=nextdoor&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=sterling-ridge-fall. Use utm_medium=organic for free Business Posts so you can compare the two.
  • A Nextdoor-only promo code such as ND25. It catches the people who see the ad, then call your main number or walk in.
  • The Nextdoor pixel, if you book online. Ads Manager can optimize toward conversions and now shows ROAS reporting, per Nextdoor’s August 2026 product update.

The budget

Say you run a two-crew lawn and landscape company in Alden Bridge. You’d run $15 a day for 60 days, about $900 total, aimed at five or six ZIP codes you can reach in 20 minutes. Keep one offer, say a fall aeration package, for the full test so you’re measuring the channel, not the creative.

The scorecard

At day 60, count booked jobs from the tracking number, UTM links and promo code, and divide $900 by that count. If Nextdoor lands within 30% of your best channel’s cost per booked job and the customers rebook, keep it. If it’s double, cut the ads and keep the free page.

What to post on Nextdoor for free

Free Business Posts land in the main newsfeed, the most visible part of the app. Write them the way a neighbor would and they can pull as well as a paid ad.

  • Useful seasonal reminders. “Hurricane season runs through November 30. Here’s the five-minute check we’d do on your gutters before the next storm.” No hard pitch.
  • Local job photos with permission. A before-and-after from a street people recognize, with the homeowner’s okay and no house number showing.
  • Offers tied to a place. A first-visit discount for Creekside Park only reads like it was meant for the reader. “10% off for new customers” reads like an ad.
  • Community stuff, like a school fundraiser you sponsor.

Skip daily posting, copy pasted from Facebook and all-caps “LIMITED TIME” offers. Neighbors mute that fast.

Neighborhood etiquette and the risk of a negative thread

Nextdoor’s biggest risk comes from the same place as its value: people talk. A recommendation thread can send you four jobs in a week, and a complaint about a missed appointment can top a neighborhood feed for days. Our rules before spending a dollar:

  1. Reply from the Business Page, once, calmly. “Thanks for flagging this, Jen. I’ve sent you a message so we can make it right.” Then fix it offline.
  2. Never argue facts in public, even when you’re right. Neighbors judge the tone, not the invoice.
  3. Don’t ask friends to pile on. Coordinated praise is obvious.
  4. Don’t pay or discount for Faves. Ask every happy customer, but don’t trade anything for it.
  5. Watch threads weekly. Search your business name in the app every Monday so a problem doesn’t sit unanswered.
  6. Answer recommendation requests fast, but only ones you actually fit.

Paid ads put more eyes on those threads. If your crew is already behind on callbacks, fix that before you buy attention.

Before you spend a dollar on Nextdoor

Do the free part tonight: the 90-day search for recommendation asks described above, plus claiming your page. If the asks are scarce, you’ve saved yourself $900 and kept a free listing. If they’re steady, run the 60-day test with its own number, UTMs and promo code. Frequently asked questions

Are Nextdoor business ads worth it?

They can be for local businesses that serve homeowners nearby, such as lawn care, pool service, pest control, pet care and remodelers. They are rarely worth it for emergency services or B2B companies. Run a 60-day test with a dedicated call tracking number, UTM links and a Nextdoor-only promo code, then compare cost per booked job with Google and Meta before you commit.

How much does Nextdoor advertising cost?

Nextdoor does not publish a rate card. You set a daily budget, and Nextdoor advertises starting points of about five dollars a day. The simple dashboard flow charges 30 days up front, while Ads Manager bills as you spend. For a meaningful local test we usually plan on ten to twenty dollars a day for about 60 days.

Is a Nextdoor business page free?

Yes. Creating, claiming and verifying a Nextdoor Business Page is free, and so are Business Posts to the neighborhood feed and customer recommendations. Since August 2026, Nextdoor requires businesses to promote themselves from a Business Page rather than a personal account, so the free page is the starting point.

Do Nextdoor ads work better than Facebook ads?

Not across the board. Facebook and Instagram reach far more people, so they usually win on volume. Nextdoor reaches a smaller audience already asking neighbors who to hire, which can mean better leads for home services. Test both with the same offer and tracking.

What is Nextdoor Opportunity Alerts?

Opportunity Alerts is a paid monthly subscription for home service businesses. It sends a text or email when a nearby neighbor posts asking for your type of service, within a 20-mile radius of an address you choose and up to three categories. Nextdoor shows the price at signup and lets you cancel anytime. Track the jobs it produces like any other paid channel.
