Ten dollars a day. That’s LinkedIn’s minimum daily budget, and it’s the most misleading number on the platform for a Houston B2B company.

At ten dollars a day you’ll get a trickle of clicks from people who look right on paper, a report full of impressions, and no way to tell whether anything happened. Then someone in the Monday meeting says “we tried LinkedIn, it didn’t work,” and the company goes back to trade shows and golf.

LinkedIn can be the best paid channel a Houston energy services firm, industrial manufacturer or professional services firm has. It’s also the easiest to waste money on. This is a playbook: seven plays in the order we’d run them, with the real platform limits, honest cost math, and the situations where we’d tell you to spend the money somewhere else.

Is LinkedIn advertising worth it for B2B companies in Houston?

Usually, if three things are true: one closed deal is worth $50,000 or more over its life, your buyers can be identified by company and job title, and you can fund at least $3,000 to $5,000 a month for a full quarter. Below that, or for fast transactional sales, Google search and direct outreach usually return more per dollar.

That threshold is our judgment, not a LinkedIn rule. It comes from how the math works when clicks are expensive and sales cycles run six to eighteen months, which is normal for anyone selling to operators, EPCs or chemical plants along the Ship Channel.

LinkedIn’s own research arm makes the same point from a different angle. The 95:5 rule, popularized by LinkedIn’s B2B Institute and based on work by John Dawes at the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, holds that about 95% of B2B buyers are out of the market at any given time. LinkedIn is where you stay familiar to the 95% so you’re on the shortlist when they come back in. If you need the 5% this month, that’s a search problem.

Play 1: Build the account list before you touch targeting

Most Houston B2B campaigns start in Campaign Manager’s targeting menu, picking “Oil and Gas” and “Director” and hoping. Start with a spreadsheet instead. Name the 300 to 3,000 companies you’d actually like to sell to, then upload them.

The mechanics, from LinkedIn’s company list documentation: a company list CSV can hold up to 300,000 records, LinkedIn matches it against its company pages, it takes 24 to 48 hours to be ready, and it can serve once the list reaches 300 matched members. LinkedIn recommends at least 1,000 companies for best results. Include the company website and LinkedIn page URL columns where you have them; names alone match poorly for companies with common words or lots of subsidiaries.

Houston’s corporate geography matters here, because it tells you where buying committees actually sit.

  • Energy Corridor. The Energy Corridor District lists bp, Shell, ConocoPhillips, CITGO, Baker Hughes, Fluor, Kiewit, McDermott, Enbridge and SBM Offshore among its largest employers, with more than 56,000 jobs in and around the district.
  • The Woodlands. Chevron Phillips Chemical consolidated its global headquarters into the Research Forest Lakeside campus, bringing together nearly 950 employees, per The Real Deal. Huntsman’s global headquarters is on Woodloch Forest Drive near Waterway Square.
  • Spring. ExxonMobil moved its corporate headquarters from Irving to its 385-acre campus at City Place (the former Springwoods Village), reported by Community Impact.
  • Greenspoint. It used to be on every energy target list. ExxonMobil, Noble Energy and Southwestern Energy all left, and Bisnow reported direct office vacancy around 42% in 2021. Today it’s mostly mid-size tenants, so target Greenspoint companies by name and industry, not by assuming a big operator is still there.

One trap with big operators: a company list targets the whole company. Upload Shell and you reach Shell employees in the Netherlands, Singapore and Louisiana. Layer a location (Houston metro, or Texas plus Louisiana for Gulf Coast work) unless global reach is the point. And split your list into tiers. Your top 50 accounts deserve their own campaign and their own creative.

Play 2: Layer job function and seniority, then check audience size

Once the company list is in, narrow it by the people who sign or influence the purchase. The options LinkedIn names on LinkedIn’s audience targeting page include job title, skills, years of experience, company and firmographics, plus matched audiences and audience expansion. In Campaign Manager you’ll also find job function, seniority, industry and company size.

Check the audience size before launch. LinkedIn’s minimum is 300 members; its recommendation is 50,000 or more for single image and text ads and 15,000 or more for message ads. A focused Houston ABM audience often lands between 5,000 and 30,000, under the recommendation. That’s fine. You’ll pay higher costs for a narrower audience, and you’ll know exactly who you’re paying to reach.

We’d also turn off the LinkedIn Audience Network on ABM campaigns, which extends ads to third-party sites and apps. It’s useful for reach, and murky for an account list where you want to know your ads appeared on LinkedIn itself. Exclude your own employees and your competitors by company name.

How much do LinkedIn ads cost for Houston B2B companies?

Here’s what LinkedIn actually publishes. Per LinkedIn’s budget guide, the minimum daily budget is $10 for any ad format ($30 per campaign when you use Dynamic Group Budget), and new campaigns need a minimum lifetime budget of $100. Costs come from an auction: per LinkedIn’s bidding guide, winning takes “a good relevancy score and an appropriate bid,” and you choose maximum delivery (LinkedIn’s default), cost cap or manual bidding.

LinkedIn doesn’t publish average CPCs, and the third-party averages you’ll find vary so much they’re not worth quoting. What we can tell you is that narrow, senior audiences cost more than broad ones, ABM audiences under the recommended size cost more still, and energy and finance titles are among the more contested. Plan with an assumption and replace it with your own numbers after 30 days.

Budget rules we’d follow: run at least a quarter before judging, keep 70% or more on your target account list, and don’t split $3,000 across six campaigns. Two or three campaigns with enough budget to produce real data beat a dozen starved ones. Play 3: Use Lead Gen Forms, with one question that filters

Lead Gen Forms open inside LinkedIn, pre-filled with the member’s name, email, company, title and location. LinkedIn’s own Lead Gen Forms guide puts the average conversion rate at 13%, against 4.02% for landing pages in an Unbounce study it cites. It recommends three or four fields, and warns that a single free-response question can cut submissions by 3 to 4 percent. Forms allow up to 12 fields and three custom questions.

The catch is the same convenience that drives the 13%. Pre-filled forms take one tap, so you get people who were curious for one second. The fix is one multiple-choice qualifying question that a serious buyer answers easily and a casual one has to think about:

  • An industrial valve distributor: “When is your next turnaround or major maintenance window?” (Next 6 months / 6-12 months / Not scheduled)
  • An engineering firm: “What stage is the project in?” (Feasibility / FEED / Detailed design / Construction)
  • A Houston law or accounting firm: “What’s driving the question?” (Acquisition / Dispute / Compliance / Just researching)

“Just researching” is a valid answer. Those people go into nurture, not to a salesperson’s phone.

Connect the form to your CRM so leads don’t sit in a CSV. LinkedIn integrates with HubSpot, Salesforce and others, and the first call should happen within the hour. Our speed-to-lead guide explains why that hour matters even in slow B2B cycles.

Play 4: Pick formats engineers and operators actually stop for

LinkedIn’s ads guide lists the formats. The ones we’d use for Houston energy and industrial buyers:

  • Document ads. A PDF, DOCX or PPTX previewed in the feed, up to 300 pages. Spec sheets, case studies, a one-page capability statement. You can let people read a few pages and gate the rest with a Lead Gen Form.
  • Thought Leader Ads. Sponsored posts from a real person at your company. A short post from your VP of Engineering about what failed on a project and what you changed will outperform a polished brand ad for most technical audiences, and it reads like LinkedIn instead of an ad.
  • Video. A 45-second shop-floor or job-site clip with captions. Engineers like seeing the actual equipment.
  • Conversation Ads. Messages with up to five buttons, good for routing people (“Book a meeting at OTC” / “Get the spec sheet” / “Talk to an engineer”). Use them sparingly; inbox ads wear out fast.
  • Event Ads. For your own webinar or an event at your booth.

Sample copy for a Houston fabrication shop, single image ad: “ASME-certified pressure vessel fabrication in north Houston. 14 weeks from approved drawings to delivery on our last three jobs. See the spec sheet.” Replace the numbers with your real ones. Specific claims a reader can check are what work with engineers.

And for a professional services firm in The Woodlands: “Selling an energy services company in the next two years? Our checklist covers the 11 things buyers’ diligence teams ask for first.” That one runs as a document ad with a two-page preview.

Play 5: Time your spend around OTC

Houston’s B2B calendar has one big spike. OTC and URTeC will co-locate May 3 to 5, 2027 at Reliant Park, per JPT, which puts a big share of offshore and unconventional buyers in one place in the same week.

LinkedIn is the best tool we know for booking meetings before a show. We’d weight spend like this:

Eight weeks out, run awareness to your target account list (document ads, Thought Leader posts). Three to four weeks out, switch to a meeting ask: “We’re at booth [number], here’s what we’re showing, grab 20 minutes.” Show week, keep a light campaign running so the name stays in feeds, and put your reps’ Thought Leader posts in front of the list. For two to six weeks after, upload booth scans as a contact list and retarget them with the case study they asked about.

Then cut spend in June and July, when attention drops and the target list has seen your ads a dozen times. Restart in late August as budgets get planned. Our trade show marketing guide covers the booth side and the venue-level geofencing that LinkedIn can’t do.

Play 6: Retarget, then measure pipeline instead of clicks

Install the LinkedIn Insight Tag on your site and build retargeting audiences from website visitors, video viewers, document ad readers and people who opened a Lead Gen Form without submitting. These are small in Houston B2B, often a few hundred people, which is exactly why they convert. Our retargeting explainer covers the basics.

Measurement is where LinkedIn programs live or die. Click-through rate and cost per lead are easy to report and nearly useless on their own. The metrics we’d put in front of a Houston CEO:

  • Target accounts reached and engaged (by account, not by person)
  • Sales-accepted leads and meetings booked, by campaign
  • Opportunities and pipeline value where the account engaged with ads in the prior 90 days
  • Closed revenue, two to four quarters later

Tie it together in the CRM, not in Campaign Manager. Our guide to measuring marketing and the broader B2B marketing guide for Houston energy and industrial companies go deeper on attribution for long cycles.

When is LinkedIn not worth it for a Houston company?

We say no to LinkedIn about as often as we say yes. These are the usual reasons.

The most common one is the second item. A lot of Houston industrial demand starts on Google: “crane rental Pasadena,” “industrial electrician Baytown,” “emergency valve repair Houston.” Those searches come from people with a problem today, and they’ll call the first credible company they find. LinkedIn can’t compete with that intent.

Professional services sit in the middle. A Houston law firm or accounting firm with an energy or M&A niche can do well with Thought Leader Ads from partners. A firm whose clients are mostly individuals usually can’t.

A 90-day LinkedIn plan for Houston B2B

Month one. Build and tier the company list, set up the Insight Tag and CRM sync, create two campaigns (top accounts and wider list), and launch with document ads and one Thought Leader post a week. Judge only delivery and engagement by account.

Month two. Add a Lead Gen Form campaign with a qualifying question. Start retargeting engaged visitors. Kill the weakest creative and replace it. Begin tracking meetings and sales-accepted leads.

Month three. Review cost per sales-accepted lead and which accounts are engaging. Move budget toward what produced meetings. If OTC is inside the next 90 days, start the pre-show sequence.

At day 90 you won’t have closed revenue yet. You will know whether the right accounts are paying attention, which is the leading indicator that matters.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum budget for LinkedIn ads in Houston?

LinkedIn’s platform minimum is $10 a day per campaign, or $30 per campaign with Dynamic Group Budget, and new campaigns need at least a $100 lifetime budget. Those are the floor, not a realistic test. For a Houston B2B company targeting senior energy or industrial buyers, we’d plan at least $3,000 to $5,000 a month for a full quarter to get enough data to judge results.

Can I target employees of specific companies in the Energy Corridor on LinkedIn?

Yes. Upload a company list with up to 300,000 records and LinkedIn matches it to company pages, usually within 24 to 48 hours. You can’t target an office park directly, so pair the list with a Houston metro location filter. Otherwise a list including bp, Shell or ConocoPhillips reaches their employees worldwide, not just the people working along I-10 west of Houston.

Are LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms better than sending people to my website?

They usually convert better. LinkedIn reports a 13% average conversion rate because the forms are pre-filled from profiles. The tradeoff is lead quality, since one tap is easy. Add a single multiple-choice qualifying question about timeline or project stage, sync leads into your CRM, and call within the hour. Landing pages still make sense for detailed quote requests.

When should a Houston company run LinkedIn ads for OTC?

Start about eight weeks before the show with awareness ads to your target account list, then switch to meeting requests three to four weeks out. OTC 2027 runs May 3 to 5 at Reliant Park, co-located with URTeC. Keep a light campaign running during show week, then retarget booth contacts with a contact list upload for several weeks afterward.

Do LinkedIn ads work for small B2B companies in The Woodlands?

They can, if deal sizes are large and the buyer is identifiable by company and title. A small engineering or consulting firm in The Woodlands selling six-figure contracts to chemical and energy companies is a good fit. A company with small transactional orders, or one whose buyers search Google when something breaks, will usually get more from search ads first.
