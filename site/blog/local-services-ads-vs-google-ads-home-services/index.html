Verdict first. If you’re a licensed HVAC, plumbing, electrical or similar company in Houston with a decent pile of reviews and someone who reliably answers the phone, start with Local Services Ads. Add regular Google Ads search campaigns for the high-ticket jobs LSA can’t single out. Most companies that stick with one or the other end up running both.

The direct answer: Local Services Ads charge per lead, sit at the top of Google for eligible home services, and rank on reviews, responsiveness and verification, with almost no control over keywords or copy. Google Ads search campaigns charge per click and give you full control over keywords, ads and landing pages. LSA is usually cheaper per lead; Search is better for choosing which jobs you get.

That comparison changed a lot in 2026. LSA moved inside Google Ads, the dispute button is gone, missed calls can now be charged, and call-only ads are on their way out. Below is what’s different now, what each one really costs in Houston, and how we’d split a budget between them for a company in The Woodlands, Spring, Cypress or Katy.

What changed for Local Services Ads in 2026

If you set up LSA a few years ago and haven’t looked closely since, a lot moved.

LSA now lives in Google Ads. Starting in August 2026, Local Services campaigns began migrating into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The first phase covered select U.S. home and storefront services such as plumbing, HVAC and roofing; a broader rollout follows in late 2026 and the rest in 2027, per Google’s transition guide. You still pay per lead, and the ads still show only on Search and Maps, keywordless, based on your service categories and areas.

Bidding got simpler, and blunter. Weekly budgets become daily averages (weekly divided by seven), monthly spend is capped at the daily average times 30.4, manual bidding is gone, and per-category targets are replaced by one campaign-level Target CPA. Search Engine Journal flagged the obvious problem: a company selling $90 drain cleanings and $12,000 AC systems now shares one cost target unless it splits campaigns.

Disputes are over. Manual lead disputes aren’t supported in the new setup. Google credits some invalid leads automatically, and leads for job types or areas you don’t serve aren’t credited at all. We covered how that works in our LSA lead credits guide.

Missed calls cost money. Since October 1, 2026, an unanswered call during business hours where the caller stays on more than 20 seconds can be charged, per PPC Land.

One badge. Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and license verification were folded into a single Google Verified badge in October 2025, per Google’s Verified badge page. Details in Google Guaranteed vs. Google Verified.

And on the regular Google Ads side, call-only ads can’t be created anymore and stop serving in February 2027. Calls now come from call assets on responsive search ads.

How much do Local Services Ads cost compared with Google Ads in Houston?

Start with the side that has public numbers. LocaliQ’s 2025 home services search benchmarks, based on campaigns from April 2024 through March 2025, put median search ad costs per lead at $127.74 for AC installation and repair, $129.02 for plumbing, $93.69 for electricians and $228.15 for roofing. Median clicks ran about $9.70 to $12.20. Those are national medians; competitive Houston suburbs often run higher in July.

LSA cost per lead is harder to pin down because Google doesn’t publish it and it moves with trade, job type, area and season. It’s generally lower than search for the same trade, which is why LSA is the usual starting point. But a cheaper lead isn’t automatically a cheaper job. What matters is cost per booked job, and that depends on how many leads you actually close.

Which shows up first: Local Services Ads or Google Ads?

For eligible categories, the LSA unit usually sits at the very top of the results, above the text ads, then the map pack, then organic results. On a phone in Spring searching “water heater repair,” that LSA box can fill most of the screen.

That placement is why LSA leads feel “warmer.” The searcher sees your rating, review count, years in business and the Verified badge before they ever see an ad headline. It’s also why reviews matter so much more here than in Search.

The layout isn’t fixed. Google tests it constantly, and AI Overviews can push everything down on informational searches. Don’t build a plan that depends on any one position.

How Local Services Ads decide who gets the lead

Per Google’s LSA ad ranking page, LSA ranks providers on three things: your bid, how likely your ad is to produce a lead, and your profile quality. “Likely to produce a lead” covers your responsiveness, how relevant your services and bio are to the search, and whether you turn on messages or booking. Profile quality covers your rating, number of reviews, response time, photos and completed verification.

A few Houston-specific notes. If you have locations in Katy and The Woodlands that both serve Cypress, only your highest-ranking ad shows for a given search, so overlapping service areas don’t buy you two spots. And your business hours come from your Business Profile, so if your profile says 7 to 7 but the office stops answering at 5, those last two hours are where missed-call charges and ranking damage pile up.

Reviews do the heavy lifting. A company with 300 reviews at 4.8 has a hard-to-beat edge over one with 40 at 4.9. If you need a review process that holds up, start with our Google reviews guide.

Who can run Local Services Ads in Houston, and what Google checks

LSA is open to a list of home service categories, from HVAC, plumbing and electrical to roofing, pest control, cleaning, garage doors, locksmiths and more. To show, you pass Google’s screening, which can include a background check, insurance and a license check where the trade is licensed.

In Texas, that means TDLR licenses for A/C and refrigeration contractors and electrical contractors, and a license from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners for plumbing companies. Roofers have no statewide roofing license, so verification leans on insurance and business checks instead. Make sure the name on your license matches your Google Business Profile exactly; mismatched DBAs are the most common reason we see verification stall.

After migration, your name, address and hours sync from your Business Profile and have to be edited there, per Google’s transition guide. Big name or address changes can trigger a fresh review that pauses the campaign for a day or two, so don’t rebrand the week before a heat wave.

When Local Services Ads win, and when Search campaigns win

Here’s how we decide where the next dollar goes for a home service company in Greater Houston.

LSA is the better first dollar for emergency and repair work, for companies with strong reviews and someone answering every call, and for owners who don’t have time to manage keywords. Search earns its place for high-ticket jobs (AC replacements, whole-home generators, repipes, tankless conversions), for services LSA lumps together, for new service areas where your reviews are thin, and for seasonal pushes like pre-summer AC tune-ups.

A search campaign that complements LSA looks like this: keywords such as “ac replacement the woodlands,” “generator installation kingwood,” “tankless water heater install spring tx” and “repipe house katy.” Negatives like jobs, salary, training, school, license, diy, how to, parts, home depot, lowes, used and free. A landing page per service with prices or price ranges, financing and real photos. That campaign isn’t fighting LSA for the “plumber near me” call. It’s going after the $9,000 job.

Should I run both? How we split a Houston home services budget

Usually, yes. Our default for a company new to paid search is roughly 60% LSA and 40% Search for the first 60 days, then shift monthly toward whichever has the lower cost per booked job. There’s no magic in 60/40. It just gives both enough budget to produce data.

Houston’s calendar should shape the split. AC demand climbs from May through September. Freezes (think February 2021) make January and February spike for plumbers. Hurricane season runs June through November, and after a storm, roofing, tree and generator searches jump overnight. Our hurricane season playbook covers that side.

Two rules for the busy months. Raise LSA budgets only if you can answer the extra calls, because missed calls during business hours can now be billed. And use Search to protect margin: in July, a dollar is better spent on “ac replacement” than on a fourth tune-up call you can’t schedule until August.

Keep service areas honest in both. Leads from outside your areas aren’t credited on LSA, and a Search campaign aimed at all of Harris County from a shop in Magnolia wastes money on drive time.

Putting LSA and Search on one scoreboard

The two systems count differently. LSA counts leads. Search counts whatever conversions you set up. Your bank account counts booked jobs. Pick that last one as the scoreboard.

  • Turn on call recording for LSA and listen to a sample weekly.
  • Use call tracking on your website and Business Profile so Search, organic and profile calls are separated.
  • Tag each lead in your CRM or field service software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber) with its source.
  • Import booked jobs back into Google Ads so Search bidding learns from revenue, not rings.
  • Review cost per booked job by channel every month, not cost per lead.

If the numbers say LSA is beating Search for everything, move the money. If Search is bringing in the replacements that pay the bills, protect it. Either way, decide from booked jobs.

Mistakes we see in Houston LSA and Google Ads accounts

  • Leaving every job type on in LSA. Services you don’t do produce charged leads you can’t get credited.
  • One Target CPA for everything. After migration, split high-ticket and low-ticket work into separate campaigns if the costs differ a lot.
  • Search campaigns on broad “near me” terms only. That’s LSA’s turf. Aim Search at specific, valuable jobs.
  • Profile hours that don’t match reality. Missed calls in those hours can be charged.
  • No negatives. Houston search terms are full of job seekers and DIYers.
  • Judging by leads. A $60 lead that never books costs more than a $140 lead that does.

Frequently asked questions

How much do Local Services Ads cost per lead in Houston?

Google doesn’t publish LSA lead prices, and they vary by trade, job type, area and season, so a Woodlands plumbing lead and a Katy roofing lead can be priced very differently. LSA leads are usually cheaper than search ad leads for the same trade. For comparison, LocaliQ’s 2025 search benchmarks put median search costs per lead around $94 to $228 for home services. Track cost per booked job to see your real number.

Can I run Local Services Ads and Google search ads at the same time?

Yes, and most home service companies that get good results do. They’re separate products with separate placements and billing, even now that LSA lives in Google Ads. The trick is giving each a different job. Let LSA handle broad, trust-driven searches like “plumber near me,” and aim search campaigns at specific high-value jobs, such as AC replacement or repipes, with your own landing pages.

Why am I being charged for missed calls on Local Services Ads?

Since October 1, 2026, Google can charge a missed call during your business hours as a valid lead if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds. Calls outside your hours aren’t covered. If you use a phone menu, the timer starts after the caller presses a key. The fix is coverage: overflow answering, accurate hours in your Business Profile and fast callbacks.

Are Local Services Ads worth it for roofers in Houston?

Often, especially after storms, when roofing searches spike across Houston and Montgomery County. Texas has no statewide roofing license, so Google’s checks lean on insurance and business verification. The risk is lead quality, since storm season brings many insurance-claim tire-kickers and out-of-area calls. Keep your service area tight, record calls and judge LSA on inspections booked and roofs sold, not raw leads.

Do I need a Google Business Profile for Local Services Ads?

In practice, yes. After the move into Google Ads, your LSA business name, address and hours sync from your Google Business Profile and are edited there. A complete, verified profile with accurate hours, real photos and a steady flow of reviews also helps your LSA ranking, because Google weighs rating, review count and responsiveness when it decides which providers to show.
