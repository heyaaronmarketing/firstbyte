Two companies get named over and over. One of them answers the post itself, with a first name and a phone number. That one gets the job.

Marketing a business in Katy and Cypress works a lot like that thread. The area is huge and still growing, but people buy inside small circles: their master-planned community, their school zone, their side of the Grand Parkway, the groups and events they already belong to.

We work out of The Woodlands, on the other side of the Grand Parkway, and the pattern is the same up here, but the details aren’t. How fast Katy and Cypress are growing

Katy sits across three counties: Harris, Fort Bend and Waller. Cypress and the wider Cy-Fair area are unincorporated Harris County. All three counties are growing quickly.

Census Bureau estimates for July 2024 to July 2025, as reported in InnovationMap’s write-up of the Census estimates: Harris County added 48,695 residents and passed 5 million, the largest numeric gain of any U.S. county. Fort Bend added 24,163 to reach 975,191, eighth in the nation by numeric growth. Waller County grew 5.69% to 69,858, second among U.S. counties by percentage. Census QuickFacts for Fort Bend County puts Fort Bend’s growth since April 2020 at 18.3%.

The schools tell the same story. Katy ISD gained 6,927 students from 2021 to 2026, third in the Houston area for total gains, per Community Impact’s look at TEA enrollment data. Cy-Fair ISD is the third-largest district in Texas with 114,697 students, according to Texas Tribune’s Schools Explorer.

The growth isn’t even, though. Cy-Fair ISD was about 91% built out as of 2025, with most remaining growth on the western edge near the Grand Parkway, per Community Impact. In Katy ISD, Katy ISD’s November 2025 demographic study found the northwest quadrant growing fastest, around Elyson, Anniston, Bridgeland and Sunterra. Older parts of both districts are flat or shrinking.

For marketing, that means two different jobs. In the newer areas, households are choosing a dentist, a pediatrician, a lawn service and a favorite Tex-Mex place for the first time, and the first business they find often keeps them for years. In the established areas around Cinco Ranch, Copperfield or Jersey Village, you’re mostly persuading people to switch, which takes reviews and a reason.

Master-planned communities are their own markets

A family in Elyson doesn’t say they live in Katy. They say they live in Elyson. That identity shapes where they look for recommendations, which events they go to and what they type into Google.

The west side is selling homes at a pace few markets in the country match.

What to do with that:

  • Use community names in your copy and on your service pages where you genuinely serve them. “Lawn care in Elyson and Cane Island” matches how people search and how they talk.
  • Bid on community names plus your service in Google Ads (“Bridgeland dentist,” “Towne Lake pool service”). Volumes are small and the intent is strong.
  • Ask about advertising in community newsletters, magazines and event sponsorships through the developer or HOA. Rules vary a lot by community, so ask before you print anything.
  • Time new-homeowner offers to the building pace. A neighborhood that closed 80 homes last quarter needs blinds, fences, pest control and a dentist this quarter.

Commuters: I-10, US-290 and the Grand Parkway

A lot of Katy and Cypress works somewhere else. Katy households commute east on I-10 toward the Energy Corridor, the Galleria and downtown. Cypress households take US-290 into the city or the Grand Parkway north and south between the two. The Grand Parkway (SH 99) connects them directly, and it’s why a Katy business can now realistically pull customers from Bridgeland.

What commute patterns change in your marketing:

  • Target where people live. In Google Ads, use “Presence: people in or regularly in your locations” and draw locations around homes. Weekday daytime traffic to your site might be people at their desks in the Energy Corridor, but they’ll buy near home.
  • Schedule around the drive. In accounts we’ve run, calls to service businesses here cluster early in the morning and after people get home. Make sure someone answers then. Our speed-to-lead guide explains why the first callback wins.
  • Use the drive. Streaming audio and radio reach commuters on 290 and I-10 in the car, and billboards along the Grand Parkway reach the same people every day. Both work better as reminders than as the whole plan.
  • Say which side you serve. “Same-day service north of I-10” or “Cypress side of the Grand Parkway” removes doubt. Nobody in Fulshear wants to wait on a tech coming from Jersey Village at rush hour.

Katy ISD and Cy-Fair ISD calendars run your season

Family-heavy suburbs plan their lives around the school calendar. Restaurants, tutors, camps, orthodontists, pediatric dentists, gyms and home services all see the effects. And Katy ISD and Cy-Fair ISD don’t share the same breaks.

Dates from Katy ISD’s approved 2026-27 calendar and CFISD’s 2026-27 calendar. CFISD also has Thanksgiving week off (Nov. 23-27) and winter break from Dec. 21 to Jan. 1.

Notice that spring breaks are a week apart. A camp, family entertainment center or kids’ dentist serving both districts gets two spring breaks, so run two campaigns with two date ranges and two audiences. The same applies to fall break, which is new for CFISD this year.

A rough calendar we’d use:

  • Three to four weeks before a break: promote camps, family dining deals, appointment openings for kids out of school.
  • During a break: daytime ad scheduling shifts, since parents are home and searching midday.
  • Late July: back-to-school (physicals, haircuts, tutoring, uniforms, carpool-friendly hours). Our back-to-school marketing post has more.
  • Late April into May: graduation, summer camps, pool openings and summer HVAC.

Check each district’s official calendar before you lock in ads, since bad weather days can shift dates. Our Greater Houston seasonal marketing calendar covers the rest of the year.

Local SEO when you serve Katy, Cypress and everything between

Most businesses here serve more than one town. A plumber in Cypress takes calls from Tomball, Jersey Village and Bridgeland. A Katy med spa draws from Fulshear and Brookshire. Google needs to understand all of that.

Start with your Business Profile. Under Google’s service area rules, you can list up to 20 service areas, by city or ZIP code, and the whole area shouldn’t extend beyond about two hours’ drive from your base. A radius isn’t allowed. If customers don’t visit you, hide your address. Pick the areas you really serve. Listing all 20 doesn’t make you rank in all 20; the map pack still leans heavily on how close you are to the searcher.

Then build one real page for each main area on your site. A Katy page should mention Katy things: the ZIPs you cover (77449, 77450, 77493, 77494), the communities, how fast you get there, photos of jobs done there, and reviews from customers there. A Cypress page covers 77429 and 77433, Bridgeland, Towne Lake, Fairfield. If your Katy and Cypress pages are the same text with the town name swapped, Google will treat them as thin, and so will customers.

Ask for reviews that mention where you worked. Don’t script them, and don’t offer anything in exchange, but customers often add their neighborhood on their own if you ask them to describe the job. Facebook community groups: how to show up without getting kicked out

Neighborhood Facebook groups in Katy and Cypress can be the busiest recommendation engines in the area. Every master-planned community has a few, plus groups for moms, newcomers, school zones and buy-sell-trade. They’re also run by volunteer admins who ban businesses for posting ads.

The pattern that works: the owner, or one senior person, joins with a personal profile, follows the rules, and answers questions helpfully even when there’s no sale in it. When a “who do you use?” thread comes up, you respond once, with your name and how to reach you. You don’t copy-paste a flyer. Happy customers tagging you does more than anything you post yourself.

Many groups allow business posts on one day a week, or sell sponsorships. Ask the admin. A few hundred dollars to sponsor a community group for a quarter can beat the same amount on ads, because the audience is exactly your neighborhood. Nextdoor works similarly in some neighborhoods; our Nextdoor advertising review covers whether the paid side pays off.

Paid Facebook and Instagram ads still matter. Target by city or a radius around your service area, use real photos from local jobs, and name the community in the copy. “We just finished three backyards in Towne Lake this month” stops a scroll. Our paid social team runs this kind of campaign.

Asian and Hispanic communities, and the language question

Katy and Cypress are among the most diverse suburbs in Texas. In Fort Bend County, 23.9% of residents are Asian and 25.4% are Hispanic, and 41.3% of people over age five speak a language other than English at home, per Census QuickFacts for Fort Bend County. Cy-Fair ISD’s students are 45.7% Hispanic, per Texas Tribune’s Schools Explorer. In the 2020 Census, Asian residents made up more than 20% of some parts of the Katy area south of the Katy Freeway and west of the Grand Parkway, per Community Impact’s read of the 2020 Census.

Katy Asian Town, on Grand Circle Boulevard in the 77449 ZIP, anchors much of that community’s shopping and dining, and hosts events such as KAT LunarFest, which ran Feb. 14-15, 2026.

Practical advice, without stereotypes:

  • Ask your customers. Your intake form or point-of-sale can ask what language a customer prefers. That beats guessing from a ZIP code, and it tells you whether Spanish, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Hindi, Korean or something else is worth the investment for you.
  • Only advertise in a language you can answer the phone in. A Spanish ad that reaches an English-only voicemail is worse than no ad.
  • Write it, don’t translate it. Native speakers should write ad copy and landing pages. Literal translations read as foreign, and people notice.
  • Show up where the community already gathers. Lunar New Year and Diwali events, cultural associations, community centers, language-specific social media groups and local publications.

Local events and chambers in Katy and Cypress

Events here are big, family-focused and mostly free to attend, which makes them good for awareness and terrible for direct response unless you plan the follow-up.

  • Katy Rice Harvest Festival. Run by the Rotary Club of Katy in Historic Downtown Katy every October, with proceeds going to scholarships and community grants. This year’s festival ran Oct. 10-11, 2026.
  • Light the Towne. The Boardwalk at Towne Lake held its 10th annual free holiday tree lighting, Light the Towne, on Nov. 19, 2026, with booths from local businesses along Candy Cane Lane.
  • Cy-Fest. A free fall festival at Lone Star College-CyFair. Cy-Fest lists the next one for Saturday, Oct. 2, 2027.
  • KAT LunarFest at Katy Asian Town around Lunar New Year.

For a booth or sponsorship to pay off, bring a way to capture names (a drawing, a QR code to a booking page), staff it with people who can answer real questions, and follow up within a week while the event is fresh. Track it with a dedicated URL or offer code so you know what it produced.

Chambers are worth a look too, mostly for referrals and B2B work. The Katy Area Chamber of Commerce and the Cy-Fair Houston Chamber of Commerce both run networking events, ribbon cuttings and member directories, and the Katy Area Economic Development Council works with larger employers. Go to three events before judging. The value is in the regulars, and you meet them on visit three, not visit one.

Frequently asked questions

How do I market a small business in Katy, TX?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile listing the Katy-area cities or ZIPs you serve, plus one real page on your site for Katy with local photos and reviews. Use master-planned community names where you serve them, take part in neighborhood Facebook groups as a person rather than an ad, and time promotions to the Katy ISD calendar. Add Google Ads on service plus community keywords once tracking works.

What is the best way to advertise in Cypress, TX?

For most local businesses, Google search and the map pack come first, since people search when they need something. Then add Facebook and Instagram ads targeted around your service area with real local photos, community group participation in places like Bridgeland and Towne Lake, and event sponsorships such as those at The Boardwalk at Towne Lake. Track every channel so you can cut what doesn't produce customers.

Is Katy, TX still growing?

Yes. Census estimates for July 2024 to July 2025 show Fort Bend County adding 24,163 residents to reach 975,191, Waller County growing 5.69%, second among U.S. counties by percentage, and Harris County adding the most people of any county in the country. Katy ISD gained 6,927 students from 2021 to 2026. Growth is concentrated in northwest Katy communities like Elyson and Sunterra.

How many service areas can I add to Google Business Profile in Katy and Cypress?

Google lets you list up to 20 service areas by city or ZIP code, and the whole area should stay within about two hours of driving from your base. You can’t use a radius. Listing more areas doesn’t guarantee rankings in each, because proximity still matters a lot in the map pack, so pair your profile with a genuinely local page for each main area like Katy and Cypress.

When are Katy ISD and Cy-Fair ISD spring breaks in 2027?

Katy ISD’s spring break is March 8-12, 2027, and Cy-Fair ISD’s is March 15-19, 2027, a week apart. Both districts started the 2026-27 year on Aug. 12, 2026. Businesses that serve families in both districts, like camps, kids’ dentists and family entertainment, should plan separate promotions for each break and check the official calendars for any weather-related changes.
