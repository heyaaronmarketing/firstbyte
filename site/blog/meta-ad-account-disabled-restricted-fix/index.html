Meta Ad Account Disabled or Restricted: How to Get It Back

Meta ad account disabled or restricted? Find what was hit in Business Support Home, request a review the right way, fix payment flags and prevent a repeat.

First week of July, 7:40 a.m. An AC company in Spring has its tune-up campaign queued for the heat wave the forecast has been promising all weekend. The owner opens Ads Manager and gets a red banner instead: “Your ad account has been disabled. We noticed unusual activity.” Nobody on the team changed anything. Leads stop by lunch.

We get some version of that call every few weeks from business owners around The Woodlands and Houston. The panic is understandable, and it pushes people to do the two things that make it worse: firing off a pile of appeals, or opening a brand-new account the same afternoon.

To get a disabled or restricted Meta ad account back, find out exactly which asset was restricted (ad account, Page, personal profile or business portfolio) in Meta Business Support Home, read the stated reason, then fix anything you can fix: clear a failed payment, secure a compromised login, complete identity or business verification. Then request one review from an admin, write it plainly, and wait. Most restrictions that come from payment hiccups, security flags or mistaken policy calls can be reversed. Some can’t, and it helps to know which kind you have before you spend a week on it.

First, figure out what actually got restricted

Meta uses “disabled” and “restricted” for several different things, and the fix depends on which layer got hit. People lose days appealing an ad account when the real problem is the personal profile of the person who runs it.

Meta’s own help center has separate articles for these: Troubleshoot a disabled or restricted account, plus a advertising restrictions help page that covers a Facebook account, Page or Business Manager account with advertising restrictions. Meta’s documentation says the business portfolio’s status affects the ad accounts and assets it owns, and your personal Facebook account’s status affects your personal ad account and your access to some advertising tools, per Meta’s Business Support Home.

Restrictions also come in degrees. Guides that quote Meta’s restriction page list four levels: a daily spend limit or lower payment threshold, loss of some payment features, loss of some advertising features, and losing the ability to advertise at all (see AdsInfra’s restriction guide and Leadsie’s recovery guide). A spend cap is annoying. A full disable is a different conversation.

Where to look: Business Support Home (the old Account Quality)

Meta Business Support Home is the dashboard for all of this, at facebook.com/business-support-home. Older guides, and a few menus, still call it Account Quality. Per Meta’s Business Support Home, it shows the status of your business portfolios, ad accounts, commerce accounts, catalogs and Pages, whether any of them are disabled or restricted, rejected ads, and your feedback score.

Reading the reason: unusual activity, policy, payment or identity

The reason line usually falls into one of four buckets, and each wants a different first move.

“Unusual activity” or “integrity” flags. This is Meta’s systems deciding something about the account looks like fraud, a takeover or automation. A new device or location for an admin, a sudden jump in spend, a payment method that was recently used somewhere Meta didn’t like, or a pile of new users added in one day can all trigger it. Jon Loomer described getting “Certain actions have been restricted due to unusual activity” on his own profile after sharing a link to Meta’s documentation in his group, followed by a driver’s-license check that failed with no reason given. If it can happen to one of the best-known Meta ads educators, it can happen to a roofer in Conroe.

Policy (Advertising Standards). A run of rejected ads, or one ad Meta treats as severe, can restrict the account. Look at the rejected ads list in Business Support Home for the pattern. Common local-business culprits we see: before-and-after weight loss or skin photos, “are you struggling with…” copy that implies a personal attribute, and housing, employment or credit ads run without the right special ad category.

Payment. A declined card, a balance Meta couldn’t collect, or a payment method that was reported. Meta has a dedicated article, Troubleshoot a Disabled Ad Account Due to Payment Failure, and it’s usually the most fixable bucket.

Identity or security. Meta wants an admin to confirm who they are, or thinks a login was compromised. Unrecognized charges or campaigns you didn’t create go to Troubleshoot Unrecognized Activity on Your Ad Account first, before any appeal.

Meta’s article Request a review if you are restricted from advertising is the official version. Here is how the flow works in practice, as also described by Leadsie’s recovery guide:

Only admins can request the review, so if your agency or a former employee was the only admin, that’s problem number one. (Our post on who owns your ad accounts is about Google, but the same rule applies on Meta: the business should own the portfolio, and the agency should be a partner.)

If there’s a free-text box, keep it short and factual. Something like this:

We’re a licensed HVAC contractor in Spring, Texas (business portfolio ID 1234…). Our ad account was disabled on July 2 for unusual activity. On July 1 our office manager logged in from a new laptop and added a second card after our primary card expired. Both cards are in the business’s name. We have turned on two-factor authentication for all admins and removed one former employee. Our ads promote AC repair and maintenance and follow the Advertising Standards. Please review and restore the account.

No anger, no “I spend $5,000 a month,” no threats to leave. The reviewer, human or automated, is checking whether the flag was right. Give them the reason it wasn’t.

After a restriction is lifted, campaigns don’t restart by themselves. Go turn them back on in Ads Manager and check that budgets and schedules came back intact.

Identity verification: get it right the first time

Plenty of reviews now start with a “confirm your identity” step, usually in the mobile app, with a photo of a government ID. Retries can be limited, so treat the first attempt carefully.

  • Use the ID that matches the name on the Facebook profile exactly. If your profile says “Mike” and your license says “Michael,” expect trouble. Fix the profile name first if Meta allows it.
  • Photograph the ID flat, in good light, all four corners visible, no glare from the Texas license’s hologram.
  • Do it from a device and network you normally use. A hotel Wi-Fi in Cabo or a VPN is the wrong place to prove you’re you (that’s our experience, but it’s consistent).
  • Expect silence afterward. Jon Loomer’s check failed and he still had no clear path five days later.

Meta has been testing video selfies for recovering compromised accounts, matched against your profile photos. Per Meta’s newsroom, it launched in the UK, EU and South Korea in March 2025, so don’t count on it for a Houston account yet.

Business verification is a separate thing, done for the portfolio in Meta Business Suite’s Security Center. It confirms the business is real and that you’re authorized to act for it. Meta’s Meta’s Business Help Center (business verification) lists the requirements: a business registered with local authorities, an official business phone number or mailing address, and the ability to receive a code at that number or email (or verify your domain). For a Texas LLC, that means the legal name on your Secretary of State filing, or the assumed name you filed, matching what you type in. Start at Verify Your Business in Meta Business Suite.

Payment-related disables

These are the most common disables we see for small local advertisers, and the most recoverable. A typical sequence: the card on file expires or a bank’s fraud filter blocks a Meta charge, the balance can’t be collected, ads stop, and a few days later the account flips from “payment failed” to “disabled.”

  1. Pay the outstanding balance with a working method, from Billing and payments in Ads Manager or the prompt in Business Support Home.
  2. Call your bank and tell them to stop blocking Meta. Business cards from local banks sometimes flag a burst of ad charges as fraud.
  3. Don’t dispute a legitimate Meta charge with your bank to “get Meta’s attention.” In our experience a chargeback on an active ad account almost always deepens the restriction.
  4. Then request the review, noting the balance is paid.

Meta’s Best Practices for Preventing Payment Failures page covers prevention. Our version: one business credit card in the company’s name as primary, a second as backup, both with the same billing name and address, and someone who gets the billing emails and actually reads them.

New accounts have a related issue. Meta applies a Daily Spending Limit to new ad accounts, Pages and portfolios that rises with time in good standing, per Jon Loomer. A brand-new account that tries to spend $1,500 on day one looks exactly like a stolen card to Meta’s systems. Ramp up.

What an appeal can and can’t fix

Setting expectations saves a lot of grief.

Meta’s enforcement is heavily automated, and the appeal path isn’t always generous. In June 2026 Meta’s Oversight Board said account disablements lack due process and clear notice, and that even Meta Verified subscribers paying for support weren’t getting meaningful help, per TechCrunch. Separately, Search Engine Land’s summary of a Financial Times report described internal guardrails that routed high spenders’ content to human reviewers. Meta called that reporting inaccurate. Either way, a small local advertiser should assume the first look is automated and write the appeal so it’s easy to approve.

How long Meta reviews take

Meta’s review guidance, as quoted by both Leadsie’s recovery guide and AdsInfra’s restriction guide, says most cases are reviewed within 48 hours and some take longer. We’d treat 48 hours as the good case. In accounts we’ve helped with, payment and security cases often clear in a few days, and policy cases can take one to three weeks, especially if Meta asks for more information midway.

Track the request under Your Support Cases in Business Support Home and answer any follow-up there. A second, third and fourth request doesn’t speed anything up, and review requests can be limited. In March 2026, WSMV reported in March 2026 that Meta was rolling out a centralized 24/7 support hub on Facebook and Instagram, which may make the chat-with-support route easier to find. If you spend enough to have a Meta rep, this is the moment to use them.

Don’t sit on it either. Third-party guides citing Meta’s help center put the window to request a review at roughly six months, and prepaid funds on an account that stays disabled can be forfeited. Request the review the same week.

Hacked accounts are a separate track

If you see campaigns you didn’t create, charges you don’t recognize, or admins you’ve never heard of, it’s a takeover, and an appeal comes second. Secure the personal Facebook logins of every admin, remove unknown users and partners from the portfolio, remove payment methods you don’t recognize, then follow Troubleshoot Unrecognized Activity on Your Ad Account. Call your card issuer about the fraudulent charges.

This happens to local businesses constantly. A Houston boutique owner told KPRC Click2Houston in 2021 that she was locked out of her new business page moments after it launched, with her password and recovery email changed, and that Facebook never responded to her. Most of the takeovers we’ve cleaned up started with one admin clicking a fake “copyright violation” or “Page will be disabled” message.

Backup admins and the setup that prevents most of this

Most of the long, ugly recoveries we’ve seen had one thing in common: a single person held every key. Meta’s Meta’s Business Help Center (business portfolio security) recommend keeping two people with full control (ideally no more than 10), removing people who haven’t logged in for 90 days, requiring two-factor authentication, and removing users with personal email domains that aren’t tied to the business.

To require 2FA: in Meta Business Suite, go to Settings, then Business portfolio info, scroll to Business options, and use the Two-factor authentication dropdown to require it for admins or everyone. Only people with full control can change it, and Meta says certain portfolios older than 90 days must have it on, per Meta’s Business Help Center (two-factor authentication requirement).

Creative matters too. Meta changed its penalty system in 2023 for personal profiles to give more warnings before restrictions, but ad accounts still accumulate rejected ads, and the pattern counts. Run anything borderline past someone who reads the Advertising Standards for a living before it goes live. Our guide to Facebook and Instagram ads for local businesses and post on fixing bad lead ad quality show what compliant, working local creative looks like.

When to stop appealing and start fresh

Sometimes the honest answer is that this account is gone. Signs you’re there: the review came back “we’ve reviewed your account and it doesn’t comply,” with no further option; the business portfolio itself is permanently disabled; or the restriction sits on a personal profile that failed identity checks.

Starting fresh legitimately means a new asset under the people and business Meta has no problem with. If an old agency-built ad account was disabled and your own portfolio and profile are clean, creating a new ad account inside your verified portfolio is normal. If the restriction is on you or your business, making a new profile or portfolio to keep advertising is the kind of thing Meta’s Advertising Standards treat as an account integrity problem, and in our experience the new asset gets linked and disabled within days, often taking your Page with it.

If Meta is off the table for a while, don’t go dark. Search ads and your Google Business Profile carry most local lead flow anyway. Our guide to Google Ads suspensions covers the other platform’s version of this mess, and this one covers Business Profile suspensions.

Frequently asked questions

Why was my Meta ad account disabled for unusual activity?

Unusual activity usually means Meta’s systems suspect fraud, a takeover or automation. Common triggers are a login from a new device or location, a sudden spend increase, a new or recently flagged payment method, or many users added at once. Check Business Support Home for the exact reason, secure every admin’s login with two-factor authentication, clear any payment issue, then request one review explaining what changed and why it was legitimate.

How long does a Meta ad account review take?

Meta’s review guidance says most cases are reviewed within 48 hours and some take longer. In accounts we’ve helped with, payment and security cases often clear within a few days, while policy cases can take one to three weeks, especially if Meta asks for more information. Track the case in Business Support Home under your support cases and avoid submitting duplicate requests, since review requests can be limited.

Where is Account Quality in Meta now?

Account Quality has been folded into Meta Business Support Home, found at facebook.com/business-support-home. It shows the status of your business portfolios, ad accounts, Pages, catalogs and commerce accounts, any advertising restrictions, rejected ads and your feedback score. Open the Outstanding tab under recent account issues to see current problems and the actions available, including requesting a review.

Should I create a new ad account if mine is disabled?

Only if the restriction is limited to that ad account and your profile, Page and business portfolio are in good standing. In that case a new ad account in your own verified portfolio is normal. If the restriction is on you or your business, creating new profiles or portfolios to keep advertising is treated as circumvention, and the new assets tend to get linked and disabled, sometimes along with your Page.

How do I prevent my Meta ad account from being restricted?

Keep two trusted people with full control of the business portfolio, require two-factor authentication, remove inactive users and anyone using a personal email that isn’t tied to the business, and complete business verification. Pay with a business card in the company name and keep a backup card on file. Ramp new accounts up gradually, and review borderline creative against Meta’s Advertising Standards before it runs.
