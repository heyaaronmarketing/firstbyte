Tuesday in late July. Your exterior crew in Kingwood started at 7 a.m. to beat the sun, quit the south wall at noon because the siding was too hot to touch, and lost the afternoon to a thunderstorm. Meanwhile you have nine estimates out from last week, and you haven’t heard back from seven of them.

Houston painting contractors don’t usually have a lead problem in spring. What they have is a scheduling problem in summer, a follow-up problem all year and a slow stretch in the winter holidays.

Painting contractor marketing in Houston that works does three things. It wins the Google Maps and Local Services Ads results for the work you want most. It shows real before-and-after photos everywhere a homeowner might look. And it follows up on every estimate until you get a yes or a clear no. Add the hooks this market hands you, like HOA color approvals in The Woodlands and Cinco Ranch and lead-safe certification for older homes inside the Loop, and you can fill the calendar in the months other painters go quiet.

This is written for residential painters doing interior, exterior and cabinet work across Greater Houston.

What Houston’s heat and humidity mean for your marketing calendar

Weather sets the painting calendar here more than any ad budget. Paint makers say the same thing. Sherwin-Williams’ exterior application FAQ advises that the temperature should stay in the suggested range and above the dew point for at least 48 hours after application, to avoid painting in direct sunlight in hot summer months, and warns that moisture and cool temperatures can cause surfactant leaching, mildew and adhesion problems. It also suggests waiting at least a day after heavy rain.

Anyone who has painted on a humid Houston morning knows what that means in practice. Dew sits on the siding until mid-morning, the west wall is too hot by 2 p.m., and an afternoon storm can wash out a day’s work. Exterior capacity is limited from June through September, and hurricane season (June 1 to November 30) adds rain days and the occasional week when nobody’s thinking about paint.

So plan the marketing calendar around crew capacity, not the other way around:

  • January to March: Book exterior work for spring. Run exterior campaigns hard; homeowners decide early here, and spring is the best exterior weather Houston gets.
  • April to May: Exterior demand peaks and HOA approvals stack up. Keep ads on, raise prices before you cut ad spend.
  • June to September: Shift budget to interior and cabinets. Air-conditioned work keeps crews busy, and “interior painters near me” searches stay steady.
  • October to November: Second exterior window. Promote “done before the holidays” interior work and fall exteriors.
  • December: Cabinet refinishing and pre-listing work for spring sellers. Book January exteriors now.

Put your weather policy on your website, too. A short page on “what happens if it rains” or “why we start the south side early” answers the question homeowners are too polite to ask and makes your crew look like professionals.

Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads for painters

The map pack is the first fight. Primary category Painter for most residential shops, with secondary categories added only when they match work you actually do. Under Services, list each line separately: interior painting, exterior painting, cabinet painting, drywall repair, fence and deck staining, wallpaper removal. Each one is a search someone runs.

Photos matter more for painters than nearly any other trade. Upload finished jobs every week, with a mix of wide shots, details (cut lines, trim, cabinet doors) and before-and-afters taken from the same spot. Name the neighborhood in the caption: “Exterior repaint in Cinco Ranch, Sherwin-Williams Duration.” Our map pack guide covers the rest of the profile work.

Local Services Ads are worth a test. Google’s Local Services Ads category list includes a Painter category, and you pay per lead instead of per click. Availability varies by area, so start the sign-up with your address. LSA rewards reviews and fast responses, so if you have 11 reviews, build those first. HOA color approval: the hook most painters ignore

In Houston’s master-planned communities, the homeowner can’t just pick a color and call you. They need approval, and most of them hate the process. That’s an opportunity.

In The Woodlands, The Woodlands Residential Standards list “all roof replacement or house repainting” and exterior color changes among projects requiring prior approval, encourage muted earth tones and prohibit gloss paint; painting brick or masonry needs its own review, per the Township’s standards summary. Applications run through the Township’s online portal, and per the Township’s permitting page, contractors can apply once the owner has registered an account. In Cinco Ranch, a Cinco Ranch II association notice says exterior paint changes need Architectural Control Committee approval before work starts, the committee can take up to 30 business days, and a same-color repaint doesn’t need an application. Bridgeland and other master-planned communities have their own guidelines, so check each association’s documents before promising anything.

Turn that into marketing:

  • Build a page per big community (“Exterior painting in The Woodlands: HOA approval made simple”) explaining the process, the typical timeline, and what you handle.
  • Offer to prepare the application: color names and codes, a photo of the house, sample swatches, the product sheet. Many homeowners would pay for that alone.
  • Keep a running list of colors and combinations you’ve had approved in each village or section. That’s content nobody else in town has.
  • Time your ads to the review calendar. If approvals take weeks, homeowners need to start in February for April work, so your exterior campaigns should start in January.

Don’t claim to guarantee approval, and don’t start work before the letter arrives. A crew painting without approval puts the homeowner in violation, and that story spreads fast on neighborhood Facebook groups.

EPA lead-safe (RRP) certification for pre-1978 homes

Plenty of Houston housing predates 1978: much of the Heights, Montrose, Garden Oaks, Oak Forest, Meyerland and Bellaire, plus older parts of Pasadena and Baytown. Painting those homes falls under the EPA’s Renovation, Repair and Painting rule, which Texas DSHS confirms EPA administers directly in Texas.

The rule applies to paid work that disturbs paint in pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities, unless the work disturbs 6 square feet or less per room inside or 20 square feet or less outside, or the paint has been tested and documented as lead-free, per EPA’s RRP page for contractors. Firms need EPA firm certification, workers need a certified renovator on the job, and homeowners must get the “Renovate Right” pamphlet before work begins. Window replacement is covered regardless of square footage.

The marketing part is in one sentence on EPA’s page: firms can’t advertise or perform covered renovation work without firm certification. If you aren’t certified, don’t run ads for “historic home painting” in the Heights. If you are, say so prominently:

  • Put your EPA firm certification number on your site and older-home landing page.
  • Explain your containment in plain English, with photos of plastic sheeting, HEPA vacuums and cleanup verification.
  • Target pre-1978 neighborhoods with that page and copy. Parents of young children in older homes are specifically worried about lead dust, and most painters never mention it.

Photo-led social: Instagram, Facebook and Nextdoor

Painting is the most visual trade there is. Social media is your portfolio, and in our experience the before-and-after reel is the single best piece of content a painter can make.

What works on Instagram and Facebook:

  • Before-and-after shots from the exact same angle. Tripod or a tape mark on the floor.
  • Short videos: a cabinet door being sprayed, a crisp cut line along a ceiling, a full exterior time-lapse in 20 seconds.
  • The crew lead explaining one thing: why you prime bare wood, why you caulk before the second coat, why you don’t paint siding at noon in July.
  • Color stories. “This Spring homeowner went from builder beige to a deep green” gets shared in neighborhood groups.

For ads, run your best before-and-afters to homeowners within your service area and retarget website visitors with a gallery carousel. Our paid social team handles this, and our Facebook and Instagram ads guide covers the basics.

Nextdoor deserves a mention because neighbors ask for painter recommendations constantly. Claim your business page, ask happy customers to recommend you there, and post finished jobs by neighborhood. Our Nextdoor advertising review covers whether paid ads there make sense.

Estimates and follow-up: where painters lose the most money

Back to those seven quiet estimates. Most homeowners get two or three painting bids, and the one who follows up politely and clearly often wins even when they’re not the cheapest. Most painters send one estimate and wait.

A simple sequence that we’ve seen work:

  1. Same day: Send the written estimate by email and text, with photos of the areas you discussed and a link to three similar finished jobs.
  2. Day 2: A short text: “Any questions on the estimate? Happy to walk through color options.”
  3. Day 5: Something useful: HOA application help, a product comparison, your current start date.
  4. Day 10: A call from the estimator. Ask what’s holding them up.
  5. Day 30: A final check-in, then move them to a quarterly email list.

Write estimates in a way that wins on comparison: itemize prep (pressure wash, scrape, caulk, prime), name the product and sheen, state the number of coats, and include the warranty. A one-line “Paint house exterior: $6,800” loses to a detailed bid at $7,400 more often than painters think.

Speed matters before the estimate too. A homeowner who fills out three forms books the first painter who calls back. Our speed-to-lead guide shows the response times worth aiming for.

Reviews, referrals and repeat work

Painting customers come back. A homeowner who had the exterior done in 2024 needs a kitchen in 2026 and an interior before they sell. Keep a list, and send a short note every six months with a recent project photo.

Ask for a review the day of the final walkthrough, while the house looks perfect. Same link to every customer, no discounts in exchange for reviews, and reply to every one. Reviews that mention the neighborhood, the type of work and the crew lead help most, and they come naturally if the request reminds people what you did. Our complete guide to Google reviews has templates:

“Thanks again for letting us paint your home in Sienna. If you’re happy with how it turned out, a Google review helps other homeowners find us: [link]. If anything needs a touch-up, text me and we’ll be back this week.”

Real estate agents and property managers are worth cultivating for interior repaints before listing and between tenants. One agent in The Woodlands or Katy can send a dozen jobs a year.

Frequently asked questions

How do painting contractors get more customers?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile in the Painter category, weekly photos of finished work and a steady flow of reviews. Add Google search ads split by exterior, interior and cabinet work, and test Local Services Ads once reviews are strong. Post before-and-after photos on Instagram, Facebook and Nextdoor. Then follow up on every estimate for at least ten days, since that is where most painters lose jobs.

What is the best time of year to market exterior painting in Houston?

Start exterior campaigns in January and keep them running through May, because homeowners plan spring work early and HOA approvals can take weeks. Summer heat, humidity and afternoon storms limit exterior work, so shift budget toward interior and cabinet painting from June through September. October and November are a second exterior window, and December is a good time to book January jobs.

Do painters need EPA lead-safe certification in Texas?

Yes, for most paid work that disturbs paint in homes or child-occupied facilities built before 1978. EPA administers the Renovation, Repair and Painting rule directly in Texas. Firms need EPA firm certification and a certified renovator on the job, unless the work is very small or the paint is documented as lead-free. EPA also says uncertified firms cannot advertise covered work, so certification affects your ads too.

Do I need HOA approval to paint my house in The Woodlands?

Generally yes. The Woodlands Residential Standards list house repainting and exterior color changes among projects that need prior approval, encourage muted earth tones and prohibit gloss paint. Applications go through the Township’s online portal, and contractors can apply once the owner registers. Other communities differ; Cinco Ranch II, for example, requires approval for color changes but not a same-color repaint. Check your association’s rules first.

What negative keywords should a painting company use in Google Ads?

Block job seekers (jobs, hiring, painter salary), DIY searches (how to, diy, tutorial, best paint for), retail and product searches (Home Depot, Lowe’s, paint store, paint sprayer), art and hobby terms (canvas, painting classes, portrait, paint and sip), and auto painting if you don’t do it. Then review the search terms report weekly for the first month and keep adding new negatives.
