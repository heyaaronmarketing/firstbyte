Thursday afternoon, somewhere off Kuykendahl. You just finished a driveway, and the stripe between the cleaned half and the dirty half was so clean the homeowner took a picture of it before you did. Two neighbors walked over while you were rolling up the hose. One asked for a card. The other asked if you “do roofs too,” and you said yes, and then realized you’d left your cards in the other truck.

That’s the pressure washing business in a nutshell. The work sells itself to anyone standing on the sidewalk. Getting it in front of people who aren’t standing on the sidewalk is the marketing.

To get more pressure washing customers in Houston and The Woodlands, own the Google Maps results for the services you’re best at (house soft washing, driveways, roofs, commercial flatwork), run search ads on high-intent terms with a hard negative list, and turn every job into before-and-after content plus a door-hanger drop on that street. Then sell maintenance plans so the same customers book you every year. The humidity here does half the selling for you. Mildew comes back.

Below is the setup we’d run for an owner-operator or a small crew working Spring, Conroe, Cypress, Katy, Kingwood or anywhere inside the Beltway.

Why Houston is a good market for washing (and a crowded one)

Houston grows things on houses. North-facing siding in The Woodlands goes green under the pines. Fences turn gray-black. Roofs get those dark streaks that every homeowner assumes are “dirt.”

Those streaks are usually algae. According to the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association, Gloeocapsa magma is the most common species behind roof discoloration, and it’s most widespread in the Gulf States, where it thrives in warm, humid conditions. That’s us. A roof that’s cleaned in March can look streaky again within a few years, and siding on the shady side of a house often needs attention every year.

The catch is that the barrier to entry is a trailer and a Facebook page. Every spring, new operators show up in local groups offering “$99 driveways.” You won’t beat that on price. You beat it by looking like the company that will still exist next year: a real website, a full Google profile, reviews with photos, proof of insurance.

Demand isn’t flat. In accounts we’ve worked for home service companies, calls for exterior cleaning climb from late February into May, sag in the worst of the summer heat, and pick back up in the fall before the holidays, when people want the house to look good for family. Hurricane season (June 1 to November 30) adds a wave of debris and storm cleanup calls after a big storm. Plan budget around that curve, and save some for the fall push most operators ignore.

Roof cleaning: say how you do it, in writing

Roof jobs pay well and scare homeowners, who’ve heard pressure washing ruins shingles. They’re right.

The industry group for shingle makers is blunt. the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says “DO NOT use a power washer or any type of brush or broom to clean algae from the roof surface,” recommends a cleaning solution per the shingle manufacturer’s directions, and says the solution should stay on the roof at least 15 minutes and no more than 20. That’s soft washing.

Put that on your roof cleaning page in plain words. Explain your mix in general terms, how you pre-wet and rinse plants, what happens to gutters and downspouts, and that you carry liability insurance. Link the ARMA page. A homeowner in Kingwood comparing three quotes will pick the one who explained the process over the one who said “we’ll make it look new.”

Also say what you won’t clean, or will only clean after a look. Tile, metal and very old roofs each have their own problems.

HOA mildew letters and master-planned communities

This is a lead source almost nobody markets to on purpose. Large parts of Houston’s suburbs sit inside master-planned communities and HOAs with deed restrictions, and those restrictions usually require homes to be kept up.

The Woodlands is the obvious example. The Woodlands Township’s covenant FAQ says the deed restrictions “require that properties be kept in good order and repair,” and that once a violation has been established, notices go to the owner. The Township even publishes a flyer on algae and mildew warning residents that painting over algae and mildew is a mistake, and that periodic cleaning with a power washer using water only may help prevent the problem.

When a homeowner gets a letter, they have a deadline and a problem, and they search right away. Build for that moment:

  • A landing page titled something like “Got an HOA mildew notice? House washing in The Woodlands, Spring and Cypress.” Say how fast you can usually get out there, and offer to send a dated after photo they can forward to the HOA.
  • A search ad group for “HOA violation house washing,” “HOA letter mildew,” and “deed restriction violation pressure washing.” Low volume, very high intent.
  • Neighborhood pricing. When one house on a street gets a notice, its neighbors often got one too. Offer a few dollars off per house when three or more homes on the same street book together, and print it on your door hangers.

Don’t pretend to speak for any HOA, and keep community logos out of your ads.

Google Ads for pressure washing: keywords, negatives and settings

Search ads suit this trade. Someone typing “house washing Conroe” wants a house washed. Someone typing “pressure washer” wants to buy a machine, and that’s where most budgets leak.

Build one campaign with an ad group per service from the table above, each pointing to its own page. Use phrase and exact match for the core terms (“house washing,” “soft wash,” “roof cleaning,” “driveway cleaning,” “pressure washing service”) with town names and “near me.” Add the commercial terms in a separate campaign so the residential budget doesn’t get eaten by one restaurant chain manager searching at 2 a.m.

A few settings matter more than the rest:

  • Location: under Campaign settings, then Locations, then Location options, choose “Presence: people in or regularly in your included locations.” The default also shows ads to people merely interested in your area.
  • Radius: draw it around where you actually want to drive a trailer. From The Woodlands, maybe Spring, Conroe and Tomball but not Pearland.
  • Call assets scheduled for the hours someone answers, and calls counted as conversions. Our tracking guide shows how.
  • Ad copy that answers the obvious objections: “Soft wash for roofs and siding. Plants pre-wet and rinsed. Insured. Starting at [your price] for most driveways.”

Click costs vary a lot by town and season. Our Houston CPC benchmarks give a feel for home service ranges. For a solo operator, we’d start between $800 and $1,500 a month on search alone (illustrative, not a quote) and judge it on booked jobs after six weeks, not on clicks.

Local Services Ads: what’s available for washing right now

Owners ask us about the “Google Guaranteed” ads at the top of the page all the time. For this trade the answer is mostly no, for now.

When we checked Google’s Local Services Ads category list in November 2026, there was no pressure washing, power washing or soft washing category. The nearest categories are Window cleaning services and House cleaning services. Sterling Sky’s running tracker of LSA category changes, which logs every addition, showed no washing category added through its November 3, 2026 update.

If you really do run a window cleaning line, that category may be worth applying for, and you can check eligibility for your address in the sign-up. Don’t join a category to show up for work you don’t do; Google screens businesses, and you’ll waste money on leads you can’t serve. Watch the list, because Google has been adding categories steadily. Before-and-after content that actually gets used

Every operator has a phone full of before-and-after shots. Most never get posted, and the ones that do are shot from different spots in different light. Make the comparison easy to read.

Where to put it:

  • Google Business Profile. Upload a few fresh photos every week, tagged by service in the caption, and note the town (“House soft wash, Alden Bridge”). Fresh, real photos are one of the easier ways to look active next to competitors.
  • Reels and TikTok. The surface cleaner stripe is one of the most watchable things on the internet. Fifteen seconds, no music needed, the town in the caption.
  • Meta ads. A before-after carousel aimed at homeowners in a tight radius, with a link to a quote form. Our paid social team runs these for service businesses, and the instant forms need qualifying questions or you’ll drown in tire-kickers (see fixing bad Facebook leads).
  • Your quote follow-up. Send three before-afters from the same neighborhood with every estimate.

Ask before you post someone’s house. Blur the house number anyway.

Nextdoor and door hangers: the street where you just worked

The best time to market to a street is the hour after you finish a job on it.

Door hangers. Hit five houses on each side and the ten across the street before you leave. Print a photo of a local job, your Google rating, a starting price, a QR code to the quote form, and a “neighbor rate” that expires in 14 days. Hang them on doors, not mailboxes: per the Postal Service, unstamped flyers placed in mailboxes are illegal under federal law, and only postage-paid mail delivered by authorized carriers belongs there. Also check HOA and city rules on solicitation; some neighborhoods restrict it.

Nextdoor. Claim your free business page, ask happy customers to recommend you there, and answer “anyone know a good pressure washer?” threads as the business, not with a fake neighbor account. Paid Nextdoor ads can work for a hyperlocal service like this one. We weigh the pros and cons in is Nextdoor advertising worth it.

Give door hangers their own QR code and promo code so you know whether they pay for the printing.

Recurring contracts: the part that makes the business sellable

One-time driveway jobs pay the bills. Recurring work builds a company that’s worth something if you ever sell it, and it smooths out the slow summer.

Residential plans. Offer an annual house wash plus driveway at a set price, booked in the same month every year, with a small discount for signing up at the first job. Put a reminder in your CRM, and text the customer two weeks before. Get marketing-text consent separately from job updates (our Texas SMS rules post covers why).

Commercial accounts. Restaurants (dumpster pads, drive-thru lanes, patios), gas station canopies and aprons, apartment breezeways, retail center sidewalks, medical office entries along Research Forest and I-45. Property managers buy reliability: a set schedule, after-hours work, photos after every visit, one invoice a month, and a certificate of insurance ready the day they ask. Build a one-page commercial capability sheet and a commercial page on the site, then call 30 property managers within your radius. Our commercial cleaning marketing post goes deeper on selling to facility managers.

Fleet washing. HVAC and plumbing companies, landscapers, delivery operations and oilfield service yards along the Hardy Toll Road and FM 1960 all run fleets that look bad fast. Fleet managers care about scheduling around routes and about what happens to the water.

Wastewater rules for commercial and fleet washing

That last point is a real selling point and a real liability. Commercial buyers increasingly ask how you handle runoff, and the answer “it goes in the drain” can lose you the account or worse.

The rules are mostly local, enforced under the state Water Code. Harris County’s best-practices brochure for power washers (hosted by the City of Hedwig Village) says washwater must be completely collected and may not go to the storm drain, and lists penalties of up to $25,000 per day per violation under Texas Water Code Section 7.102 and up to $1,000 per day under Harris County’s storm water regulations. The City of Sugar Land’s pressure washing page says discharge of pressure washing wastewater to the storm drain is prohibited and that it must be captured and hauled to a disposal facility or sent to the sanitary sewer. Inside Houston, Houston’s storm sewer (MS4) page says illicit discharges are handled by the Health Department with police and Public Works, and lists 311 and a hotline (713-640-4399) for reports.

At the state level, the closest TCEQ guidance is TCEQ’s car wash guide (RG-434), revised June 2026. It says to get approval from your local wastewater treatment facility before discharging wash water to its sewer, and points other outdoor washing operations to TCEQ’s Small Business and Local Government Assistance section or local authorities. That’s the right call to make before you sign a fleet contract.

How to turn it into marketing: describe your recovery setup (drain covers, berms, a vacuum recovery system, where the water goes) on your commercial page, and put a short “environmental practices” paragraph in every commercial proposal. Property managers forward that to their corporate office. We’re marketers, not environmental lawyers, so confirm the details for each city and county you work in.

Pricing per square foot as a marketing tool

The most common call you get starts with “how much to wash a house?” Answering it online saves you hours of drive-by estimates and screens out the bargain hunters before they waste your Saturday.

The square footage line is a nice local trick. Harris County and Montgomery County appraisal district records list a home’s living area, so a homeowner can look it up in a minute. An instant-quote form that asks for the address and square footage lets you price most houses without a visit, then confirm on arrival.

We aren’t publishing market rates here; there’s no reliable public source for Houston pricing, and it moves with chemical and fuel costs. If you price above the guys on Facebook, the page should say why.

Reviews, follow-up and a first 90 days

Ask for the review the moment the customer sees the result. Text the link before you leave the driveway, with one line: “Thanks for having us out. If you’re happy with how it turned out, a quick Google review helps a small business a lot.” No discount for reviews, no only-asking-the-happy-ones. Our complete guide to Google reviews has the rules and templates.

Then answer leads fast. A homeowner who fills out three quote forms on a Saturday morning books whoever calls back first, which is why we push owners to reply within minutes (more in speed to lead).

A first 90 days for a small operator:

  • Days 1 to 30. Rebuild the Google profile (services, photos, hours, service area). Build service pages and one HOA-notice page. Set up call tracking and a quote form that asks for address and square footage. Start the review text on every job.
  • Days 31 to 60. Launch search ads on house washing, driveway and roof cleaning with the full negative list. Start door hangers on every job street with a tracked QR code. Post before-afters weekly.
  • Days 61 to 90. Launch the annual plan offer. Build the commercial page and capability sheet, and start calling property managers. Read the search terms report weekly and cut what isn’t booking.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more pressure washing customers in Houston?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile that lists each service separately, with fresh before-and-after photos and steady reviews. Build a page for each service and your main towns. Run search ads on terms like house washing, roof cleaning and driveway cleaning with negatives for equipment and DIY searches. Hang door hangers on every street you work, and sell annual plans so customers return each year.

Is there a Google Local Services Ads category for pressure washing?

Not as of November 2026. Google’s Local Services Ads category list has no pressure washing, power washing or soft washing category. The closest categories are window cleaning services and house cleaning services. If window cleaning is a real part of your business, check eligibility for that category in the sign-up. Otherwise, rely on regular search ads and the map pack until Google adds a washing category.

What negative keywords should a pressure washing company use?

Block equipment and DIY searches first: pressure washer for sale, rental, Home Depot, Harbor Freight, psi, parts, repair, surface cleaner, soap and how to. Add job seekers (jobs, hiring, salary) and car wash terms unless you do fleet work. Then read the search terms report weekly for the first month and keep adding anything that isn’t a customer looking to hire.

Can I drain pressure washing water into the storm drain in Harris County?

No. Harris County’s best-practices guidance for power washers says washwater must be collected and may not go to the storm drain, and Sugar Land says the same. Wash water generally has to be captured and hauled for disposal or sent to the sanitary sewer with the utility’s approval. Penalties can be steep, so confirm the rules with each city and county you work in.

How do pressure washers get HOA and commercial contracts in The Woodlands?

For HOA-driven work, build a page and ad group for homeowners who got a mildew or maintenance notice, and offer street pricing when several neighbors book together. For commercial work, create a commercial page and one-page capability sheet covering scheduling, insurance and wastewater recovery, then call property managers for restaurants, retail centers, offices and apartments near your base.
