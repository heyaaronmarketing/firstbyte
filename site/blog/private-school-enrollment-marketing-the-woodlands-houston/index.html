The Saturday open house went well. Forty-one families signed the iPad in the front hall, the eighth graders gave tours without wandering off, and the coffee held out. On Monday the admissions director exports the sign-ins and starts sorting. Twelve already had an inquiry on file. Nine of the emails bounce or are clearly typed by a toddler. Nobody can say which ones came from the Instagram ads, the mailer that hit Sterling Ridge, or a friend at pickup.

Application deadlines at a lot of Houston schools land between mid-December and the end of January. That leaves about eight weeks to turn those 41 families into applications.

Private school enrollment marketing in The Woodlands and Houston comes down to getting in front of parents during the few months they’re actually comparing schools, making the tour easy to book, and then following up like a person instead of an autoresponder. Everything else (ads, Niche, reviews, the new Education Freedom Accounts) feeds that funnel. This guide is for heads of school, admissions directors and marketing leads at independent, faith-based and Montessori schools from Houston up through Spring, The Woodlands, Conroe and Magnolia.

The admissions funnel: inquiry, tour, application, enrollment

Every school has the same four stages, whatever the software calls them. Each one leaks for a different reason.

Inquiry. A form fill, a call, a Niche inquiry or an open house RSVP. The leak here is speed. A parent who fills out your inquiry form at 9:40 p.m. on a Tuesday has usually filled out two others that night. The school that replies by morning with a real name and a tour link tends to get the visit. We cover response times in more detail in our speed-to-lead guide.

Tour. The biggest drop-off in most funnels we see is from inquiry to tour. Fix it by making tours bookable online, offering a weekday morning and at least one evening or Saturday slot, and sending a reminder text the day before.

Application. Families who tour and don’t apply are usually stuck on cost, a spouse who hasn’t visited, or doubt about getting in. Answer those in the follow-up.

Enrollment. An acceptance isn’t a deposit. Between decision letters in March and contract deadlines a few weeks later, families are weighing your offer against other schools, aid packages and the public school down the street. This is where a call from the head of school or a current parent from the same neighborhood earns its keep.

Track all four stages by source, every month. Counting only inquiries keeps you funding channels whose families never visit.

The Houston and The Woodlands admissions calendar

Plenty of Houston private schools run on a fixed cycle. The Post Oak School’s admissions page lists a first-round application deadline of December 18, 2026, supporting documents due January 15, decisions on March 5, 2027 and contracts due March 24. In The Woodlands, The John Cooper School’s admission FAQ says its deadline is in January and decisions typically go out the first Friday in March, though it accepts applications after the deadline.

Parish schools, Montessori programs and smaller Christian schools in Spring, Tomball and Conroe often run rolling admissions. Your families still live on the selective schools’ calendar, because many applied there too.

That shape drives the media plan. Here’s how we’d set it for a school with a January deadline:

  • August to September. Re-enrollment communication starts for current families, and the first open house dates go live on the website, Google Business Profile and Niche. Light awareness ads.
  • October to mid-January. The main push. Search ads on full budget, Meta ads promoting specific open house dates, and retargeting of everyone who visited the admissions pages.
  • February to March. Ads shift to late applicants and rolling-admission grades. Families who were waitlisted elsewhere start looking again in March, which is a quiet opportunity for schools with seats.
  • April to July. Relocation season. Families transferring to Houston search all summer; keep a smaller campaign running for them.

Pre-K and kindergarten families start earlier than you’d expect, often the fall before. If your youngest grades are the ones you need to fill, start those campaigns in September.

Texas Education Freedom Accounts: what changed for private schools

Senate Bill 2, passed in 2025, created Texas Education Freedom Accounts (TEFA), the state’s education savings account program. Here’s what it means for a school’s marketing, kept to what the law and the Comptroller have actually said.

The money. Under the enrolled text of Senate Bill 2, an account receives 85% of the statewide average state and local funding per student, up to $30,000 for a child with a disability, and up to $2,000 for a homeschooled student. Program spending for the biennium starting September 1, 2025 was capped at $1 billion. For 2026-27 that worked out to $10,474 for a private school student, per the Comptroller’s December 2025 release. Families receive funds in installments; the Texas Private School Association lists October 1, 2026 and February 1, 2027 as the second and third payments.

Which schools qualify. Section 29.358 requires a private school to show accreditation by an organization recognized by the Texas Private School Accreditation Commission or the Texas Education Agency, annual administration of a nationally norm-referenced test (or the state test) in grades 3 through 12, and continuous operation of a campus for at least the two school years before it applies. A new microschool in Magnolia that opened last fall isn’t eligible yet, whatever its quality.

What you can charge. Section 29.365 says a participating school can’t charge a TEFA student more than its standard amount for the same service. There’s no separate tuition cap, and the Texas Private School Association notes that schools can bill families for the gap between the account and tuition or cover it with aid. Section 29.368 protects your admissions standards and religious character from state interference.

Who got funded in year one. This is the part that should change your ad copy. More than 274,000 students applied, and when funding ran out, awards went by priority tier, per Community Impact’s breakdown of the first-year numbers.

So the typical family touring a school off Research Forest Drive, earning well above 200% of the poverty line, didn’t get an account in year one. Ads that shout “Use your TEFA funds here!” to that audience set up a disappointment that lands on your admissions office. What we’d do instead:

  • Build one plain TEFA page on your site: whether you participate, your accreditor, what the account covers against your tuition, how the gap is billed, and a link to the official program. Update it when 2027-28 details come out. The Texas Private School Association expects applications to open in late winter or spring 2027.
  • Target TEFA messaging where it fits. Schools serving families in Tier 1 and Tier 2 (including many parish schools and schools with strong learning-support programs) have a real story. Say it in English and Spanish; our Spanish-language marketing guide has the details.
  • Never promise an award. The Comptroller decides eligibility and priority, not you.
  • Remember funded families don’t reapply each year; they file an intent to continue. Your re-enrollment packet should remind them.

How parents in Houston search for a private school

Parents search in a few distinct ways, and each one needs a different landing page.

  • By place. “Private schools in The Woodlands,” “private schools Spring TX,” “Catholic school Kingwood,” “private school near Grand Parkway and 249.” These are the bulk of searches and they pull heavily from Google Maps.
  • By type or philosophy. “Montessori school Conroe,” “classical Christian school Tomball,” “IB school Houston,” “school for dyslexia Houston.” Low volume, high intent.
  • By money. “Private school tuition Houston,” “private schools that accept TEFA,” “private school financial aid Texas.”
  • By name. Your school’s name, and your competitors’. These searches mean a family is already comparing.

Parents read a lot before they call. a GreatSchools survey of more than 1,000 K-12 parents found 70% turn to official school websites and third-party sites like GreatSchools. In our experience one parent does the early research on a phone, and the second shows up at the tour asking about cost and outcomes.

Google Ads for private school enrollment

Search ads are the closest thing to a sure bet in school marketing, because the parent has told you what they want. A practical setup for a K-12 school in The Woodlands:

  • Area campaign. “Private school the woodlands,” “private schools near me,” “private school spring tx,” “best private schools the woodlands.” Land on an admissions overview with dates, tuition range and a tour button.
  • Division campaigns. Separate ad groups for early childhood, lower, middle and upper school, each with its own page. “Private preschool Woodlands” and “private high school Houston” are different parents.
  • Program campaigns. Only for what you truly offer: Montessori, IB, learning differences, a STEM program, a faith tradition.
  • Brand. Cheap, and it keeps a competitor or a directory from sitting above your own name during application season.

Negatives to add on day one: jobs, careers, teacher, salary, employment, “public school,” ISD, charter, “free,” online, virtual (unless you run a virtual program), homeschool (unless you serve homeschoolers), college, university, and “calendar” searches from current families looking for the holiday schedule.

Set location to people in or regularly in your area, rather than people merely interested in it (see Google Ads location options). Otherwise you pay for clicks from a grandparent in Ohio. A radius of 10 to 15 miles around campus usually covers a Woodlands school’s real draw, wider for boarding or specialty programs.

Sample ad for October:

Headline 1: Private School in The Woodlands
Headline 2: Open House Sat., Nov. 14
Headline 3: PK3-12th Grade, Small Classes
Description: Tour campus, meet teachers and see real classrooms. Applications for 2027-28 due Jan. 15. Book your visit online.

Budget, honestly: in local accounts we’ve run, a few thousand dollars a month from October through January is enough to learn what works for one campus. Measure it by cost per tour and cost per application, never by clicks. Our Houston CPC benchmarks give a feel for click costs, and our search marketing team builds these campaigns.

Facebook and Instagram: reaching parents with fewer targeting tools

Meta reaches parents who aren’t searching yet, like the family in Creekside Park that’s mildly unhappy with middle school. It’s also gotten blunter to target.

Meta has been removing and consolidating detailed targeting for years, and the latest round took effect January 15, 2026, per Social Media Today. Parent-based options still show up under Demographics in many accounts, but check Ads Manager before you plan around them. Separately, since February 2023 advertisers can target teens only by age and location, with gender and engagement-based targeting removed, per PetaPixel’s report on the change. If your upper school wants to reach students directly, your creative has to do the work.

Meta also won’t approve ads that assert or imply personal attributes about the viewer. That trips up schools more than you’d think.

What we see working for schools:

  • Event ads for a specific open house or a “coffee and campus tour” morning, with date, time and a link to RSVP.
  • Short phone videos: a teacher explaining a project, a lower school morning meeting, the robotics team in the hallway. Polished brand videos rarely beat these.
  • Lead forms with two or three questions (grade for 2027-28, current school, best time to call). A form with zero questions gets you names, not families. Our post on fixing low-quality Facebook leads covers the settings.
  • Retargeting everyone who viewed the tuition or admissions pages, with the next open house date.

“Kindergarten tours in Spring, Wednesdays at 9” finds kindergarten parents in Spring without any interest targeting at all.

Virtual tours and the pages that do the selling

A virtual tour won’t replace a campus visit, but it gets the second parent and the out-of-state relocator to agree to one. A three-minute walk-through led by a student, shot on a good phone, beats a ten-minute drone production.

The page parents want most and schools resist most is tuition. Publish it. Families who can’t find a number assume it’s higher than it is, or they assume you’re hiding something. Put tuition by division, fees, what’s included, payment plans, and a clear line on financial aid right next to it. If you participate in TEFA, show the math on the same page.

The rest of the admissions section should answer the questions the admissions office hears on every call: key dates for this cycle, what the application requires by grade, testing, class sizes, before- and after-care, bus routes or carpool from Spring and Conroe, uniforms and lunch. Each division page should link to booking a tour. Our team builds these under web design and SEO.

For Google reviews, the moments to ask are when a family is happiest: after the first-week welcome, after a strong parent-teacher conference, after a big event. A simple text from the division head works:

“Hi Jen, it was great seeing Ava at the science fair last night. If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps other families in The Woodlands find us: [link]. Thank you!”

Don’t offer a dress-down day, raffle entry or tuition credit for reviews. Google’s prohibited content policy prohibits incentives for reviews. Reply to every review, briefly, and never discuss a specific student’s situation in a public reply. The complete guide to Google reviews covers requests and replies in depth.

Re-enrollment is your cheapest enrollment

A family that leaves after fifth grade costs you seven years of tuition. Re-enrollment contracts usually go out in January or February, right when admissions is busiest, so plan it like a campaign:

  • Know your attrition by grade. Transition years (PK to K, 5th to 6th, 8th to 9th) are where families reconsider. If your sixth-grade class shrinks every year, that’s a middle school marketing problem aimed at your own fifth-grade parents.
  • Run a “next year in middle school” night for current families before contracts go out.
  • Communicate well all year. The same GreatSchools survey found 90% of parents who would recommend their school are satisfied with its communication, and parents unlikely to recommend their school were far more likely to say they’d missed a deadline or opportunity because of unclear communication.
  • Call every family that hasn’t returned a contract by the deadline. Admissions or the division head, not an email.

Financial aid and tuition messaging

Cost is the most common reason a family that loved the tour doesn’t apply. Say plainly that aid exists and how it works. Many Houston schools use a third-party platform for need-based aid; The John Cooper School, for example, uses Clarity, per its admission FAQ. Name your platform, give the aid deadline (usually close to the application deadline), and explain that applying for aid doesn’t affect admission decisions if that’s your policy.

If you publish an aid statistic, keep it current and be able to show your work. For TEFA-participating schools, a simple example on the tuition page (tuition minus the account amount equals the balance before aid) answers the math parents are doing in their heads.

What to track, and a first 90 days

Your enrollment system already holds most of this data; the work is connecting it to sources. Give each channel a tracked phone number, tag every form with its source, and log open house sign-ins against existing inquiries. Our guide to tracking forms and calls in GA4 and Google Ads walks through the setup.

The numbers worth a monthly look: inquiries by source, tour rate, application rate, acceptance yield, re-enrollment rate by grade, and marketing cost per enrolled student by channel. After one full cycle you’ll know which channels bring families who enroll and stay, which is the only answer that should move next year’s budget.

Frequently asked questions

How do private schools in The Woodlands and Houston increase enrollment?

Start with the funnel: reply to inquiries within hours, make tours bookable online, and follow up after every tour with answers about cost and admissions odds. Then fund the channels that bring tours, which for most schools means Google search ads during application season, open house promotion on Facebook and Instagram, a strong Google Business Profile, and claimed Niche and GreatSchools profiles. Protect re-enrollment, especially in transition grades.

When should a Houston private school start advertising for next year?

For schools with December or January deadlines, the main push runs from October through mid-January, with open house dates published by September. Pre-K and kindergarten families often start the fall before, so those campaigns can start in September. Keep a smaller campaign running from April through July for families relocating to Houston for work, who often decide quickly and enroll for August.

Can private schools advertise that they accept Texas Education Freedom Accounts?

Only if the school is approved to participate. Under SB 2, a school needs accreditation by an organization recognized by TEPSAC or TEA, annual norm-referenced testing in grades 3 to 12, and two school years of continuous operation. In 2026-27 the account was $10,474 for private school students, and awards went first to students with disabilities and lower-income families, so never promise a family an award.

Can I target parents with Facebook ads for a private school?

Partly. Meta has removed many detailed targeting options, most recently on January 15, 2026, and parent options vary by account. Teens can only be targeted by age and location. Meta also rejects ads that imply personal attributes about the viewer. Location radius plus specific creative, such as a kindergarten open house date in Spring or The Woodlands, usually finds the right parents.

Should a private school pay for a Niche Premium Profile?

It can be worth testing, especially for upper schools where families compare many options. Niche's Premium Profile adds your own story, photos, video, events and inquiry delivery into systems like Finalsite, Ravenna, Blackbaud and FACTS. Claim the free GreatSchools profile first, then run Niche for a full admissions cycle and judge it by tours and applications rather than profile views.
