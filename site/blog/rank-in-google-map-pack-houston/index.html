How to Rank in the Google Map Pack Across Greater Houston and The Woodlands

How to rank in the Google Map Pack in Houston and The Woodlands: what Google weighs, why you vanish 5 miles away, and a fix list for categories and reviews.

Search “AC repair” standing on Market Street in The Woodlands, then drive 25 minutes to Kingwood and search it again. Different three businesses. Search it from Hughes Landing and from Conroe’s downtown square and you’ll probably get a third set.

That’s the first thing to understand about the map pack in Houston. There isn’t one ranking. There are thousands of them, one for every spot someone searches from, and your job is to win as many of those spots as your location and profile allow.

This guide is set up as a diagnosis: what Google says it weighs, what practitioners say matters most in 2026, the symptoms we see when a Houston business can’t get in, and the fixes in the order we’d do them.

How do you rank in the Google Map Pack?

Short answer: pick the most accurate primary category, keep your Business Profile complete and consistent with your website, earn a steady stream of recent reviews, and build a service page for each thing you sell. You’ll rank best near your address. Ranking farther out takes more reviews, stronger pages and local links.

Google is unusually direct about this. Per Google’s own help page on local ranking, local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence, and “there’s no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google.” Ads can appear above or inside the map results, but they don’t change where your organic listing ranks.

The chart above comes from Whitespark’s 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, where 47 local search experts scored 187 factors. Look at the top four: your primary category, how close you are to the searcher, keywords in your business name, and whether your address is in the city being searched. Three of those are mostly fixed by decisions you made when you signed a lease.

That’s not a reason to give up. It’s a reason to put effort into the factors you control and to be realistic about the ones you don’t.

Relevance: tell Google exactly what you do

Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. The biggest lever is your primary category, and it’s also the most common mistake we find.

A family dental practice in Sugar Land categorized as “Dental clinic” when “Dentist” is the category its competitors use. An HVAC company listed as “Contractor.” A med spa on “Beauty salon.” Each one is fighting with one hand tied. Search your main keyword, click into the three businesses in the pack, and see which primary category they use (free browser extensions will show you). Match the one that describes your core business. Then add secondary categories for real services, keeping in mind that Google’s Business Profile guidelines ask you to “use as few categories as possible.”

Beyond categories, relevance comes from:

  • Services and products in the profile. List each one with a short description.
  • Your website. The page your profile links to should name the service and the area in its title and headings. A plumber whose homepage title is “Welcome!” is giving Google nothing to work with. Our local landing pages guide shows how to build service and area pages that support the profile.
  • Review text. When customers mention “water heater” and “Cypress” in reviews, that’s relevance Google can read.

About keywords in your business name: they do help, which is exactly why so many fake listings are named “Best Plumber Houston TX 24/7.” Google’s guidelines say your name should reflect your real-world name with no taglines. If your legal and signage name is “Spring Garage Door Repair,” great. Adding keywords to a name that isn’t yours is a fast route to a suspension, and we’ve watched it cost businesses months.

Distance: why you disappear five miles away

This is the factor owners argue with most, and the one they can change least.

Google measures distance from the searcher (or the place named in the search) to your business address. Service-area businesses that hide their address are still ranked from that hidden address. Listing 20 towns in your service area tells Google where you work. It doesn’t move your pin.

The map shows the pattern we typically see for a single-location business near the center of The Woodlands. Strong in The Woodlands, Shenandoah and much of Spring. Patchy in Conroe and Tomball. Rarely in the pack in Katy, Pearland or Kingwood without a much larger review count than the local competitors there.

What you can do about distance:

  • Win close-in first. Make sure you own the 3-5 mile radius around your address before worrying about the far edge of the metro.
  • Rank organically farther out. The regular results under the map pack aren’t tied to your pin the same way. A well-built “AC repair in Katy” page can rank there even when your profile can’t.
  • Use ads for the far towns. Search ads and Local Services Ads cover the places your pin won’t reach. See Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads.
  • Open a real second location only if you’d actually staff it. Virtual offices and coworking desks you never use aren’t eligible, per Google’s guidelines, and they get caught.

One Houston-specific wrinkle: mailing addresses. Plenty of businesses in The Woodlands have Spring or Conroe mailing addresses, and that affects which “city” Google thinks you’re in. We wrote up how The Woodlands, Spring and Conroe addresses affect local SEO. If you’re moving, read how to change your address without a suspension first.

Prominence: reviews, links and real-world reputation

Prominence is Google’s read on how well known and trusted you are. Google’s help page lists reviews, links and articles about your business, and notes that your standing in regular web results counts too.

For most local businesses, reviews are the fastest prominence lever you control. Whitespark’s survey put high ratings at No. 6, the number of Google reviews with text at No. 9, and recency at No. 11 among map pack factors. Our complete guide to Google reviews has the system and scripts.

Links and mentions are slower but compound. In Houston, the realistic ones are your chamber of commerce membership pages (The Woodlands Area Chamber, Greater Conroe, Katy Area and the rest), local sponsorships, supplier and manufacturer dealer locators, and coverage in Community Impact or the Houston Chronicle. Whitespark’s 2026 survey also noted that for AI search visibility, citations and mentions mattered more than ever. Our posts on getting press coverage in Houston and local sponsorships cover the practical side.

Do photos, posts and the other profile extras help you rank?

Less than the people selling them say, and more than zero.

Photos don’t appear anywhere near the top of the ranking factor lists. They matter for the second half of the job, which is getting the click once you’re in the pack. A profile with real photos of your trucks in a Magnolia driveway, your front desk and your team gets chosen over one with three stock images and a logo. Add a few new ones each month. Skip the photo geotagging tricks; we’ve never seen them move a ranking.

Google posts (offers, updates, events) are similar. They won’t move you from seventh to second, but a current post with a seasonal offer or a hurricane-prep reminder makes the profile look alive, and an expired one from 2023 makes it look abandoned. Once or twice a month is plenty.

Two extras do matter more. Products and services: fill them in, because they’re relevance Google can read directly. And the profile description: with Q&A gone, it’s one of the main sources the Ask button pulls from, so write it plainly with what you do, where and for whom.

Shared addresses are worth a warning. Two businesses in one suite at a Research Forest office park, or a husband-and-wife pair of companies at one house in Spring, can trigger Google’s filter so only one shows. If that’s you, use clearly separate suite numbers, phone numbers and signage, and expect Google to ask for proof.

What changed on Google Business Profiles in 2025 and 2026

Some of the older advice you’ll find online is now out of date.

  • Q&A is gone from public view. Google removed the public Q&A section and replaced it with an AI-powered “Ask” button that answers from your business information and relevant reviews, per Search Engine Roundtable in December 2025. Your profile description, services and reviews now do the work Q&A used to.
  • Video verification is common. New profiles and edited ones often get asked for a short video of signage, the location and proof of management. Our video verification guide walks through what passes.
  • Spam fighting got more aggressive. Google removed over 13 million fake profiles and blocked 79 million inaccurate edits in 2025, per Search Engine Roundtable’s write-up of Google’s 2025 Maps numbers. Good news for honest businesses, bad news for anyone with a virtual office listing.
  • Reviews show up in more places. Per BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 45% of consumers now use AI tools as a review source and 27% use Apple Maps. Claim Apple Business Connect and Bing Places too; they take an hour.

None of this changes the three core factors. It does mean AI Overviews and chat answers increasingly decide which businesses get mentioned before anyone sees the map. We cover that in AI Overviews and local search in Houston.

How to track your map pack ranking (properly)

Searching from your own phone at the office tells you almost nothing. Google personalizes, and you’re standing on top of your own pin.

Use a grid rank tracker (Local Falcon, BrightLocal and Whitespark all sell them). It checks your rank for a keyword at points across a grid, say a 7x7 grid with a mile between points, and shows you a heat map. Run it monthly for your three most valuable searches. The goal is a growing share of grid points where you’re in the top three.

Then watch what matters more than rank. In your Business Profile’s Performance view: calls, website clicks and direction requests, plus the search terms people used. In Google Search Console, filter for the URL your profile links to (add a UTM tag so you can separate profile traffic in GA4). Our guide to tracking calls and form submissions in GA4 shows the setup. A grid that turns greener while calls stay flat means you’re ranking for the wrong searches.

A 90-day plan to climb the map pack in Houston

Most stuck profiles move within a few months once the basics are fixed, if the business is reasonably close to the searches it wants. How fast depends on how strong the three businesses ahead of you are. A new roofer in Pearland against competitors with 900 reviews each is a longer project than a new dentist in Magnolia.

A few things we’d skip: buying citations in bulk from hundreds of directories nobody visits, “GMB posting” packages that publish daily filler, fake suites, and anything sold as a guaranteed No. 1. The local SEO myths post has more of those.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my business show up in Google Maps in The Woodlands but not in Houston?

Distance from the searcher is one of Google’s three core local ranking factors, and your profile is ranked from your actual address even if it’s hidden. A business in The Woodlands will usually rank well nearby and fade toward Houston, Katy or Pearland. To reach farther, build area service pages that rank in regular results, increase your review count, and use search ads for distant towns.

Does adding cities to my service area help me rank in more Houston suburbs?

Not much. The service area tells customers and Google where you work, but rankings are still calculated from your business address. Google’s guidelines say the overall area shouldn’t extend beyond about two hours of driving time. Listing Katy, Sugar Land and Pearland won’t put a Spring-based business in those map packs. Area pages on your website and ads do more.

How long does it take to get into the Google 3-pack?

If the problem is a wrong category, missing info or a suspended profile, fixes can show results within weeks. Building enough reviews and links to beat established competitors usually takes three to nine months, depending on how strong they are and how close you are to the searches. A grid rank tracker run monthly will show if you’re gaining ground.

Is it OK to put keywords in my Google Business Profile name?

Only if they’re part of your real business name as it appears on your signage, website and legal documents. Google’s guidelines prohibit adding taglines, service keywords or locations that aren’t part of your actual name. Keywords in the name do help rankings, which is why competitors abuse it, but the risk of a suspension isn’t worth it. You can report violators with a suggested edit.

Can I pay Google to rank higher in the map pack in Houston?

No. Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can pay for ads that appear above or within the map results, including search ads with location assets and Local Services Ads, but those don’t change your organic position. Organic map rankings come from relevance, distance and prominence, which means categories, address, reviews, content and links.
