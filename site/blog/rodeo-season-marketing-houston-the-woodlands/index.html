“Should we do something for the rodeo?” A Woodlands restaurant owner asked us that in the second week of March one year. By then the answer was mostly no. The boots had been bought, the group dinners booked, and every Instagram feed in Houston was already wall-to-wall fringe.

RODEOHOUSTON is the biggest stretch of the year for a long list of Houston and The Woodlands businesses, and most of the money moves before opening day. This post is a calendar. Dates first, then a week-by-week plan running from January through the last concert, then what tends to spike and the trademark line you shouldn’t cross.

All dates below are for 2027. Where the Rodeo hadn’t confirmed a date when we wrote this, we say so.

RODEOHOUSTON 2027 dates and the pre-rodeo events

The 2027 Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo runs Tuesday, March 2 through Sunday, March 21, per RODEOHOUSTON’s Go Texan Day page. That’s a 20-day run, one day shorter than 2026, per 93Q Country.

Spring break matters for planning. Conroe ISD’s 2026-27 calendar puts it at March 15-19, the third week of the Rodeo. Check your local district too, since a family weekday at NRG Park looks a lot like a family weekday at your trampoline park.

How big rodeo season is for Houston businesses

The scale is the reason to plan. Total grounds attendance was 2,621,765 in 2026, after a record 2,735,690 in 2025, per the Rodeo’s attendance page.

The Rodeo’s 2024 economic study, done by Economics Analytics Consulting, put total economic activity in Greater Houston at $597 million, and found 28% of the more than one million unique attendees came from outside Greater Houston, per the Rodeo’s 2024 economic study. That 28% sleeps somewhere, eats somewhere and buys a hat somewhere.

Most of it isn’t spent at NRG Park. It’s spent on the way there and in the weeks before: outfits, group dinners, a car detail before date night, a hotel near the Medical Center for the cousins from Lufkin.

What spikes during rodeo season, and when

Here’s our read on who sees a lift, built from public reporting where it exists and from patterns we’ve watched in Houston accounts. Use it as a starting hypothesis and check it against your own last two years of sales.

Western wear is the clearest case. Republic Boot Co. in the Heights estimates boot sales reach the thousands in the weeks before the Rodeo, its busiest season, and a Lucchese executive called rodeo season the “bread and butter” of its selling year, per Houstonia. A co-owner of Patron Western Wear told Click2Houston in 2026 that customers who used to spend $500 on boots alone are now spreading the same $500 across a full outfit, and advised shopping before the grounds, where prices run high. That shopper is planning a whole look. Sell it to them as one.

Restaurants have a different window. Office groups book Go Texan Day lunches in early February. Concert-night dinners spread across all three weeks, with Friday and Saturday filling first. For The Woodlands, the Township’s Woodlands Express runs a rodeo park and ride from the Sawdust lot at 701 Westridge Drive with reservations required, per the Rodeo’s park and ride page. Restaurants near Sawdust and I-45 can market a “dinner, then the bus” night out.

Week-by-week rodeo marketing calendar for Houston and The Woodlands

Here’s the calendar we’d run, keyed to the 2027 dates. Shift a week either way depending on how your customers buy.

Early to mid-January: plan and shoot. Lock offers, book the photo or video shoot (real staff in real boots beats stock every time), and set up landing pages. If you want rodeo-themed Google Ads, write them now so they clear review before demand starts. Pull last year’s February and March numbers by week.

Last two weeks of January: start the early buyers. Western wear and salons start social ads and email. Restaurants announce group menus for Go Texan Day and concert nights. Event rental and caterers reach out to HR managers at offices along Research Forest, Hughes Landing and the Energy Corridor, since Go Texan Day parties get planned now.

Feb. 1 to 21: the heaviest spend. This is when outfits get bought and dinners get booked. Raise budgets on Meta and Google, push Instagram Reels and TikTok, and run local search ads on “western wear near me”-type terms. Restaurants should have reservation links on the Google Business Profile and in every post.

Feb. 22 to 28: kickoff week. BBQ contest, Go Texan Day on Friday the 26th, trail rides into Memorial Park, parade Saturday the 27th. Post daily. Run Go Texan Day offers (“wear your boots, get [your offer]”). Car washes and detailers push truck and trailer washes.

March 2 to 21: the run. Shift from “get ready” to “before and after.” Pre-concert dinner, late-night food, recovery brunch, rideshare and party bus bookings. Watch the concert lineup and add budget around the headliners your customers care about. Spring break, March 15-19, brings families out on weekdays.

March 22 onward: after. Clearance on western wear. A thank-you post with customer photos (with permission). Tag and save the content you’ll reuse next January, and write down what sold out early.

Local ad timing: Google, Meta, TikTok and the rest

A few timing rules that apply across channels in rodeo season.

  • Meta and Instagram need a two to three week runway to build frequency in a tight radius. Start in late January, not the day before Go Texan Day. Radius around your store, around 10 to 15 miles in the suburbs, smaller inside the Loop.
  • TikTok works for outfit content and restaurant “where to eat before the rodeo” posts. Organic first; boost what already got traction.
  • Google Search catches people who already decided. Bid on your category plus “near me” and town names (Spring, Conroe, Katy, Cypress, Sugar Land), and add negatives for “tickets,” “lineup,” “schedule,” “livestock show results” and “jobs,” which pull rodeo fans you can’t sell to.
  • Google Business Profile posts and photos need refreshing in early February so Maps shows your rodeo inventory or menu, not your Christmas window.
  • Streaming TV and radio are expensive in February because everyone wants rodeo season. If you buy them, book in December. Our Houston radio and TV cost guide has ranges.

Budget-wise, we’d put most of a small business’s rodeo spend into February. A rough pattern we use for planning (illustrative, not a benchmark) is about 15% in late January, 55% across February, 25% during the run, and 5% for after. The run itself is crowded and expensive, and much of the buying is done.

Trademark caution: rodeo logos and “official” claims

This is where local businesses get letters. The Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo owns its names and marks. RODEOHOUSTON is a registered federal trademark, registration No. 8209761, registered April 14, 2026, covering rodeos, concerts and live entertainment plus clothing like hats and t-shirts.

the Rodeo’s own brand standards say third-party use of Rodeo logos requires approval from its Marketing Director, that official sponsors get a provided logo lockup that can’t be altered, and that materials representing the Show go through the marketing department. If you aren’t a sponsor, you don’t have a lockup.

We’re not lawyers, so here’s our practical line. You can say true, plain things about dates and the season: “Open late during rodeo season,” “Boots for Go Texan Day,” “Dinner before the concert at NRG.” Avoid the Rodeo’s logos, its bucking horse art or anything resembling it, the RODEOHOUSTON wordmark styled like theirs, and any phrase that implies a relationship: “official,” “partner,” “presented by,” “the rodeo’s favorite.” Don’t imply a ticket giveaway is connected to the Rodeo, and don’t use performer names or photos to sell your product. If you want a real tie-in, the Rodeo has sponsorship programs. Ask them.

Same goes for ad platforms. A trademark owner can file complaints with Google and Meta about ads using its marks, and an ad that gets flagged in the middle of February is a bad week to lose.

Rodeo content ideas that don’t look like everyone else’s

Every feed in Houston gets the same boot flat-lay in February. A few ideas that tend to perform better:

Go Texan Day is worth its own plan. The Rodeo encourages people to wear western clothes that day, and encourages organizations to hold western-themed events with dress-up, decor, barbecue or cook-off contests, per RODEOHOUSTON’s Go Texan Day page. Schools get into it too. On Go Texan Day, trail riders visited seven southwest Houston schools as part of HISD’s celebration, per HISD’s news blog. We didn’t find any district that closes school for a “rodeo day,” so don’t plan a weekday family promo around one; plan around Go Texan Day dress-up and spring break instead.

School-age demand shows up in other ways: kids’ boots and hats, rodeo-week birthday parties, photographers booking western mini-sessions. Families from Magnolia, Tomball, Conroe and Kingwood drive in on weekends, so suburban businesses can market “on the way home from NRG” as much as “on the way there.”

Where rodeo customers come from around Houston

NRG Park is on the south side of the Loop. Your customers aren’t all there. Rodeo traffic flows from every suburb on 288, I-45, US 59/I-69 and the 610 Loop, and much of the spending happens near home.

For a Woodlands, Spring or Conroe business, the money is in the trip planning: the outfit, the dinner, the park and ride. For a Pearland, Sugar Land or Bellaire business, the money is also in the drive itself, because the trip to NRG is short and a lot of people stop on the way.

Frequently asked questions

When is the Houston rodeo in 2027?

RODEOHOUSTON 2027 runs Tuesday, March 2 through Sunday, March 21 at NRG Park, a 20-day run. Go Texan Day and Rodeo Roundup are Friday, February 26, the trail rides arrive at Memorial Park that Friday, and the Downtown Rodeo Parade is Saturday, February 27 at 10 a.m. The barbecue contest is expected February 25 to 27, but confirm that on the Rodeo’s website.

When should Houston and The Woodlands businesses start rodeo marketing?

Plan and shoot content in early January, start ads in the last two weeks of January, and spend the most from February 1 through Go Texan Day weekend. Most outfit shopping and group dinner booking happens before opening day. During the run itself, shift to pre-concert, late-night and after-show offers, and save a small budget for post-rodeo clearance and thank-you posts.

Can my business use the Houston rodeo logo in ads?

Not without permission. The Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo owns its names and logos, RODEOHOUSTON is a registered trademark, and the Rodeo’s brand standards require approval for third-party logo use. You can describe the season truthfully, such as open late during rodeo season, but avoid logos, look-alike art, and words like official or partner unless you have a sponsorship agreement.

What businesses get busier during rodeo season in Houston?

Western wear and boot stores see the clearest spike, with local shops reporting boot sales in the thousands before opening day. Restaurants and bars see group bookings and pre-concert dinners, hotels see concert-night and out-of-town demand, and salons, detailers, party buses, caterers and event rental companies often see lifts around Go Texan Day and big concert weekends. Check your own sales history.

What is Go Texan Day in Houston?

Go Texan Day is the unofficial kickoff of the Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo, held the Friday before opening day. In 2027 it falls on February 26. People wear jeans, boots and hats to work and school, Rodeo Roundup takes place downtown from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., and trail rides arrive at Memorial Park. Many offices and schools hold western-themed events.
