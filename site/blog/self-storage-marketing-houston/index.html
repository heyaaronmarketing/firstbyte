Sunday, 9:15 p.m. A couple in an apartment off Kuykendahl closed on a house in Magnolia that won’t be ready for six weeks, and their lease ends Friday. One of them types “storage units near me” and gets four results. Two are national brands with prices, a “Rent Now” button and a 50%-off first month. One is your facility, where the website says “call for pricing” and the office closed at six. The fourth is a SpareFoot listing for your own facility.

They rent the 10x10 at the national brand before the kids are in bed.

Self storage marketing in Houston works when an independent operator matches the big REITs on the basics (prices online, rent online any hour, a solid Google Business Profile for every facility) and then wins on the things a national brand can’t fake: local answers, specific unit types, and people who pick up the phone. This guide covers how we’d do that, from Google Ads and aggregators to street rates, hurricanes, Texas lien notices and boat storage near Lake Conroe.

What you’re up against: the REITs in Houston

The three big public operators you see on every Houston corner have advantages that would be silly to ignore.

Public Storage held interests in 3,171 facilities at the end of 2025, and nearly three quarters of its new rentals were completed through its eRental and Rent by Phone process that year, per Public Storage’s 2025 annual report. Extra Space became the largest operator in the industry when it completed its merger with Life Storage on July 20, 2023, creating more than 3,500 stores, per Inside Self Storage. CubeSmart rounds out the trio. All three run revenue management software that adjusts street rates constantly, and they buy brand search and generic search across the whole metro.

Where independents win is everything local. A REIT facility page is a template. Yours can say the gate code works until 10 p.m., the climate-controlled building has a loading dock that fits a 26-foot truck, you have 12-foot-wide drive-up units for a bass boat, and Danny in the office will hold a unit until Saturday if you call. Those details win searches like “boat storage near Lake Conroe” and they win the comparison once a renter is looking at three tabs.

The Houston storage market right now

Houston is one of the most supplied storage markets in the country, which means you’re competing on visibility and price, not on a shortage.

StorageCafe’s Houston market page counted 405 facilities and about 28.5 million rentable square feet in Houston as of October 2026, or 6.9 square feet per person. Its average street rate for a 10x10 was $97 a month non-climate and $119 climate-controlled. The Yardi Matrix’s June 2026 self storage report forecast about 789,519 square feet of new Houston supply for 2026, roughly 3% of existing stock, and put the national average street rate across all unit sizes at $135, down 1.5% from a year earlier.

Demand has a structural tailwind here. a StorageCafe analysis of 40 years of building found Houston built more apartments than any other U.S. city from 1985 through 2024 (240,659 units), and apartments built since 2015 average 44 square feet smaller. Smaller apartments, more moves and long commutes from The Woodlands, Katy and Cypress all mean more stuff that needs a home.

In practice, a new facility opening a mile away can change your occupancy faster than any marketing campaign, so watch construction permits and rate changes around your site at least monthly.

Demand drivers: moves, storms and renovations

Storage demand in Houston follows life events more than seasons, though the seasons are real.

  • Moves. Summer is peak, from late May through August, with spikes at month-end when leases turn over. Closings with a gap between houses are the classic 10x15 rental. Partner with movers and apartment communities; our moving company marketing post is written from the mover’s side of that relationship.
  • Storms. After a flood or a roof full of tree limbs, families need somewhere dry for furniture while contractors work. The big operators move fast: U-Haul offered 30 days of free self-storage to affected Houston residents after the May 2024 windstorm and again after Hurricane Beryl in July 2024. Decide your storm offer before June, not during the outage.
  • Renovations and foundation work. Kitchen remodels, flooring jobs and foundation repairs on Houston’s clay soil all empty rooms for weeks. Remodelers and flooring companies make good referral partners.
  • Downsizing, estates and divorce. Steady year-round, longer stays, and the renters most likely to want a human on the phone.
  • Business storage. Contractors, pharma reps and e-commerce sellers who want inventory near I-45 or the Grand Parkway. They care about access hours and deliveries.

Write a short storm playbook now: a pre-written Google Business Profile post, an ad that can go live within hours, a landing page for displaced homeowners, and a rule for how long your office stays open after a storm. Our seasonal marketing guide for Greater Houston covers how to plan around the calendar.

Google Business Profile and local SEO for each facility

Most storage rentals start in Google Maps. Each facility needs its own profile, its own page on your website, and its own reviews.

Use your real business name exactly as it appears on your signage. Google’s Business Profile guidelines don’t allow keyword stuffing in the business name, so “Main Street Storage” can’t become “Main Street Storage Climate Controlled Boat RV Conroe TX.” Pick “Self-storage facility” as the primary category and add secondary categories only where they’re true (boat storage facility, RV storage facility). Post office hours clearly and explain gate access hours in the description or with whatever additional-hours options your category offers, because “Is it open now?” is a common reason renters skip a listing.

Photos matter more than operators think. Show the gate, the hallways, a 10x10 with the door up, the loading area, the cameras and the drive aisles wide enough for a truck. A renter who’s about to drive a box truck through your gate wants to see the turn.

On the website, give each facility a page with unit sizes and live prices, features, gate and office hours, a map, directions from the nearest landmarks (Hughes Landing, Market Street, the FM 1488 and I-45 interchange), and its own reviews. The REIT pages are thin, and a facility page with real local detail can outrank them for “storage units in Spring TX” or “storage near Woodforest.” Our map pack guide explains the ranking factors, and multi-location local SEO covers running several facilities without the profiles cannibalizing each other.

SpareFoot and the aggregators: do the math

Aggregators like SpareFoot put your units in front of renters who are comparison shopping, and they charge you for it. The pricing is clearer than most operators realize.

Under SpareFoot’s OneTime pricing, the move-in fee is your bid multiplied by the unit’s price, so a $100 unit with a bid of 2 costs $200 per move-in, per SpareFoot’s own help article. If your move-in rate (reservations that actually move in) falls below the network average of 50%, an extra fee can apply. Under RentPercent pricing, you instead pay a monthly percentage of rent for as long as the tenant stays, with the percentage set by your account.

Three things to check before you decide:

  • How many of your aggregator move-ins would have found you anyway, through your own Google profile or brand search? Ask every new tenant how they found you and record it in your management software.
  • Your average length of stay. A RentPercent arrangement on a tenant who stays two years costs far more than a one-time fee.
  • Which units you list. Aggregators are useful for filling hard-to-rent sizes and new facilities in lease-up. They’re expensive for units that rent themselves.

We’re not against aggregators. We’re against paying a marketplace for move-ins your own listing would have won.

Online rentals and move-ins at any hour

When a REIT completes most new rentals through an online and phone process, renters learn to expect it everywhere. If your website only takes reservations, or worse, only takes phone calls, you’re losing the 9 p.m. renter.

Most current management software (SiteLink and storEDGE among them) offers an online rental flow that collects ID, signs the lease and takes payment. Turn it on, put the “Rent Now” button next to every price, and test it on your own phone from a parking lot. Count the taps.

A few details that move the needle:

  • A size guide with photos of real furniture in real units. “A 10x10 fits a two-bedroom apartment” is the most useful sentence on a storage site.
  • Reservation follow-up. Someone who reserves and doesn’t show should get a text that afternoon and a call the next morning. Abandoned online rentals deserve the same.
  • Call answering. Route after-hours calls to a call center or a staffed line that can rent a unit, not a voicemail box.
  • Track calls and online rentals by source so you can tie them back to Google Ads and your profile. Our call tracking guide covers setup.

Pricing, street rates and promotions

Price is visible, and renters compare it in seconds. You don’t have to be cheapest, but you have to be clear.

  • Show street rates on the site. “Call for pricing” loses to a number almost every time.
  • Show the real cost of moving in. If there’s an admin fee, required lock or required insurance, list it next to the rate. Surprises at the counter turn into one-star reviews.
  • Promotions should be specific and short. “First month $1 on 10x10 climate units at our Spring location through November 30” beats a permanent “specials available.” If the promo applies only to certain sizes, say so on the unit row.
  • Rate increases on existing tenants are a normal part of the business. Give notice the way your lease requires, explain it plainly, and expect some reviews to mention it. Reply calmly.

Watch the three or four facilities your renters actually compare you with, by unit type. A weekly check of their 10x10 and 10x20 rates is usually enough for an independent operator.

Google Ads for self storage in Houston

Search ads catch renters at the moment they decide. Structure by facility and by unit type.

  • Facility campaigns. “Storage units [town],” “self storage near [landmark],” “storage units near me,” with a radius of roughly three to five miles around each facility. Land on that facility’s page.
  • Unit-type ad groups. Climate-controlled storage, drive-up units, boat storage, RV storage, business or wine storage. Each to a page or a filtered view of those units.
  • Brand. Your name, so aggregators and competitors don’t sit above you.

Negatives: storage containers for sale, shed, sheds, bins, totes, cabinets, furniture, “storage auction” (unless you want auction buyers), jobs, careers, manager, cloud storage, iCloud, data, Amazon, PODS (unless you rent portable containers), “free storage” and “cheap storage boxes.” Check your search terms weekly for the first month.

Use presence-based location targeting, so you’re paying for people in or regularly in your area rather than those merely interested in it, per Google Ads location options. The exception is relocation: someone moving to The Woodlands from Dallas may search from Dallas, so a small separate campaign for “storage units The Woodlands TX” with interest targeting can make sense.

Sample ad:

Headline 1: Storage Units in Spring, TX
Headline 2: Climate-Controlled From [your price]
Headline 3: Rent Online in 5 Minutes
Description: Gate open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., cameras throughout, truck-friendly drive aisles. Near I-45 and FM 2920. See sizes and rent online.

Spend: in accounts we’ve run, a single facility can learn a lot on a modest budget because storage searches are tightly local. Set the budget from a target cost per move-in, then compare it against your average tenant value. Be careful with Performance Max for a single facility; we explain why in Performance Max vs. Search for local businesses. Our search marketing team runs these accounts.

Texas lien notices, auctions and your reputation

Lien sales aren’t marketing, but they show up in your reviews, so they belong in this conversation. This is a summary for marketers, not legal advice; your attorney and your state association should sign off on your process.

Under Texas Property Code Section 59.043, a lessor’s notice to a delinquent tenant has to include an itemized account of the claim, the lessor’s name, address and phone number, a statement that the contents have been seized under the contractual landlord’s lien, a warning that if the claim isn’t paid within 14 days the property may be sold at public auction (or a vehicle towed under the Occupations Code), and a notice in bold or underlined type asking tenants in military service to report that status immediately. The notice can be delivered in person, by verified mail or by email. Email delivery is allowed only if the rental agreement includes a conspicuous, underlined or bold statement that the tenant may receive notice by email.

That email clause has a marketing side. Collect a working email and mobile number at move-in, make sure your lease language is current, and use the same contact info for autopay reminders and rate-change notices. Tenants who get a friendly payment reminder before day five are much less likely to end up in a lien process and much less likely to write an angry review about one. The rules for advertising the sale itself are in Section 59.044, and the Legislature has considered changes to them recently, so confirm the current version with counsel before your next auction.

When a lien-related review does appear, reply briefly, don’t share account details, and offer an office phone number. Future renters read those replies.

Boat and RV storage near Lake Conroe

Lake Conroe covers 20,118 acres across Montgomery and Walker counties, per Texas Parks and Wildlife, and the boats on it have to live somewhere the rest of the year. Many neighborhoods around The Woodlands, Montgomery and Conroe have deed restrictions that keep boats and RVs out of driveways, and in our experience that comes up in almost every boat storage inquiry.

Boat and RV storage is a different customer from household storage. These renters stay longer, care more about covered spaces, security, wash-down areas, dump stations and easy access to FM 1097, SH 105 and the boat ramps, and they’ll drive farther for the right setup.

  • Run a separate ad group for “boat storage Lake Conroe,” “covered boat storage Montgomery TX,” “RV storage Conroe” and “RV storage near me,” landing on a page that lists space lengths, heights and pricing.
  • Photograph the actual spaces with a boat or trailer in them, plus the turn into the lot.
  • If you have a waitlist, put a waitlist form on the page and promote it. Covered spaces can fill up by spring, so start your marketing before boating season ramps up.
  • Partner with marinas, boat dealers and RV service shops in Montgomery County for referrals.

Reviews that win the comparison

When a renter has three facilities open in three tabs, review count and recency often decide it. The REITs have volume. You can have warmth and specifics.

Ask at two moments: right after move-in (“Was everything easy today?”) and around 60 days in. A text from the manager works:

“Hi Marcus, thanks for choosing us for your move. If the unit and the gate have worked well for you, a quick Google review helps other folks in Conroe find us: [link]. Thanks, Danny”

Don’t offer a discount, free month or lock for reviews; Google’s prohibited content policy prohibits incentives. Reply to every review, mention the facility and the feature they praised, and keep replies short. More detail is in the complete guide to Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get more customers for my self storage facility in Houston?

Make it easy to choose you online. Give every facility its own Google Business Profile and website page with live prices, photos and gate hours, turn on online rentals so people can rent at night, and ask new tenants for reviews. Then run Google search ads by facility and unit type with strong negatives, and use aggregators like SpareFoot only where they bring move-ins you wouldn't get anyway.

Is SpareFoot worth it for a self storage facility?

It can be, especially in lease-up or for hard-to-rent sizes. Under SpareFoot's OneTime pricing you pay your bid times the unit price per move-in, so a $100 unit with a bid of 2 costs $200, with an extra fee possible if your move-in rate falls below 50%. RentPercent pricing charges a monthly share of rent for the whole stay. Compare against your own Google cost per move-in.

What keywords should self storage Google Ads target?

Target local, high-intent searches: storage units plus your town or a nearby landmark, storage units near me, climate controlled storage, drive up storage, and boat or RV storage if you offer it. Add negatives like storage containers for sale, sheds, bins, jobs, cloud storage, iCloud and Amazon. Use presence-based location targeting with a tight radius around each facility in Houston or The Woodlands.

What notice does Texas law require before a storage lien sale?

Texas Property Code Section 59.043 requires an itemized claim, the lessor's contact details, a statement that contents were seized under the contractual landlord's lien, a 14-day warning that property may be sold at auction or a vehicle towed, and a bold or underlined military-service notice. Delivery can be in person, by verified mail, or by email if the lease has a conspicuous email clause. Confirm details with an attorney.

How do I market boat and RV storage near Lake Conroe?

Treat it as its own business. Build a page with space lengths, heights, covered versus open pricing and photos of real boats in the spaces, then run a separate ad group for searches like boat storage Lake Conroe and RV storage Conroe. Promote a waitlist before spring, and build referral relationships with marinas, boat dealers and RV service shops in Montgomery County.
