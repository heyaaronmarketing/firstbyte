That’s what Airbnb’s own fee explainer says a host keeps from a $100 nightly price under its single 15.5% host-paid fee, if the host didn’t raise prices when the fee changed. On a $2,400 July week at a lake house on Lake Conroe, that gap is real money. And Vrbo just moved too.

The short answer: direct bookings pay off for Houston-area hosts once you have repeat guests or a property people remember. Keep Airbnb and Vrbo for discovery, build a simple booking site, get listed on Google’s vacation rental results through a connectivity partner, and email past guests before every peak season. Budget for the taxes and admin the platforms used to handle.

This playbook is for owners and small managers with one to maybe fifteen units around Lake Conroe, Galveston, The Woodlands, Montgomery and the Bolivar side. It covers the money first, then the local rules, then the marketing.

How much do Airbnb and Vrbo really cost a Houston-area host?

More than most hosts think, and the math changed twice in the last year.

Airbnb says the split fee is being phased out, and the single fee is mandatory for hosts who use property management software. Once you switch, you can’t switch back. Vrbo is retiring its annual subscription option and moving everyone to pay-per-booking; subscription hosts move over at the end of their term.

Two cautions before you get excited about that chart. First, the platforms bring guests you’d never find alone. A first-time Houston family searching “Galveston beach house with pool” is on Airbnb, and they’re not typing your URL. Second, direct bookings come with costs the chart ignores: software, your time, a damage policy you enforce yourself, chargebacks, and taxes you now have to collect. More on that next.

So the realistic goal for most hosts isn’t leaving the platforms. It’s getting 20% to 40% of nights booked direct within a couple of years (that’s the range we’d aim for, not a benchmark), mostly from repeat guests and their friends.

What changes when a guest books direct

The biggest one is tax. Per the Texas Comptroller’s hotel tax FAQ, the state hotel occupancy tax is 6% and applies to rentals of 29 days or less. When a short-term rental platform agrees to collect and remit state HOT for you, and you rent only through that platform, you aren’t required to collect and remit it yourself. Take one direct booking and that’s no longer true. You collect the 6% state tax on that stay, file with the Comptroller, and handle any city or county tax too.

Other things you inherit:

  • Payments and disputes. A guest who disputes a charge with their bank is now your problem, not Airbnb’s. A signed rental agreement and a clear cancellation policy at checkout are your evidence.
  • Damage. There’s no AirCover. Use a refundable deposit or a damage waiver product, and confirm what your insurance covers for direct-booked guests.
  • Guest screening. No platform reviews of the guest. Ask for ID and the names of adults staying.
  • Calendar sync. Your booking site has to block dates on Airbnb and Vrbo instantly, or you will double-book a July 4 weekend. A channel manager or PMS does this; iCal alone can lag.

None of that is a reason not to do it. It’s a reason to set it up properly once.

Galveston short-term rental rules: registration, GVR numbers and hotel tax

Galveston is the most regulated STR market in the Houston area, and the rules got stricter in 2025.

Some context: ABC13 reported in April 2025 that Galveston went from about 27 short-term rentals in 2019 to about 4,900 in 2024, per the Park Board’s CFO, with roughly 1,000 still unregistered. HVS noted cruise passenger volume hit a record 1.7 million in 2024. That’s a lot of competition and a lot of enforcement attention.

Regulation moved from the Park Board to the City of Galveston on October 1, 2025, per Avalara’s summary of the ordinance, so older blog posts telling you to register with the Park Board are out of date.

For your marketing: put the GVR number on your own booking site, on every listing page, and on any Facebook or Instagram post that advertises a specific property. Platforms are required to pull listings without valid numbers when the city asks, and the city is checking. And when a guest books direct, that 9% city tax plus 6% state tax (15% combined) is yours to collect and file. Build it into your checkout so the guest sees it as a line item rather than a surprise.

If December 31 is a few weeks away when you’re reading this, renew now.

Lake Conroe, The Woodlands and Montgomery County: what to check

North of Houston the rules are less centralized, which makes them easier to miss.

The Woodlands. The Woodlands Township levies a 9% hotel occupancy tax, with the state’s 6% on top, for 15% combined. It applies to anyone collecting payment for stays under 30 days in the Township, which includes short-term rentals. Starting January 1, 2026, filers who consistently collect less than $2,500 a month can file quarterly. A host with a guest house near Hughes Landing who takes a direct booking for a big event weekend owes that tax.

Lake Conroe. The lake covers roughly 19,640 acres on the West Fork of the San Jacinto River in Montgomery and Walker counties, per the Texas Water Development Board. Rental homes around it sit in the city of Conroe, the city of Montgomery, Willis, or unincorporated county land, and each can mean a different local tax picture and different HOA rules. The state’s 6% applies to all of them. For local tax, ask the city you’re in, and if you’re unincorporated, ask the county; we’re not going to guess at rates we couldn’t confirm. Many Lake Conroe subdivisions also have deed restrictions on short-term rentals, which matter more than any marketing plan.

Houston. In April 2025, ABC13 reported Houston City Council was set to vote on requiring short-term rental owners to register, with $1.6 million for software that matches online listings to registrations. If you have a unit inside the city limits, check the city’s current requirements before you advertise direct.

Not legal or tax advice. A CPA who files hotel tax for other hosts is worth the fee in your first year.

Build the direct booking site guests trust

A direct booking site has one job: make a guest who already likes your place feel safe paying you instead of Airbnb. That’s a trust problem more than a design problem.

What we’d put on it, page by page:

  • One page per property with 25+ real photos, a floor plan sketch, bed sizes per room, the dock or beach access explained honestly (“four-minute walk to the seawall, one street to cross”), and the GVR number if it’s in Galveston.
  • A live calendar and instant booking through your PMS or booking engine. “Email us for availability” loses most direct guests.
  • Total price before the last step, with nightly rate, cleaning fee and taxes itemized. Hidden fees feel worse on a small site than on a platform.
  • Reviews from Airbnb and Vrbo, quoted with the platform and date. You can’t embed them all, but you can quote them, and a page showing 140 five-star stays does more than any headline.
  • House rules, cancellation policy and the rental agreement visible before checkout.
  • Local guide pages: boat rentals and launch spots on Lake Conroe, Strand and Seawall restaurants in Galveston, Market Street and the Waterway in The Woodlands. These pages pull in search traffic and they’re useful to guests already booked.

Use your own domain and a real email address at that domain. A Gmail address and a PayPal link on a free site builder looks like a scam to a cautious guest. Our website cost guide gives honest ranges if you’re pricing this out.

Get listed on Google’s vacation rental results

Search “Galveston vacation rentals” or “Lake Conroe cabin” and Google shows a vacation rentals module with photos, prices and a map. That’s free distribution most independent hosts never claim.

Per Google’s vacation rentals help page, vacation rentals show across Google Search, Maps and Google’s travel lodging search, with filters for price, amenities and guest rating. You connect through an integration that sends Google your rates, availability and content. The booking links can point to your own website, and Google says “there are no fees for Google-generated referrals or bookings.”

Individual hosts usually get there through one of Google’s connectivity partners, which are often the same property management and booking-site companies you’d use anyway. When you’re shopping for direct booking software, ask each vendor if it’s a Google vacation rentals partner. That one question can decide which tool you pick.

Then keep the data clean. Wrong prices or a calendar that shows open dates you’ve already sold will hurt you with Google and with the guest who clicks.

Turn platform guests into direct repeat guests, without breaking Airbnb’s rules

This is where direct bookings actually come from. Not ads. Past guests.

But be careful. Airbnb’s off-platform policy prohibits asking guests to communicate off-platform before booking, offering discounts to book outside Airbnb, putting links in listings or messages that take people off the platform, and rebooking an existing stay outside Airbnb. Penalties range from warnings to removing listings or accounts. So no “book direct next time and save 10%” message in the Airbnb inbox, and no URL in the listing.

What works and stays clean:

  • A branded property name and a printed welcome book in the house with your website, so the guest remembers where they stayed.
  • A guest Wi-Fi splash page or a QR code on the fridge for the local guide, which offers an email list sign-up for “first notice on summer dates.” Opt-in, clearly worded.
  • Everyone who books direct goes on the list automatically, with consent at checkout.
  • Email the list before each booking window: late January for spring break and summer, mid-summer for Labor Day and fall weekends, September for Thanksgiving and holidays. Four to six emails a year is plenty.

Texts need extra care. Texas brought marketing texts under its telephone solicitation law in 2025, and the email marketing basics post covers list building if you’re starting from zero. Email is the safer, cheaper channel for this.

Paid ads for direct bookings around Houston: keep them small and specific

Most hosts shouldn’t start with ads. Once you have a booking site, a Google listing and a guest list, a small paid budget can fill gaps.

Google Ads. Bid on your property name (cheap, and it stops Airbnb and Vrbo ads from sitting above your own name) and on specific phrases: “Lake Conroe lake house rental with boat dock,” “Galveston beach house sleeps 12,” “pet friendly rental The Woodlands.” Negatives: jobs, apartments, “for sale,” long term, monthly, RV, campground and hotel brand names. Our search team sets these up.

Meta. Facebook and Instagram are good for retargeting people who looked at your site and didn’t book, and for reaching Houston audiences with a short dock-at-sunset video when you have open midweek dates. Keep targeting to the Houston metro, which is where most drive-to guests start. See our paid social service.

An illustrative starting budget for a two-to-five unit host is $300 to $800 a month in peak season and close to zero in slow months. That’s our starting point, not a benchmark. Judge it by booked nights, not clicks.

Lake Conroe hosts can also partner locally. Boat rental outfits and marinas see the same guests, and our boat dealer and marina marketing post shows how they market. A “10% off a pontoon day with your stay” arrangement is a better direct-booking perk than a coupon. A 90-day plan for your first direct bookings

  • Days 1 to 30. Confirm registrations and tax accounts: Galveston GVR, Texas Comptroller, Township or city as needed. Choose a PMS or booking engine that syncs calendars and is a Google vacation rentals partner. Buy your domain.
  • Days 31 to 60. Launch the booking site with full property pages, itemized pricing and taxes, rental agreement and storm policy. Connect to Google vacation rentals. Put the welcome book and QR code in every unit.
  • Days 61 to 90. Send the first email to past direct guests and opt-ins ahead of the next season. Start a brand-name Google Ads campaign. Track direct nights as a share of total nights each month.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to collect hotel tax on direct bookings in Texas?

Yes. The Texas state hotel occupancy tax is 6% on stays of 29 days or less. If you rent only through a platform that collects state tax for you, you don’t have to collect and remit it yourself, but direct bookings change that. You collect and file state tax with the Comptroller, plus any local tax, such as Galveston’s 9% city tax or The Woodlands Township’s 9%.

How do I register a short-term rental in Galveston?

Register each unit with the City of Galveston, which took over from the Park Board on October 1, 2025. The annual fee is $250, renewals open December 1 and are due December 31, and a $500 late fee applies. Each unit gets a GVR number that must appear on all listings, and you need a 24-hour local contact who lives within one hour of the property.

Can I ask Airbnb guests to book directly next time?

Not through Airbnb. Its off-platform policy prohibits asking guests to communicate off-platform before booking, offering discounts to book outside Airbnb, and putting links in listings or messages that lead off the platform. Instead, brand your property, leave a welcome book with your website, and offer an opt-in email list through a QR code or Wi-Fi page in the home.

How do I get my vacation rental on Google?

Google shows vacation rentals in Search, Maps and its travel lodging search, and the booking links can go to your own website at no charge for referrals or bookings. Individual hosts usually connect through one of Google’s connectivity partners, often a property management system or booking-site provider. Ask software vendors whether they’re a Google vacation rentals partner before you choose one.

What does Vrbo charge hosts in 2026?

Starting October 29, 2026, Vrbo moved hosts to a pay-per-booking commission, typically a flat 12% in the U.S. that includes payment processing, with rates of 12% to 15% depending on region and brand. Vrbo is retiring its subscription model, and subscription hosts switch at the end of their term. Vrbo says the traveler service fee will typically be lower.
