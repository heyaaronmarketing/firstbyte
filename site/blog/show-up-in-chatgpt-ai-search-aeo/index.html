How to Show Up in ChatGPT and AI Search (AEO): A Playbook for Houston and The Woodlands Businesses

“Why does ChatGPT recommend my competitor?” We hear some version of that from Houston owners most weeks now. Usually they typed something like “best roofer in The Woodlands” into ChatGPT, got three names, and theirs wasn’t one of them.

Short answer: to show up in ChatGPT and AI search, make your website readable by AI crawlers, state plainly what you do and where, keep your Google Business Profile complete with a steady flow of reviews, and get mentioned on local sites that AI tools already trust. AEO (answer engine optimization) is good local SEO with a few extra settings. There’s no secret file.

This is the step-by-step version, with the settings to check, the copy to change, the things to skip, and a simple way to measure whether any of it is working.

How many Houston customers actually ask AI for recommendations?

Enough that ignoring it is no longer the safe choice. In BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 45% of U.S. consumers said they’d used ChatGPT or another AI tool to find local business recommendations, up from 6% the year before. That made AI the third most popular source in the survey.

The audience is huge on both sides. ChatGPT passed 900 million weekly users in February 2026, per TechCrunch, and Google says AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly users since its global expansion, per Alphabet’s Q2 2026 earnings remarks.

The catch is clicks. A Pew Research Center study found Google users clicked a regular result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% when one didn’t, and clicked a link inside the summary itself on just 1% of visits. The answer box is increasingly the whole experience. If you aren’t named in it, a lot of people never see your listing at all.

Where ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini and Copilot get their answers

Each tool assembles answers differently, and that tells you where to work. Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode may use “query fan-out,” issuing several related searches across subtopics and data sources to build one response, per Google’s “AI features and your website” page. The others work along similar lines: search, pick sources, write.

Step 1: Make sure AI crawlers can read your site

This is the step nobody checks, and it takes ten minutes. Some sites block AI crawlers without the owner knowing, through a security plugin, a hosting setting or a robots.txt file someone copied from a blog post in 2023.

OpenAI runs separate crawlers for separate jobs, per OpenAI’s crawler documentation. OAI-SearchBot surfaces websites in ChatGPT’s search features; block it and your pages won’t appear in ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot crawls content that may be used to train OpenAI’s models; blocking it opts you out of training without affecting search. ChatGPT-User fetches a page when a person asks ChatGPT to look at it.

So a business can reasonably block training and still allow search. Most local service businesses we work with allow both, since there’s little to protect on a page about water heater installation in Tomball, but it’s your call.

Then check the basics that matter for every engine. Pages need to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in Google to appear in AI features, according to Google’s “AI features and your website” page, which also says there are no additional technical requirements. Use the URL Inspection tool in Search Console on your key service pages. And verify your site in Bing Webmaster Tools, since Copilot leans on Bing’s index and most small businesses have never logged in there.

Step 2: Say plainly who you are, where and for whom

AI tools quote specifics. Vague copy gives them nothing to quote. When an answer says “X Plumbing is a family-owned company in Spring that handles slab leaks and repiping across north Harris County,” it pulled that sentence, or something close, from a page that said it clearly.

Go page by page and make sure these facts are stated in plain sentences, not buried in images or sliders: your business name exactly as it appears on Google, what you do (each main service named), where (towns and neighborhoods you actually serve, like Shenandoah, Oak Ridge North or Atascocita), who you serve, hours, licenses and how long you’ve been doing it.

Then add what only you can say. Google’s generative AI guide calls this non-commodity content, meaning “unique expert or experienced takes that go beyond common knowledge and the ordinary,” and describes it as a major factor in long-term visibility, per Google’s guide to optimizing for generative AI features. For a Houston business, that’s things like why clay soil in Katy cracks slabs differently than sandy soil in Magnolia, what you actually charge for a diagnostic, or how you handle insurance after a hail storm. A generic “10 tips for a healthy lawn” page is commodity content. AI can write that itself and doesn’t need to cite you.

Keep schema markup accurate (LocalBusiness, services, FAQ where it fits) but don’t expect it to work miracles. Google says there’s no special structured data you need to add for AI features, and that markup should match the visible text on the page, per Google’s “AI features and your website” page.

Step 3: Treat your Google Business Profile as an AI source

For local questions, the Business Profile may matter more than the website. Google’s guide says AI responses can include local business information, and that Business Profiles can help your products and services show up in AI responses as well as regular search results, per Google’s guide to optimizing for generative AI features.

What to check, in Business Profile manager:

  • Primary category matches your main money service. Secondary categories cover the rest of what you really do.
  • Services are listed one by one, with short descriptions written like you’d explain them on the phone.
  • Hours, holiday hours and the service area are accurate. AI answers repeat wrong hours with total confidence.
  • Attributes (women-owned, veteran-owned, wheelchair accessible, Spanish spoken, online estimates) are filled in where true. They match the qualifiers people add to AI prompts.
  • Photos are recent and real.

Reviews feed AI answers directly. BrightLocal found 82% of consumers read AI-generated review summaries, 47% won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only care about reviews from the last three months, per BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey. A profile with 300 reviews from 2021 and nothing since reads stale to people and to software. Our complete guide to Google reviews has request scripts, and ranking in the Houston map pack covers the rest of the profile work.

Step 4: Get mentioned where AI tools already look

When an AI answer recommends three businesses, it’s often echoing a “best of” list, a news story, a Reddit thread or a review site. Your own site saying you’re great counts for little. Other credible sites saying it counts for a lot.

Local places that tend to get cited for Houston-area questions include Community Impact, the Houston Chronicle and local TV station sites, chamber of commerce directories (The Woodlands Area Chamber, the Greater Houston Partnership, Conroe and Tomball chambers), industry association member directories, Yelp, Nextdoor, BBB, and subreddits like r/houston and r/TheWoodlands.

How to earn the mentions without faking them:

  1. Pitch a useful local story to Community Impact or a TV station: a storm-prep tip, a data point from your own jobs, a new location. Our press coverage guide has the pitch format.
  2. Join the chambers and associations you’d join anyway, and fill out the directory profile completely. Our chamber guide compares the local ones.
  3. Sponsor something real (a youth league, a school fundraiser) that produces a link and a local mention.
  4. Answer questions honestly in community forums when you can help, under your real name, without spamming links.

Don’t buy mentions or plant fake ones. Google’s guide specifically lists inauthentic brand mentions among the tactics site owners can skip, per Google’s guide to optimizing for generative AI features. Fake Reddit praise also tends to get spotted and screenshotted, which is the opposite of what you wanted.

Step 5: Answer the questions people actually type into ChatGPT

People talk to AI tools in full sentences with conditions attached. They don’t type “plumber Spring TX.” They type “who can fix a slab leak in Spring this week and do they work with insurance?”

Collect those questions from your own phone calls and emails for two weeks. Typical Houston examples we see:

  • “Which AC companies in The Woodlands are open on Sunday in August?”
  • “Best pediatric dentist near Kingwood that takes Medicaid”
  • “How much does foundation repair cost in Katy, and how long does it take?”
  • “Family law attorney in Conroe who offers payment plans”

Then make sure your site answers them in plain language: on the relevant service page, in an FAQ section, or in a blog post if the question needs room. Put the direct answer in the first sentence or two, then the detail. That format works for featured snippets and AI answers alike.

Don’t go the other way and spin up a page for every possible variation. Google’s guide warns that creating pages for every possible AI sub-query, mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses, can violate its scaled content abuse policy, per Google’s guide to optimizing for generative AI features. Thirty genuinely useful answers beat 300 near-duplicates.

What doesn’t help: llms.txt, AI rewrites and schema overload

Plenty of AEO advice online is selling something. Per Google’s guide to optimizing for generative AI features, Google Search ignores AI text files, special markup and Markdown versions of pages, so creating them won’t help or hurt your visibility in Google. The same guide says you don’t need to chop content into tiny pieces for AI and that page length should suit your audience.

A word on llms.txt, since owners ask about it constantly. It’s a proposed file that lists your key pages for AI tools. We publish one ourselves because it takes five minutes and costs nothing, and some non-Google tools may read it. We’d never pay anyone to “optimize” one, and we wouldn’t expect it to change a single Google answer.

Also skip: rewriting your whole site in a robotic Q&A style, stuffing every schema type you can find onto every page, and tools that promise to “submit” your business to ChatGPT. There’s no submission form.

How do you track whether ChatGPT is recommending you?

Imperfectly, but better than a year ago. Three sources, from most to least reliable:

GA4 referrals. In GA4, go to Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition, and look at session source for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com and gemini.google.com. ChatGPT also adds a utm_source=chatgpt.com tag to many links it shows. Counts are small for most local businesses, and people who read your name in an answer often call or search your name instead of clicking, so treat this as a floor.

Bing’s citation data. The AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools, launched in public preview in February 2026, shows how often your pages are cited in Copilot and Bing’s AI summaries, which pages get cited, and a sample of the queries the AI used to find them. It’s one of the first citation reports to come straight from a major engine. Google’s Search Console doesn’t break AI Mode out the same way.

A monthly prompt audit. Write 15 to 20 prompts your customers would use, run them in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini and Copilot on the same day each month, and log the results in a sheet like this one.

Does AEO work differently for The Woodlands than for Houston proper?

A little. AI tools interpret “near” loosely. Ask for “the best electrician near The Woodlands” and you may get businesses in Spring, Conroe and Tomball, because the tool is matching on the region, the reviews and the pages that mention the area. A business in Shenandoah with a page about The Woodlands and reviews mentioning Creekside Park can beat a closer company whose site never names a neighborhood.

Inside the Loop it’s the reverse: lots of competitors in a small area, so the qualifiers in the prompt (open late, Spanish-speaking, takes insurance, free estimates) do more of the sorting. That’s an argument for filling in every Business Profile attribute that’s true and writing those details on your pages in plain words. If you serve both, our post on The Woodlands, Spring and Conroe addresses explains how Google treats the area’s overlapping place names.

A 90-day AEO plan for a Houston small business

None of this needs a new budget line. Most of it is cleanup and consistency that also helps your regular Google rankings, which is why we fold it into search marketing work rather than selling it separately.

Expect results to trail the work. AI answers are built from what’s indexed and written about you, and new reviews, mentions and pages take weeks to months to get crawled and reflected. Google itself says changes to its controls can take several days to several months to show up, per Google’s “AI features and your website” page. Run the prompt audit monthly and give it a full quarter before judging.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my business recommended by ChatGPT in Houston?

Allow OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot crawler in your robots.txt, state your services and the Houston-area towns you serve in plain sentences on your site, and keep your Google Business Profile complete with recent reviews. Then earn mentions on local sites ChatGPT tends to cite, such as Community Impact, chamber directories, Yelp and industry lists. There is no form to submit your business to ChatGPT.

Does schema markup help with Google AI Overviews?

Accurate schema helps Google understand your business, but Google says there is no special structured data required to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and markup should match the visible text on the page. Use LocalBusiness and relevant service or FAQ markup where it fits. Clear page content, indexing and a strong Business Profile matter more than adding every schema type.

Should I block GPTBot on my website?

It depends on whether you mind your content being used to train OpenAI's models. Blocking GPTBot opts you out of training but does not remove you from ChatGPT search, which uses a separate crawler called OAI-SearchBot. Most local service businesses allow both. If you block anything, make sure OAI-SearchBot, Googlebot, Bingbot and PerplexityBot can still crawl your service pages.

How can I see if ChatGPT sends traffic to my website?

In GA4, open Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition, and look for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com and gemini.google.com as sources. ChatGPT also tags many links with utm_source=chatgpt.com. Numbers are usually small because people often call or search your name after reading an answer, so pair GA4 with a monthly prompt audit and Bing Webmaster Tools citation data.

Is AEO different from SEO for a local business in The Woodlands?

Mostly no. Google says its AI features run on its core ranking systems and need no special optimization, so the foundation is the same local SEO: indexed pages, clear service and area content, a complete Business Profile and reviews. AEO adds a few checks, such as AI crawler access, Bing Webmaster Tools, more question-and-answer content and earning mentions on sites AI tools cite.
