The Woodlands, Spring or Conroe? How to List Your City and ZIP for Local SEO

Should your address say The Woodlands, Spring or Conroe? USPS city names by ZIP, Google Business Profile rules, citations and what not to fake.

One dental office, one suite, three addresses. The website footer said The Woodlands, TX 77380. Google Business Profile said Spring, TX 77380. Yelp said Shenandoah. The owner asked us which one was wrong.

Technically, none of them. That’s the whole problem.

The Woodlands is not a city, its ZIP codes belong to post offices named Spring, Conroe and Magnolia, and the U.S. Postal Service will deliver to the same suite under more than one city name. For mail, that’s fine. For local SEO, where Google and every directory try to match your business across the web, three versions of one address look like three businesses, or one sloppy one.

Below: how city names actually work for The Woodlands ZIP codes, what to put on Google, your website and your citations, and the few things you should never do to look more “Woodlands” than you are.

Why The Woodlands has no city limits (and Spring doesn’t either)

The Woodlands is a master-planned community governed by The Woodlands Township, a special-purpose district. Residents voted in November 2021 to keep that model instead of incorporating as a city, per the Township’s incorporation page. It spans Montgomery and Harris counties, and the counties handle roads and policing, as ABC13 explained before the vote.

Spring isn’t a city either. It’s a large unincorporated area, mostly in Harris County, and “Spring, TX” on an envelope mostly means “served by the Spring post office.” Meanwhile, the small cities wedged against The Woodlands are very real. Shenandoah, for example, is an incorporated city of about 2 square miles along I-45 that celebrated 50 years in 2024, per Community Impact. Oak Ridge North sits just south of it. Conroe is a city, and a big one.

So when a customer types “dentist The Woodlands,” Google isn’t checking a city limit line, because for most of The Woodlands there isn’t one. It works from where your business is, where the searcher is, and what your profile and the rest of the web say about you.

What USPS actually accepts for The Woodlands ZIP codes

USPS’s own ZIP Code FAQ says it directly: “ZIP Codes do not necessarily adhere to city or municipal boundaries.” A ZIP code is built around a post office and its carrier routes. Each ZIP has one default city name, and many have additional acceptable names the post office will also deliver to.

We checked ZIP databases built from USPS city-state data for every ZIP that touches The Woodlands. They agree that The Woodlands is an accepted city name across the area. They don’t all agree on which name comes first: some list Spring, Conroe or Magnolia as the main name for these ZIPs, others list The Woodlands. That disagreement is exactly why you should check your own address with USPS before you print anything.

The 77381 to 77393 rows come from zip-codes.com’s Spring list; the linked ZIPs go to their individual records. ZIPs don’t follow village lines either. One example: HAR lists Sterling Ridge homes in 77354 under Magnolia, even though they’re squarely inside The Woodlands.

Two caveats. Third-party ZIP sites lag USPS updates, so confirm your exact street address with the USPS ZIP Code lookup, which returns the standardized address and the recognized city names. And “accepted” isn’t the same as “the only right answer.” For a Woodlands business in 77380, “The Woodlands, TX 77380” and “Spring, TX 77380” will both get your mail. You just have to pick one.

Which city name should a Woodlands business use?

Our rule is simple. If The Woodlands is an accepted USPS city name for your ZIP and your location is physically in The Woodlands, use “The Woodlands.” It’s what customers say, it’s what they search, and it’s what your signage and lease probably say already.

Exceptions we’d make:

  • You’re in Shenandoah or Oak Ridge North. You’re in an actual city with its own permits and taxes. Using “The Woodlands” in your mailing address is allowed in ZIPs where USPS accepts it, but your city name in business filings and permits should be the real municipality. We usually keep Shenandoah on the profile for these clients and say “next to The Woodlands” in the copy.
  • You’re in Spring proper, Conroe proper or Magnolia. If your ZIP doesn’t list The Woodlands as acceptable, don’t use it. Full stop.
  • Your address is already established everywhere as Spring. If ten years of citations, your bank, your state filings and your Google profile all say Spring, a switch has a cost. It can be worth doing; just do it once, everywhere, in one push.

Whatever you pick, the name has to match your Secretary of State filing, your bank, your lease and your Google profile closely enough that a verification reviewer sees one business. If you’re mid-move, read our guide to changing your business address on Google without a suspension before you touch the profile.

How Google Business Profile handles your address

Google’s rules on addresses are short and strict. Per Google’s guidelines for representing your business, use “a precise, accurate address and/or service area,” and P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations aren’t acceptable. A virtual office, meaning a rented address where you don’t actually operate, isn’t eligible for a Business Profile. A co-working space only qualifies if you have clear signage, receive customers there during business hours and keep it staffed.

Two things to know about the city field specifically:

  1. Google places the pin by geography, not by your city label. Ranking leans on distance, which Google describes as how far the business is from the person searching, per Google’s page on local ranking. Typing “The Woodlands” instead of “Spring” doesn’t move your pin one foot.
  2. Google may display its own locality. In profiles we manage, Google sometimes shows a different city name on Maps than the one the owner entered, based on its own map data. Don’t fight it with repeated edits. Repeated address edits are one of the things that trigger re-verification.

What you control is consistency and relevance: the same name, address and phone everywhere; a primary category that matches what you do; and a website that clearly says where you are and who you serve. Our guide to ranking in the Houston map pack goes through the rest.

Storefront, service area or hybrid?

A lot of the “which city” anxiety is really a service-area question. A plumber based in a Conroe home office wants to show up for “plumber The Woodlands.” The fix is a service area, and a fake Woodlands address makes things worse.

Google’s rules, from Google’s service area help page and Google’s guidelines for representing your business:

  • If you don’t serve customers at your address, remove the address from your profile and set a service area instead.
  • You can set up to 20 service areas by city, postal code or other area.
  • The overall area shouldn’t extend more than about 2 hours of driving time from where the business is based.
  • Hybrid businesses, which serve customers at the address and also go out to them, can show both.

To edit it: open your Business Profile, choose Edit profile, then Location, and edit the Service area. Google says the service area “helps people find your Business Profile.” It doesn’t say it boosts ranking in every listed town, and in our experience it doesn’t. A Conroe-based service business will still rank best near Conroe. The way to compete in The Woodlands is with reviews from Woodlands customers, a strong Woodlands service page and, often, paid search covering the gap.

Your website and citations: one version, everywhere

Citations are mentions of your name, address and phone on other sites: Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places, the Chamber directory, Nextdoor, industry directories. Google says prominence draws on information from across the web, like links, articles and directories, per Google’s page on local ranking. Consistency matters most here, because these sites copy each other.

On your own website:

  • Put the exact chosen address in the footer and on the contact page, as text rather than an image.
  • Use LocalBusiness structured data with a PostalAddress that matches. Google’s LocalBusiness structured data docs list address and name as required properties and the address as “the physical location of the business.” Use the same city there that you use everywhere else.
  • Write naturally about the area. “Our office is off Research Forest, five minutes from Market Street” helps people and search engines more than a footer stuffed with every ZIP from 77380 to 77393.

Cleaning up citations is tedious but finite. Start with the big data sources and the sites that rank on page one for your business name, then work down. Don’t create a second listing to “cover” Spring; you’ll just make a duplicate that Google may merge or suspend.

Spring vs. The Woodlands in search: what customers actually type

The city on your address and the city in a customer’s search don’t have to match, and often won’t.

Someone in Creekside Park has a Spring mailing address and still searches “pediatrician The Woodlands.” Someone in Oak Ridge North might search “Spring” or “The Woodlands” depending on which side of I-45 they think of as home. Someone in a Conroe ZIP near the north end of The Woodlands probably doesn’t search “Conroe” at all.

So don’t pick your address city to chase keywords. Pick it for accuracy and consistency, then cover the search terms with content:

  • Your homepage and service pages can say “serving The Woodlands, Spring, Shenandoah and Conroe” once, in a sentence a human would write.
  • Your Google Business Profile description can name the areas you serve in plain language.
  • Your reviews will do some of this for you. A customer writing “best plumber in Spring” on a Woodlands profile helps more than anything you’d type yourself, which is one more reason to ask every customer.

To see what people actually search before they find you, open Google Search Console, go to Performance, and filter queries containing “woodlands,” then “spring,” then “conroe.” In accounts we’ve looked at, the split is rarely what the owner expects. That report tells you which area page to write next, and which one you don’t need.

If you’re already listed as Spring

Switching an established Spring listing to The Woodlands is optional, not urgent. If you decide to do it, do it in this order: confirm the new format with USPS, update your state filing and bank if needed, update Google Business Profile once, update the website footer, contact page and schema the same day, then work through the citation sites over the next few weeks. Expect Google to ask for re-verification. Don’t make other profile changes (name, category, phone) in the same week, so that if something goes sideways you know what caused it.

And if your address is fine as Spring, leave it alone. A consistent Spring address beats an inconsistent Woodlands one every time.

What not to do: fake addresses and city-page spam

Every few months a business owner asks us to set up a Woodlands address “just for Google.” These are the versions we hear, and why we won’t do any of them.

  • Virtual offices and mailbox stores. Ineligible under Google’s guidelines, and the most common reason we see listings suspended. Search Engine Land lists virtual offices, P.O. boxes and UPS Store boxes among the main suspension causes.
  • An employee’s house. If you don’t serve customers there and there’s no signage or staff, it’s not your business location.
  • City names in the business name. “Smith Plumbing The Woodlands” is not your name. Google’s guidelines don’t allow location information in the name unless it’s part of your real-world brand.
  • Copy-paste city pages. Google’s spam policies name “pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page” as doorway abuse, and “blocks of text that list cities and regions” as keyword stuffing. A real page about your work in Creekside Park, with real photos and reviews, is fine. Thirty near-identical pages aren’t.

Frequently asked questions

Is The Woodlands, TX a city?

No. The Woodlands is an unincorporated master-planned community governed by The Woodlands Township, a special-purpose district. Voters chose in November 2021 to keep that model instead of incorporating. It spans Montgomery and Harris counties, and the counties handle roads and law enforcement. Nearby Shenandoah, Oak Ridge North and Conroe are incorporated cities, while Spring is an unincorporated area served by the Spring post office.

Which ZIP codes accept The Woodlands as a city name?

ZIP databases built from USPS data list The Woodlands as an accepted city name for 77380, 77381, 77382, 77384, 77385, 77386, 77389 and 77354, plus the P.O. box ZIPs 77387 and 77393. Some databases list Spring, Conroe or Magnolia first and others list The Woodlands, so confirm your exact street address with the USPS ZIP Code lookup, since third-party ZIP sites can lag behind postal updates.

Should my Google Business Profile say Spring or The Woodlands?

Use the city name that USPS accepts for your ZIP and that matches where your business really is. For a location inside The Woodlands, we usually recommend The Woodlands. Then use that exact address everywhere, including your website, citations and business filings. The city label does not move your map pin, and Google ranks by distance and relevance, so consistency matters more than the label.

Can I use a virtual office address in The Woodlands for Google?

No. Google’s guidelines say a virtual office, meaning a rented address where your business does not operate, is not eligible for a Business Profile. P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes are not acceptable either. Co-working spaces only qualify with clear signage, staff on site and customers received during business hours. If you serve customers at their locations, hide your address and set a service area instead.

How do I show up in The Woodlands if my office is in Conroe or Spring?

Set a service area in Google Business Profile that includes The Woodlands, but expect to rank best near your actual location, since distance is a ranking factor. Build a genuine Woodlands service page with local projects and photos, ask Woodlands customers for reviews, and consider paid search for Woodlands keywords. Do not create a fake address or a second listing to cover the area.
