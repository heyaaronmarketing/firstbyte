“Can I run TikTok ads only to people in The Woodlands, for like $10 a day?”

It's one of the most common TikTok questions Houston owners ask. The answer is yes to the first half and no to the second. TikTok will let you target a handful of ZIP codes. It won't let you spend $10 a day doing it.

The short answer: TikTok ads for a Houston or Woodlands business start at a $50 daily campaign budget and a $20 daily ad group budget, so a real local test runs about $1,000 to $1,500 a month. You can target ZIP codes, cities, counties or the Houston DMA, but you can't exclude areas. It works best for visual businesses whose customers are under 50.

This is a setup guide. It walks through Ads Manager in the order you'll click through it, with the settings we'd pick for a local business and the places TikTok's defaults will cost you money. How much do TikTok ads cost for a Houston business?

TikTok doesn't publish average prices, and the third-party “average CPM” numbers floating around rarely say where they came from. What TikTok does publish are the floors. Per TikTok's budget help page, a campaign's daily budget has to be more than $50 and an ad group's daily budget more than $20. On a lifetime budget, the ad group minimum is $20 times the number of days in the flight, so a 31-day ad group needs at least $620.

Those floors shape everything. One campaign at the minimum is roughly $1,500 a month. Split it into two ad groups (Spring and Klein ZIPs in one, The Woodlands and Shenandoah in the other) and each gets thinner.

A few budget rules from the same page that trip people up:

  • Keep the campaign budget at or above the ad group budget, or delivery gets weird.
  • While an ad group is in its learning phase, raise the budget no more than 40% at a time. After it exits, no more than 30%. Wait at least two days between changes.
  • You can't switch between daily and lifetime budgets once something is live. Pick daily for a first test.

The learning phase matters for small accounts. TikTok's learning phase article says volatility starts to come down after about 25 results or 7 days. If you're optimizing for leads and you get four a week, you'll be “learning” for a long time. That's why we often start local accounts on a more frequent event, like landing page views, and move to lead optimization once there's volume.

Is your customer on TikTok?

Before any setup, check the age math. Pew Research Center found 37% of U.S. adults use TikTok, up from 21% in 2021. Use falls hard with age:

That's national data, and your own customer list is the better guide. Still, a business that sells mostly to people over 60 will find more of them on Facebook and YouTube.

Businesses we think are good TikTok candidates in the Houston area:

  • Restaurants, bars, coffee and dessert spots. The food is the ad. A 12-second pour or a cheese pull near Market Street sells itself. Our restaurant marketing post covers the rest of the mix.
  • Med spas, salons, lash and brow studios, gyms. Before-and-after (where it's allowed), trainer tips and treatment walk-throughs. See med spa marketing for the policy side.
  • Visual home services. Pressure washing, pool remodels, epoxy floors, landscape lighting. Satisfying transformations are basically a TikTok genre.
  • Boutiques and local retail, especially with new arrivals weekly.

Weaker fits: estate planning attorneys, commercial B2B, anything sold to a procurement department, and emergency services where the customer is searching on Google with water on the floor. Someone whose slab leak just started isn't scrolling for inspiration.

Setting up TikTok Ads Manager, click by click

Here's the order we'd set things up in, before spending a dollar.

1. Create the account at ads.tiktok.com. Use a company email that won't leave when an employee does, and add a second admin. Set the time zone to Central so daily budgets and reports line up with your business day instead of someone else's midnight.

2. Connect the pixel before the first campaign. The path, per TikTok's pixel setup guide: Tools > Events Manager > Connect Data Source > Web, then enter your URL and pick Partner Integration (Shopify and Google Tag Manager are both listed) or Manual Setup. TikTok recommends setting up the Events API alongside the pixel, which sends events from your server and survives ad blockers and browser privacy settings better.

3. Pick an objective. Go to the Campaign tab and click + Create. TikTok's guide to choosing an objective lists Reach under Awareness; Traffic, Video views, Community interaction and Branded Mission under Consideration; and App promotion, Lead generation and Sales under Conversion. For most local businesses it's one of three:

ObjectiveUse it whenSkip it when
ReachYou want as many locals as possible to see a new location, menu or offerYou need leads this month
Video viewsYou're building a warm audience to retarget laterThe video is weak; you'll just pay to prove it
TrafficYou have a fast mobile landing page and no reliable conversion event yetYour site takes four seconds to load on a phone
Lead generationYou sell consultations, estimates or appointmentsNobody can call the lead back within the hour
SalesOnline booking or ecommerce, with the pixel firing on the confirmation pageFewer than a handful of conversions a week

4. Set up the ad group. This is where placement, targeting, budget, schedule and optimization live. Leave TikTok as the placement and turn off the partner app placements for a first local test; it keeps the reporting readable. Set location (next section), age if your business genuinely has a floor (a med spa's 18+ is reasonable), and leave interests broad. TikTok's delivery does a decent job of finding the right people inside a small area, and stacking interests on top of ten ZIP codes starves the ad group.

5. Build the ad. Upload videos or add Spark posts, write the caption, choose a call to action and a destination. Use UTM parameters on every URL so Google Analytics can see the traffic. Then click Publish all and expect a review before ads go live.

Local targeting in Houston and The Woodlands: what TikTok can and can't do

TikTok's location targeting page lists the U.S. options: country, state, DMA region, county, city and ZIP code. That's better than many owners expect. But there are three rules every Houston advertiser needs to know before setup:

  1. No exclusions. “TikTok does not support the exclusion of locations or zip codes.” If you target Harris County, you get all of it, from Baytown to Katy. To leave something out, you have to build the area from smaller pieces.
  2. No ZIP+4, and some ZIP codes may not be targetable at all.
  3. Small areas cost more. The page warns that targeting small areas or small sets of ZIP codes can significantly reduce reach and may raise your cost per result.

Radius and pin targeting aren't mentioned on TikTok's page, so don't plan around a 5-mile circle like you would on Meta or Google. ZIP codes are the tool. Up to 3,000 selections per ad group, so you'll never run out.

How we'd build a few common service areas:

  • The Woodlands core: 77380, 77381, 77382, 77384, 77385, then 77389 if you want Creekside Park. Add 77354 for Magnolia and 77386 for Spring north of the Grand Parkway if you serve them.
  • North Houston suburbs along I-45: the list above plus Spring (77373, 77379, 77388) and Conroe ZIPs, rather than the Houston DMA, which drags in counties you'll never drive to.
  • West side: Katy and Cypress ZIP codes for a Grand Parkway corridor business. Don't use “Katy, Texas” the city alone; many Katy addresses sit outside city limits.

Size check. Ten Woodlands-area ZIPs is plenty for $50 a day. Three ZIPs at $50 a day means the same few thousand people see your ad until they're sick of it, so watch frequency and widen the area early.

One more limit: TikTok's location page notes some advertisers fall under its housing, employment and credit policy. Home builders, apartment communities, lenders and job ads should expect fewer targeting options.

Spark Ads: put money behind real posts

If you take one thing from this guide, make it this. TikTok's Spark Ads overview describes Spark Ads as a native format that lets you advertise organic TikTok posts, with the views, likes and follows credited to the original post. The ad shows your real handle and a real follow button and reads like any other post on the For You feed.

Two ways to use them:

  • Your own posts. Post organically first, see which videos hold attention, then boost the winners. Your organic account becomes the test kitchen.
  • Creator or customer posts. A local creator or a happy customer posts about you, then authorizes you to run it as an ad. Per the Spark Ads creation guide, the creator taps the three dots on the video, taps Ad settings, and taps Generate to create a code. In Ads Manager you open the TikTok posts tab in the creative section, click Add post, and paste it. You can add up to 20 codes at once, and the authorization period shows under each post.

The catch: a Spark Ad's display name and text come from the original post and can't be edited in Ads Manager. If the caption says “OMG best tacos in Katy!!” that's your ad copy. Agree on captions with creators before they post, and put the agreement (pay, usage period, whether you can edit) in writing. Our UGC vs. influencers post covers what those deals usually look like.

Lead generation with Instant Forms, and keeping the leads real

For appointment businesses, TikTok's lead forms can work. They can also fill your CRM with people who tapped through without reading. The setup, from TikTok's lead generation setup guide: choose the Lead generation objective, set the ad group's Optimization Location to Instant Form, then create or attach a form in the ad's Destination section.

The setting that matters most is the form type. Per the Instant Form help page, More Volume is the default and skips any review screen. Higher Intent adds a review screen and a CAPTCHA after someone taps Submit. For a local service business, we pick Higher Intent nearly every time. Fewer leads, but you can call them back without wasting a morning.

Other form choices we'd make:

  • One qualifying question that a casual tapper won't bother with. “Which service are you interested in?” with real options, or “What part of town are you in?” with The Woodlands, Spring, Conroe, Tomball and “Somewhere else.”
  • The required privacy section: your company name and a link to a real privacy policy page on your site.
  • CRM sync. TikTok supports real-time CRM sync, and leads are also available by CSV download. CSV downloads mean someone has to remember to check, and a lead from Tuesday that gets a call on Friday is mostly gone.

Speed is the whole game with social leads. These people weren't searching for you; they were watching dog videos. Call within five minutes if you can and text right away. What creative works for local businesses on TikTok

On TikTok the ads that work look like posts. The ones that flop look like TV commercials somebody shrank down.

The technical floor from TikTok's in-feed ad specs: vertical 9:16 is recommended (at least 540 x 960 pixels; we shoot 1080 x 1920), files up to 500 MB, and videos up to 10 minutes. Non-Spark captions can't include clickable links, @ mentions or hashtags. Most local ads we'd run are 9 to 30 seconds.

Hooks we've seen hold attention for Houston service businesses, all of them specific to a place or a moment:

  • “This is what three Houston summers do to a pool deck.” (Then the pressure washer.)
  • “Things I'd never let a patient do before a filler appointment.” (A med spa nurse injector, talking to camera.)
  • “New on our menu this week, and yes it comes with queso.” (Owner holding the plate, Market Street patio behind.)
  • “You asked what a [your price] bathroom refresh looks like in Spring. Here.” (Before, after, price on screen.)

Say the town out loud. A viewer in Kingwood who hears “Kingwood” in the first two seconds stops scrolling at a much higher rate in our experience than one who hears “the Greater Houston area.”

Music: business accounts should use the Commercial Music Library, TikTok's pre-cleared library of about a million songs for organic posts, video ads and branded content. TikTok says businesses can't use the general music library commercially. That trending audio your 22-year-old employee wants to use is often off limits for a business ad.

Plan on fresh videos every two to four weeks; a small ZIP set sees the same ad a lot. Ten rough verticals beat one polished spot here.

Pixel, Events API and measuring TikTok honestly

TikTok's reporting is generous to itself, like every platform's.

Start in Events Manager. After the pixel is in, verify it with the TikTok Pixel Helper Chrome extension and the Test Events tool, then check the Diagnostics tab, all described in TikTok's pixel setup guide. Set up events that mean money: a booked appointment, a submitted estimate form, a completed order. A “ViewContent” event on your homepage is not a conversion.

Then cross-check outside TikTok:

  • UTMs into Google Analytics, with TikTok traffic as its own source. Our GA4 tracking guide covers forms and calls.
  • Branded search. Most TikTok viewers don't tap the ad. They remember your name and Google it later. Watch branded searches in Google Search Console and branded clicks in Google Ads during the test, by week.
  • Ask at intake. “How did you hear about us?” with TikTok as a choice, on the form and at the front desk. Crude, and it catches things no pixel will.

Give a test 45 to 60 days before you judge it. One week of TikTok data at $50 a day is mostly noise, especially when the algorithm is still in its learning phase.

When TikTok ads aren't worth it for a Houston business

We'd tell you to skip TikTok ads, or wait, if any of these are true:

  • Your budget is under about $1,500 a month in total marketing. At that level, Google Business Profile, reviews and a small search campaign almost always come first.
  • You can't produce new videos at least monthly. One ad on repeat will burn out in a few weeks.
  • Your customers are searching, not browsing. AC repair in August, emergency plumbing, towing. Those are Google problems.
  • No one answers leads quickly. TikTok leads go cold faster than search leads do.
  • Your landing page is slow or confusing on a phone. Nearly every TikTok click is on a phone.

And if what attracts you is the format more than the app (short video, sound on, local targeting), YouTube Shorts gets you vertical video inside Google's ad system with ZIP and radius targeting. Our YouTube ads cost guide for Houston covers that side, and we'd often run the same videos on both.

A first 30 days on TikTok ads

How we'd run the first month for a typical Woodlands or north Houston business:

  • Days 1 to 7: Pixel and Events API installed and tested. Organic account posting three to five times. Ten vertical videos shot in one session.
  • Days 8 to 21: One campaign, one ad group, 8 to 15 ZIP codes, $50 a day. Three to five videos running, preferably as Spark Ads of your best organic posts.
  • Days 22 to 30: Cut the weakest two videos. Add a lead form or switch to the Sales objective if your event volume supports it. Compare branded search and “how did you hear” answers with the month before.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum budget for TikTok ads?

TikTok requires a campaign daily budget above $50 and an ad group daily budget above $20, according to its Business Help Center. On a lifetime budget, each ad group needs at least $20 times the number of days it runs, so a 31-day ad group needs $620. In practice, a meaningful local test for a Houston business is about $1,000 to $1,500 a month for two months.

Can I target TikTok ads by ZIP code in Houston?

Yes. TikTok lets U.S. advertisers target by state, DMA, county, city and ZIP code, with up to 3,000 location selections per ad group. It does not support excluding locations or ZIP codes and does not support ZIP+4. Very small sets of ZIP codes can limit reach and raise costs, so most Woodlands or Houston businesses do better with 8 to 20 ZIPs than with two or three.

Are TikTok ads worth it for a small business in The Woodlands?

They can be for visual businesses with customers under about 50, like restaurants, med spas, salons, gyms, boutiques and transformation-style home services. They're a weak fit for emergency services, B2B and businesses that can't make new videos monthly. If your total marketing budget is under about $1,500 a month, put the money into Google Business Profile, reviews and search first.

What are Spark Ads on TikTok?

Spark Ads let you run an organic TikTok post as an ad, either from your own account or from a creator who authorizes it with a code. The ad shows the original handle, and the engagement counts toward the original post. The caption and display name come from the post and can't be edited in Ads Manager, so agree on captions with creators before they post.

Do TikTok lead ads give good leads for service businesses?

They can if you set them up for quality. Choose the Higher Intent form type, which adds a review screen and CAPTCHA, include one real qualifying question such as service type or neighborhood, and sync leads to your CRM so they get a call within minutes. Default settings and slow follow-up are the usual reasons TikTok leads disappoint local businesses.
