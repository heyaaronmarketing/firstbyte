Ask three Houston production companies to quote “a 30-second commercial” and you can easily get numbers like $1,800, $9,500 and $38,000. All three can be legitimate. None of them is necessarily wrong.

They were quoting three different jobs that happened to share a name. That's the problem with asking what video production costs.

The short answer: in Houston and The Woodlands, expect roughly $500 to $2,500 for a freelancer half day with simple editing, $3,000 to $12,000 for a one-day shoot with a small crew and a finished spot plus cut-downs, and $15,000 to $75,000 and up for a scripted commercial with talent, locations and a full crew. Phone-shot content can cost almost nothing but time.

Those are our planning ranges from quotes we've seen and bid, not a survey. The rest of this guide is about what moves a quote up or down, so you can compare bids that look nothing alike.

Video production price tiers in Houston

Most local business video falls into one of four tiers. The big difference between them is how many people are on set and how many days the job takes. Camera gear matters less than people think.

For a sense of labor costs behind those rates, the Bureau of Labor Statistics put the national median wage for camera operators at $74,990 and for film and video editors at $75,420 in May 2025, and about 35% of people in both jobs are self-employed. Houston has a deep freelance pool thanks to its energy, medical and news sectors, which keeps day rates more reasonable than in Los Angeles or New York.

Where should a local service business start? Usually tier one or two. A plumber, a dental office or a gym in Spring gets more from 12 honest clips a quarter than from one beautiful spot a year, especially on social. Production money earns its keep when the video plays on a TV screen, when your brand sells on polish (luxury remodels, custom homes, plastic surgery), or when one spot will run across YouTube, streaming TV and maybe broadcast for a long time.

What drives the cost of a video shoot?

When two bids for the same idea are $8,000 apart, it's almost always one of these.

Crew days. The single biggest line. A one-day shoot with four people costs far less than a two-day shoot with eight. Ask every bidder how many shoot days and how many crew they're planning, by role. The cheapest way to cut a budget is to fit the shoot into one day, which usually means one or two locations near each other.

Pre-production. Scripts, storyboards, location scouting, casting. Cheap bids often skip this, and you pay for it on set when nobody knows what the next shot is.

Locations. Your own office, shop or job site is free. Renting a home in The Woodlands for a day, a restaurant before opening, or a studio in the Heights adds a location fee, and anything on public property adds permits and insurance (next section).

Talent. Real employees and customers are free and usually better. Paid actors add day rates, and union talent adds usage fees tied to where and how long the ad runs. Always ask whether the quote covers usage for your planned run.

Post-production. Editing, color, sound mix, motion graphics, captions. Make sure the quote states how many revision rounds are included; two is normal. A third and fourth round at hourly rates is where budgets quietly double.

Deliverables. One 30-second horizontal spot is one edit. The same spot plus 15- and 6-second cuts, vertical versions for TikTok and Reels, and a captioned version for the website is six or more. Each costs editing time, so list them up front.

Houston weather. Summer heat and afternoon storms push outdoor shoots into early mornings, and a June shoot in Katy needs a rain date in the contract. Hurricane season runs through November. Ask who pays when weather cancels a day.

Do you need a film permit in Houston or The Woodlands?

It depends on where you're standing. There's no statewide permit; the Texas Film Commission's permitting overview says the Texas Film Commission doesn't issue them and that public property like sidewalks, streets and parks is usually handled by the city. Filming entirely on private property needs only a location agreement with the owner, unless your crew or gear spills onto public space.

Inside City of Houston limits. The Houston Film Commission's production FAQ asks productions filming on general public property to submit a registration form before production, which gets a registration number you keep on set. You also need a liability insurance certificate of one million dollars per incident on file before filming. Blocking a lane or controlling traffic means hiring City of Houston police officers through the office. Most city parks need a separate permit, amplified playback needs a noise permit, and squibs or pyrotechnics need a Fire Marshal on set. The FAQ doesn't publish fees or lead times, so call them at 713-853-8386 early.

In The Woodlands. The Woodlands has been a Film Friendly Texas community since September 2020, and the Film Commission runs through Visit The Woodlands. Per The Woodlands Area Film Commission's FAQ, a permit is needed when filming is commercial, will be seen by the public and takes place on property including public spaces. Apply through the online application with a certificate of insurance. The current FAQ asks for at least 5 business days' notice, an older version said 10, and complex or multi-location shoots need more, so we plan on two weeks. Traffic interruptions need off-duty officers, paid directly by you, and road closures go through Montgomery or Harris County depending on the spot.

Unincorporated areas (much of Spring, Cypress, Klein and Magnolia) fall to the county for roads, and HOA-run parks and pools have their own rules. Shopping centers like Market Street and Hughes Landing are private property; ask management in writing.

State parks are slow. The Texas Parks and Wildlife Department typically needs several weeks, so Lake Houston Wilderness Park or Brazos Bend is not a next-Tuesday idea.

Drones are their own category. Anyone flying for a business needs an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate, and each drone must be registered ($5, good for three years), per the FAA's commercial operator page. A lot of the Houston area sits under controlled airspace around Bush Intercontinental, Hobby, Ellington, Sugar Land Regional and Conroe-North Houston Regional, which means an airspace authorization, usually through LAANC. Ask any drone operator for their certificate number and proof of insurance. A cousin with a DJI doesn't count.

Deliverables checklist: what to get in writing

Most disputes over video come from vague scopes. Before you sign, the quote should name every one of these:

Two items owners forget most. First, raw footage. Many shops keep it unless you ask, and it's the cheapest way to make more cuts later. Second, usage rights. You should own the finished videos outright or have a perpetual license for your business, and any paid talent or music license should match where the video will run. A spot licensed for “social media only” can't go on streaming TV without another check.

Music licensing basics for business video

Music is where small businesses get burned, usually months later when a video gets muted or a letter shows up.

A recorded song usually has two copyrights. The U.S. Copyright Office explains that a musical work is the composition and lyrics, while a sound recording is the specific recorded performance, and they're often owned and licensed separately. Putting a hit song in your ad means clearing both, which is a negotiation with a publisher and a label, priced well beyond most local budgets.

How to get more for the same video budget

A few habits that stretch a Houston video budget further than haggling over day rates:

  • Batch. One well-planned day can feed a quarter of social posts, a spot and a website video. Three separate half days cost more and look less consistent.
  • Shoot vertical and horizontal on purpose. A second camera rotated for 9:16 costs a little extra crew time and saves a reshoot for TikTok and Shorts.
  • Write the script around what you already have. Your location, your trucks, your staff. A rented mansion in Carlton Woods doesn't sell AC repair.
  • Test cheap, then produce. Run phone versions of three messages as ads for a few weeks. Spend production money on the one that got calls. Our YouTube ads guide and TikTok ads setup guide cover how to run those tests.
  • Plan the edits before the shoot. Every cut-down and vertical version you list up front is cheaper than one requested after the edit is locked.

Should you hire a production company or a marketing agency?

A pure production company makes the video and hands it to you. An agency with in-house creative writes the script around a campaign, shoots it, and then runs and measures the ads. The second costs more per video and usually less per customer, because the edit is built for the placement from the start.

Either way, ask for a reel with work for businesses like yours, a line-item quote with crew roles, shoot days, revision rounds and deliverables, and a straight answer about who owns the footage.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 30-second commercial cost to produce in Houston?

A simple 30-second spot shot in one day by a small Houston crew, with a few cut-downs, often lands between $3,000 and $12,000 in our experience. A scripted commercial with actors, multiple locations and a full crew commonly runs $15,000 to $75,000 or more. The biggest factors are crew size, number of shoot days, talent usage and how many edited versions you need.

Do I need a permit to film a commercial in Houston?

If you film on public property inside City of Houston limits, the Houston Film Commission asks for a registration form and a liability insurance certificate of one million dollars per incident before production. Parks, amplified sound, pyrotechnics and traffic control need extra permits or hired officers. Filming entirely on private property usually needs only the owner's written permission. Call the Film Commission to confirm your plan.

Do I need a permit to film in The Woodlands, TX?

Usually yes for commercial filming on property including public spaces. The Woodlands Area Film Commission, run through Visit The Woodlands, takes applications online with a certificate of insurance and asks for at least five business days of notice, more for complex or multi-location shoots. Shoots that interrupt traffic must hire off-duty officers, and road closures go through Montgomery or Harris County.

How much does a customer testimonial video cost?

A testimonial video filmed by a Houston freelancer in a half day, with two or three customers and a short edit for each, often costs $800 to $3,000 in our experience. Costs rise with multiple locations, a second camera, lighting and more edited versions. Prepare open-ended questions, get signed releases and ask for vertical cuts so the clips also work on social media.

Can I use popular music in my business's video ads?

Not without licenses for both the composition and the recording, which are often owned separately and rarely affordable for local ads. Use music you're licensed for instead: TikTok's Commercial Music Library for TikTok, the YouTube Audio Library for YouTube videos, or a paid library whose plan covers paid ads and the channels you'll use, such as streaming TV or broadcast.
