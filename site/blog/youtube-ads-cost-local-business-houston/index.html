A roofer in The Woodlands spends $1,500 on a YouTube ad, gets 31,000 views and doesn’t get a single call he can trace to it. He decides YouTube doesn’t work. Maybe it doesn’t, for him. More often the ad ran across half of Texas with the logo showing up at second 27, and nobody measured anything past views.

On YouTube ads cost for a local business, the short version is that you pay per view or per thousand impressions, and format and targeting move the price a lot. In WebFX’s survey of 270 marketers, the most common answer (43% of respondents) was $0.11 to $0.40 per view. A useful local test runs about $1,000 to $1,500 a month for two or three months. Less than that and you mostly learn what a small sample looks like.

The rest of this covers format pricing, keeping ads inside your service area, the opening seconds of the ad, and measuring results without kidding yourself.

How much do YouTube ads cost?

There’s no price list. YouTube is an auction, and what you pay depends on how you buy. Google’s video ad formats page spells out the billing for each format.

Skippable in-stream ads on cost-per-view bidding charge you when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad if it’s shorter) or interacts with it, whichever comes first. Skip at second 6 and you pay nothing. Switch the same ad to Target CPM or conversion bidding and you pay per impression, skipped or not.

  • Bumpers and non-skippable ads are always per impression (CPM).
  • In-feed video charges when someone clicks to watch.
  • Shorts ads on CPV count a view at 10 seconds, at the end of the ad, or on a click, like, comment or share.

In our experience, tightly targeted local campaigns cost more per view than national ones, because you’re competing with every advertiser who wants Montgomery County homeowners. Fine. A $0.25 view in Spring beats a $0.04 view in Lubbock.

Discovery campaigns are gone. Google’s Discovery upgrade page says remaining Discovery campaigns were automatically upgraded to Demand Gen between January and March 2024. Video action campaigns followed: per Google’s upgrade notice, new ones couldn’t be created after April 2025, existing ones were auto-upgraded in batches in September 2025, and the last stragglers were scheduled for April 2026. Video views, Video reach or Demand Gen: which campaign type?

Inside Google Ads, the format choice mostly comes through the campaign type and its bidding.

Video views uses Target CPV. Google’s page on Video views campaigns describes it as getting more views at a lower cost, spread across in-stream, in-feed and Shorts. You set an average amount you’re willing to pay per view and some views cost more or less. For a local business testing YouTube, we usually start here, mostly because you only pay for people who actually watched.

Video reach uses Target CPM and aims for unique people at a set frequency. Good for bumpers and for covering an area before a busy season, like HVAC before the first 95-degree week.

Demand Gen runs video and images across YouTube, Discover and Gmail with conversion bidding: Target CPA, Maximize conversions and their value-based versions. On paper it’s the lead-generation option. In practice, read Google’s Demand Gen budget guidance before you commit. It recommends a budget of at least 20 times your target CPA for conversion bidding, at least $100 a day per ad group to avoid a cold start, and $500 a day per ad group for “effective and efficient performance.”

Run that for a business with a $100 target cost per lead and you get a $2,000 budget recommendation per campaign before Google’s algorithm is even comfortable. Plenty of local businesses can’t feed Demand Gen enough conversions to learn, and a starved Demand Gen campaign tends to wander. We’d rather see a $1,200 Video views test plus solid remarketing than a $1,200 Demand Gen campaign that never exits learning.

Later, with dependable conversion tracking (qualified calls, booked appointments, CRM imports) and money to spare, it’s worth a look.

YouTube ads for local business: targeting by radius and ZIP code

Local YouTube campaigns flop on geography more than anything else. Google’s location targeting help gives you the tools, but the defaults weren’t built for a plumber in Tomball.

On every local video campaign we set these:

  1. ZIP codes or a radius instead of “Houston.” Radius targeting needs at least 1 km. ZIP codes work in the US, and bulk entry wants the state included (“77381, Texas”). For a Woodlands business we’ll often use 77380, 77381, 77382, 77384, 77385 and 77389 plus Spring and Conroe ZIPs, rather than a circle that drags in half of north Houston.
  2. Location options set to Presence. “Presence or interest” includes people who are merely interested in your area. Google’s help page suggests Presence when you only want people physically in your locations, which describes almost every service business.
  3. A radius that matches how far you’ll actually drive. If your techs won’t go south of Beltway 8, don’t pay for views there, and add negative locations for anywhere you can’t serve. Cheap insurance.
  4. A look at the Locations report after week one, filtered by matched location. Surprises show up fast.

Houston’s sprawl matters here. Someone living in Kingwood who commutes down 59 to Greenway Plaza watches YouTube at home in the evening. For most home and personal services, target where people live. For lunch spots near the Energy Corridor or Hughes Landing, it’s the reverse.

YouTube remarketing and Customer Match

The cheapest, most reliable YouTube spend for a local business is usually remarketing: showing video to people who already visited your site or watched your channel. They already know your name, so a 20-second clip with a couple of reviews and an offer doesn’t have to do much.

Link your YouTube channel to Google Ads, and you can build YouTube data segments for people who watched any video on your channel, watched specific videos, subscribed, visited the channel page, liked a video or saved one to a playlist. Combine those with website visitors from your Google tag, and you have a warm audience that grows every time someone finds you.

Customer Match (uploading your customer emails or phone numbers) is more restricted than people assume. Google’s Customer Match policy requires a good history of policy compliance and payment. It also still lists 90 days of account history and more than $50,000 in lifetime spend for using lists in the “Targeting” setting, while every compliant account can use them for Observation and Exclusions. Check what your own account allows before planning around it. Excluding current customers from a new-customer offer is useful even if you can’t target them.

Cap frequency (a few impressions per person per week is plenty) and rotate creative every six to eight weeks. Our retargeting explainer covers the setup across Google and Meta.

What makes a YouTube ad work: the first five seconds and the rest

Every skippable ad is a five-second pitch for the right to keep talking. Google’s ABCD reference guide distills its YouTube ad research into specific advice:

  • Introduce your brand or product in the first five seconds, with visuals or audio.
  • Use more than two shots in the first five seconds. A static opening frame loses people.
  • Brand mentions land better when spoken by someone on screen than by a voiceover.
  • Make the call to action both audible and visible.

The same guide cites Nielsen Catalina Solutions research, across nearly 500 campaigns, attributing 49% of sales contribution to creative versus 36% to media. Google notes the findings are correlational. Still, it matches what we see: we’ve never watched a targeting tweak rescue a weak ad.

For Shorts, Google’s Shorts ads guide is clear: vertical 9:16 video is best, horizontal video shows with blurred bars, under 60 seconds is recommended, and for action-focused ads 10 to 30 seconds works best. It also says ads with sound (music, voiceover or both) have “been shown to increase conversions by over 20%” and recommends ads that blend in with organic Shorts. Google’s guidance doesn’t say much about captions, but we burn them in on every ad anyway. Plenty of people watch on a phone with the sound off in a waiting room or at the kids’ practice.

A decent local 30-second ad, roughly:

  1. Seconds 0 to 5: the problem in the customer’s words, the owner or tech on camera, logo and name visible. “If your AC is blowing warm air in Spring this week, this is for you.”
  2. Seconds 5 to 20: one reason to choose you. A real job, a real review, a guarantee. Not a list of eight services.
  3. Seconds 20 to 30: what to do next, said out loud and on screen. “Call or book online, same-day in The Woodlands and Spring.”

Shooting on a phone or hiring a production company?

It comes up in almost every first meeting. Start with a phone, and pay for production later where it earns its keep.

A modern phone, a $30 clip-on mic, daylight and a steady hand produce video that fits Shorts and in-stream perfectly well. People on YouTube are used to creators shooting on phones. An owner talking straight to the camera in the shop or in front of a finished job often outperforms a glossy spot, because it looks like the content around it. Shoot five or six versions of the opening line in one session and test two of them.

Production is worth paying for when the ad will run on TV screens (shaky phone footage looks worse at 65 inches) or when your brand sells on polish, like luxury remodels, med spas and custom homes. It also makes sense if you’ll run one spot for a year across YouTube, CTV and maybe broadcast, or if phone tests already found a message that works and you want a better version of it.

We’d skip the $10,000 brand film before you know which message makes people call. Spend the first few hundred dollars learning, then spend on production. (Our creative team does both, and we’ll still tell you to start with the phone.)

YouTube on the TV screen (CTV)

YouTube isn’t only phones. According to Nielsen, YouTube accounted for 12.4% of all TV viewing time in the US in April 2025, the largest share of any media company that month. Advanced Television’s report on Nielsen’s May 2026 Gauge puts it at a platform-best 13.8% in May 2026.

On a TV, nobody clicks. The ad is closer to a TV commercial that you can target by ZIP code, which is genuinely new for a business that could never afford Houston broadcast. You can segment by device in Google Ads and check how much of your spend lands on TV screens versus phones.

TV views look great on reports and are hard to tie to leads, so we judge them on branded search, direct traffic and calls from the targeted ZIPs. Keep the phone number and a short URL on screen long enough to read from a couch. Longer than feels necessary.

If TV screens are the main reason you’re interested, compare YouTube with other streaming buys. Our post on whether streaming TV ads are worth it for small businesses walks through that, and our CTV and streaming service covers buying across Hulu, Roku and the rest.

How to measure YouTube ads without fooling yourself

YouTube reporting is generous with credit, so know which column you’re reading.

Engaged-view conversions count when someone watches a meaningful chunk of your ad and converts later without clicking. Per Google’s page on engaged-view conversions, that’s 10 seconds for skippable in-stream (or the full ad if shorter), 5 seconds for in-feed, and 5 seconds or a click for Shorts. The default window for website conversions is 3 days.

View-through conversions are looser: someone saw the ad, didn’t interact, and converted later. Google’s page on view-through conversions notes they’re not included in the main Conversions column. They show in their own column and in All conversions. Report them separately or you’ll double count what Search already gets credit for.

Brand Lift studies survey people who saw your ad against people who didn’t. Google’s Brand Lift page calls it free, but it has a spend requirement, runs over about 10 days, and isn’t available to every account; you need to go through a Google rep. We rarely see a local budget that qualifies.

For local businesses we lean on cruder tools:

  1. Track calls and forms properly first. If your Google Ads conversions aren’t clean, YouTube numbers are meaningless.
  2. Watch branded search volume and Business Profile calls in the targeted ZIPs before and during the campaign.
  3. Run a simple geo split: advertise in one set of ZIPs (say Spring and Klein) and hold back a similar set (say Humble and Atascocita) for six to eight weeks, then compare leads per household. It’s rough, but it’s honest.
  4. Ask new customers how they heard about you, and log it.

Are YouTube ads worth it for a small business?

Sometimes. We lean yes when most of these hold:

  • Your Google Search and Business Profile are already working, and you want more people searching your name.
  • Your average job or customer value is high enough that a few extra customers a month pays for $1,000 or more in media.
  • You can show what you do on camera: a finished pool, a before-and-after, a tech explaining a repair.
  • You serve a defined area you can target by ZIP code.
  • You’ll give it at least two to three months and measure it with something beyond views.

We’d hold off if your search campaigns are still leaking money, your website doesn’t convert on mobile, or you need leads next week. YouTube builds demand that Search then captures, and if the capture side is broken, YouTube just makes the leak bigger. So we usually look at search first.

Frequently asked questions

How much do YouTube ads cost per view?

There's no fixed price because YouTube runs an auction. In a WebFX survey of 270 marketers, the most common answer was $0.11 to $0.40 per view. With cost-per-view bidding on skippable ads you pay only when someone watches 30 seconds, the whole ad if shorter, or interacts. Tightly targeted local campaigns often pay more per view than broad national ones.

Are YouTube ads worth it for a small business?

They can be when your search marketing already works, your customer value is high, and you serve an area you can target by ZIP code. They are a weak fit if you need leads this week or your website and search campaigns are leaking money. Plan on two to three months of testing and measure branded searches, calls and leads, not views.

What is the minimum budget for YouTube ads?

Google Ads lets you start with a small daily budget, but a useful local test usually needs around $1,000 to $1,500 a month for two to three months. Demand Gen campaigns need more: Google recommends at least 20 times your target cost per acquisition and at least $100 a day per ad group to avoid a slow start.

Can I target YouTube ads to specific ZIP codes in Houston?

Yes. Google Ads supports US ZIP code targeting and radius targeting with a minimum radius of 1 kilometer. For local businesses, set the location option to Presence so ads reach people physically in the area rather than anyone interested in Houston, and check the locations report after the first week to confirm where the ads actually ran.

Do YouTube ads show on TV?

Yes. YouTube ads run on connected TVs as well as phones and computers, and YouTube is now the largest single source of TV viewing time in the US according to Nielsen. On a TV nobody clicks, so use a produced horizontal ad with your phone number on screen and judge results by branded searches and calls from the targeted ZIP codes.
